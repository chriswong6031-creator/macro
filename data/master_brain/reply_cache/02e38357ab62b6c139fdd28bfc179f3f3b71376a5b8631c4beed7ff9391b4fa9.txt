### 2. The Honest Case for Staying
This desk must argue the strongest honest case **for** staying with the trend. That case rests on three pillars:

**(a) The only validated edge is drawdown control, not return forecasting.**  
The detector’s backtest explicitly states: the absolute‑trend gate is *validated_risk_control* — it materially lowers volatility and shallower drawdowns/tail risk. On this market, `gate_helps` is **true**, meaning the discipline cut drawdown historically. Momentum rank is merely a focus lens (rank‑IC ~0). Therefore, adhering to the trend‑gate discipline is the rational action, not rotating out because something else looks “cheap” or “due.”

**(b) The leader exhibits a durable, trending, uncrowded profile.**  
Tech Hardware & Semis satisfies the core conditions for the gate:
- Durability bar **0.64** — well above the 0.5 threshold, indicating the trend is robust.
- HURST “trend” — no mean‑reversion behaviour, so the gate is unlikely to whip.
- Not crowded — overcrowding flags (which only cap, not fade) are absent.

**(c) No confirmed handoff exists.**  
The #2 challenger (Conglomerates & Exchange, +0.588) is tagged “mean‑revert,” not a trend candidate. #3 High‑Dividend Blue Chips (durability 0.68, trend) is a distant third in score and has not overtaken the leader. Guardrails forbid predicting the next theme; we only act on a **confirmed** leadership handoff. None is present. Thus the discipline requires staying with the incumbent.

- **Breadth/absorption** deterioration (already narrow) is a caution, but not a fade signal. Guardrails: “Do NOT fade the dominant theme on a crowding flag — cap it, do not fade it.”
