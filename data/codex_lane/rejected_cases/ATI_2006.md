# ATI 2006 — winner autopsy

## 1. Bottom line

ATI's 2006 alpha was a cycle upswing with a mix upgrade, not a single earnings surprise: tight titanium and nickel markets met accelerating commercial-aerospace demand, while Allegheny Technologies shifted investor attention from commodity stainless exposure toward scarce, higher-margin titanium, nickel-alloy and superalloy capacity. The move had already begun before the January 4 mechanical onset, but successive public evidence made the earnings power less dismissible: 2005 sales and profit acceleration, Q1 and Q2 margin expansion, a self-funded titanium-sponge build, and finally a roughly $2.5 billion, nine-year Boeing supply agreement. Cash turnover repeatedly expanded and the January breakout held into a 153% calendar-year gain, far beyond XLI or SPY. What ATI failed to sustain was the initial vertical pace: a violent May–June drawdown showed commodity-cycle sensitivity, even though later earnings and contracted aerospace runway rebuilt the trend.

## 3. Catalyst ladder

- **2006-01-05 — other / operating update.** ATI filed an 8-K containing Items 2.02, 2.06, 8.01 and 9.01, one day after the mechanical onset. The local event store supplies the filing and acceptance anchor, but the exhibit is not represented in the checkout; its precise headline is therefore null rather than inferred ([SEC ATI filing archive, filing dated 2006-01-05](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/browse/?CIK=1018963&owner=exclude&action=getcompany); `data/edgar/earnings_8k_dates.parquet`).
- **2006-01-25 — earnings.** ATI furnished year-end results: Q4 sales were $894.4 million and net income $120.4 million; full-year sales rose nearly 30% to $3.54 billion, operating profit reached $533 million/15% of sales, and management said high-performance growth investments should begin helping in 2H06 ([AIST reproduction of company release, published 2006-01-26](https://www.aist.org/allegheny-technologies-announces-strong-year-end-results); filing date 2006-01-25 in `data/edgar/earnings_8k_dates.parquet`).
- **2006-04-26 — earnings.** Q1 sales reached $1.0405 billion versus $879.6 million and operating income $169.0 million versus $74.5 million; High Performance Metals margin rose to 34.6% from 24.2% ([SEC 10-Q, filed 2006-05-05](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018963/000095012806000074/j1998901e10vq.htm); earnings 8-K filed 2006-04-26 in `data/edgar/earnings_8k_dates.parquet`).
- **2006-06-26 — capital investment.** ATI announced a new premium-grade titanium sponge facility in Rowley, Utah, with 24 million pounds of annual capacity, extending its vertical integration into a constrained aerospace input ([USGS contemporaneous quarterly summary citing ATI's 2006-06-26 release](https://apps.usgs.gov/minerals-information-archives/magnesium/mis-2006q2-magne.pdf)).
- **2006-07-26 — earnings.** Q2 sales rose 34% to $1.21 billion, net income rose 53% to $140.4 million, and segment operating margin reached 21%; High Performance Metals margin was 34.5% ([Business Wire release, published 2006-07-26](https://hk.marketscreener.com/quote/stock/ATI-INC-11627/news/Allegheny-Technolog-Allegheny-Technologies-Announces-Strong-Profitable-Growth-in-Second-Quarter-2-222242/); filing anchor in `data/edgar/earnings_8k_dates.parquet`).
- **2006-10-16 — contract win.** Boeing and ATI disclosed a long-term agreement worth roughly $2.5 billion for titanium products from 2007 through 2015, turning part of the aerospace-cycle thesis into contracted runway ([Seattle Post-Intelligencer, published 2006-10-16](https://www.seattlepi.com/business/article/Boeing-secures-titanium-supply-1217353.php); [company release reproduction, published 2006-10-16](https://www.marketscreener.com/quote/stock/ATI-INC-11627/news/Allegheny-Technolog-Allegheny-Technologies-and-Boeing-Sign-Long-Term-Titanium-Products-Supply-Agr-316935/)).
- **2006-10-25 — earnings.** ATI filed its Q3 earnings 8-K. The repository supplies a same-day filing anchor, but the precise release metrics were not recoverable from the selected source set, so they remain null (`data/edgar/earnings_8k_dates.parquet`; [SEC company filing archive, accessed 2026-07-17](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/browse/?CIK=1018963&owner=exclude&action=getcompany)).
- **2006-10-11 — research echo.** Merrill Lynch published a more favorable ATI rating and an $85 target, explicitly framing the revision as a valuation decision after the stock had risen about 50% since Q2 results ([TheStreet, published 2006-10-11](https://www.thestreet.com/investing/stocks/wednesdays-analysts-upgrades-and-downgrades-10314305)). This is not an operating catalyst, but it documents the lagged re-underwrite.

`data/edgar/material_8k_events.parquet` has no ATI rows for this era; absence here is a coverage gap, not evidence that no material events occurred.

## 4. Stage anatomy

- **Compressed prior — present.** ATI entered 2005 after a 2003 loss and only $19.8 million of 2004 net income; by onset, the market was confronting a much faster earnings transition. Price itself was not deeply compressed immediately before t0—the earlier July 2005 episode already showed strength—so the compression was primarily expectations/fundamentals, not a pristine flat base.
- **Catalyst ladder — present.** Year-end proof, two accelerating quarterly prints, titanium capacity, and the Boeing agreement progressively converted spot-cycle strength into higher-value and longer-duration earnings runway.
- **Relative breakaway — present.** The first 63/126/252-session closing highs coincided on January 4; forward excess reached +33.44pp at 21 sessions and +63.79pp at 63 sessions versus XLI.
- **Liquidity confirmation — present.** Onset dollar volume was more than four trailing-21-day standard deviations above its mean, with further $0.55–0.68 billion sessions in May, June, November and December. Cash volume cannot identify buyer direction.
- **Options convexity — unknown.** No 2006 options history exists in the repository; per-ticker history begins in 2026-06, and there is no current ATI GEX snapshot in the specified surfaces.

## 5. Fundamentals arc

The PIT-gated repo panels do not cover the episode: ATI begins in 2009 in `data/edgar/fundamentals_panel.parquet`, 2009 in `data/edgar/statements_quarterly.parquet`, 2008 in `data/edgar/eps_quarterly.parquet`, and only 2020 in the current ATI slice of `data/edgar/statements.parquet`. Consequently, no 2006 values from those files are used as if contemporaneously available.

Contemporaneous filings nevertheless show the arc. The January year-end release said 2005 sales rose from $2.73 billion to $3.54 billion, while operating profit reached $533 million/15% of sales and net income rose from $19.8 million to roughly $360 million ([published 2006-01-26](https://www.aist.org/allegheny-technologies-announces-strong-year-end-results)). Q1 then delivered 18.3% sales growth and more than doubled operating income; High Performance Metals margin expanded 10.4 percentage points to 34.6% ([10-Q filed 2006-05-05](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018963/000095012806000074/j1998901e10vq.htm)). Q2 sales grew 34%, pretax income more than doubled, and high-performance margin stayed at 34.5% ([published 2006-07-26](https://hk.marketscreener.com/quote/stock/ATI-INC-11627/news/Allegheny-Technolog-Allegheny-Technologies-Announces-Strong-Profitable-Growth-in-Second-Quarter-2-222242/)). Runway came from self-funded titanium/nickel capacity and then the 2007–2015 Boeing contract.

The 2006 annual report, published after the run and used only as ex-post corroboration, records sales of $4.9 billion, segment operating profit of $1.059 billion, net income of $571.9 million, 68% of sales from high-value products, and net debt falling to $51.3 million from $197.7 million; capital expenditures rose to $235.2 million from $90.1 million ([2006 annual report, filed/published 2007](https://www.annualreports.com/HostedData/AnnualReportArchive/a/NYSE_ATI_2006.pdf)). These figures cannot explain trades made before publication, but they show that the earnings/mix thesis did mature during the year.

## 9. Hazards & false-positive checks

- **meme_squeeze — ruled_out.** The episode predates modern meme mechanics and had a year-long ladder of reported profits, capacity investment and a major customer contract; short-interest history is unavailable, so a squeeze contribution cannot be measured.
- **one_day_binary — ruled_out.** Multiple earnings and strategic disclosures extended the re-underwrite, and the stock's forward excess persisted through 252 sessions.
- **sector_beta — ruled_out.** ATI beat XLI by 140.40pp and SPY by 139.32pp over calendar 2006; broad industrial beta is insufficient.
- **options_mirage — possible.** Historical options evidence is absent, so derivatives amplification cannot be tested. Durable cash-market relative strength makes options unnecessary to explain the move, but absence is not a negative finding.

Current hazards are not inferable for the 2006 episode from the approved historical options surfaces: **extension** was clearly present by May 2006 and produced a sharp drawdown; **call walls** are unknown; **catalyst exhaustion** became possible after Q3/Boeing as the thesis moved from discovery to execution. The deeper structural hazards were commodity input/pricing reversals, aerospace-cycle duration, and execution risk on a rapidly expanding capital program.
