## 1. Bottom line

BIDU's 2010 alpha came from a rare double re-underwriting: Google publicly put its China search franchise at risk on January 12, 2010, while Baidu's February 9, 2010 Q4 release showed that the Phoenix Nest ad-platform transition had not broken monetization and was already supporting growth and margins. The tape then kept getting new proof points: Q1 revenue and operating profit accelerated on April 28, Baidu announced a 10-for-1 ADS-ratio change the same day, Google moved China search to Hong Kong on March 22, and Baidu's Q2/Q3 prints showed that the first-quarter surprise was a business run-rate change rather than a one-off. The main reason the move sustained was that the story migrated from "Google may leave China" to "Baidu's paid-search engine is scaling faster with better unit economics," while FXI/SPY could not replicate that company-specific acceleration.

## 9. Hazards & false-positive checks

- meme_squeeze: ruled_out. The episode predates the modern social squeeze complex, BIDU was a large liquid ADR, and the run had repeated public fundamental and competitive catalysts rather than message-board mechanics.
- one_day_binary: ruled_out. February 10 mattered, but the 63d and 126d forward relative returns, April/Q2/Q3 earnings ladder, and ADS split show a multi-step repricing.
- sector_beta: ruled_out. FXI was +1.43% in 2010 while BIDU was +135.42%; SPY was +13.14%, so the move was not explainable as China beta or index beta.
- options_mirage: possible. Historical options evidence is unavailable, so options cannot be credited; absence of data also means a convexity contribution cannot be ruled out.
- Current hazards: extension after vertical move was real by May 2010; catalyst exhaustion risk rose after Google retained its China ICP license in July; call-wall/current GEX hazards are unknown because no BIDU current options snapshot exists in the repo.
