# QCOM 1999 — winner autopsy

## 1. Bottom line

QCOM's 1999 alpha was principally a re-underwriting of the economic model, not simply a dot-com/technology-beta move. Before the March breakaway, the company already had accelerating CDMA handset/chip volume and improving operating leverage; the March 24 Ericsson settlement then removed the patent/standards bottleneck, opened cross-licensing and sub-licensing rights, and enabled Qualcomm to divest the lower-margin infrastructure operation and concentrate on chips, handsets, and royalties. Follow-on proof points—stronger royalty and chip demand, closure of the Ericsson transaction, 3G chipset milestones, earnings, and later S&P 500 inclusion—kept expanding the addressable and margin narrative. The tape was correspondingly exceptional relative to both XLK and SPY, with durable forward excess, but the 1999 technology valuation regime and a stock split/index-flow component materially amplified the move.

## 3. Catalyst ladder

`data/edgar/material_8k_events.parquet` and `data/edgar/earnings_8k_dates.parquet` contain no QCOM filing anchors before 2001; this is a coverage gap, not evidence that no filings occurred. Dates below are publication dates.

- 1999-01-19 — **earnings** — Q1 revenue was $941m (+20% year over year), operating profit $77.9m (+47%), and the release cited record phone and MSM-chip shipments. This established that CDMA demand and margins were already accelerating. [Qualcomm release, published 1999-01-19](https://www.qualcomm.com/content/dam/qcomm-martech/dm-assets/documents/qc-earnings-release-q1-1999.pdf)
- 1999-03-24 — **partnership** — Ericsson and Qualcomm announced a global resolution: royalty-bearing cross-licenses, Qualcomm rights to sublicense specified Ericsson patents to ASIC customers, support for a single 3G CDMA standard, settlement of litigation, and Ericsson's purchase of Qualcomm's terrestrial CDMA infrastructure business. [Qualcomm release, published 1999-03-24](https://www.qualcomm.com/news/releases/1999/03/ericsson-and-qualcomm-reach-global-cdma-resolution)
- 1999-04-13 — **capital_return** — the board declared a 2-for-1 stock split, payable May 10. This improved trading accessibility but did not change the underlying economics. [Qualcomm release, published 1999-04-13](https://www.qualcomm.com/news/releases/1999/04/qualcomm-incorporated-declares-two-one-stock-split)
- 1999-04-20 — **earnings** — Q2 revenue rose 23% to $932m; excluding Ericsson-sale/restructuring charges, operating EPS was $0.82 and adjusted operating income was $115m (+125%). It also reported nine million MSM chips shipped in the quarter, a 1.7 ASIC book-to-bill ratio, and $77m of license/royalty/development revenue. [Qualcomm release, published 1999-04-20](https://www.qualcomm.com/media/documents/files/qc-earnings-release-q2-1999.pdf)
- 1999-05-23 — **ma_activity** — Qualcomm announced closing of the Ericsson infrastructure sale; the patent disputes were behind it and the infrastructure operation moved to Ericsson. [Qualcomm release, published 1999-05-23](https://www.qualcomm.com/news/releases/1999/05/qualcomm-and-ericsson-close-agreements)
- 1999-05-23 — **product_launch** — Qualcomm introduced the MSM5000 as its first chipset/software solution for the submitted third-generation CDMA air-interface standard, with field trials planned for early 2000 and commercial production after the trials. [Qualcomm release, published 1999-05-23](https://www.qualcomm.com/news/releases/1999/05/qualcomm-cdma-technologies-announces-worlds-first-third-generation-chip)
- 1999-07-12 — **index_inclusion** — Qualcomm said S&P had selected it for addition to the S&P 500 and that its proposed share issuance was designed partly to meet anticipated index-fund demand. [Qualcomm release, published 1999-07-12](https://www.qualcomm.com/news/releases/1999/07/qualcomm-file-registration-statement-issue-common-shares-decision-comes)
- 1999-07-19 — **earnings** — Q3 revenue reached $1.0bn; excluding non-recurring infrastructure-sale charges, record net income was $135m (+445% year over year), with increased ASIC, phone, and royalty revenue. [Qualcomm release, published 1999-07-19](https://www.qualcomm.com/media/documents/files/qc-earnings-release-q3-1999.pdf)
- 1999-09-14 — **guidance** — Qualcomm said fourth-quarter CDMA chipset and phone demand was stronger than Q3, royalty reports exceeded the prior accrual, and it expected operating earnings to meet or exceed the then analyst consensus of $0.87 per share. [Qualcomm release, published 1999-09-14](https://www.qualcomm.com/news/releases/1999/09/qualcomm-addresses-fourth-quarter-analysts-earnings-expectations)
- 1999-11-08 — **product_launch** — Qualcomm demonstrated HDR wireless-Internet technology, stated compatibility with existing cdmaOne, and described data rates up to 1.8 Mbps. [Qualcomm release, published 1999-11-08](https://www.qualcomm.com/news/releases/1999/11/qualcomm-unveils-wireless-internet-strategy)

## 4. Stage anatomy

- **Compressed prior — present, but not a depressed base.** The pre-t0 business was already growing: Q1 revenue +20%, operating profit +47%, and improving communications-system gross margin were public on January 19. The compression was strategic: infrastructure manufacturing, patent litigation, and standards uncertainty obscured the higher-margin chip/royalty model, rather than a low-volatility price base. [Q1 release, published 1999-01-19](https://www.qualcomm.com/content/dam/qcomm-martech/dm-assets/documents/qc-earnings-release-q1-1999.pdf)
- **Catalyst ladder — present.** The Ericsson agreement removed legal/standards friction and separated infrastructure from the chip/license thesis; Q2/Q3 results, 3G chip milestones, S&P inclusion and stronger-demand guidance extended the re-underwrite.
- **Relative breakaway — present.** At t0, excess was +32.63pp/21d and +67.63pp/42d versus XLK, with a fresh 63d high; by 63 sessions it was +81.25pp versus XLK and +85.93pp versus SPY. Sources: `data/research/winner_episodes.parquet`; `data/yahoo/{QCOM,XLK,SPY}.parquet`.
- **Liquidity confirmation — present.** t0 dollar-volume z21 was +4.24 and 5d/60d turnover was 2.21×; later 1999 produced repeated multi-billion-dollar dollar-volume sessions. This confirms tradeability/attention, not buyer identity. Sources: `data/research/winner_episodes.parquet`; `data/yahoo/QCOM.parquet`.

## 5. Fundamentals arc

Before t0, the January 19 Q1 release reported $941m revenue (+20% year over year), 32% gross margin versus 30%, $77.9m operating profit (+47%), and $0.65 diluted EPS; it reported more than 65 royalty-bearing licenses and higher licensee equipment shipments. During the run, the April 20 Q2 release reported $932m revenue (+23%), adjusted gross margin 34%, adjusted operating income $115m (+125%), $77m license/royalty/development fees (up 73% sequentially), nine million MSM chips shipped and a 1.7 ASIC book-to-bill ratio. The July 19 Q3 release reported $1.0bn revenue and, excluding non-recurring charges, $135m net income (+445%). The public evidence thus supports revenue growth, expanding operating profitability, royalty scaling, and a wider 3G runway; it does **not** support a fully standardized 1999 quarterly-series comparison from the repo. ## 7. Research echo

Contemporaneous public target coverage is thin, and the repo contains no analyst-target dataset. A retrospective December 23, 1999 report said First Call consensus had moved from a $71 twelve-month QCOM target on January 1 to $470 by late December—an unusually large catch-up rather than a stable published valuation framework. Qualcomm itself supplied a nearer-dated bridge on September 14: it expected Q4 operating earnings to meet or exceed the then consensus $0.87 per share after reporting stronger demand and royalties. The appropriate read is that the price repeatedly escaped published estimates as the chip/royalty/3G narrative broadened; the exact chronology of individual target revisions remains unavailable. ## 9. Hazards & false-positive checks

- **meme_squeeze — ruled_out.** The price move followed a dated legal/standards resolution and repeated revenue, margin, royalty, product, and guidance disclosures. It occurred in a speculative technology regime, but this source set does not establish a social-driven squeeze.
- **one_day_binary — ruled_out.** The initial reprice was followed by closure, Q2/Q3 earnings, product and standards milestones, S&P inclusion, and September demand/royalty guidance; the episode also has `clean_hold=true` and `blow_off=false` in `data/research/winner_episodes.parquet`.
- **sector_beta — ruled_out.** QCOM exceeded XLK by +32.63pp over 21 days and +67.63pp over 42 days, and SPY by +31.67pp and +64.51pp, respectively.
- **options_mirage — possible.** 1999 options data are absent, so convexity cannot be separated from cash-market repricing or late-cycle speculation.

Current hazards are separate from the episode: the current snapshot has a nearby $170 put wall, a $190 call wall above spot, high IV, and one day to expiry; these can produce extension/pinning volatility but are not evidence about 1999. The historical run also had late-1999 valuation/expectations risk and catalyst exhaustion risk once the Ericsson resolution and early 3G roadmap were broadly public.
