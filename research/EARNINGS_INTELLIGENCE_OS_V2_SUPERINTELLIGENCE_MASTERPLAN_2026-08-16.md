# 0. Executive ruling

The existing Earnings Intelligence estate contains valuable plumbing and a weak product.

It has:

- a large normalized transcript archive in Terminal;
- exact transcript spans, hashes, correction lineage and immutable evidence generations;
- an Earnings Wire archive;
- a Company Intelligence context object;
- ticker-dossier and Terminal projections;
- Stage Analysis earnings history;
- local Qwen qualitative analysis infrastructure;
- bounded Neural Web context;
- early company identity, event, document and release contracts;
- pieces of Group Reads, theme exposure and institutional context.

But the primary public product is intentionally a transcript-excerpt directory. The ticker module is a small synthesized teaser whose narrative claims are still not pinned to line-level evidence. The Terminal workspace is visually more substantial, but much of its “evidence” is metadata-level and several planned lenses remain shells, partials or specs. The current system does not yet provide the research workflow, cross-company intelligence, graph reasoning or forward-learning architecture described by the original program.

The V2 ruling is:

> Preserve the provenance substrate. Finish the intelligence system that substrate was built to support.

The product is no longer defined as “Earnings Wire” or “call records.” Those remain source and acquisition surfaces.

The canonical product is:

# **Mastermind Earnings Intelligence OS**

A unified event, research, graph and learning system that answers:

1. What happened?
2. What changed?
3. Why did it change?
4. What did management promise?
5. Are those promises being kept?
6. What are analysts pressing on?
7. What did management avoid, remove or quietly reframe?
8. Which companies, products, customers, suppliers and themes are implicated?
9. What did the market expect, and how did it react?
10. Which not-yet-reporting peers may receive positive or negative read-through?
11. What does this add to Mastermind's view of the company, group and market?
12. Can every material answer be verified?

# 1. Why earnings deserves a central lobe

Earnings is the recurring moment when several important information systems meet:

```text
reported financial truth
+ management's updated narrative
+ explicit forward guidance
+ analyst challenge and discovery
+ investor expectations
+ options-implied uncertainty
+ price and volume reaction
+ peer and supply-chain information transfer
+ changes in products, customers and strategy
```

Most stock products handle these as separate pages or vendors.

Mastermind's opportunity is to make the earnings event a **knowledge-producing junction** for the whole organism.

## 1.1 User value

A serious investor should be able to move from announcement to understanding in minutes rather than hours:

- see the exact numerical result and comparable basis;
- understand the material changes versus prior periods and prior guidance;
- inspect management's explanation;
- inspect the hardest analyst questions and response quality;
- compare the event with peers and prior events;
- follow citations directly into the source;
- ask grounded questions;
- save monitors and receive updates;
- see what the event may imply for companies that have not yet reported.

## 1.2 Neural Web value

Neural Web should receive a compact, point-in-time event representation containing:

- facts and deltas;
- guidance changes;
- narrative changes;
- Q&A pressure;
- management commitments;
- relationship and theme updates;
- market reaction;
- uncertainty and coverage;
- exact source handles.

It should not receive only a summary or six excerpts.

## 1.3 Prophet and research value

The product should create a forward-testable feature tape for four different questions:

1. **Fundamental surprise:** will the company beat, meet or miss decision-time expectations?
2. **Event reaction:** how will the stock reprice relative to the surprise, prior expectations and implied move?
3. **Post-event drift:** what happens after the first tradable reaction?
4. **Cross-company read-through:** which economically or narratively linked, not-yet-reporting companies may reprice or later confirm the same operating evidence?

These targets are separate. A company can beat earnings and fall. A peer can benefit from industry demand while losing share to the announcer. A customer can rise while a supplier falls. The system must represent direction, mechanism and uncertainty rather than reducing everything to one sentiment score.

## 1.4 Strategic moat

The moat is not summarization.

The moat is:

```text
complete event memory
+ exact evidence
+ company and security identity
+ narrative and commitment history
+ economic relationship graph
+ market/theme graph
+ peer reporting-wave intelligence
+ point-in-time expectations and reactions
+ correction replay
+ one object serving every Mastermind surface
+ a forward learning loop
```

Competitors generally possess only subsets of this combination.

The system should present one coherent Research OS with several lenses, not one page trying to show everything.

**Job:** understand the current reporting season and find where new information is concentrating.

First viewport:

- reports today / this week / completed season;
- source and processing freshness;
- beat/miss and guidance breadth;
- margin and demand breadth;
- strongest positive and negative narrative shifts;
- major reporting waves and unfinished peer sets;
- highest-evidence read-through events;
- new themes, new relationships and Q&A pressure clusters;
- alerts relevant to the user's watchlists.

Core lenses:

1. Calendar and event state.
2. Season scoreboard.
3. Guidance and expectations.
4. Narrative shifts.
5. Reporting waves.
6. Read-through graph.
7. Q&A pressure.
8. Topic monitors.
9. Market reaction and implied-move performance.
10. Saved searches and alerts.

## 3.2 Company Event Workspace

**Persona:** analyst researching one company/event.  
**Canonical host:** Mastermind Terminal, with a compact dossier projection in Macro.

Lenses:

- **Brief:** what happened, what changed, why it matters, uncertainty and next research action.
- **Results:** reported vs consensus, prior period, prior guide and deterministic calculations.
- **Guidance:** old range, new range, midpoint change, assumptions, qualifiers and basis.
- **Narrative:** language and strategic changes versus prior calls/filings/slides.
- **Q&A:** analyst topics, hardest questions, follow-ups, non-answers and response quality.
- **Commitments:** promises, targets and progress state over time.
- **Peers:** comparable facts, reporting-wave position and read-through implications.
- **Mentioned By / Relationships:** who the issuer names and who names the issuer.
- **Themes:** company evidence mapped to active Mastermind themes and subthemes.
- **Reaction:** expected move, realized move, volume, volatility and post-event path.
- **Transcript:** full normalized call with search, speaker, prepared/Q&A and exact citation anchors.
- **Slides:** searchable deck, key slides and prior versions.
- **Sources:** complete document manifest, versions, corrections, rights and missing-source state.
- **Ask Mastermind:** cited questions over the selected event, history, peers and pinned evidence.

## 3.3 Narrative Timeline and Management Commitment Ledger

The timeline should show:

- topics first introduced;
- topics strengthened or weakened;
- topics dropped;
- KPI definitions changed;
- targets raised, held, cut or withdrawn;
- deadlines moved;
- assumptions added or removed;
- analyst pressure increasing or fading;
- slide families appearing, changing or disappearing;
- commitments achieved, missed, modified, repeated, dropped or unverifiable.

This transforms transcripts from static documents into longitudinal accountability.

## 3.4 Q&A Intelligence

The Q&A system should model exchanges, not isolated sentences.

Each `qa_exchange` contains:

- analyst identity and firm when reliable;
- question topics;
- question polarity, uncertainty, complexity and specificity;
- requested metric or disclosure;
- follow-up count;
- management respondent and verified/source-asserted role distinction;
- answer spans;
- answer completeness;
- direct answer, partial answer, deflection, cannot-disclose, future-answer or non-answer classification;
- new information versus prepared remarks;
- contradictions or tension with prior statements;
- peer-topic cluster membership;
- uncertainty and validation state.

User-facing outputs:

- top debated topics;
- strongest analyst challenge;
- repeated unanswered questions;
- management defensiveness/evasiveness changes;
- questions unique to this company;
- questions spreading across the sector;
- direct links to the complete exchange.

## 3.5 Earnings Wave and Read-Through Workspace

An earnings wave is a point-in-time group of companies exposed to a common operating mechanism and reporting in a defined window.

Examples:

- hyperscaler AI capex;
- data-center power and cooling;
- memory pricing;
- consumer credit stress;
- airlines and fuel costs;
- homebuilding demand;
- defense procurement;
- pharmaceutical demand or trial catalysts;
- payments volumes;
- industrial automation.

A wave is not merely a GICS industry.

It may be formed from:

- industry/subindustry;
- explicit customer/supplier relationships;
- product or end-market overlap;
- shared topic and narrative exposure;
- correlated fundamental sensitivities;
- residual co-movement communities;
- analyst topic overlap;
- curated Mastermind themes.

The workspace should answer:

- who has reported and who has not;
- what evidence has accumulated;
- which metrics and narratives are confirming or diverging;
- whether the market already repriced each peer;
- which relationships imply positive, negative or ambiguous read-through;
- what conditions must hold for a catch-up thesis;
- what upcoming report will falsify or confirm the wave.

## 4.6 Read-through hypothesis object

```json

  "source_event_id": "...",
  "target_company_id": "...",
  "target_event_id": "scheduled_or_expected_event",
  "mechanism": "shared_end_market|customer_supplier|competitive_share|input_cost|topic_confirmation|other",
  "direction": "positive|negative|mixed|unknown",
  "source_facts": [],
  "relationship_paths": [],
  "historical_analogs": [],
  "market_incorporation": {},
  "known_at": "...",
  "authority": "research_only",
  "falsifiers": [],
  "status": "candidate|accruing|confirmed_context|falsified|expired"
}
```

The system must be able to say:

- **positive industry demand:** likely positive for several peers;
- **firm-specific share gain:** positive for announcer, potentially negative for competitors;
- **customer strength:** positive for exposed suppliers only if the spending category is relevant;
- **input-cost inflation:** negative for margin-sensitive users, potentially positive for upstream providers;
- **one-off accounting beat:** little or no read-through;
- **already priced:** mechanism may be real but catch-up opportunity may be absent.

# 5. Read-through and catch-up intelligence

## 5.1 Why the idea is valid enough to research

Academic evidence documents information transfer from earnings announcements to peers and economically linked firms, while also showing that the mechanism and timing matter. Industry-wide attributions can cause positive peer transfer; firm-specific competitive moves can cause negative transfer. Customer information can lead supplier returns, but attention around the supplier's own announcement can accelerate price discovery and reduce the anomaly.

Therefore the system must not use:

```text
announcer beat -> every peer bullish
```

It should test:

```text
specific event fact
+ explicit mechanism
+ verified exposure path
+ historical transmission behavior
+ target expectations
+ target report timing
+ current market incorporation
```

## 5.2 Candidate relevance decomposition

A transparent research-priority score may combine separately visible components:

```text
readthrough_relevance =
    economic_exposure
  × evidence_specificity
  × mechanism_transferability
  × target_expectation_sensitivity
  × target_report_proximity
  × historical_pair_or_wave_transfer
  × information_not_yet_incorporated
  × source_confidence
```

This is a research-priority measure, not a return probability.

Each component is separately inspectable.

## 5.3 Economic exposure

Potential evidence:

- disclosed customer/supplier share;
- product/end-market overlap;
- geography and segment overlap;
- competitor relationship;
- common customer or input;
- management references;
- correlated segment fundamentals;
- validated relationship graph.

## 5.4 Mechanism transferability

Classify the source evidence:

- broad demand;
- pricing;
- input cost;
- inventory/channel;
- capex cycle;
- regulation;
- supply constraint;
- market share;
- company execution;
- accounting/tax/one-off;
- capital allocation;
- financing.

Broad mechanisms should transfer differently from firm-specific execution.

## 5.5 Information incorporation

Measure whether the target already moved through:

- event-window abnormal return;
- residual return relative to source/peer basket;
- options repricing;
- analyst estimate changes;
- news/attention intensity;
- volume and liquidity;
- gap versus historically expected transfer.

A potential catch-up candidate is a target with strong, transferable evidence and weak incorporation—not merely a stock that has not risen.

## 5.6 Earnings-wave state machine

```text
scheduled
 -> first_report
 -> evidence_accruing
 -> partial_confirmation
 -> broad_confirmation
 -> divergence
 -> completed
 -> graded
```

At each step, update:

- reported members;
- remaining members;
- cumulative evidence;
- confirmations and contradictions;
- expectation changes;
- peer repricing;
- next event/falsifier.

## 5.7 Product language

Before validation, the product may say:

- “read-through candidate”;
- “evidence may matter for”;
- “not yet reflected”;
- “confirmation is accumulating”;
- “competitive divergence”;
- “next report will test this mechanism.”

It may not say:

- “guaranteed rerating”;
- “expected return”;
- “high-conviction buy”;
- “probability” unless a calibrated model earned that label.

# 6. Truth and document plane

## 6.1 Canonical event

One issuer-keyed event survives ticker changes, share classes, source revisions and date corrections.

The event binds:

- scheduled/actual report time;
- release;
- Exhibit 99.1;
- 8-K;
- 10-Q/10-K;
- transcript revisions;
- presentation;
- audio metadata;
- consensus snapshot;
- market reaction;
- corrections.

## 6.2 Required source classes

Priority order:

1. company IR / SEC first-party documents;
2. properly licensed transcript/audio/consensus sources;
3. internal deterministic market data;
4. approved derived research objects.

Each source carries a rights profile covering storage, model processing, internal retrieval, end-user display, public excerpt, public derivative, export, embedding, retention and deletion.

## 6.3 Source spans

Citation addresses may be:

- transcript segment and time;
- paragraph/line;
- filing block;
- table cell;
- slide page/region;
- audio interval.

A verified text byte range does not automatically verify speaker role, semantic interpretation or relationship type. Attribute confidence must be explicit.

## 6.4 Deterministic facts

Calculate in code:

- actual/prior/consensus/guide deltas;
- percentages and basis points;
- guidance midpoints;
- margins;
- segment comparisons;
- implied and realized moves;
- relative and excess returns;
- mention counts and denominators;
- breadth/frequency metrics.

No model performs arithmetic when code can.

## 6.5 Corrections

A changed source revision must invalidate or regenerate:

- facts;
- claims;
- summary;
- graph edges;
- search index;
- dossier;
- Terminal cache;
- alerts;
- public stories;
- X/email derivatives;
- Neural Web context;
- ungraded forecasts that depend on the source.

The logical event identity remains stable.

# 10. Earnings Catalyst and learning system

## 10.1 Forward ledger

Every eligible candidate around earnings produces an immutable record containing:

- company/event identity;
- decision-time calendar vintage;
- Prophet/Stage state;
- consensus and revisions;
- guidance state;
- peer-wave evidence;
- options-implied move and skew when available;
- price/volume/relative-strength context;
- relationship and theme exposures;
- source availability and freshness;
