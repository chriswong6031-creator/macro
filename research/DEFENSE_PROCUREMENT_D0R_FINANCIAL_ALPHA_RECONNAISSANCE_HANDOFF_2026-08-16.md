# 8. Workstream D — Historical market behavior and event casebook

## D1. Research objective

Determine which defense-company events have historically mattered, on what horizons, for which archetypes, through which mechanisms, and under what expectations.

Do not assume:

- war is always bullish;
- contracts are always material;
- legal settlements always create buying opportunities;
- large selloffs always mean overreaction;
- backlog growth always becomes cash;
- procurement headlines dominate earnings and analyst revisions.

## D2. Academic research review

Review primary papers and document:

- sample, period, geography, method, outcome, limitations;
- whether the finding concerns returns, volatility, expected returns, forecast dispersion, or spillovers;
- whether it is event-specific or general;
- how it should influence architecture;
- what it does not justify.

Minimum topics:

- what drives large defense-stock moves;
- geopolitical acts versus threats;
- short versus long horizons;
- wartime demand and forecast uncertainty;
- regional and company heterogeneity;
- war/de-escalation event studies;
- contractor contagion and firm-specific attribution;
- political connections/lobbying as context;
- innovation/R&D and program positioning.

## D3. Minimum reviewed casebook

Build at least 60 event records as specified in V3, with balanced positive, negative, ambiguous, and correction cases.

Possible seed families:

- 9/11 and Iraq-war demand shocks;
- Russia's invasion of Ukraine;
- Wagner/de-escalation-shaped event;
- other conflicts with local versus global effects;
- major Golden Dome/missile-defense policy or contract changes;
- multiyear munitions/production agreements;
- FMS notifications and later conversions;
- program wins/losses and protests;
- major fixed-price/program charges;
- classified-program charge cases;
- shipbuilding guidance/FCF changes;
- legal/pricing/cyber/quality settlements;
- Challenger-style contractor contagion;
- production-capacity projects;
- supplier shortages;
- budget cuts/restructures/cancellations;
- M&A/divestiture and program-right changes.

## D4. Casebook selection law
