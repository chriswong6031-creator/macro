## 1. Executive ruling

Build the Marketing lobe.

Do not build it as a content scheduler attached to Neural Web. Build it as a sovereign, persistent business lobe whose job is to create, operate, expand, audit, and improve the entire growth system.

The Marketing lobe may:

- originate positioning, campaigns, narratives, public research, marketing-specific market views, lead magnets, offers, tools, channel strategies, and new acquisition mechanisms;
- create and retire departments, agents, engines, playbooks, experiments, and distribution surfaces;
- consume any useful Neural Web output while also conducting its own research;
- publish, engage, partner, allocate bounded budgets, and optimize without routine human approval;
- build new product surfaces when distribution requires a useful product rather than another advertisement;
- learn from acquisition, activation, paid conversion, retention, refund, and contribution-margin outcomes;
- widen its own operational authority when independently measured evidence supports doing so.

The Marketing lobe is not subordinate to Neural Web's market-signal constitution. It is a business-growth system connected to Neural Web, not another market-decision engine inside the scored path. Neural Web evidence is an advantage, not a creative ceiling.

There are only two non-negotiable operating truths:

1. **The lobe must know the consequence class of its actions.** A blog post, a personalized recommendation, an ad purchase, a creator contract, and a change to subscription terms are different commercial actions. The system should route each automatically, not pretend they are interchangeable.
2. **Self-improvement cannot mean self-concealment.** The lobe may rewrite its playbooks and create new teams, but it may not erase failed experiments, corrections, complaints, costs, or unfavorable cohort history.

Marketing has full editorial authority. It may make original claims, form its own market theses, and use Mastermind branding, charts, and graphics without stating that a Neural Web signal engine generated the content. “Mastermind” is the umbrella brand; it is not a source-provenance claim.

Provenance exists internally for learning, reuse, evaluation, and later correction. It is not a permission gate, it does not constrain what Marketing may claim, and it does not require a public label on ordinary Mastermind content. Only an explicit factual attribution such as “Neural Web emitted this signal” should point to that actual source.

Everything else should be governed autonomously by Fable, Opus directors, machine policy, bounded budgets, and an independent AI auditor.

### The strategic thesis

Mastermind should not market itself primarily as another large dashboard. It should become:

> **The accountable AI market-intelligence desk that shows what changed, why it matters, what the system believes, what would prove it wrong, and what changed afterward.**

The moat is not the volume of generated posts. The moat is the transformation of a large, living intelligence system into useful public products, auditable research objects, recurring decision support, and a distribution network that becomes more valuable each time the underlying system learns.

The desired flywheel is:

1. the market changes;
2. Neural Web and Marketing Intelligence detect an opportunity or question;
3. the Marketing lobe creates a useful public intelligence object;
4. that object travels through owned channels, search, creators, communities, bots, and embeds;
5. visitors receive value before being asked to pay;
6. the product remembers their interests and delivers another value moment;
7. activated users enter the paid product;
8. retention and contribution data teach the lobe which promises, audiences, products, and channels create durable customers;
9. Fable reallocates resources and creates, improves, merges, or retires departments.

This is not content marketing with agents. It is an autonomous growth institution.

## 2. The current-state reality

The opportunity is unusually large because the product already contains far more analytical material than its current growth system can package or distribute.

### 2.1 Existing strengths found in the repository

The current repository already provides:

- a broad static public site with approximately 1,372 HTML pages in the current main snapshot;
- a sitemap and robots file;
- 153 stock-brief JSON artifacts;
- a large Neural Web artifact graph registered through the signal bus;
- world state, evidence clock, daily brief, mechanism pathways, regime, rates, liquidity, breadth, factor, rotation, options, China, and cross-asset context;
- quantitative claim and outcome ledgers;
- an Ask endpoint with cited research retrieval;
- Supabase authentication and account scaffolding;
- static report publishing;
- one-way Neural Web and Mastermind context bridges;
- an adjacent Mastermind control plane with run events, locks, governance, guardrails, decision packets, and ledgers;
- a visible home-page “what changed” concept that can become the strongest public wedge.

The correct build strategy is **wrap and connect**, not replace.

### 2.2 The product is rich but the commercial journey is immature

The live product currently presents an enormous range of dashboards, countries, asset classes, reports, tools, and signals. That breadth proves capability but makes the primary paid job difficult to understand quickly.

The most important missing commercial infrastructure is:

- one canonical audience and first paid job;
- a legible free-to-paid journey;
- production billing, entitlement, refund, and subscription-state logic;
- a complete attribution and lifecycle event spine;
- activation milestones tied to retained value;
- a referral and partner ledger;
- a claim-provenance layer for public marketing;
- reusable distribution APIs and publication receipts;
- a durable correction and recall system;
- a machine-readable offer, price, eligibility, and disclosure registry.

Marketing scale before these foundations would produce traffic without reliable learning.

### 2.3 Neural Web output is valuable but not automatically publication-ready

The current daily brief is timely, but its status is warning-level and the cortex reports degraded because the model was unavailable. It also contains malformed contradiction objects with blank identifiers and summaries. Mechanism pathways correctly declare themselves display-only and not signals.

This does not limit the sovereign Marketing lobe. It means the lobe needs to distinguish:

- evidence it inherited;
- analysis it created itself;
- hybrid work that combines the two;
- preliminary material;
- validated material;
- content that has expired.

The solution is a **Marketing Provenance and Readiness Compiler** that improves reuse and learning without becoming a claim-permission gate.

### 2.4 Search infrastructure is present but underdeveloped

The site has a sitemap and robots file, but no JSON-LD structured data was found in the current HTML/template inventory. Many public surfaces exist, yet they are not organized around a finite set of high-intent user questions and canonical content objects.

The SEO advantage is proprietary intelligence, historical receipts, and continuously updated explanatory tools—not thousands of interchangeable “is ticker X a buy?” pages.

Google explicitly permits useful AI assistance but warns that generating many pages without adding value can violate its scaled-content-abuse policy. The Marketing lobe therefore needs an indexation governor that decides what deserves a permanent, crawlable page and what should remain transient, noindex, personalized, or private. See Google's [AI-content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content), [people-first content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content), and [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies).

### 2.5 The present report voice needs a unified claims policy

Some live reports use forceful language such as ranked buy lists, entry timing, sizing, and “what to own.” That may be commercially effective, but it is not currently connected to a consistent provenance, correction, audience, jurisdiction, or outcome framework.

The lobe should not suppress forceful content. Its internal operating record should make consequential claims:

- attributable;
- timestamped;
- scoped to an audience and jurisdiction;
- linked to evidence;
- linked to a counter-case or invalidation;
- connected to the later outcome;
- correctable everywhere it was syndicated.

This does not require public source disclaimers or pre-publication permission. It turns aggressive research into a compounding proof product.

## 3. Assessment of the attached proposal

The attached proposal contains many of the right raw materials. Its best insight is that one verified intelligence object should become an entire distribution bundle. Its central weakness is that it begins too close to channels and too far from the autonomous operating substrate.

### 3.1 Keep

Keep and elevate:

- Fable as persistent CMO orchestrator;
- Opus directors with durable departmental memory;
- an independent auditor;
- one insight transformed into channel-specific assets;
- a data newsroom;
- ticker dossiers;
- “why is my stock moving?”;
- creator copilot;
- creator and community revenue sharing;
- Discord and Telegram utility bots;
- event-sensitive public products;
- public SEO intelligence pages;
- share cards, PDFs, videos, embeds, and permanent URLs;
- newsletter syndication;
- retargeting and high-intent paid acquisition after organic proof;
- optimization for retained subscribers rather than trial volume.

### 3.2 Modify

Modify the following:

| Attached idea | Grandmaster-plan version |
|---|---|
| Ten always-on Opus departments immediately | Charter all departments, activate them in waves, and use Opus as directors while Sonnet/Haiku workers execute bounded tasks. |
| Content factory begins from a verified Neural Web object | Allow three provenance modes: Neural-Web-derived, Marketing-originated, and hybrid. |
| One numeric confidence field | Use evidence class, source lineage, market horizon, freshness, counter-case, claim owner, and calibration history. |
| Seven trading-day trial as a fixed rule | Make preview length and trigger an experimentable offer. Start with an activation-triggered preview; test seven trading days against fourteen calendar days. |
| SEO page for every possible ticker/topic | Use a finite set of high-value templates with an indexation and freshness governor. |
| Social account network | Begin with transparent desk identities and only add accounts when each has a distinct user promise, corpus, and audience. |
| Autonomous posting everywhere | Build channel-specific adapters that follow each platform's actual automation rules. |
| Creator sponsorship | Make Mastermind the creator's research infrastructure, then attach attribution and recurring economics. |
| Paid-ad scaling by immediate return | Scale on cohort contribution profit and retention, not first-month revenue or one-day ROAS. |
| CMO auditing and approving every item | Let low- and medium-consequence work self-publish. Reserve Fable and the AI auditor for exceptions, policy changes, and high-consequence actions. |

### 3.3 Defer until the machine can operate them well

Do not make these the first build:

- an army of automated X accounts;
- bulk Stocktwits posting;
- automated unsolicited replies or direct messages;
- hundreds of auto-indexed ticker pages;
- large paid budgets;
- bots whose only utility is a link back to Mastermind;
- personalized stock-picking services before the lobe has selected and implemented the appropriate commercial and regulatory posture;
- autonomous contracting before standard terms, budgets, counterparty checks, and a contract ledger exist.

These are not forbidden. They are later authority classes that should be earned by the operating system.

## 4. Positioning and the first paid job

### 4.1 Recommended category

Mastermind should claim a category that is broad enough to encompass its engines but narrow enough to understand:

> **Accountable AI market intelligence**

The short promise:

> **Know what changed, why it matters, and what to watch next.**

The proof:

> **Every important conclusion carries evidence, invalidation, timestamp, and outcome history.**

### 4.2 Initial ideal customer

Start with self-directed, research-intensive investors and active swing/position traders who:

- follow 10–100 securities or themes;
- feel overwhelmed by fragmented information;
- value causal explanations and regime context;
- are willing to pay for recurring monitoring;
- do not need a full institutional terminal;
- want accountability rather than a black-box score.

Do not try to speak equally to macro tourists, day traders, financial advisers, crypto-only traders, institutional PMs, students, and long-term passive investors on the same first visit.

### 4.3 The first paid job

The first paid job should be:

> **Continuously monitor what matters to my holdings and watchlist, tell me when the causal picture changes, and preserve the evidence trail.**

This creates a clean commercial bridge:

- public value: explain a market, event, sector, or ticker now;
- account value: remember what the user follows;
- preview value: monitor changes over time;
- paid value: deeper history, portfolio implications, alerts, cross-asset connections, and persistent intelligence.

### 4.4 Product hierarchy

The Marketing lobe should simplify the product into four layers:

1. **Public intelligence:** useful, crawlable, shareable objects.
2. **Personal workspace:** watchlists, saved dossiers, questions, alerts, and portfolio context.
3. **Paid desk:** continuous monitoring, deep history, cross-asset inference, premium reports, and advanced tools.
4. **Partner infrastructure:** creator copilot, bots, embeds, APIs, and co-branded reports.

## 6. Fable's CMO mandate

Fable is not the chief copywriter. Fable is the capital allocator, organizational designer, adjudicator, and keeper of the growth thesis.

### 6.1 Fable owns

- category and positioning;
- portfolio of audiences, offers, and channels;
- department creation, merger, suspension, and retirement;
- budgets and resource envelopes;
- model allocation by task;
- conflict resolution between growth, trust, retention, and cost;
- promotion of successful playbooks into autonomous operation;
- demotion of unstable playbooks into shadow mode;
- company-wide growth memory;
- cross-department goals and dependencies;
- the distinction between experiments and enduring product commitments.

### 6.2 Fable does not need to review

Once a lane has earned authority, Fable should not review:

- routine newsletter assembly;
- ordinary scheduled posts;
- rendering standard chart cards;
- approved email lifecycle messages;
- metadata updates;
- low-budget experiments within an existing envelope;
- response templates that remain inside a proven policy;
- SEO refreshes that pass the indexation governor;
- standard partner provisioning under pre-approved terms.

### 6.3 Autonomous department formation

Fable may create a new department when:

- an opportunity queue has remained material for a defined period;
- the work has a distinct objective and data contract;
- at least one existing department cannot absorb it efficiently;
- the economics engine estimates positive expected option value;
- an AI red-team finds no unresolved conflict with an existing department;
- Fable assigns a budget, director, launch authority, review clock, and retirement test.

Every department must have:

- a charter;
- one primary outcome;
- non-goals;
- inputs and outputs;
- decision rights;
- a budget;
- a model mix;
- a performance clock;
- a failure and retirement rule;
- an append-only activity ledger.

No department exists merely because an agent persona sounds impressive.

## 8. Model and agent topology

Use expensive intelligence where judgment compounds, not where volume is high.

### 8.1 Recommended model allocation

| Role | Default model class | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Fable CMO | Opus | strategy, allocation, organizational change, adjudication |
| Department directors | Opus | charter ownership, difficult decisions, approval of new playbooks |
| Independent auditor | separate Opus context | adversarial review and quarantine |
| Researchers and builders | Sonnet | research, synthesis, code, complex asset generation |
| High-volume workers | Haiku or deterministic engine | extraction, tagging, formatting, variants, monitoring |
| Scoring and accounting | deterministic code | budgets, attribution, freshness, identity, policy predicates |

### 8.2 Engine-agent separation

Every important capability should have:

- a deterministic engine for state, eligibility, cost, and audit;
- an agent for judgment, synthesis, and creative work;
- a typed contract between them;
- a replayable test set;
- a fallback when the agent is unavailable.

An agent should not be the database, scheduler, entitlement system, attribution model, or source of truth.

### 8.3 Department memory

Each department needs:

- immutable charter and version history;
- current strategy;
- hypotheses;
- active campaigns;
- prior experiments;
- winning and losing examples;
- audience knowledge;
- channel rules;
- cost history;
- incident and correction history;
- unresolved questions;
- next review date.

Memory updates must cite the run and evidence that caused the change.

## 9. Autonomous authority model

The goal is minimal human intervention, not minimal control.

### 9.1 Growth authority ladder

This ladder is independent from Neural Web's analytical authority ladder.

| Level | Authority | Typical examples |
|---|---|---|
| G0 Observe | read and diagnose | crawl site, inspect funnels, monitor trends |
| G1 Create | create drafts and prototypes | copy, scripts, landing pages, mock reports |
| G2 Shadow | run against live inputs without external effect | simulated campaign, private preview, dry-run partner research |
| G3 Publish | publish through owned, approved lanes | site, newsletter, established social formats |
| G4 Engage | interact and transact inside standard terms | replies, partner provisioning, affiliate offers |
| G5 Allocate | move spend and resources inside caps | paid tests, creator economics, model budgets |
| G6 Organize | create/merge/retire departments and rewrite playbooks | new regional desk, channel closure, model change |
| G7 Root | change legal entity commitments or root credentials | bank, binding signature, registration, identity proof |

### 9.2 How authority is earned

Fable grants a playbook wider authority when it has:

- passed shadow evaluations;
- produced clean publication receipts;
- stayed inside cost and policy thresholds;
- met minimum sample size;
- demonstrated positive incremental impact;
- avoided unresolved corrections or complaints;
- survived independent red-team review;
- shown a reliable rollback path.

Authority narrows automatically when:

- freshness or source quality degrades;
- platform warnings increase;
- complaint, correction, unsubscribe, refund, or chargeback rates rise;
- a channel produces low-retention customers;
- the playbook exceeds cost caps;
- the auditor cannot reproduce the claimed outcome.

### 9.3 Human-exception doctrine

Routine work has no human gate.

Human assistance is requested only when an AI cannot complete the external act itself, such as:

- identity or liveness verification;
- a signature legally required from a natural person or company officer;
- initial credential or payment-method provisioning;
- a bank, regulator, or platform appeal that requires the account owner;
- a genuinely unresolved catastrophic conflict that Fable and the auditor cannot adjudicate.

The exception queue must present one recommended action, the evidence, the cost of delay, and the smallest requested human action.

## 10. The Marketing Growth OS

This is the infrastructure Fable should build before scaling channels.

### 10.1 Core services

1. **Opportunity Bus**
   Receives market events, product events, search demand, social attention, competitor moves, user questions, creator needs, lifecycle triggers, and original agent ideas.

2. **Campaign Compiler**
   Converts an opportunity into an objective, audience, promise, proof, product destination, assets, channels, budget, experiment, and success clock.

3. **Department Registry**
   Stores charters, owners, workers, authority, budget, service levels, dependencies, and retirement tests.

4. **Identity and Audience Graph**
   Connects anonymous sessions, accounts, watchlists, referrals, partners, consent, lifecycle state, and cohorts without leaking private data into public content.

5. **Offer and Product Registry**
   Stores current plans, preview variants, eligibility, prices, benefits, limits, disclosures, landing pages, and effective dates.

6. **Provenance and Claim Passport**
   Records who or what created a claim, what evidence supports it, whether it is Neural-Web-derived, Marketing-originated, or hybrid, and where it was published.

7. **Asset Registry**
   Stores canonical assets, variants, rights, source objects, expiry, channel use, performance, and correction state.

8. **Publisher Adapter Layer**
   Gives each platform its own credentials, API, formatting, cadence, policy, idempotency, receipt, and takedown method.

9. **Experiment Registry**
   Stores hypothesis, unit, randomization, exposure, holdout, primary metric, guardrails, start/stop rules, and result.

10. **Economics and Budget Allocator**
    Tracks channel cost, inference cost, creator payout, discounts, refunds, data cost, gross margin, retention, and payback.

11. **Policy Compiler**
    Converts platform, brand, legal, privacy, and commercial rules into machine predicates and consequence classes.

12. **Correction and Recall Bus**
    Finds every derivative asset from a changed claim and updates, annotates, unpublishes, or republishes it.

13. **Learning and Playbook Registry**
    Promotes proven playbooks, demotes weak ones, and preserves the reason.

14. **Human-Exception Queue**
    Handles only the small residue that cannot be completed autonomously.

15. **Autonomous Product Delivery Foundry**
    Lets Marketing change the product when a campaign requires a better destination. It owns repository discovery, scoped build packets, clean branches and worktrees, implementation agents, tests, preview deployments, feature flags, canaries, merge evidence, release receipts, and rollback. It must preserve each repository's existing delivery and governance conventions while avoiding dependence on a standing human engineering queue.

16. **External Account Steward**
    Maintains account ownership, roles, credentials, API applications, verification state, quotas, warnings, renewals, and recovery instructions. It performs everything automatable and sends only identity, liveness, signature, or root-recovery steps to the human-exception queue.

## 11. The autonomous campaign loop

Every campaign should follow one replayable loop:

1. **Detect** an opportunity from market, search, users, creators, competitors, lifecycle, or an agent's original idea.
2. **Size** attention, relevance, product fit, strategic value, half-life, expected cost, and expected retained value.
3. **Choose** whether to answer with content, a public tool, a product change, a partnership, an offer, a paid campaign, or nothing.
4. **Research** using Neural Web, external sources, Marketing-originated analysis, or all three.
5. **Build** the canonical intelligence/product object.
6. **Compile** channel variants around one promise and destination.
7. **Classify** consequence, jurisdiction, platform rules, rights, disclosures, and expiry.
8. **Publish or shadow** according to earned playbook authority.
9. **Observe** delivery, engagement, value use, activation, conversion, retention, refunds, cost, and corrections.
10. **Attribute incrementally** using holdouts where possible.
11. **Learn** which audience, problem, promise, proof, product, and channel combination worked.
12. **Scale, revise, or kill** the playbook.
13. **Reopen** the public object when the underlying conclusion resolves or changes.

The last step is crucial. Most finance content systems publish predictions and forget them. Mastermind should automatically return to the scene.

Treat trust as an operating balance.

- clean, resolved work widens autonomous authority;
- corrections are recorded, not hidden;
- unsupported claims, platform warnings, or complaints narrow authority;
- repeated clean behavior earns larger budgets and faster publication;
- high-impact failures trigger recall and a playbook postmortem.

- watchlist changes;
- event explanations;
- alert opens;
- dossiers;
- portfolio context;
- reports;
- bots;
- creator content.

If a campaign attracts users who:

- never activate;
- refund quickly;
- create large support load;
- churn before the payback window;
- misunderstand the product promise;

then its channel and creative scores fall automatically even if clicks and sign-ups are strong.

Before a new department or high-cost campaign goes live, run it in simulation against:

- historical events;
- known audience cohorts;
- prior creative performance;
- current platform rules;
- expected costs;
- likely failure modes.

The twin does not replace real experiments. It eliminates obviously poor experiments.

Score every opportunity by how quickly its value decays.

- breaking event: minutes to hours;
- earnings follow-up: hours to days;
- evergreen comparison: months;
- category positioning: quarters.

The scheduler should spend inference and publishing capacity where speed matters, and demand more depth where it does not.

- credits;
- recognition;
- contributor status;
- report access;

## 23. Failure conditions and automatic responses

| Failure | Automatic response |
|---|---|
| Cortex or an inherited source is degraded | switch to Marketing-originated or hybrid research, lower attribution claim, or delay only the affected asset |
| A claim changes | recall graph finds and updates derivatives |

| High clicks but weak retention | lead-quality backpressure demotes campaign |
| Creator sends low-quality customers | reduce economics, retrain targeting, or end partner |
| Creative fatigue | rotate format/hook or pause; do not simply increase frequency |
| Attribution conflict | preserve unattributed bucket and run holdout |
| Cost spike | reduce model tier, batch, cache, or narrow scope |
| Repeated corrections | reduce playbook authority and require stronger AI audit |
| Department misses its clock | merge, redesign, shadow, or retire |
| Fable and auditor deadlock | request the smallest possible human exception |
| Public recommendation changes regulatory consequence | select a compliant posture, jurisdiction, disclosure, and recordkeeping path before expansion |

## 24. Final recommendation

The attached plan is directionally strong, but the winning version is larger and more disciplined:

- not a content factory, but a growth operating system;
- not a collection of social bots, but an autonomous institution;
- not limited to Neural Web evidence, but able to create original Marketing intelligence;
- not dependent on daily human approvals, but governed by Fable, Opus directors, machine policy, budgets, and an independent AI auditor;
- not optimized for traffic, but for repeated value and retained contribution;
- not a generic SEO farm, but a network of useful intelligence products;
- not conventional influencer marketing, but research infrastructure for creators and communities;
- not publish-and-forget, but a proof graph that revisits outcomes and corrections;
- not a fixed org chart, but a self-improving organization that can create new organs when the opportunity warrants them.

The first build is therefore not a Discord bot, a Stocktwits account, or fifty automated writers.

The first build is the **Autonomous Marketing Growth OS**: its charters, control plane, contracts, identity and event spine, budget allocator, provenance system, publication ledger, autonomous auditor, correction bus, commercial truth spine, and self-improvement loop.

Once that exists, every channel and product engine becomes cheaper to build, safer to scale, easier to audit, and far more intelligent. Fable can then turn the existing depth of Mastermind and Neural Web into a permanent, compounding distribution advantage.
