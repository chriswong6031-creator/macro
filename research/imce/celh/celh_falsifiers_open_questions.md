## 1. Mechanism hypotheses, each with its falsifier

### H1 — The 2024 revenue decline was primarily a channel-inventory event, not an end-demand event
**Evidence for:** four consecutive quarters where issuer-cited retail sell-through exceeded reported
revenue growth (2024Q1 +72.1% vs +36.9%; Q2 +36.5% vs +23.4%; Q3 +7.1% vs −30.9%; Q4 +2.0% vs −4.4%);
issuer attribution of a −$123.9M largest-distributor revenue change "primarily" to "the distributor's
inventory optimization" (E025).

**Named falsifier:** a quarter inside M2 in which reported revenue growth **exceeds** issuer-cited
retail sell-through growth on the *same* Circana denominator. One such quarter breaks the
"sell-in below sell-through throughout" claim. (No such quarter exists in the four measured; the test
is stated so a future re-read can fail it.)

**Second falsifier:** any disclosure showing the largest distributor's inventory *rose* during a quarter
in which revenue fell. Would invert the mechanism.

**Competing explanation (not excluded):** the wedge is partly a *definition* artifact. The denominator
changed mid-episode (MULOC → MULO+ w/C, S008), and retail scan channels exclude club/e-commerce where
CELH has material volume — so "retail sell-through" and "total end demand" are not the same quantity.
A share-losing brand inside a growing tracked channel can show positive scan growth while true total
sell-through is flat. **This competing explanation cannot be closed with the sources in hand.**

### H2 — 2023 reported growth was flattered by a channel build the market could not see
**Evidence for:** ~$25M Q1-2023 "inventory buildup" benefit, disclosed 2024-05-07 (E017); the Q1-2023
10-Q filed 2023-05-09 contains **zero** occurrences of "pipeline", "days on hand", or "inventory
buildup" (E016).

**Named falsifier:** locate any CELH disclosure dated on or before 2023-12-31 that quantifies channel
inventory or a pipeline-fill contribution. Its existence would collapse the 364-day retro-disclosure
claim to a much shorter lag. Reproduce the null: full-text scan of every 2023-vintage CELH filing.

**Competing explanation:** ~$25M is ~9.6% of Q1-2023 revenue — material to the *level* but not, on its
own, sufficient to explain the era's ~95–112% growth rates. H2 says the growth read was *flattered*,
not that it was *manufactured*. Any stronger reading is unsupported.

---

### H3 — The Q3-2025 CELSIUS brand +44% was a base effect plus re-load, not a demand recovery
**Evidence for:** brand revenue +44% against brand sell-through +13% (a +31pp inverse wedge, E038/E039);
the denominator quarter (Q3 2024) had itself been cut 30.9% by the destock; CELSIUS dollar share still
*fell* 0.5pt year over year in the same quarter.

**Named falsifier:** brand sell-through growth catching up to or exceeding brand sell-in growth in the
following two quarters would indicate real demand pull-through rather than a channel re-load. **This
falsifier has since partially fired against the "pure re-load" reading and against the recovery reading
alike:** Q1 2026 came in at +6%/+6% (converged), then Q2 2026 at −11.7%/−2% (wedge reopened negative).
The sequence is consistent with a re-load that subsequently unwound, and inconsistent with a durable
demand recovery.

**Competing explanation:** part of the +44% is genuine distribution expansion (CELSIUS distribution
points +20% in FY2025, E040). Distribution-point growth mechanically raises sell-in ahead of
sell-through. That is a real operating gain, not an artifact — but it is also nearly exhausted at
98.5% ACV.

### H4 — The 2026 CELSIUS decline is a deliberate assortment/trade reset, not demand collapse
**Evidence for:** issuer describes SKU optimization with "planned moderation in innovation activity",
accepts fewer points of distribution, and reports dollars per point of distribution +16% QoQ (E045/E046);
brand sell-through is −2% while brand revenue is −11.7%, i.e. the shipment leg is cut harder than demand.

**Named falsifier:** dollars per point of distribution failing to hold or improve in the next two
quarters, or CELSIUS brand sell-through deteriorating materially below −2%, would indicate the reset is
tracking demand loss rather than driving productivity.

**Competing explanation:** "planned" is the issuer's characterization (`issuer_claim_directional`).
A share decline from 11.0% (2025Q2) to 9.5% (2026Q2) is equally consistent with losing shelf to Alani
and to competitors and describing the retreat as a plan. The +16% productivity figure is **one
observation with no historical comparator** (S013) — it cannot distinguish the two readings.

### H5 — Trade/promotional intensity per dollar of revenue is rising structurally
**Evidence for:** accrued promotional allowance $99.8M (2023-12-31) → $453.0M (2026-06-30), a 4.5×
increase, versus quarterly revenue 2.4× over the same span; the ratio moves from 28.7% to 55.4% of
quarterly revenue; gross margin −340bps YoY in 2026Q2 attributed to "higher promotional and incentive
activity" (E048, E044).

**Named falsifier:** the accrued-promotional-allowance-to-revenue ratio reverting to the 2023–24 range
(~29–40%) for two consecutive quarters while revenue holds.

**Competing explanation:** the balance is an *accrual*, and portfolio mix changed twice inside the
window (Alani 2025-04-01, Rockstar 2025-08-28). Acquired brands may simply carry different trade terms,
which would make the ratio a mix artifact rather than an intensity trend. **Per-brand accrual is not
disclosed**, so mix cannot be separated from intensity with public sources. Unresolvable as of this wave.

---

### H6 — The portfolio acquisitions mask continued core-brand erosion in consolidated numbers
**Evidence for:** consolidated revenue +83.9%/+172.9%/+117.2%/+137.7% across 2025Q2–2026Q1 while the
CELSIUS brand lost share every quarter measured (11.0% → 9.5%, 2025Q2 → 2026Q2); Alani reached 8.7%
share, within 0.8pt of the core brand (E036, E047).

**Named falsifier:** CELSIUS brand dollar share stabilizing or rising for two consecutive quarters
while portfolio revenue grows.

**Competing explanation:** category-level share loss for CELSIUS may be partly *internal* substitution
to Alani within the same owner — economically neutral to the holding company. Share arithmetic alone
cannot separate cannibalization from competitive loss; that needs household/buyer-panel data, which is
`rights_blocked` (S009).

---

### H7 — The 2022 Pepsi transition's cost was absorbed below gross profit and financed by the incoming distributor
**Evidence for:** Q3-2022 selling and marketing expense of $198.8M = 105.6% of that quarter's revenue,
while gross margin was 41.8% — materially unchanged from the surrounding quarters (E012/E013);
Pepsi upfront payments held as restricted cash usable only for legacy-distributor termination payments
or repayable to Pepsi (E014).

**Named falsifier:** evidence that termination costs were charged into cost of goods sold, or that CELH
funded terminations from unrestricted cash.

**Competing explanation:** none material — this is close to an `accounting_identity` reading. It is kept
as a hypothesis only because "the transition was economically cheap for CELH" is a stronger claim than
"the charge sat below gross profit", and the packet asserts only the latter.

## 2. What would falsify the *overall* mechanism interpretation

The autopsy's central claim is that **CELH's large reported waves are generated by the gap between
sell-in and sell-through, opened and closed by one dominant counterparty's working-capital policy and
by the issuer's own trade investment — not by end demand moving at the amplitude the revenue line
implies.**

That interpretation fails if any of the following is established:

1. A quarter in which reported revenue and issuer-cited sell-through move in the same direction *and*
   similar magnitude across a full destock/re-load cycle on a single stable denominator.
2. Evidence that the Circana denominator changes (S008) account for most of the measured wedge — i.e.
   the wedge is a definitional artifact rather than a channel phenomenon.
3. Disclosure of actual channel inventory levels showing the swings were too small to produce the
   observed revenue amplitude.
4. Evidence that club and e-commerce volumes (outside the tracked scan channels) moved opposite to the
   tracked channels by enough to close the wedge. The 2024Q3 club channel −4% figure is one data point
   in the *supporting* direction, but it is not a series.

**Item 3 is the decisive one and is `not_reconstructable` from public sources (S010).** The autopsy's
core mechanism is therefore *consistent with* the evidence but not *identified* by it.
