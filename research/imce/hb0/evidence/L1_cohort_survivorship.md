## Q1–Q5 direct answers

**Q1 — Which U.S. public homebuilders died or were absorbed in 2006–2011, and what was each terminal event?**
Within the strict 2006–2011 window: TOUSA (Ch.11, Jan 2008, liquidated), WCI Communities Inc-2008 (Ch.11, Aug 2008, recapitalized private), Kimball Hill (Ch.11, Apr 2008, wound down), Dominion Homes (2008 Nasdaq delisting → 2010 debt-for-equity restructuring, went private), Levitt and Sons LLC / parent Levitt Corp-Woodbridge (Ch.11 of the homebuilding sub, Nov 2007; parent exited homebuilding), Brookfield Homes Corp (absorbed into Brookfield Residential Properties Inc., Apr 2011, internal reorg not bankruptcy). Orleans Homebuilders' Ch.11 (Mar 2010) also falls inside this window. Ryland/Standard Pacific/CalAtlantic and the later WCI/UCP/Landsea/MDC terminal events fall **after** 2011 and are listed in Part 2 as "adjacent" per the commission's own scope language.

**Q2 — Do the historical filings still exist on EDGAR, and through what period?** Yes for 14 of the 16 named cases (all except Mercedes Homes and John Wieland Homes, which were never SEC registrants — `not_applicable`, not a gap in EDGAR coverage but an absence of any registrant to have coverage). Coverage quality varies sharply: some (Ryland, UCP, CalAtlantic/Standard Pacific, Avatar/AV Homes) have full, clean, unbroken runs through their terminal filing; others (TOUSA, Orleans, Dominion Homes) stop filing 10-Ks for their **final** fiscal year — the annual report covering the period immediately before failure was never filed, so EDGAR's own record has a built-in blind spot for exactly the worst quarter/year of each company's life. This is itself load-bearing: **the survivorship correction is constructible for identity/timeline purposes for 14/16 names, but is NOT fully constructible for terminal-year financial detail on at least 3 of the bankruptcy cases (TOUSA, Orleans, Dominion Homes) because the relevant 10-K was never filed.**

**Q3 — Which mortality cases have resolvable identity continuity, which do not?** Resolvable at the entity-identity level: Ryland→Standard Pacific/CalAtlantic→Lennar, WCI(2013)→Lennar, Avatar/AV Homes→Taylor Morrison, William Lyon→Taylor Morrison, UCP→Century Communities, MDC→Sekisui House (registrant only). **Not resolvable, or resolvable only with major caveats**: Brookfield Homes→Brookfield Residential (different accounting regime, 40-F/6-K vs 10-K), Levitt/Woodbridge parent vs. Levitt and Sons subsidiary (no subsidiary-level filer), and — for every "resolvable" case above — whether post-merger *segment metrics* survive separately was not checked in this pass (flagged as a systematic gap, not resolved).

**Q4 — Full-window survivors outside the frozen six:** MTH, MHO, BZH clean; HOV clean-but-restructured (flag on the 2021 dereg's scope); MDC/Sekisui conditional (filer-continuous, equity-terminated 2024). Named with first/last 10-K dates in Part 4 above.

**Q5 — If the survivorship gap cannot be closed, exactly which classes of claim become impossible?**
1. **Cohort means/medians for any homebuilder financial or operating metric in 2006–2011** computed only from the frozen six — mechanically excludes every firm that failed, biasing the level and trend of the "typical" builder upward through the worst years.
2. **Cross-issuer dispersion statistics** (variance, interquartile range, tail/worst-performer characterizations) for 2006–2011 — with the failed names excluded, dispersion is structurally understated; the actual tail of the distribution is not observed at all in a six-name roster.
3. **Cycle-trough severity claims** ("how bad the homebuilder sector got") — the six survivors were, by definition, the strongest balance sheets of the era; any "trough" read from them alone understates the 2007–2009 downturn relative to the true population, which included at least the ~9 failed/absorbed-under-distress names documented above.
4. **Mortality/hazard-rate statistics** (what fraction of public homebuilders failed) — this wave supplies the numerator (named deaths) but explicitly does **not** supply a denominator (the full population of public homebuilders at each point in time); a hazard rate is therefore **not constructible from this packet alone** — a separate, harder census would be needed.
5. **Time-series continuity for any acquired name past its merger date** — e.g., "CalAtlantic's FY2017" or "WCI's FY2016" cannot be traced into Lennar's post-merger segment reporting without an explicit purchase-accounting/segment bridge, which was not verified in this pass.
6. **Any claim that "the public homebuilder sector survived the GFC intact"** drawn from the frozen six alone is false on its face once ≥9 failure/absorption-under-distress cases are counted — the six-name roster is a *survivor* sample by construction, not a random or representative one.
7. **Any backtest or signal-construction exercise that samples "today's roster" for pre-2012 history** (a classic look-ahead/survivorship-bias construction) — every one of the seven post-2011 entrants (Part 5) structurally cannot contribute pre-2012 data, and using them anyway silently changes the sample's composition mid-window.
