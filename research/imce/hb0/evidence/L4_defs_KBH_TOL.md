### KBH issuer-specific resolved items
- **Fiscal year end: November 30.** **[VERIFIED]** via SEC EDGAR company record (`fiscal-year-end: 1130`).
- **Cancellation-rate denominator (Q3 core answer)**: current, explicitly footnoted definition = "the total number of contracts for new homes cancelled during a period divided by the total (gross) orders for new homes generated during the same period" **[VERIFIED, FY2025 10-K]**. Consistently gross-orders-denominated from at least FY2008 10-K (filed 2009-01-29) forward. **A genuine internal inconsistency was found**: the FY2008 10-K's own narrative used "cancellation rate based on net orders" in one place vs. "based on gross orders" in its segment-table caption — both **[VERIFIED]** from the same filing. FY2005-2007 filings disclose a cancellation-rate % without an explicit stated denominator in sections reviewed (GAP).
- **BTO vs. inventory/spec split**: BTO = construction generally commences only after a signed purchase contract; non-BTO/spec = "homes started without a corresponding buyer and partially constructed homes where the initial buyer cancelled." Historically 60-70% of deliveries were BTO; FY2025 was ~55% BTO **[VERIFIED, FY2025 10-K Item 1]**. BTO share is stated as a percentage of **homes delivered**, not of orders. Earliest disclosure year of a quantified BTO % is NOT verified — GAP.
- **Build time / cycle time**: KBH explicitly discloses "average build time of four to five months from construction start to home completion" and a "six to seven months" home-sale-to-delivery window in FY2025 10-K Item 1 **[VERIFIED]**, with FY2023-FY2025 narrative on build-time recovery. Earliest disclosure year NOT verified — GAP.

## 3. Incomparability table (KBH/TOL concepts not economically comparable to a volume builder's same-named metric)

| Concept | Issuer | Why it does not map cleanly to a volume-builder (e.g., DHI/LEN) same-named metric |
|---|---|---|
| Backlog / cycle time | KBH | KBH's BTO model means construction typically starts only AFTER contract signing ("we generally commence construction of a home only after we have a signed purchase contract"), so a KBH backlog unit often has NOT yet started construction, unlike a volume builder's spec-heavy backlog. KBH's disclosed 4-5 month build time + 6-7 month sale-to-delivery window is a structurally different object than a volume builder's shorter, spec-driven window. |
| Spec/non-BTO share | KBH | KBH frames non-BTO homes as a residual/overflow category ("homes started without a corresponding buyer and partially constructed homes where the initial buyer cancelled"), not a deliberate strategic inventory position the way spec homes function for a volume builder — a rising KBH "spec share" (60-70%→~55% BTO) signals supply-chain-driven inventory buildup, not a deliberate speed-to-close strategy shift. |
| Net Signed Contracts (net orders equivalent) | TOL | TOL nets ALL cancellations occurring in the reporting period against gross signs, REGARDLESS of whether the cancelled contract originated in that period or a PRIOR period, and credits newly-selected options on old contracts as current-period sales. A volume builder's "net orders" (current-period gross minus current-period cancellations only) is a different formula — TOL's figure can be pulled down by a cancellation of a contract signed many quarters earlier, something a standard "net orders" figure would not reflect. |
| Cancellation rate (dual denominator, 10-K-silent) | TOL | TOL discloses cancellations BOTH as a % of beginning-quarter backlog AND as a % of signed contracts in the quarter, simultaneously, as two different numbers (2.8% vs. 5.4% in Q1 FY2026) - a single "the cancellation rate" figure does not exist for TOL the way KBH's single gross-orders-denominated rate does. Additionally, TOL's 10-K contains NO cancellation-rate percentage or formula at all (confirmed by full-text inspection) - the metric exists only in the quarterly press release, unlike KBH (footnoted formula in the 10-K itself), D.R. Horton (stated formula, per sibling-lane finding), or Lennar (bare 10-K percentage, per sibling-lane finding). |
| Cycle time (absence) | TOL | TOL discloses NO numeric build-time/cycle-time figure in the documents reviewed (only qualitative "generally longer" language for mid-/high-rise product) — a longitudinal cycle-time series that exists for KBH (and, per general industry practice, some volume builders) simply does not exist as a citable public number for TOL. Any cross-company cycle-time comparison involving TOL must be typed `missing`, not estimated. |
| Home Sales Gross Margin / lots owned-controlled / backlog / deliveries | TOL | All carry an explicit or structural "Total Consolidated" framing that excludes Land Development JVs, Home Building JVs, and (until their FY2025 partial sale) Apartment Living JV activity — TOL's substantial JV footprint ($956.5 million of "Investments in unconsolidated entities" at Jan 31, 2026) means TOL's consolidated-only headline metrics understate TOL's TOTAL controlled land/production footprint relative to a volume builder with little or no JV structure, where "consolidated" and "total controlled" are nearly the same thing. |
| Apartment Living / City Living units | TOL | These are, respectively, for-rent multifamily product (not a home "sale," so structurally outside any homebuilding order/backlog/closing count) and JV-developed for-sale condos whose consolidation treatment is ownership-dependent and not resolved in this pass — a volume builder with no analogous segments has no equivalent exclusion/inclusion question to answer. |
| Community count (average vs. period-end) | KBH vs. TOL | KBH discloses BOTH an "average" and an "ending" community count per period; TOL's exhibits reviewed in this pass show only a period-end figure — a cross-company community-count comparison using KBH's "average" against TOL's "period-end" would silently compare two different point-estimators of a moving quantity. (TOL's use of an average count elsewhere in the full window was NOT ruled out — GAP.) |
