## 4. FAILURE MODES & ADVERSARIAL QUESTIONS (PART XXX, §301–308) — captured in full

- §301. Themes may be retrospective stories (price rises → media invents theme → model sees theme → falsely claims theme predicted price). Countermeasure: point-in-time semantic evidence, causal chronology, residual returns, delayed validation, explicit counterfactuals.
- §302. Narrative attention may be mostly endogenous to price (price drives search, search can drive demand). Use lag structures, instrumental research, source decomposition, event timing; goal = find when attention has incremental state information, not prove pure causality.
- §303. "Smart money" labels may become folk mythology (a seat with 5 wins may lack skill; 13F holder may be hedged; options flow may be dealer hedging). Rule: `ParticipantInference != GroundTruthIntent` — always probabilistic.
- §304. Theme graphs may become too dense/useless. Use edge thresholds, relevance, temporal decay, hierarchy, purpose-specific views.
- §305. Cross-market signals may disappear after discovery (arbitraged away). Every relationship needs current reliability / decay / last validation. "Do not canonize yesterday's alpha."
- §306. LLM may over-interpret sparse data. Require minimum evidence, confidence, novelty, explicit missing-data flags. Best answer can be "not yet sufficiently confirmed."
- §307. System may learn the wrong lesson from one episode. Semantic memory must not update from one anecdote — require repeated evidence or research validation. "Observation ≠ Rule."
- §308. A beautiful composite score may mask regime failure. Keep raw components; always permit "why did score fall?"; backtest score components by regime.

1. GLOBAL SENSORIUM — price, volume, options, ETFs, filings, news, macro, China microstructure, commodities, policy, alt data

6. MEMORY + EXPERIENCE RETRIEVAL — episodic/semantic/procedural/historical analogues

8. PROPHET / OUTPUT ORGANS — probabilities, theme rankings, stock rankings, risk, dislocations, transitions, expected returns, horizons
9. OUTCOME EVALUATOR — expected vs observed, surprise, error taxonomy
10. LEARNING SYSTEM — reliability, weights, memories, regimes, procedures
   → feeds RESEARCH CORTEX (recurring failures → hypotheses → missing capability → backfill → replay → validate → proposed improvement)

- §339. Theme Graph is deliberately mid-stack: it's the connective tissue macro↔companies, stories↔economic reality, capital↔assets, global↔local, history↔current. Without it Neural Web has "many sensors but fewer structured relationships."
- §340. **Key reframe: Prophet becomes an OUTPUT ORGAN, not the whole brain.** "Neural Web understands the world. Prophet expresses expectations from that understanding." Prevents every idea being crammed into one stock-picking formula.
- §341. Theme Graph gives Prophet hierarchical predictions: P(global theme strengthens), P(local theme strengthens), P(theme transitions to Expansion), P(company outperforms theme), P(company catches up), P(theme enters fragility) — stock prediction inherits context from each level.
- §342. Hierarchical error attribution: a failed forecast can be diagnosed ("thesis correct at theme level; company-specific expression failed because economic exposure was overstated") instead of flat "prediction failed."
- §343. Closed loop: SENSE → UNDERSTAND → REMEMBER → NOTICE → THINK → PREDICT → EXPERIENCE → LEARN → DISCOVER → IMPROVE SENSING → REPEAT.

## 8. THEME BASKET CONSTRUCTION AS RESEARCH INSTRUMENT (PART XL, §394–410)

- §394. **No single "true" theme index** — a theme is the durable intelligence object; indices/baskets are lenses. Maintain multiple basket *views* per theme: Economic Purity / Trading Proxy / High Optionality / Emerging Beneficiary / Narrative Proxy / Supply-Chain Bottleneck / Leader / Secondary-Catch-Up Index. Resolves "is Company X really an AI company?" by reporting economic/narrative/trading/optionality separately instead of forcing a binary verdict.
- §395. **Economic-Purity Basket** — evidence: segment revenue/profit, backlog, customer concentration, contracts, direct capex, capacity, guidance. Weight = Exposure × Confidence × Materiality × Liquidity Adjustment. Weakness: lags genuine emerging optionality (accounting lags reality) — hence a separate optionality basket, not a reason to change this one.
- §396. **High-Optionality Basket** — evidence: R&D, new products, announced capacity, partnerships, capex, early customers, management commitment, regulatory approval, supply-chain positioning. Fields: CurrentExposure, FutureExposureRange, ProbabilityOfMaterialization, TimeToMateriality, EvidenceVelocity. Useful for tech transitions where winners look "economically impure" before becoming obvious.
- §397. **Trading-Proxy Basket** — securities the market currently uses to express the narrative (residual theme beta, event-day co-movement, intraday sync, options co-movement, relative turnover, attention, repeated reaction to catalysts). Changes fast by design — measures market behavior, not corporate identity; a name can be in/out within 3 months without business change.
- §398. **Narrative-Proxy Basket** — news co-mentions, analyst-note language, earnings-call mentions, search associations, social discourse, thematic-ETF ownership, broker baskets. Useful for measuring attention/speculative breadth/"theme washing" — not economic purity.
- §399. **Bottleneck Basket** — companies controlling scarce inputs/capabilities constraining theme expansion; called "potentially one of the most valuable second-order baskets." Examples given: AI (GPU, HBM, advanced packaging, power transformers, grid interconnection, cooling); Space (launch capacity, rad-hard components, satellite buses, propulsion, specialty materials); Defense (rocket motors, energetics, radars, rare materials, shipyard capacity). Question: "where does the economic system break if demand accelerates faster than supply?"
- §400. **Leader Basket** — Price/Liquidity/Narrative/Fundamental/Institutional leader are NOT assumed to be the same security (example: NVDA = fundamental anchor, a different high-beta name = trading leader, a VRT-like name = power bottleneck, a speculative proxy = attention leader).
- §401. **Secondary/Catch-Up Basket** — high economic relevance + historically high theme beta + low current residual return + no negative catalyst → test whether laggards catch up during healthy Expansion states. Explicitly more rigorous than screening for "cheap-looking" related stocks.
- §402. Weighting method should match the question — store several (Equal/Cap/Float/Economic-Exposure/Narrative-Exposure/Trading-Beta/Risk-Parity/Liquidity-Capped), don't debate one "best."
- §403. Concentration caps aren't just portfolio rules — if one mega-cap explains 70% of a theme index, theme return ≈ single-stock return, which is bad for measuring theme *state*. Maintain both a Raw Economic Basket and an Analytical De-Concentrated Basket (breadth/residual-factor/theme-specific behavior).
- §404. Theme Factor (research: prioritize signal purity/exposure-neutrality/sector-neutrality/market-neutrality) ≠ Investable Index (needs liquidity/turnover controls/concentration limits/rebalance rules) — not synonyms; product may expose both.
- §405. Point-in-time reconstitution — each rebalance uses only evidence available by that date; every membership decision logs candidate/evidence/evidence_time/score/decision_time. "A theme index with today's membership backfilled through history is not historical intelligence. It is a hindsight chart."
- §406. Entry/exit hysteresis — entry threshold, exit threshold, minimum persistence, to stop churn from a score oscillating around 0.60. Different basket types get different hysteresis (Trading Proxy more dynamic than Economic Purity).
- §407. Membership confidence ≠ portfolio weight/exposure — two separate dimensions, don't blindly multiply (e.g., confidence 95%/exposure 20% vs confidence 60%/exposure 80% are different situations).
- §408. Basket should expose its reasoning per member: why included / what evidence / which role / what exposure / what changed (full worked example given in source for "Company X" in "AI Power Infrastructure").
- §409. **Theme basket drift is itself intelligence** — constituent/weight changes over time show how the economic story is evolving (example: AI Infrastructure 2024 GPU/HBM-dominant → 2026 power/cooling/generation weights rising). Proposed field: `ThemeSemanticDrift`, feeds historical memory.
- §410. "Pure play" is context-dependent — pure economic exposure can be a poor trading proxy (conglomerate effects, hedging, regulation, capital structure, foreign listing, illiquidity) and vice versa; don't let the marketing term collapse the distinctions.

## 8b. FORMAL THEME DISCOVERY PIPELINE (PART XLI, §411–420)

- §411. Two distinct problems: **Known-Theme Maintenance** (which companies belong, how exposure is changing) vs **Unknown-Theme Discovery** (why are these companies/documents/catalysts suddenly clustering — no name yet). Treat separately; the second is much harder.
- §412. Known-Theme Maintenance pipeline: Source documents → Entity+segment extraction → Evidence claims → Theme relevance inference → Economic/Narrative/Trading scores → Temporal comparison → Edge update candidate → Validation. Must explain "why did exposure change?"
- §413. Unknown-Theme Discovery pipeline (12 steps): (1) detect semantic novelty (2) detect unusual co-mentions (3) detect new market co-movement (4) detect common catalysts (5) detect supply-chain coherence (6) detect attention diffusion (7) cluster candidate entities (8) generate possible concept descriptions (9) search for prior/existing theme overlap (10) validate economic coherence (11) create temporary candidate node (12) observe whether candidate persists. "Do not create permanent nodes immediately."
- §414. Candidate Theme Incubation — probation period with fields Evidence/Age/Confidence/Status ("Incubating"), worked example "Orbital Computing" (5 companies, 3 source clusters, 2 contracts, cross-sectional co-movement, growing attention, age 12 days, confidence 0.72). Graduation requires persistence + economic coherence + distinctiveness + enough securities + market/research relevance.
- §415. Theme merge/split — ontology relation types `MERGED_INTO`, `SPLIT_FROM`, `RENAMED_FROM` with historical validity. Examples: AI Infrastructure → AI Compute + AI Power (split); Space Communications + Direct-to-Device (temporary merge).
- §416. Semantic neighbors ≠ parent/child (e.g., AI Power's parent = AI Infrastructure, but Grid Modernization/Nuclear Renaissance/Gas Turbines are neighbors, not children) — distinction matters for contagion modeling.
- §417. Temporary event themes allowed (bank rescue, specific tariff, earthquake reconstruction, Olympics, election trade) — may deserve nodes for weeks/months. Add `ThemeType = Structural | Cyclical | Event` with different decay/archival behavior.
- §418. Theme naming should follow market language without being captive to it — store aliases (e.g., "AI Power" / "Data Center Power" / "AI Electricity Demand") so the graph tracks changing terminology without spawning duplicate themes.
- §419. **Multilingual ontology is a major cross-market requirement** — 商业航天/Commercial Space, 算力/Compute, 低空经济/Low-Altitude Economy, 人形机器人/Humanoid Robotics. Store: canonical global concept + local-language label + local market definition + market-specific subthemes.
- §420. Local narratives can be economically distinct even when translation looks similar — "AI" (US) and 人工智能 (A-share) activate different company sets due to domestic supply chains/policy/listing universe/investor conventions/national tech priorities. Core rule: `GlobalConcept ≠ IdenticalLocalBasket`.
