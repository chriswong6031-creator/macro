# 0. Executive ruling

The project should no longer be framed primarily as a better government-contract search product.

GovTribe is useful as evidence that procurement records can be searched, related, monitored, and automated. It is not the benchmark that should determine the final Mastermind product. Its center of gravity is business development and capture. Mastermind's center of gravity must be public-market causality, financial transmission, timing, market incorporation, downside, and asymmetric return.

The V3 destination is:

> **A point-in-time Defense Procurement and Industrial Base Intelligence OS that models how threats, wars, policy, budgets, weapon consumption, inventories, acquisition programs, production capacity, contracts, execution, company financials, expectations, valuation, and market positioning interact—and uses that causal model to identify which listed companies have improving or deteriorating economics, which events remain unpriced, which selloffs are temporary dislocations rather than impairments, which themes are real rather than promotional, and which independently validated evidence should eventually influence Prophet.**

The system must answer six different questions without collapsing them into one score:

1. **Demand:** What mission, conflict, policy, fleet, stockpile, or readiness requirement is creating demand?
2. **Access:** Which programs, vehicles, primes, suppliers, and legal entities can actually receive that demand?
3. **Monetization:** Can the issuer manufacture, deliver, recognize revenue, earn margin, and convert cash?
4. **Expectations:** What did management, analysts, options, price, and the market already anticipate?
5. **Asymmetry:** What upside, downside, timing, uncertainty, and falsifiers remain?
6. **Authority:** Is this only descriptive context, a research hypothesis, a calibrated forecast, or evidence that has earned a bounded Prophet role?

The project is successful only when those questions work end to end for real companies and real events in production.

# 1. The decisive research finding: defense stocks are not contract tickers

A contract headline is sometimes important. It is rarely sufficient.

Historical research, official acquisition evidence, company filings, and recent live cases point to a more complete model:

```text
defense-stock return
=
broad market and factor movement
+ earnings and estimate revisions
+ geopolitical demand shock
+ budget/funding translation
+ program/contract exposure
+ contract quality
+ production and delivery capacity
+ execution, margin, and cash conversion
+ valuation and prior expectations
+ options/flow/positioning
+ idiosyncratic surprise
```

A procurement event can be bullish in official-dollar terms and bearish in equity terms. Examples include:

- a large fixed-price development award with unfavorable risk transfer;
- a backlog increase that requires years of capex and working capital before cash arrives;
- a program win whose economics were already fully expected;
- a ceiling or IDIQ with little funded work;
- an award that is immaterial to a diversified parent;
- an urgent demand signal that the company cannot physically supply;
- a production increase that creates near-term margin pressure;
- a government settlement that is small in dollars but reveals broader compliance risk;
- a protest that delays revenue but does not destroy the underlying opportunity;
- a stock that rises on war headlines even though its products are not consumed in that theater;
- a high-quality award announced after a large pre-event run-up and options repricing.

The lobe must therefore model causal transmission, not count positive headlines.

## 1.1 Earnings and analyst revisions remain primary

The defense industry has unique information channels, but it is still an equity industry. Formal earnings announcements, guidance, analyst revisions, margin, free cash flow, working capital, and valuation often move stocks more than an isolated contract notice.

This creates a binding product requirement:

> **Government Revenue may own official procurement truth, but the defense alpha system cannot be complete until it consumes the canonical earnings, filings, financial, estimate, valuation, and market owner planes.**

It must never create a second SEC, transcript, estimate, market-data, or financial-statement store.

## 1.2 Geopolitical acts are not a monotone bullish factor

Research consistently supports a positive defense-sector response to some war and attack events, but the effect is heterogeneous and time-varying.

The important distinctions are:

- threat versus realized act;
- surprise versus anticipated escalation;
- war initiation versus expected termination;
- geographic proximity;
- NATO/alliance status;
- national procurement policy;
- product relevance to the actual conflict;
- defense revenue purity;
- company size and liquidity;
- R&D intensity;
- political/customer access;
- production capacity;
- pre-event valuation and run-up;
- market regime;
- expected conflict duration.

The engine must therefore forbid the rule:

```text
war headline -> every defense stock bullish
```

The correct model is:

```text
threat / conflict event
-> mission and weapon demand
-> consumption or readiness gap
-> funding and acquisition response
-> production and supplier feasibility
-> issuer exposure and purity
-> expected earnings transmission
-> market incorporation
```

A peace, ceasefire, de-escalation, cancellation, or war-termination event can be negative for previously rerated defense names. That is a first-class catalyst family, not an exception.

## 1.3 Stable demand can lower uncertainty without creating immediate upside

Defense spending can make procurement-intensive cash flows easier to forecast. That can reduce forecast dispersion and volatility without necessarily producing a large positive near-term abnormal return.

The lobe must distinguish:

- **uncertainty reduction;**
- **earnings acceleration;**
- **multiple expansion;**
- **event underreaction;**
- **already-priced demand;**
- **crowded safe-haven behavior.**

A stable demand backdrop can support valuation while leaving no tactical entry edge.

## 1.4 Backlog is only useful when its quality is known

Backlog must be decomposed into:

- funded versus unfunded;
- fixed-price versus cost-reimbursable;
- development versus production versus sustainment;
- option and IDIQ status;
- customer and country;
- program;
- expected realization window;
- margin quality;
- working-capital intensity;
- capacity dependency;
- cancellation/termination exposure;
- currency and escalation protection;
- export/local-content constraints;
- probable cash conversion.

The product must never publish “record backlog” as a complete investment conclusion.

## 1.5 Execution can dominate demand

Recent defense-company results demonstrate that strong demand and record backlog can coexist with:

- reach-forward or EAC losses;
- schedule delays;
- technical immaturity;
- supplier quality failures;
- labor and workforce constraints;
- inventory and contract-asset growth;
- negative free cash flow;
- margin resets;
- withheld deliveries;
- program-specific charges;
- guidance reductions.

The V3 engine therefore adds a formal **Program Execution and Contract Accounting Risk Engine**.

## 1.6 Capacity is both a moat and a constraint

Scarce capacity can create pricing power, long demand visibility, and strategic value. It can also create:

- capex burden;
- low initial learning-curve margins;
- labor inefficiency;
- supplier bottlenecks;
- quality problems;
- late deliveries;
- working-capital absorption;
- government intervention;
- inability to monetize demand inside the investor's horizon.

The engine must estimate capacity readiness and production elasticity rather than treating scarcity as automatically bullish.

## 1.7 Price is a statement about expectations

The alpha question is not:

> Is the procurement event good?

It is:

> **Is the event economically better or worse than the market's point-in-time expectation, and is the remaining gap large enough relative to uncertainty, downside, timing, liquidity, and opportunity cost?**

The V3 core object is therefore an expectations-adjusted scenario, not a headline score.

## 9.4 Temporary versus structural classifier

### Temporary-dislocation evidence

- direct cost bounded;
- no loss of program/customer;
- funded backlog intact;
- issue isolated;
- remediation underway;
- prior execution record strong;
- estimates stabilize;
- insiders/company repurchase only as context;
- recovery catalyst scheduled;
- residual selloff exceeds conservative impairment.

- repeated charges;
- contract model fundamentally uneconomic;
- customer trust/eligibility damaged;
- program cancellation;
- capacity permanently inadequate;
- technical design failure;
- financing/dilution risk;
- management credibility collapse;
- estimates continue falling;
- no identifiable recovery clock.

The classifier must be calibrated against historical cases and may abstain.

6. inspect market and options behavior;
7. inspect historical analogues;
8. see recovery clocks and falsifiers;
9. save a monitor;
10. watch the hypothesis grade forward.

## 13.6 Honest abstention

The defense contribution must abstain when any load-bearing condition is absent:

- unresolved issuer or historical ownership;
- stale, corrected, quarantined, rights-blocked, or incomplete evidence;
- source event discovered too late for the declared decision clock;
- no economically comparable denominator;
- unsupported revenue or margin transmission;
- event already fully incorporated;
- feature constant across tonight's eligible names;
- only one eligible defense name in the comparison frame;
- insufficient calibrated coverage;
- unresolved contradiction;
- model or source health unavailable;
- duplicate evidence would spend a second vote.

An abstention is a successful safety decision, not an error and not a zero.

## 13.7 Promotion ladder

After preregistered forward gates, permit only a capped movement among already-admitted names in the same stage bucket. One configuration switch disables it.

Not authorized by this masterplan. It requires a separate architecture and governance decision after substantial evidence.

- mission-capable/readiness indicators where public;
- maintenance burden;
- replacement program schedule;
- upgrade alternatives;

The output is a replacement-pressure distribution and beneficiary map—not a claim that every old fleet will be replaced on time.

- preserve disclosed financial amounts and contract class;
- bind exact filing/call evidence;
- use only reviewed, public cross-source links;
- show an uncertainty envelope;
- prohibit invented program identity;
- track later declassification or management clarification.

## 15.3 Multi-clock law

Every observation distinguishes at least:

- `effective_at`: when the fact applies;
- `source_published_at`: when the source says it published;
- `first_observed_at`: when Mastermind first saw it;
- `retrieved_at`: when bytes were retrieved;
- `known_at`: earliest defensible decision time;
- `compiled_at`: when a derived artifact was built;
- `served_at`: when it became available to the product;
- `market_as_of`: market observation clock;
- `corrected_at`: correction clock.

A source may omit some clocks; omission is explicit. `as_of` may not substitute for `known_at`.

For non-exact dates, use:

```text
precision
lower_bound
upper_bound
bound_rule
timezone_or_calendar
source_language
normalization_rule
```

There is no single exact-value field when the source did not provide one.

## 15.5 First-known and latency intelligence

The system should measure the information path itself:

```text
official source publication
→ Mastermind observation
→ mainstream/news recognition
→ company acknowledgement
→ analyst revision
→ price/volume/options response
```

This yields lawful research features such as:

- source-to-market latency;
- obscure-source advantage;
- late-discovery exclusion;
- time-to-corporate confirmation;
- time-to-consensus revision.

It must never claim an edge from a source we did not actually collect at that historical time.

## 15.6 Correction and invalidation

A correction may:

- supersede a source document;
- change an entity relationship;
- revise a date or amount;
- invalidate a graph path;
- retract a candidate;
- close or re-grade a forecast;
- rebuild downstream read models.

Corrections are append-only acts. Historical bytes remain audit-visible. Derived artifacts carry dependencies so a corrected fact can invalidate the correct descendants without deleting history.

## 15.7 Global coverage

The system should support U.S. issuers first but remain architecturally global:

- issuer/security identity is market-independent;
- budgets and procurement carry country/currency/fiscal calendars;
- FMS and allied procurement have separate lifecycle semantics;
- local-content rules, export controls, FX, and sovereign risk are explicit;
- cross-listings and depositary receipts resolve to canonical securities;
- global company and program coverage expands only when source rights and clocks are credible.

# 18. Historical casebook and evaluation architecture

## 18.1 Why the casebook is required

Defense-stock intuition is dominated by vivid anecdotes: wars, huge awards, contract losses, and program charges. A serious engine needs a structured casebook that can falsify those intuitions.

D0R should build the design and golden roster for a casebook spanning:

- geopolitical escalation and de-escalation;
- spending and policy shocks;
- budget/program changes;
- contract wins, losses, and modifications;
- production and capacity announcements;
- earnings, guidance, analyst revisions, and program charges;
- protests and corrective action;
- accidents, quality defects, and technical failures;
- legal, cyber, pricing, and compliance actions;
- supply-chain disruptions;
- M&A, divestitures, and program ownership changes;
- theme rotations and crowding unwind.

## 18.2 Minimum D0R casebook design

At least 60 reviewed events, with:

- no fewer than 20 positive-looking events;
- no fewer than 20 negative-looking events;
- no fewer than 10 ambiguous or correction cases;
- U.S. and international issuers;
- every major archetype;
- large, mid, and small-cap names where data permits;
- direct primes, suppliers, diversified issuers, and services companies;
- both heavily anticipated and obscure-source events;
- at least two de-escalation/peace cases;
- at least two broad contagion cases;
- at least two fixed-price charge cases;
- at least two legal/compliance selloffs;
- at least two protest/recompete cases;
- at least two production-capacity cases.

The casebook is not a training set until its point-in-time and selection properties are reviewed.

## 18.3 Event record

Each case includes:

```text
case_id
issuer/security identity
archetype
event family
source document and exact span
source published / first observed / known / first tradable clocks
prior public expectations
program and economic path
direct damage or opportunity estimate
market, defense, theme, factor, and peer controls
pre-event run-up
options/attention state
residual returns by horizon
MFE / MAE / volatility / volume
fundamental confirmation by later filings/calls
corrections
classification confidence
alternative explanations
falsifiers
research verdict
```

## 18.4 Historical hypotheses to test

- earnings announcements and analyst revisions explain more large moves than routine contract announcements;
- geopolitical acts produce stronger reactions than threat language;
- short-horizon panic and longer-horizon procurement benefit can coexist;
- defense purity and mission relevance matter more than broad sector labels;
- capacity-constrained suppliers can outperform platform primes when demand accelerates;
- huge ceiling values have weak information content without funding and materiality;
- multiyear procurement and rate increases carry more durable information than routine options;
- peace/de-escalation can reverse crowded war-beneficiary trades;
- contract losses matter most when incumbent economics, margin, and replacement probability are high;
- legal settlements are often temporary when economically immaterial and operationally contained, but structural when tied to business-system failure, debarment risk, recurring conduct, or key-program disruption;
- program charges create opportunity only when the market's residual damage exceeds the updated economic loss and management credibility remains repairable;
- government/company divergence can lead earnings and estimate revisions;
- pre-event options and price run-up reduce remaining edge;
- attention lag and obscure-source discovery increase underreaction probability.

These are preregistration candidates, not conclusions.

## 18.5 Negative controls

Include:

- routine task orders with no economic novelty;
- large ceiling announcements with minimal funding;
- contracts already disclosed by management;
- sector-wide moves with no issuer-specific event;
- unlinked recipient records;
- events discovered after the price move;
- repeated press releases about the same agreement;
- non-material settlements;
- awards to private entities with no reviewed public-owner path.

A model that cannot reject these is not ready.

## 18.6 Market outcomes

Measure, as appropriate:

- first tradable return;
- 1, 5, 21, 63, and 126-session return;
- market-, defense-, archetype-, theme-, and factor-relative return;
- MFE and MAE;
- time to peak and trough;
- realized volatility and volume;
- gap and overnight contribution;
- drawdown and recovery;
- options repricing;
- estimate and target revisions.

## 18.7 Fundamental outcomes

- revenue and organic growth;
- segment sales;
- backlog and funded backlog;
- book-to-bill;
- margin and program charges;
- cash flow and working capital;
- guidance;
- program and capacity commentary;
- deliveries and throughput;
- later award/order confirmation;
- cancellation, delay, or correction.

## 18.8 Statistical design

- issuer and event-wave clustering;
- overlapping-event controls;
- matched control firms and synthetic/theme controls;
- time-blocked validation;
- survivorship and delisting accounting;
- correction and late-discovery separation;
- multiple-testing/FDR control;
- honest distinct-event N;
- predeclared subgroup and regime slices;
- simple baselines before complex models;
- calibration and coverage, not only average return.

## 18.9 Forward measurement factory

Every forecast version writes before outcome:

```text
forecast_id
issue_time
decision_cutoff
universe
eligibility
source snapshot
identity/graph snapshot
feature/model version
scenario distribution
abstention
expected horizon
falsifiers
authority
```

Graders later attach market and fundamental outcomes without mutating the original forecast. Null and negative results remain visible.

# 20. Program-level acceptance, red-team gates, and kill criteria

## 20.1 Truth gate

- official and licensed sources have explicit rights and clocks;
- raw evidence and correction lineage are retained;
- no current-only identity or data leaks into historical decisions;
- source, transport, graph, and serving freshness are separate;
- valid empty, unavailable, unresolved, and corrected states are visible.

## 20.2 Identity gate

- reviewed issuer paths cover a material share of the target defense universe and observed economics;
- coverage denominators are published by source, event family, archetype, and dollar amount;
- acquisitions, divestitures, JVs, novations, and historical securities are handled;
- ambiguous paths cannot create ticker-level evidence.

## 20.3 Product gate

A serious investor can:

1. discover a material change;
2. understand why it matters;
3. trace the exact causal and ownership path;
4. assess financial transmission and market incorporation;
5. inspect counterevidence and falsifiers;
6. open source evidence;
7. save a watch or next action;
8. perform the same job on desktop and mobile;
9. understand every failure or partial state.

## 20.4 Intelligence gate

The system provides more than source aggregation:

- archetype-aware analysis;
- mission/program/product/supplier linking;
- backlog and contract-quality intelligence;
- capacity and bottleneck reasoning;
- government/company divergence;
- theme lifecycle;
- market residualization;
- scenario distributions;
- dislocation and read-through hypotheses.

## 20.5 Learning gate

- casebook and forward ledgers are live;
- forecasts are registered before outcomes;
- corrections do not rewrite history;
- nulls and negative results remain visible;
- simple baselines and ablations exist;
- coverage and calibration are reported;
- failed models are killed, not repackaged.

## 20.6 Neural Web gate

- compact causal state is published through canonical contracts;
- cross-lobe joins cite exact graph/evidence paths;
- source gaps and contradictions are carried;
- no independent liveness, user-state, theme, or trading control plane is recreated;
- LLM reasoning is cited and authority-safe.

## 20.7 Prophet gate

Before any order effect:

- independent forward lift is demonstrated;
- overlap with existing families is measured and bounded;
- availability/variance floors pass prospectively;
- no candidate admission or sizing occurs;
- every rank movement has a trace;
- impact is capped and reversible;
- an explicit authority decision is merged.

## 20.8 Red-team questions for every major feature

- Could this be a ceiling mistaken for funding or revenue?
- Could the issuer path be wrong at the decision time?
- Could the event be a restatement of already-known information?
- Could the stock move be broad defense/theme beta?
- Could options or attention already be voting elsewhere?
- Could capacity prevent monetization?
- Could fixed-price economics make demand bad for margin?
- Could a positive budget request fail to become appropriation or obligation?
- Could FMS notification fail to become a sale?
- Could a legal settlement signal a larger structural problem?
- Could a source correction reverse the claim?
- Could our collector have learned the event after the market?
- Does the test have independent evidence, or is the claim checking itself?
- Can the user complete the job in the real browser?

## 20.9 Kill criteria

Kill or hold a model/feature when:

- point-in-time reconstruction is impossible;
- issuer attribution remains materially ambiguous;
- coverage is too concentrated or sparse;
- apparent lift disappears after defense/theme residualization;
- results rely on one crisis, issuer, country, or archetype;
- calibration is poor or unstable;
- turnover/slippage consumes the benefit;
- overlap with existing Prophet evidence explains the result;
- forward performance fails frozen gates;
- source rights or availability are not durable;
- user value does not justify operational cost.

A killed predictive feature may remain useful descriptive intelligence when labeled honestly.

## 20.10 Chairman review points

Return for explicit review after:

1. D0R current-state, benchmark, archetype, graph, and experience freeze;
2. D1 signed-in production proof;
3. D2 identity coverage and review workflow;
4. first complete company/program golden slice;
5. first three theme war rooms;
6. first historical casebook and baseline findings;
7. first matured forward cohort;
