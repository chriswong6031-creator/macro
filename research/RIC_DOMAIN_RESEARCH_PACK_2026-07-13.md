## Lane: opex-mechanics

### narrative
The monthly options expiration (OPEX) cycle generates mechanical dealer hedging flows that are among the most studied structural features of US equity markets. The core mechanism is delta-neutral hedging by options market makers (OMMs). When OMMs sell options to customers, they take the opposite gamma exposure and must continuously rebalance their delta hedge as the underlying moves. Approaching expiration, three second-order Greeks dominate: gamma (delta sensitivity to spot), charm (delta sensitivity to time), and vanna (delta sensitivity to implied volatility). In the final days before monthly or quarterly OPEX, dealer books generate predictable directional flows. Charm forces delta adjustments every calendar day as OTM options decay. Vanna forces adjustments whenever the volatility surface shifts. When vanna and charm both point in the same direction, for example falling IV combined with charm-driven delta contraction in OTM puts, the flows can mechanically bid index futures without any fundamental catalyst.

The pinning phenomenon is the most rigorously measured OPEX effect in academia. Ni, Pearson, and Poteshman (2005, JFE) documented that optionable stocks close at strike prices on expiration dates, with aggregate market cap redistribution of at least 9 billion dollars per expiration and an average return alteration of approximately 16.5 basis points per stock per expiration day. Avellaneda and Lipkin (2003, Quantitative Finance) derived the SDE mechanism: large open interest at a strike creates a singular drift term through aggregate delta-hedging price impact that pulls the stock toward that strike. Golez and Jackwerth (2012, JFE) extended pinning to S&P 500 futures, documenting anti-cross-pinning with at least 115 million dollars in notional futures value shifted per expiration day. These are all pre-2012 results; the post-2022 0DTE regime has not been formally retested for pinning persistence. Dealer gamma imbalance and its link to realized volatility and intraday momentum is increasingly well-supported. Baltussen, Da, Lammers, and Martens (2021, JFE) showed across 60-plus futures spanning 1974-2020 that last-30-minute returns are significantly predicted by rest-of-day returns when aggregate gamma positioning is negative, consistent with OMMs buying into rallies and selling into declines to hedge short gamma. Barbon and Buraschi (Gamma Fragility, 2020-2021 working paper) showed the same interaction at the individual stock level: negative gamma imbalance plus illiquidity predicts intraday momentum; positive gamma predicts mean-reversion. They also connected gamma imbalance to flash crash frequency and magnitude. A 2022 forex paper quantified the channel numerically: negative 1,000 billion dollars net gamma exposure translates to 0.7-0.9 percent absolute volatility increase in EURUSD and USDJPY.

The window of weakness (post-OPEX bearishness) is the practitioner thesis with the longest historical backtest but least satisfying mechanistic validation. Quantifiable Edges documented an average SPX return of negative 0.9 percent during the week after monthly options expiration across 1960-2024 (64 years), with particularly bearish records for the weeks after September, June, and March expirations. Stivers and Sun (2013, JBF) found the inverse: S&P 100 stocks showed systematically elevated returns during expiration week versus control weeks. Neither paper provides formal t-statistics in search-accessible summaries. The practitioner mechanism is coherent but the causal attribution through dealer flows is unproven in peer-reviewed literature. The 0DTE regime change since 2022 is the most important structural shift for OPEX-calendar signals. SPX 0DTE volume grew from 5 percent of total SPX options in 2016 to 43-50 percent by 2023, driven by Cboe adding Tuesday and Thursday SPX expirations in April-May 2022. Academic consensus is that 0DTE options do not amplify volatility: CBOE 2023 research found net market maker gamma from 0DTE activity at 0.04-0.17 percent of daily S&P futures notional. Dim, Eraker, and Vilkov (2024, SSRN) found high OI gamma in 0DTEs does not propagate past volatility, and realized SPX volatility is 60 annualized basis points lower on days when 0DTE trading is active due to counter-directional hedging from positions rolling into same-day expiry. The structural consequence is that gamma concentration has dispersed across the calendar. Monthly OPEX retains dominance by OI but its peak GEX is now estimated at only 2-3 times a typical daily session. Quarterly OPEX retains the highest concentration at an estimated 5-8 times a daily session. The JPMorgan JHEQX collar, approximately 35,000 SPX contracts per leg at roughly 22.7 billion dollars notional per leg at SPX 6500, is the best-documented single-position OPEX effect: its quarterly roll coincides with notable SPX intraday moves and structurally suppresses SPX put skew and quarterly-tenor implied volatility.

### [WEAKENED] (orig conf: strong)
CORRECTED: In the current dealer-net-long-gamma regime (2018–2024 sample), the presence of same-day-expiry SPX options is associated with LOWER realized S&P500 volatility — about 60 annualized bps lower on 0DTE-present days — because longer-dated positions rolling DOWN into same-day expiry (~24bps of hedging needs) force market makers to trade counter-directionally, far more than new same-day 0DTE trading (~3bps) [Adams-Dim-Eraker-Fontaine-Ornthanalai-Vilkov 2025, SSRN 5641974; foundational Dim-Eraker-Vilkov SSRN 4692190]. This is a regime-conditional dampening tendency, NOT a law that 0DTE never propagates volatility: the effect is contingent on dealers being net LONG gamma (the paper's own inventory-sign result, Anderegg-Ulmann-Sornette 2022, and CBOE show negative dealer gamma AMPLIFIES intraday vol, up to +6.4pp/30-min), the causal 60bps is identified off a pre-May-2022 Tue/Thu weekday contrast that is no longer re-identifiable now that 0DTE trade every business day, and a peer study (Brogaard-Han-Won) finds volatility positively correlated with the 0DTE volume share — so the literature is mixed. Encode as a display-tier, dealer-gamma-sign-gated prior at moderate (not strong) confidence.

Pre-existing positions rolling into 0DTE drive 24bps of hedging needs; new same-day 0DTE positions contribute only 3bps.

### [WEAKENED] (orig conf: strong)
CORRECTED: Naive OI-based GEX assumes a fixed dealer sign convention — per the SqueezeMetrics white paper, dealers net SHORT calls and net LONG puts (note: some other vendors assume the opposite for indices). This convention is MORE reliable for SPX/index options — SqueezeMetrics validated it on SPX and explicitly reported "mixed/inconclusive" results on single equities — and UNRELIABLE for individual equity options, where net-customer positioning sign is genuinely variable per name (Ni-Pearson-Poteshman 2005/2020: single-stock delta-hedgers are sometimes net-long, sometimes net-written options, flipping hedging dynamics). Caveat: even index sign is not immutable — overwriting-ETF flows, vol-target strategies, and the post-2022 0DTE regime can invert aggregate dealer gamma, so 'empirically reliable for SPX/SPY' should be softened to 'more reliable, but still requires flow confirmation.' The OI aggregation error (a strike's OI built from offsetting buy/sell flows can net to ~zero dealer exposure while naive GEX reports large exposure) is real and cleanly demonstrated by Glassnode's taker-flow methodology — though that demonstration is on CRYPTO options (Deribit), used here as a general mechanism illustration, not equity-specific evidence. Encode as a moderate-confidence prior: trust OI-GEX sign for index, down-weight or require signed-flow confirmation for single names.
EVIDENCE: SqueezeMetrics white paper (2016, public PDF at squeezemetrics.com). Glassnode taker-flow methodology demonstrates the aggregation error: a strike with equal buyer-taker and seller-taker OI has near-zero net dealer exposure while naive GEX reports large exposure. SpotGamma and multiple practitioners acknowledge this limitation.

### [WEAKENED] (orig conf: strong)
CORRECTED: Charm exposure and vanna exposure are BOTH fully computable at end-of-day from open interest, the IV surface, spot, and an assumed dealer sign (+1 calls / -1 puts) — neither requires trade-direction data, and the per-vol-point/per-day flow magnitudes are deterministic Black-Scholes quantities. The genuine asymmetry is only in the SIGN of the REALIZED flow: charm's driving variable is the passage of time (Δt), whose sign is known and always positive, so the projected one-day charm flow direction is knowable in advance under the ceteris-paribus (spot- and IV-constant) assumption. Vanna's driving variable is the change in implied volatility (ΔIV), whose sign is exogenous and not known ex ante — so converting the (computable) vanna exposure into a signed buy/sell flow requires a forecast or contemporaneous observation of IV direction. It is incorrect to say vanna's 'magnitude' or exposure needs a forecast; only the realized directional sign does. Both flows are 'deterministic' only counterfactually — realized next-day delta drift mixes charm, gamma (spot moves), and vanna (IV moves) together. Practitioner CHEX/VEX formulas (Σ Greek × OI × 100 × [spot] × dealer_sign) and the charm-vs-vanna contrast are consistent across the cited FlashAlpha/GEXBoard sources; the '10x charm acceleration near expiry' figure is illustrative order-of-magnitude for an OTM strike, and charm is non-monotonic in moneyness (near-zero for exactly-ATM options).
EVIDENCE: Practitioner consensus from OAS documentation (optionsanalysissuite.com), FlashAlpha CHEX and VEX methodology pages, and SpotGamma. Charm formula: sum across strikes of (charm per contract times OI times 100 times spot times dealer sign). Charm accelerates non-linearly: an option at 30 DTE may lose 0.01 delta per day versus 0.10 delta per day at 2 DTE (10x acceleration).

### measurable_constructs
- GEX_t = sum over all strikes and expirations of [gamma(k,T,sigma_k) times OI(k,T) times 100 times S-squared times 0.01 times dealer_sign(k)]; dealer_sign = +1 for calls (dealer assumed short), -1 for puts (dealer assumed short); units = dollars per 1 percent S move; aggregate across nearest N expirations
- CHEX_t = sum over all strikes and expirations of [charm(k,T,sigma_k) times OI(k,T) times 100 times S times dealer_sign(k)]; units = dollars of dealer delta change per calendar day; computable from EOD OI plus IV surface with no trade direction needed; charm accelerates non-linearly as DTE approaches zero
- VEX_t = sum over all strikes and expirations of [vanna(k,T,sigma_k) times OI(k,T) times 100 times S times dealer_sign(k)]; units = dollars of dealer delta change per 1-vol-point IV move; requires IV surface by strike and expiry; directional implication is conditional on realized IV direction (rising versus falling)
- Gamma_Flip_level = spot price S* where aggregate GEX(S*) equals zero, computed by evaluating GEX at each spot-grid point holding strikes and OI fixed; identifies the price level where dealer regime switches from stabilizing (long gamma) to amplifying (short gamma)
- Call_Wall = argmax over strikes k of [call_gamma(k,T) times OI_call(k,T)]; strike with highest call gamma-weighted OI; functions as resistance and pinning attractor in positive gamma regimes
- Put_Wall = argmax over strikes k of [put_gamma(k,T) times OI_put(k,T)]; analogous attractor on the downside; functions as support and pinning anchor
- OPEX_calendar_flag = binary indicator: value 1 on Monday through Friday of the third week of month when Friday is a standard monthly OPEX; separately flag quarterly OPEX weeks (March, June, September, December third Friday); value 0 otherwise
- post_OPEX_window_flag = 1 for trading days T+1 through T+5 after monthly OPEX Friday; used to condition return distribution on the post-gamma-roll-off period associated with the documented negative average weekly SPX return of -0.9 percent
- GEX_roll_off_fraction = (GEX_pre_expiry minus GEX_post_expiry) divided by GEX_pre_expiry; fraction of gamma that expires at OPEX; values above 0.2 mark structurally significant expirations where the window-of-weakness mechanism should be strongest
- charm_front_month_flow_usd = restricted sum of (charm_k times OI_k times 100 times S times dealer_sign_k) over contracts with DTE less than 30; measures the accelerating daily structural bid or offer building into monthly OPEX Friday; positive values mean dealers must buy underlying each day, negative means they must sell
- GEX_normalized = GEX_t divided by (SPX_ADV_notional times 0.01); expresses net gamma as fraction of 1 percent of daily S&P futures notional; CBOE 2023 benchmark: normal 0DTE contribution equals 0.04 to 0.17 percent; extreme readings beyond 1.3 to 1.9 percent are stress-regime thresholds

### pitfalls
- GEX sign convention is a model assumption, not an observation: for single-name equities, institutional flow can position dealers long gamma in calls (for example writing covered calls against long inventory), inverting the expected hedging direction. Do not apply naive GEX signs to individual equity options without validation via flow data or 13F filings.
- EOD OI lags the live book by up to one full day. Intraday positions opened on OPEX day itself never appear in prior EOD OI. GEX computed from T-1 OI is structurally stale for OPEX-day intraday strategies and can misidentify pin levels when large positions are opened same-day.
- Pinning evidence from Ni-Pearson-Poteshman (2005) and Golez-Jackwerth (2012) is pre-2012. No peer-reviewed replication has formally tested whether pinning persists in the post-0DTE, post-weekly-option era when gamma is dispersed across hundreds of expirations rather than concentrated in monthly contracts.
- Post-OPEX window of weakness (negative 0.9 percent average per Quantifiable Edges) has no published formal t-statistic or p-value in accessible sources. The 64-year sample conflates pre-options (1960-1973) and post-options eras. Crash-heavy periods like 2008-2009 and March 2020 may dominate the average. Out-of-sample validation post-2022 is absent from peer-reviewed sources.
- Vanna flows are conditional on a volatility regime change. If IV is flat, charm is the only systematic pre-OPEX force. A signal engine must condition vanna-flow sign on an IV forecast or contemporaneous IV change, not just on the expiration calendar, or vanna and charm signals will be conflated.
- 0DTE volume dilution of monthly OPEX gamma concentration is real but incompletely quantified. The claim that monthly OPEX GEX is 2-3 times a daily session is a practitioner estimate without peer-reviewed calibration. The pre-2022 baseline concentration was also never formally measured in academic literature.
- JHEQX collar market impact lacks causal identification. The March 2026 3 percent SPX rally attribution is coincidental timing with other macro catalysts present simultaneously. Using JHEQX roll dates as a systematic return signal risks overfitting to a small number of well-publicized anecdotes.
- The gamma-momentum effect from Barbon-Buraschi and Baltussen et al. is strongest in illiquid underlyings. For large-cap liquid names and SPX index futures, the effect may be swamped by other order flow. Do not extrapolate individual equity illiquid-name results to index futures without liquidity conditioning.
- All GEX and CHEX computations from public EOD data assume Black-Scholes delta hedging by dealers. In practice, dealers use vol-surface-aware delta under sticky-strike, sticky-delta, or local-vol models, so actual hedging flows differ from naive BS-delta-based GEX formulas in strikes where skew is steep.
- The Avellaneda-Lipkin price elasticity constant required to estimate pinning probability from their SDE model is unobservable from public data and varies by stock, liquidity, and market conditions. Without empirical calibration this parameter cannot be estimated reliably out-of-sample.
- Backtests of OPEX-calendar effects to 1960 conflate pre- and post-options listing regimes. CBOE equity options launched 1973, S&P 500 index options 1983, S&P futures 1982. Pre-1973 data has no options channel and should be excluded from calibration of dealer-flow-based OPEX signals.
- Kolanovic-era JPMorgan research on derivatives rebalancing conflated options dealer gamma rebalancing with systematic strategy rebalancing from CTAs, vol-target funds, and risk-parity. These are distinct mechanisms operating on different timescales. Signal engines should separate options-specific gamma flows from vol-targeting strategy flows to avoid double-counting or mislabeling the source of directional pressure.

## Lane: release-forecasting

### narrative
Forecasting major U.S. macro prints — CPI, PPI, NFP, and jobless claims — is a mature but fragmented field. Practitioners blend real-time high-frequency indicators, component-level bottom-up models, and market-implied signals; researchers evaluate each empirically. The picture that emerges is of strong but regime-dependent predictability, with major structural instabilities post-2020 making pre-pandemic backtests unreliable guides.

For CPI, the Cleveland Fed's daily nowcast is the benchmark open-source model. It combines daily oil prices, weekly retail gasoline prices (EIA), and lagged monthly CPI reads to produce separate nowcasts for core, food, and energy, then aggregates. In real-time evaluation (2023 commentary), it outperforms the Blue Chip consensus across all four timing windows within a reference month, and beats the SPF median by 0.41 pp RMSE on headline CPI and 0.25 pp on PCE; core matches SPF parity but is available at daily frequency. The dominant bottom-up opportunity is the shelter/OER complex: BLS samples ~50,000 housing units, each revisited every six months (one-sixth of the sample per month), so a single unit's lease change propagates as a sixth-root approximation. Market rent indices — Zillow Observed Rent Index (ZORI), Apartment List, BLS's own New Tenant Repeat Rent Index (NTRR) — lead official OER by roughly four quarters (NTRR) or one quarter (all-tenant repeat rent). In January 2022, ZORI was running at 16.5% annualized while CPI rent was 4.4%; by December 2022 CPI rent was 8.3% and still rising while ZORI had peaked in March and was declining. This 12-month lead, corroborated by the SF Fed and Richmond Fed, is one of the highest-confidence predictive relationships in CPI nowcasting. The Manheim Used Vehicle Value Index leads the CPI used-cars component by approximately 1-2 months at the wholesale-to-retail transition; it peaked at 258 in January 2022 and the subsequent 28% decline through mid-2024 accurately signaled the multi-year CPI used-car disinflation. For energy, pass-through from crude oil to retail CPI is documented but distributed over time: direct effects are faster (weeks to months), second-round effects on food and core extend to roughly 8 quarters. The Kalshi prediction market for CPI headline recorded ~40% lower average forecast error than Bloomberg consensus between February 2023 and mid-2025, with ~67% outperformance in high-surprise episodes. Inflation swaps (1-year zero-coupon) are roughly unbiased CPI predictors historically but failed the 2021-2022 surge along with surveys; they recovered faster post-peak. Swap rates embed an inflation risk premium (~10-30 bp) that must be stripped for pure expectation extraction.

PPI is less studied as a standalone forecast target but is heavily used as a leading indicator for CPI/PCE. The ISM Manufacturing Prices Paid index closely tracks PPI intermediate demand and is the earliest signal (released on the first business day of the month). Cleveland Fed 2018 research found ISM Prices Paid most correlated with PPI, less so with CPI, and essentially noise for core PCE. Critically, several PPI sub-components feed directly into PCE price calculations via BEA's bridge: PPI health care services are the primary deflator for health care PCE; PPI portfolio management enters PCE financial services. These components drive the CPI-PCE wedge: health insurance weight in PCE is more than twice its CPI weight, and portfolio management inflation has run 121 pp above overall PCE cumulatively. PPI final demand services can thus move PCE without affecting CPI. PPI revision structure was updated in November 2021: indexes now go through five iterative updates over four months before finalization, reducing terminal revision magnitude versus the old single-revision-at-four-months regime.

NFP forecasting has the weakest signal-to-noise of the four series. The BLS's own 90% confidence interval on the monthly change is ±122,000 jobs. Mean absolute revision from second to third estimate is historically ~34,000. ADP's pre-2022 series had a full-period R² vs. BLS of 0.98, but that collapses to 0.42 excluding the COVID period, and to 0.01 for 2021-2022. The new Stanford methodology (August 2022) produces R² of 0.13 ex-COVID and approximately -0.03 for 2021-2022. ADP now explicitly disclaims any intent to forecast BLS. The most productive high-frequency inputs are: (1) initial jobless claims — models using claims reduce RMSFE by >22% vs. random walk; (2) WARN Act notices — they lead state-level UI claims and improve pseudo-real-time unemployment forecasts; (3) job postings (LinkUp) — NFP forecasts beat Reuters consensus 65% of the time over 2023-2025; (4) continuing claims trough-to-current as a recession early-warning with an average 11-month lead. QCEW-anchored benchmark revisions are partially predictable: with three-quarters of QCEW data available, the sign of the annual revision is inferable. The 2024 preliminary revision implied -818,000 jobs and 2025 implied -911,000 — both flagged in advance by analysts monitoring the QCEW-CES gap. Low survey response rates (declined from ~61% in 2016 to ~43% by 2025) mechanically increase initial-estimate error.

Jobless claims have their own forecasting pathology. The BLS shifted from multiplicative regression-based to structural time series seasonal adjustment in March 2024 — a methodology break that complicates historical model continuity. Holiday-week prints are systematically noisy: July 4th is distorted by auto plant retooling; the four-week moving average is the standard practitioner correction. State-level fraud waves — California's pandemic UI fraud (10-27% of benefits) and Minnesota's ongoing issues — contaminated the 2020-2021 seasonal baseline. The BLS permanently treats the weeks of March 21, 2020 through June 19, 2021 as additive, not subject to further revision.

Print-day market reactions are strongly regime-dependent. The GSS (Gürkaynak-Sack-Swanson 2005 AER) framework established that CPI releases move long-end yields through changes in long-run inflation expectations. In the pre-COVID regime, bond yields barely reacted to inflation surprises even with high rate uncertainty. Post-COVID, the CPI x short-rate uncertainty interaction became positive and highly significant across 2y, 5y, 10y Treasuries (BIS WP 1361, 1998-2024 sample). The 2022-2023 tightening regime produced a classic 'good news is bad news' dynamic: stronger NFP or hotter CPI was equity-negative because rate-path repricing dominated. A 2026 study confirms asymmetric S&P 500 reactions to CPI surprises were strongly regime-dependent, with disinflationary signals generating the most pronounced equity gains during the high-inflation period. A 100,000+ NFP surprise in January 2024 moved 10-year yields approximately 10 bp. Bauer-Swanson (NBER 2022) showed pre-FOMC monetary policy surprises have R² of 10-40% against pre-announcement macro/financial data, meaning conventional high-frequency surprises are partially endogenous — a critical warning for event studies on release days.

- New Tenant Rent Index (NTRR) spread vs OER YoY: BLS quarterly R-CPI-NTR minus CPI OER YoY — positive spread predicts OER deceleration with ~4-quarter lead. Zillow ZORI YoY minus CPI Rent YoY gives faster signal (1-2 quarter lead)
- Manheim Used Vehicle Value Index month-over-month change, lagged 1-2 months, as leading indicator for CPI used-cars-and-trucks; available mid-month (preliminary) and end-of-month (final) from Cox Automotive
- ISM Manufacturing Prices Paid level vs 50 threshold: above 50 signals rising PPI intermediate demand; first-business-day release for prior month; historically correlated with PPI intermediate demand processed goods 1-2 months forward
- GSCPI reading vs zero: NY Fed monthly Global Supply Chain Pressure Index; positive = above-average pressure; correlated with PPI and import prices 1-3 months forward; free download from NY Fed website
- Inflation swap 1y zero-coupon rate minus SPF or Blue Chip 1y CPI median: gap signals directional deviation of market from consensus; strip ~10-30 bp estimated risk premium for pure expectation; available real-time via Bloomberg or ICE
- Initial claims 4-week moving average deviation from threshold: inverse predictor of NFP; >22% RMSFE improvement over random walk for unemployment rate in Kansas City Fed research; threshold has drifted upward with labor force growth
- Continuing claims ratio (SA continuing claims divided by civilian labor force): ratio trough to current is recession early-warning with average 11-month lead on recession starts; ratio from FRED CCSA divided by FRED CLF16OV
- QCEW-CES payroll gap: CES private payroll level minus QCEW-implied level; positive gap predicts negative annual benchmark revision; constructable from BLS QCEW state UI records vs CES establishment survey; quarterly with ~5-month lag
- WARN Act notices 30-day rolling count: leads mass layoff events and initial claims by several weeks; DOL data from state WARN registries and DOL aggregate; signals emerging labor market stress before it hits claims
- PPI-to-PCE bridge components: PPI Services for Outpatient Care, Hospital Care, Physician Services feed BEA health PCE deflator; PPI Securities Brokerage and Portfolio Management feed BEA financial services PCE deflator; track these sub-indices for PCE-vs-CPI divergence signals
- CPI surprise event regression with regime dummy: regress 30-minute intraday yield change on CPI surprise (release minus Bloomberg median); include pre-2022 vs post-2022 regime interaction; expected post-2022 coefficient is substantially larger, especially on 2-year Treasury
- Prediction market median implied CPI print (Kalshi) vs Bloomberg consensus median: directional delta is tradeable edge signal; magnitude of gap predicts whether consensus is above or below where market aggregates
- Birth-death model January drag: BLS publishes monthly birth-death adjustment with each NFP release; January adjustment historically large negative (~200K); deviations from the seasonal norm distort headline SA payrolls in predictable direction

### pitfalls
- ADP-BLS R-squared of 0.98 is COVID-contaminated: excluding the 2020 crash, R-squared for the new ADP methodology is 0.13 ex-COVID and approximately -0.03 for 2021-2022 — using ADP as a directional NFP predictor in normal times is not supported by measured evidence
- OER nowcasting with NTRR has a sample-size limitation: the BLS NTRR is based on only 500-1,500 new-tenant observations per quarter, more than 80% apartments, reducing robustness for non-apartment OER prediction and adding sampling noise
- Inflation swap rates embed a risk premium (~10-30 bp) requiring stripping: using raw swap fixings as pure CPI forecasts introduces systematic upward bias, especially at short horizons and in stressed inflation environments
- Holiday-week claims are unreliable standalone observations: July 4th week (auto retooling), Thanksgiving, Christmas, and New Year weeks produce 30,000-50,000+ artificial swings; always use 4-week moving average and flag holiday-contaminated weeks in any signal
- Pre-2020 event-study coefficients for CPI/NFP on yields substantially understate the post-2022 regime: the CPI x rate-uncertainty interaction was essentially zero pre-COVID and became highly significant post-COVID (BIS WP 1361); deploying pre-pandemic regression weights in the current environment is a regime-break hazard
- Sign of equity reaction to CPI surprise flips with monetary regime: in easing or low-inflation regimes a lower CPI can signal weaker growth (bad for equities); in tightening regimes a lower CPI signals fewer hikes (good for equities). Any signal system must encode current regime before applying a reaction function
- Birth-death model distortions peak in January and April-June: seasonal adjustment of the birth-death component interacts with calendar-driven hiring and layoff patterns to create systematic forecast errors at turning points, especially when actual new-business formation diverges from the model's historical average
- QCEW benchmark revision sign is more predictable than magnitude: historical data shows preliminary estimates of the revision tend to understate the final magnitude by approximately 120,000 on average (preliminary revisions are biased toward smaller corrections), so a quant should trade sign not precise level
- Shelter CPI lag creates an irreducible nowcast floor: even with perfect real-time market rent data, the BLS 6-month sampling rotation and sixth-root monthly conversion impose approximately a 2-quarter smoothing delay on OER that cannot be removed by any nowcasting technique — the remaining lag is structural
- California and other state fraud waves contaminated 2020-2021 seasonal patterns: BLS permanently froze pandemic-period SA adjustments as additive and non-revisable; models trained on windows including 2020-2021 claims will have corrupted seasonal factors for holiday weeks and seasonal turning points
- GSCPI link to CPI core is weak: local projection results show the NY Fed supply chain index explains substantial PPI variance but is nearly noise for core PCE; using GSCPI as a direct core CPI predictor overstates its signal content relative to its demonstrated PPI relationship
- PPI-to-CPI pass-through timing is regime-unstable: under inflationary supply-side shocks the pipeline accelerates; under normal demand-side dynamics lags extend to several quarters; a fixed-lag model estimated in one regime will misfire in the other — this was not visible in pre-2021 backtests
- Kalshi outperformance may reflect platform mechanics and thin liquidity: the market prices well-defined discrete round-number outcomes and may systematically mis-price unusual decimal outcomes; accuracy statistics may partly reflect how contracts resolve rather than pure probabilistic skill
- NFP survey response rate decline from 61% in 2016 to 43% by 2025 mechanically increases initial-estimate error and revision magnitude: models trained before this deterioration will underestimate monthly NFP nowcast uncertainty and over-rely on initial prints
- BLS SA methodology break for claims in March 2024 creates a non-comparable historical series: the shift from multiplicative regression to structural time series models means seasonal factors are not comparable before and after the break date; backtest windows crossing March 2024 require re-estimation

## Lane: rate-path-modeling

### narrative
Market-implied rate paths are extracted from multiple instruments, each carrying different biases. The dominant tool remains CME FedWatch, which reads 30-day fed funds futures (ZQ contracts) and constructs a binary probability tree assuming 25bp increments. The critical methodology: each ZQ contract settles on the average daily EFFR over an entire calendar month, so months containing an FOMC meeting require day-weighted interpolation separating pre- and post-meeting days. CME also runs SOFRWatch using SOFR options to derive full probability distributions over three-month compounded SOFR. The Atlanta Fed Market Probability Tracker uses pure options on three-month SOFR to extract the full probability distribution -- methodologically cleaner for tail risk than the binary FedWatch tree. All futures-implied paths carry a term-risk premium that must be stripped before treating them as pure rate expectations. Piazzesi and Swanson (2008) documented that excess returns on fed funds futures are positive on average and strongly countercyclical, well-predicted by employment growth and Treasury yield spreads. A 1pp drop in nonfarm payroll growth correlates with approximately 20bp excess return on 4-month contracts; the bias reaches 36-72bp at 9-month horizons, implying 12-36% upward adjustment to raw hike probabilities. The direction reverses across the cycle: futures over-predict hikes in booms and over-predict easing in downturns, with the largest errors ahead of turning points.

Term structure decomposition is the backbone of any serious yield-modeling system. The NY Fed ACM model (Adrian-Crump-Moench 2013), updated daily from 1961, uses five principal components in a regression-based affine framework. The Kim-Wright (KW) model uses three factors plus Blue Chip survey data via Kalman filter, making it more survey-anchored. ACM has historically averaged 26bp higher than KW with 0.86 correlation. A 2024 Fed FEDS Note analyzing the 2023 Treasury Tantrum quantified this divergence: ACM attributed 116bp of the 115bp total July-October 2023 yield spike to term premium, while KW attributed only 69bp to term premium and 44bp to revised rate expectations. The practical rule: 10y-2y slope moves map approximately 1:1 to term premium; 2-year yield moves split approximately 50-50. The ACM 10-year term premium stood at approximately +0.73% in April 2026, at the 35th percentile of the 65-year distribution, exiting the 2014-2023 negative regime (average -0.30%, floor -1.34% in July 2020). The 10-year yield of 4.45% as of April 2026 decomposed as 3.72% expected path plus 0.73% term premium. Cleveland Fed separates TIPS breakevens into expected inflation, inflation risk premium (approximately 20-60bp), and a TIPS liquidity premium. The r-star debate is directly load-bearing: HLW estimated the real neutral rate near 0.84-1.0% real in 2025 while 10-year TIPS implied 2.25-2.5%, a 1.5pp gap. NY Fed Liberty Street research confirmed TIPS yields have essentially no predictive power for real rates three years out (R-squared near zero vs HLW R-squared of 0.16).

The Fed reaction-function literature establishes key signal-system constraints. Orphanides (2001, 2003) proved that real-time Taylor rule recommendations diverge substantially from ex-post-revision estimates: real-time output gap estimates ran 1-2pp biased versus subsequent revisions, and the 1970s inflation disaster would have been replicated even under a formal Taylor rule using real-time data. This means Taylor rule deviations in a signal system must use ALFRED vintage archive data, not current-vintage BEA. The SEP dot plot track record is nuanced: a 2023 Fed Board paper found dots beat consensus surveys, VAR models, and market-based measures at many horizons, but private forecasters systematically over-weight stale dots between quarterly updates, slowing information absorption. As of June 2026, incoming Chair Warsh abstained from submitting his own dot entry and launched a task force to potentially eliminate the dot plot, creating structural break risk for any signal built on SEP inference. FOMC text-based hawk-dove indices have measured predictive content: approximately 69% of FOMC members have persistent preferences (39% hawk, 30% dove). NLP-based scoring with FinBERT carries statistically significant predictive power for immediate market reactions, but out-of-sample evidence on 3-6 month rate decision prediction is limited and model-dependent.

The yield forecasting literature has a settled first-order result: at horizons under 3 months, Treasury yields are near-impossible to beat versus a random walk. Duffee (2002) showed standard affine models perform worse than the random walk; dynamic Nelson-Siegel models beat the random walk out-of-sample at 12-month horizons but not at 1-month. The practical exception is trend-following: Moskowitz-Ooi-Pedersen (2012) documented significant time-series momentum across 58 instruments including bond futures, persistent over 1-12 month lookback periods. AQR century-of-evidence research shows bond trend-following has been profitable since at least 1880. The SG Trend Index gained 27.3% in 2022 (record year) from short-bond positioning. The key regime-break risk: rapid Fed pivots create whipsaw losses that levered trend strategies cannot easily survive.

Supply-side mechanics are now load-bearing drivers of long yields after 2022. The November 1, 2023 QRA announcement that Treasury would hold coupon supply roughly constant triggered an 89bp 10-year yield decline over the following two months -- one of the largest non-FOMC-triggered yield moves on record. A $100bn increase in Treasury coupon issuance raises repo rates approximately 5-6bp (Fed FEDS Note, February 2025); a 1% increase in supply-to-GDP raises long yields 9bp in the post-2010 period versus 2bp in 1992-2010, a 4-5x increase in sensitivity due to post-Dodd-Frank dealer balance sheet constraints. Laubach (2003) estimated 25bp per 1pp increase in projected deficit-to-GDP in forward long rates, broadly replicated in IMF cross-country work. The ON RRP buffer was essentially fully drained by early 2025, removing the absorber between QT and direct reserve scarcity. Foreign ownership of Treasuries has declined as a share of the float: from $3.1T to $9.5T since 2008, but total debt quadrupled from $6.4T to $31.4T. The Treasury convenience yield fell from approximately -20bp to -60bp between 2022-2025, with a 1pp increase in debt-to-GDP associated with 0.37-0.86bp erosion. Auction bidding elasticity declined from -0.9 (1992-2010) to -0.2 (2011-2025), meaning tail risk per unit of supply surprise is now much larger.

### [UPHELD] (orig conf: strong)
CORRECTED: CME FedWatch reads 30-day fed funds futures (ZQ), whose price reflects the average daily EFFR over the contract month, and constructs an expanding binary probability tree in 25bp increments (adapting to 50/75bp branches in crisis regimes). For a calendar month containing an FOMC meeting it isolates the post-meeting implied rate by arithmetic day-weighting — pre-meeting rate × days-before + post-meeting rate × days-after = monthly-average rate × total days — then linearly interpolates between adjacent 25bp levels to assign probabilities. Correct as a description of the mechanism, but the resulting figures are RISK-NEUTRAL probabilities biased by fed funds futures risk premia (~35-61bp/yr), can be badly amplified/distorted when meetings fall in consecutive months with few post-meeting days (~9x noise inflation), and become unreliable at multi-meeting horizons due to independence and piecewise-flat term-structure assumptions — so encode it as a market-expectation proxy, not a calibrated true-probability.
EVIDENCE: CME Group FedWatch Methodology documentation (cmegroup.com/articles/2023/understanding-the-cme-group-fedwatch-tool-methodology.html) and CME FedWatch User Guide

### [WEAKENED] (orig conf: strong)
CORRECTED: The Atlanta Fed Market Probability Tracker uses CME options on three-month compounded-average SOFR futures to extract a full risk-neutral probability density over future three-month compounded SOFR (via a Breeden-Litzenberger / Shimko volatility-surface inversion with Generalized Pareto tail fitting). ### measurable_constructs
- ZQ-implied meeting-by-meeting rate path: for each FOMC date, arithmetic interpolation of current and next ZQ contract prices assuming 25bp move grid; pre-meeting expected rate = (contract_implied_rate * days_in_month - post_meeting_expected_rate * days_after_meeting) / days_before_meeting
- Risk-premium-adjusted implied path: subtract Piazzesi-Swanson countercyclical correction proportional to trailing 3-month nonfarm payroll growth z-score and IG-Treasury spread from each forward node; adjustment 0bp at under 45 days scaling to 20-36bp at 6-9 months
- ACM term premium regime indicator: daily THREEFYTP10 from FRED; track 1y, 2y, 5y, 10y term premiums separately; binary flag for positive vs negative regime; monitor KW minus ACM divergence as expectations vs premium split signal
- 10y-2y slope as term premium proxy: slope move maps approximately 1:1 to term premium in yields-only statistical models; 2y move maps approximately 50-50 between expectations and premium
- Cleveland Fed inflation decomposition: extract expected inflation, inflation risk premium, and TIPS liquidity premium separately; use as inputs to 5y and 10y real yield signal rather than raw breakevens
- 5y5y breakeven gap to Cleveland model expected inflation: difference measures combined inflation risk premium plus liquidity premium; use this gap rather than raw breakeven during stress periods
- Real-time Taylor rule gap: (fed funds target) minus (Taylor rate computed with ALFRED real-time vintage of core PCE and real-time CBO potential output); positive gap indicates accommodation, negative indicates restriction
- QRA coupon supply shock: for each TBAC refunding announcement compute change in announced coupon auction size vs prior quarter and vs dealer survey median; net positive surprise maps to approximately 5-9bp yield pressure per $100bn excess coupon supply
- TGA balance change signal: weekly WTREGEN from FRED; TGA drain equals reserve add with short-rate pressure lower; rebuild equals reserve drain with short end yield pressure higher
- ON RRP balance threshold: RRPONTSYD from FRED; near $200bn signals buffer exhausted and tightening-adjacent repo market conditions amplifying QRA sensitivity
- Auction demand composite Z-score: for each 10y and 30y auction compute tail z-score plus indirect bid pct vs 12-month trailing average z-score plus dealer takedown pct vs 12-month trailing average z-score; positive composite is bearish next-week yield signal
- Bond futures trend signal: sign of 12-month return of continuous 10y Treasury futures; cross-validate with 1-month and 3-month returns; apply volatility scaling; historical Sharpe approximately 0.4-0.6 for long-only trend
- Fiscal deficit forward projection gap: CBO 5-year forward projected deficit-to-GDP minus prior forecast; each 1pp increase maps to approximately 25bp long-yield upward pressure per Laubach (2003)
- Foreign ownership share of Treasury float: TIC data monthly; track quarterly change as slow-moving structural backdrop signal amplifying supply sensitivity
- Convenience yield proxy: 3-month OIS minus 3-month T-bill yield adjusted for credit; declining convenience yield signals structural demand erosion and amplifies supply effects on long yields
- 5y5y TIPS real rate vs HLW r-star gap: use as risk premium estimate rather than rate forecast given near-zero predictive power of TIPS yields for realized rates at 3-year horizon

### pitfalls
- ZQ interpolation breaks when two meetings fall within one month or during intra-meeting emergency actions -- FedWatch binary tree assigns zero probability to emergency moves and mismaps months with two meetings
- Risk premium direction reversal near cycle turning points: the Piazzesi-Swanson premium is positive on average but reverses ahead of policy pivots, so mechanically applying the adjustment at turning points overcorrects in the wrong direction
- ACM vs KW divergence of up to 47bp on the same yield move is not a bug to average away: choosing the wrong model changes whether a signal is classified as term premium surprise or rate expectations surprise, with opposite policy implications
- TIPS liquidity premium is time-varying and spikes precisely when the inflation signal is most needed: during COVID March 2020 breakevens went deeply negative while actual expected inflation stayed positive -- raw breakeven is unusable as a standalone inflation signal in stress periods
- 5y5y TIPS-implied real rate has essentially zero predictive power for realized real rates 3 years out (R-squared near zero per NY Fed 2025 research) -- treating it as a rate anchor is folklore not measured evidence
- Dot plot anchoring effect means private forecasts and market pricing are NOT independent of the SEP -- using both in a forecast ensemble double-counts the same information, especially in the 6-12 weeks following an FOMC meeting
- Dot plot structural break: any signal built on inferring FOMC intent from dots faces near-term structural break risk under Warsh's June 2026 communications reform initiative; the dot may be eliminated
- Real-time Taylor rule requires ALFRED vintage data at the exact estimation date; using current-vintage BEA data introduces hindsight bias of 1-2pp on the output gap, the most consequential input in the rule
- Treasury supply effects on yields are regime-conditional: post-Dodd-Frank dealer balance sheet constraints made post-2010 yield sensitivity approximately 5x the 1992-2010 level -- historical regressions over the full sample badly understate current sensitivity
- Trend-following in rates requires autocorrelated rate moves; rapid Fed pivots (2019 mid-cycle cut, 2022 hiking acceleration, 2024-2025 cut-then-hold cycle) create whipsaw losses that can be catastrophic for levered trend strategies
- QRA market impact identification is contaminated by simultaneous events: the November 2023 episode involved supply guidance surprise, multiple CPI beats, and Fed communication shifts occurring within the same event window
- TGA and RRP plumbing signals are ambiguous during debt ceiling episodes: Treasury reduces bill supply (yield-lowering supply effect) while simultaneously raising X-date risk premium (yield-raising risk effect), and the net direction varies by tenor
- Foreign demand timing signal is near-zero despite clear secular trend: the declining share of foreign Treasury holdings is a multi-year structural headwind with essentially no quarterly or monthly timing information for yield moves
- Convenience yield is not directly observable -- all proxy measures are constructed from spreads with embedded assumptions, and estimates diverge substantially across models in stress periods
- FOMC hawk-dove NLP scores trained pre-2026 face severe regime-change risk under Warsh's shorter statements, no-forward-guidance philosophy, and five-task-force communications overhaul
- Shadow rate models (Wu-Xia, Krippner) mechanically converge to the observed rate above the ZLB -- at current fed funds rates above 4% they provide no additional information and should not be used as a monetary stance measure

## Lane: regime-patterns

### narrative
Rate and inflation regimes form the deepest structural layer of macro context a signal system can possess. The 1965-2026 arc divides into three grand episodes: the great inflation (1965-1981), the secular disinflation (1982-2020), and the post-pandemic reflation and normalization (2020-present). Understanding the anatomy of turning points within each episode is the primary task.

Rate cycle turning points from 1981 onward reveal a consistent anatomical sequence: a catalyst event compresses the prior trend, a confirming follow-through signal (Fed pivot or data break) defines the turn, and duration of the subsequent cycle is measured in years not quarters. The secular top at 15.68% in October 1981 was confirmed by Volcker's policy reversal in August 1982, itself forced by Mexico's debt default and recession depth. The 1984 secondary peak near 13.9% was followed by a sharp drop driven by falling inflation and a growth scare; the 1987 peak near 10% preceded the October crash. Each post-1981 yield trough was confirmed by a Fed ease pivot: 1993 troughed near 5.2% (bond bull exhaustion before the 1994 bear steepener), the 2003 trough at 3.1% followed the end of the dot-com ease cycle, the 2012-2016 range around 1.4-2.1% represented QE-anchored secular lows, and the August 2020 all-time low of 0.55% was pandemic-emergency-policy-driven. The 2022-2023 bear market in bonds was the fastest tightening since 1980, taking the 10Y from 0.55% to 5.02% by October 2023 — a 447bp swing in roughly 38 months. The October 2023 top was confirmed by a dual signal: below-expected CPI prints in October/November 2023 and Treasury's unexpected reduction in long-dated supply plans. The 2024-2026 arc has re-steepened after the record 793-day inversion (July 2022-October 2024), with the steepening type contested between bull (Fed cutting) and bear (term premium expansion) dynamics.

Inflation regimes carry a distinct taxonomy with documented regime-path properties. The 1970s double-peak episode (CPI ~12% in December 1974, then 14.8% in March 1980) established that disinflation after a first peak can be incomplete — the Burns Fed allowed re-acceleration — while only a genuine policy regime change (Volcker, October 1979 Saturday Night Special, real rates driven strongly positive) achieved durable break. Volcker-era disinflation, when measured with today's CPI methodology (adjusting pre-1983 shelter treatment per Bolhuis-Cramer-Summers NBER WP30116), required approximately 5pp of core CPI decline, not the official 11pp — because mortgage rates were embedded in pre-1983 CPI. The 2021-2022 cycle peaked at headline CPI 9.1% (June 2022), goods outside shelter peaked at ~10.6% and fell to near 1% by mid-2023 (rapid supply-chain normalization), while shelter peaked later at ~8.2% in March 2023 and remained sticky through 2025 due to OER lag. The structural services-vs-goods bifurcation is the key disinflation-path feature: goods deflation cleared quickly; services/shelter proved the 'last mile' problem.

Equity sector behavior by rate regime has both robust and contested components. The robust findings: financials benefit from rising short rates via NIM expansion — 2022 demonstrated 17.9% NIM growth YoY; utilities and REITs are reliably hurt by rising long rates (-12.4% and -15.8% respectively in 2022). The growth-vs-value duration trade is well-measured post-2020: S&P 500 Growth -29.4% vs Value -5.2% in 2022, a 24pp spread, second-largest annual value advantage since 1975. Exchange operator stocks (CME/ICE/NDAQ) exhibit a differentiated sensitivity: CME benefits from both elevated volatility (volume-driven clearing fees) and higher rates (collateral float income). CME's cash performance bond investment income jumped from ~$307M (2021) to $5.07B (2023), net of the distribution paid to clearing firms. Gold vs real yields showed a -0.73 to -0.82 rolling correlation (2003-2021) but the relationship broke materially post-2022: correlation dropped to near zero (3-7%) as central bank structural buying (1,000+ tonnes/year 2022-2024, vs 473t average 2010-2021) replaced ETF flow as the marginal price-setter — driven by post-Russia-sanctions reserve diversification.

FOMC-cycle and calendar anomalies require sharp regime-awareness. The Lucca-Moench pre-FOMC drift (49bp average excess return in 24h pre-FOMC, 1994-2011, accounting for ~80% of annual equity premium) disappeared after January 2016. The Cieslak-Morse-Vissing-Jorgensen even-week FOMC cycle (11.8% gross annualized excess return in even weeks, 1994 sample) weakened as early as 2004 and is statistically insignificant through end-2023; the mechanism (biweekly Fed board meetings enabling informal signaling) ceased when meeting schedules changed. Both effects are best treated as historically documented but currently inoperative without out-of-sample confirmation. Midterm-year seasonality (weak first half, Q4 rally, ~17.5% average intra-year drawdown) has an ~15-17 observations sample size since 1946; healthcare outperformed the S&P 500 by ~17% on average in midterm election years 1994-2024. Pre-election years average +14.2% (S&P 500, since 1926) — the strongest year of the 4-year cycle. Bitcoin's 4-year halving cycle overlaps the US election cycle by arithmetic coincidence; post-2022 the election/liquidity driver appears stronger than the supply-shock mechanism, and with only 3-4 data points the statistical inference is near-zero power.

### pitfalls
- Regime-break risk in yield curve signals: the 2022-2024 inversion was 793 days with no recession — the longest in FRED history. The yield-curve-inversion-to-recession rule now has a confirmed recent exception. Systems that encode this as a near-certain recessionary signal will have produced a sustained false positive during 2022-2024.
- Bull vs bear steepening conflation: bull steepening (Fed cuts, recession fear) is historically bearish for equities; bear steepening (growth/inflation) is historically bullish. Encoding only the direction of spread change without decomposing the driver produces opposite-sign signals for the same spread movement.
- Gold-real yield correlation regime break: the -0.73 to -0.82 historical correlation (2003-2021) collapsed post-2022. Systems treating real yields as a primary gold driver without a regime-switch detection layer will systematically misforecast gold when central-bank structural demand dominates the marginal price-setting role.
- CME collateral income gross vs net confusion: CME passes through ~93% of collateral income to clearing firms as an operating expense. The $5.07B gross figure (2023) overstates the net earnings benefit. Building CME earnings sensitivity on gross collateral income is a material error — the correct input is net retained spread.
- Pre-FOMC drift and FOMC cycle anomalies are currently inoperative: both Lucca-Moench and Cieslak et al. effects have failed out-of-sample post-2016 and post-2004 respectively. Encoding them as active signals risks capturing expired effects and data-mining noise.
- Midterm and presidential cycle seasonality: sample sizes are 15-17 observations (midterm years since 1946) and ~24 observations (presidential cycle years since 1926). Point estimates of sector outperformance (healthcare +17%) are drawn from 6-8 cycles. These should be encoded as weak Bayesian priors, not strong signals with tight thresholds.
- 1970s double-peak lesson misapplication: the structural conditions (no inflation targeting, no Fed credibility, fiscal dominance, oil cartel power, cost-push shocks) differ substantially from 2021-2026. Pattern-matching the two episodes mechanically overweights the second-wave risk and underweights the institutional change in monetary policy.
- OER/shelter inflation look-ahead trap: current OER reads a lagged signal (12-18 months behind market rents). Using current OER to forecast near-term inflation is using a backward-looking indicator as contemporaneous. Leading indicators are market-rent indices (Zillow, Apartment List), which peaked before OER and can signal normalization earlier.
- BTC 4-year cycle small-N: only 4 halvings and 3-4 election-cycle overlaps have occurred. All pattern claims about BTC-election alignment have effective sample size in single digits. These claims cannot be statistically validated and should be treated as narrative/anecdote, not structural signal.
- Exchange stock volatility sensitivity is nonlinear: CME/ICE/NDAQ benefit from short-burst volatility (volumes spike), but sustained extreme volatility can trigger deleveraging, reduced open interest, and margin call liquidations that ultimately reduce clearing volumes. The benefit function is not monotonic with volatility level — it likely peaks at moderate elevated volatility.
- Bolhuis-Cramer-Summers adjusted CPI is not a market-traded series: their 5pp adjusted Volcker disinflation is an academic construct. Markets traded on the official CPI throughout history. Signals built on the adjusted series will diverge from how markets actually responded to contemporary official releases.
- Sector rate-beta instability: rolling betas of sector ETFs vs yield changes are unstable across regimes. In a growth-scare bear steepening, both financials AND utilities can decline together (growth fear dominates rate benefit for banks). Rate-beta signals require regime conditioning rather than unconditional application.

## Lane: event-window-risk

### narrative
The macro announcement premium literature originates with Savor and Wilson (2013, JFQ), who documented that U.S. equities earn roughly 11.4 bps excess return on scheduled announcement days (CPI, unemployment, FOMC-adjacent) versus 1.1 bps on all other days — more than 60% of the realized annual equity risk premium concentrated in roughly 13% of trading days across 1958–2009. The mechanism proposed is a risk-based one: investors demand compensation for bearing unresolved uncertainty about macro fundamentals. Hu, Pan, Wang and Zhu (2019/2021, JFE) extended this into a two-factor framework where the pre-announcement equity return is specifically tied to the accumulation and resolution of 'impact uncertainty' — uncertainty not just about the data outcome but about how large the market impact will be. In their model the VIX serves as a gauge of impact uncertainty, and they document large overnight returns with no abnormal variance before NFP, ISM, and GDP announcements, mirroring pre-FOMC behavior. This line is contested. Ghaderi and Seo (2025, Management Science) argue that conditional return volatility barely drops at macro-announcements — a prediction of the premium hypothesis — and that the apparent premium is mostly attributable to monetary policy surprises plus small-sample luck that does not average out. Ernst, Gilbert and Hrdlicka (SSRN 2019) found that once you control for day-of-month fixed effects and inadvertent sample selection (announcements cluster mid-month when drift is positive anyway), the announcement premium approximately halves, accounting for roughly half the annual equity risk premium rather than all of it. Post-2020 survival is regime-dependent: announcement-return sensitivity rose during COVID as macro news dominated firm-specific signals, and attention-conditional premia of 12–26 bps per standard deviation of prior macro attention remain detectable through 2023 (RFS 2022). However, the pre-FOMC 24-hour drift of Lucca and Moench — averaging 49 bps in 1994–2011 — effectively disappeared after January 2016, attributed to reduced uncertainty following the ZLB liftoff and Fed press-conference transparency. The FOMC-cycle effect of Cieslak, Morse and Vissing-Jorgensen (JF 2019) carried through approximately 2016 but its post-2020 persistence is not firmly established in published work.

Event-day vol pricing is the more precise and practically implementable domain. The canonical extraction method uses variance additivity: for an option spanning an event, Total_Variance = Regular_Daily_Variance × (DTE − 1) + Event_Variance × 1. Solving for Event Variance and converting to expected move (annualized event vol divided by 16, then times 0.8 for the mean-absolute-deviation straddle approximation) gives a market-implied event move that can be compared to historical realized moves. Londono and Samadi (Fed IFDP 2023) operationalize this using neighboring daily SPX expirations: the forward equity and variance risk premium for CPI, FOMC, NFP, and GDP-spanning options versus non-event adjacent expirations are all significantly elevated, increasing with risk aversion and with monetary policy and real economic uncertainty. The Knox, Londono, Samadi and Vissing-Jorgensen 'Equity Premium Events' paper quantifies FOMC, CPI, and NFP as the three releases with the largest average ex-ante equity premia, with average IEP values around 7.35 bps per forward period; the largest single event in the sample was the March 2022 FOMC (SVIX premium 8.01 bps, IEP premium 21.92 bps). The Fed's 'Which Days Matter for Global Equity Markets' FEDS Note (Oct 2025, sample Sept 2023 – July 2025) quantifies annualized event risk premia using the LVIX measure at 45 bps for CPI, 53 bps for FOMC, and 53 bps for NFP in U.S. equities. Wright (NBER 2021) using Wednesday/Friday-expiring Treasury and equity futures options found that on FOMC days roughly half of options-implied variance is a variance risk premium while for employment reports only a small share is VRP — a sharp contrast suggesting FOMC uncertainty is more 'priced' relative to the physical risk it carries. The non-spanning MDN paper (arXiv 2606.12872, May 2022 – August 2025 SPX data) finds CPI has the strongest scheduled-jump identification (79.5% of events cleanly identified), FOMC next (69.6%), and NFP weakest (33.3%).

Regime dynamics within the 2022–2026 arc define the practical character of event risk. In 2022–2023, CPI was the binding macro risk variable: the average NDX move on CPI days in 2022 was ±2.51% (vs ±1.64% on all days), with November 2022 the single largest single-day move at NDX +7.49%, and December 2022 straddle pricing the most expensive in the sample. SPX CPI-day moves in this period frequently exceeded the implied straddle, making systematic vol-selling on CPI days loss-making on average. Crucially, the reaction asymmetry was pronounced: disinflationary positive surprises generated large, statistically significant abnormal CARs exceeding +1%, while inflationary negative surprises generated negative abnormal returns of similar magnitude but without statistical significance (Taylor and Francis 2026, sample 2021–2025). This asymmetry reflects the regime: in a hiking cycle, a cool CPI print revises down the rate path through both the discount rate and growth outlook channels, while a hot CPI print merely confirms the tightening, generating smaller incremental bearish impact. By 2024–2025, with headline CPI normalizing toward 2–3% and the Fed in a cutting or pausing cycle, labor data reclaimed dominance. By mid-2025, CPI-day SPX straddles cheapened to as low as 55 bps, the lowest since August/November 2021.

The gamma-OPEX interaction with event days is practitioner consensus with credible mechanism and some empirical anchoring. In positive-gamma regimes, dealer hedging flows are countercyclical (buy dips, sell rallies), dampening realized intraday vol. In negative-gamma regimes, hedging is procyclical, amplifying moves. A one standard deviation increase in gamma imbalance is associated with roughly a 20 bps reduction in absolute returns (approximately 20% of a standard deviation), based on cited Gamma Fragility research. OPEX week (third Friday) mechanically removes the largest tranche of gamma as front-month OI rolls off; the post-OPEX pattern of reduced dampening is described as reproducible across decades of SPX data. When CPI or FOMC lands in a high positive-GEX week, dealer hedging absorbs some of the event shock, muting realized moves relative to implied; the converse holds in low-gamma or negative-gamma weeks. FOMC press conferences produce a documented two-event structure: 2:00 PM decision and 2:30 PM presser can flip net gamma sign, with roughly 40% of initial post-decision moves partially or fully reversing by close. SPX moves 20–30% larger at dot-plot SEP-releasing meetings than non-SEP meetings, a quantifiable calendar interaction. Multi-event clustering (CPI + FOMC same week, or OPEX + CPI) elevates term straddle vol non-additively as both events embed jump components in the same expiration. The Kalshi prediction-market literature (NBER WP34702, sample July 2021 – January 2025) provides a new quantitative handle: implied IQR from Kalshi CPI contracts predicts absolute surprise magnitude with slope 0.75 (R² = 0.20, N = 45), with the relationship concentrated in the high-inflation sub-period; the implied median predicts surprise direction with only 57% hit rate (near-chance). For NFP the magnitude-direction decomposition was uninformative given the smaller panel (N = 33).

### measurable_constructs
- Implied event move (IEM): IEM_annualized = sqrt(max(sigma_term^2 * DTE_post_event - sigma_base^2 * (DTE_post_event - 1), 0)); straddle-equivalent expected move = IEM_annualized / sqrt(252) * 0.8. Use shortest available post-event expiration ATM IV vs shortest pre-event expiration ATM IV.
- Event variance risk premium (EVRP): EVRP = implied_event_variance (from post-event expiration) minus realized_event_variance (actual day return squared); track rolling 12-month mean and z-score per event type (CPI/FOMC/NFP) separately.
- IV-to-RV ratio around event: rolling ratio of ATM IV (5 days pre-event) to realized vol (5-day trailing); FOMC baseline 1.42, non-FOMC 1.18; flag events where ratio is >1.5x the trailing 12-month median as elevated overpricing.
- Surprise z-score: (actual_release - consensus_estimate) / ewm_std(historical_surprises, halflife=12 events); flag >2-sigma and >3-sigma events separately; compute directional sign and cumulate into rolling surprise index per data type.
- Prediction market implied IQR: from Kalshi binary CPI contracts, recover implied CDF IQR immediately pre-release as ordinal event-magnitude risk proxy (R²=0.20 vs realized surprise; informative in high-inflation regimes).
- Dealer gamma exposure (GEX) on event day: net GEX = sum over strikes of (call_OI - put_OI) * gamma * contract_size * spot; sign determines regime (positive = dampening, negative = amplifying); can be approximated from public options chain end-of-day.
- OPEX calendar flag: binary indicator for whether event falls within 5 trading days before monthly third-Friday OPEX; interacts with GEX signal — pre-OPEX high-OI concentration vs post-OPEX GEX vacuum.
- Multi-event cluster score: count of Tier-1 events (CPI, FOMC decision, NFP) within rolling 5-trading-day window; score 0/1/2; clusters of 2+ imply term straddle pricing is non-additive and IEM decomposition is under-identified.
- Surprise history dispersion: rolling 12-event EWM standard deviation of absolute surprises per release type; regime switch from 2021 (low) to 2022 (high) to 2024+ (normalizing); use as calibration multiplier on base IEM estimates.
- Dot-plot meeting flag: binary for FOMC meetings with SEP release; multiply base FOMC IEM by 1.20–1.25 to reflect documented larger realized moves at SEP vs non-SEP meetings.
- Regime classification input: CPI YoY vs 3% threshold + Fed funds rate trend (rising/flat/falling) to classify binding macro variable (CPI-dominant vs labor-dominant regime); weight event importance in composite score accordingly.
- Ex-ante event risk score (composite, night-before): standardized Z = [IEM / 12m_avg_IEM_same_type] + [EVRP_z] + [GEX_regime_sign: +1 negative GEX, -1 positive GEX] + [surprise_dispersion_z] + [cluster_score * 0.5] + [dot_plot_flag * 0.25 if FOMC]; output as percentile rank vs own 36-month history per event type.

### pitfalls
- Regime non-stationarity is the dominant confound: CPI 2022–2023 was a 40-year outlier; implied event moves, VRP levels, and reaction asymmetries calibrated on that period will overestimate event risk in normalization periods (2024+). Maintain regime-conditional parameters (e.g., CPI YoY > 3% vs < 3%).
- Small-sample problem is severe: ~12 CPI prints per year and ~8 FOMC meetings means a 5-year backtest contains only ~60 CPI and ~40 FOMC events — insufficient to distinguish regime from noise. Ghaderi-Seo (2025) formally shows the announcement premium estimate is dominated by small-sample components.
- Look-ahead bias in consensus: Bloomberg/Refinitiv consensus figures are end-of-day snapshots; real-time consensus before 8:30 AM is slightly different. Use only forecasts published the night before, not revised post-release consensus.
- Variance-additivity formula assumes isolated event within the expiration window and constant background daily vol. Multi-event clustering (CPI + FOMC within 5-day window) makes the decomposition under-identified without additional structure.
- 0DTE growth since 2022 breaks pre-2020 vol surface regularities: standard term structure models fitted to weekly/monthly expirations misestimate event vol in 0DTE contracts, and GEX sign can flip within hours intraday, making pre-open estimates stale by the event time.
- Direction of CPI surprise does not mechanically map to equity direction: in demand-driven inflation (strong growth + hot CPI), the positive growth signal can partially offset the rate-hike signal. The 2024 no-landing episodes showed limited equity damage on hot prints.
- Straddle implied move systematically overstates FOMC 2:00 PM to close realized move (implied 1.0–1.2%, realized 0.7–0.9%), but this VRP can invert violently in uncertainty-spike events (Dec 18, 2024: −3%, worst FOMC day since March 2020). Selling event vol has a negative-expectation tail.
- Kalshi IQR-to-surprise magnitude R² of 0.20 means 80% of surprise variance is unexplained even by prediction-market dispersion; it is an ordinal signal (high vs low alert), not a precise quantitative forecast.
- OPEX calendar interactions lack peer-reviewed measurement with clean effect sizes; the practitioner GEX dampening claim is mechanistically credible but may be overwhelmed by event shock in extreme surprise scenarios.
- FOMC cycle (even-week) effect documented through ~2016 has not been confirmed to persist through 2022–2024 hiking cycle in any published work. Pre-FOMC drift disappearance is confirmed. Treat as dormant until replicated post-2020.
- COT data for pre-event de-risking is published weekly with a 3-day lag covering Tuesday snapshots; it cannot capture within-week position changes ahead of Thursday NFP or next-day CPI, limiting usefulness for event-week signals.
- NFP jump identification from SPX options is only 33.3% reliable in the non-spanning MDN framework, suggesting NFP event risk is often absorbed into the continuous vol surface rather than priced as a discrete jump — IEM extraction for NFP is noisier than for CPI or FOMC.

## Lane: transmission-channels

### narrative
Yield moves transmit to equity groups through at least seven distinct causal channels, each with its own speed, direction, and regime stability. The critical practitioner insight is that these channels operate on radically different timescales — some close within the same trading session (homebuilders to 10Y moves, gold to real yields in the pre-2022 regime, exchange stocks to MOVE spikes), while others require one to four quarters to manifest in reported earnings (bank NIM repricing, exchange float income, life insurer reinvestment yield improvement). Building a signal system requires keeping these timescales separate: fast channels support tactical triggers, slow channels require leading-indicator positioning ahead of the lag.

The exchange sector (CME, ICE, CBOE, NDAQ) illustrates the most frequently misunderstood mechanism. The folk narrative says 'lower rates hurt exchanges.' The measured truth has two separable sub-channels. First, rate VOLATILITY — not rate level — is the primary driver of CME's interest rate complex volumes. The MOVE index (Treasury implied vol) averaged 73 in Q2 2026 (down 39% year-over-year), and interest rate ADV at CME dropped 45% month-over-month in April 2026 when volatility compressed; June 2026 saw a 17% YoY rate volume surge coinciding with elevated uncertainty. This MOVE-to-CME-volume relationship is near-instantaneous. The second, slower sub-channel is float income from margin collateral: CME distributes approximately $3.7–4.7B in collateral reinvestment income back to members (2023–2024), implying gross interest income in that range tied directly to fed funds level. When rates fall to zero (2020–2021), this collapses to near zero. ICE adds a third negative leg: its Mortgage Technology segment ($2B revenue in 2024, 22% of total) is sensitive to mortgage origination volumes, which crater when the 30Y mortgage rate spikes — this partially offsets vol/float benefits. Nasdaq is structurally least rate-sensitive given its heavy mix of recurring SaaS/solutions ARR (grew 7–8% organically in 2022–2023 regardless of rate environment).

Banks and insurers are the canonical rate-transmission sectors but the mechanism is more heterogeneous than often assumed. Bank NIM sensitivity to rate levels was negative in three of the four tightening episodes before 2015: NIM fell roughly 4–7 bps per 100 bps of rate increase in 1994–1995, 1999–2000, and 2004–2006 cycles because deposit competition intensified faster than asset repricing. The 2015–2018 cycle was the first to show NIM expansion (+20 bps per 100 bps FFR increase). The 2022–2023 cycle initially followed the newer pattern (NIM expanded from ~2.5% to ~3.3% cycle peak by mid-2023) but then reversed as deposit betas normalized upward (community bank cumulative betas reached 42–44%, vs. 22% in the prior cycle). SVB illustrated the HTM/AFS duration-loss channel at the extreme: $15.2B in HTM unrealized losses on a 6.2-year weighted-average duration portfolio destroyed equity before any deposit run — an accounting mechanism that hid losses until forced liquidation. Life insurers faced 56% of total adjusted capital in unrealized losses in 2022, but their negative duration gap cushioned the blow; insurance stocks fell 6.4% vs. banks' 12.8% during the March 2023 SVB-contagion window. Long-dated life insurers benefit from rising long rates via reinvestment: book yield improved 46 bps in 2022 and 37 bps in 2023, and net investment income rose from $192.6B to $225.0B industry-wide between 2019 and 2023.

Duration equities — utilities, REITs, and unprofitable growth — exhibit the highest same-day rate sensitivity of any equity group, with the mechanism running through discount-rate repricing of long-dated cash flows. Gormsen and Lazarus (Journal of Finance, 2023) demonstrated that equity factor return premia are substantially driven by duration differentials: growth stocks have longer duration than value stocks, making them disproportionately exposed to pure discounting shocks. In practice: VNQ (REIT ETF) fell 26.2% in 2022 as 10Y yields rose approximately 230 bps; XLU (utility ETF) managed only +1.4% for the full year with a 13.2% 3-year drawdown trough. ARKK lost 29.9% in Q1 2022 alone (worst U.S. equity fund in Morningstar coverage) as real yields moved from deeply negative to positive. Homebuilders exhibit the most direct and fastest rate transmission of any single-name equity group, operating through the mechanism 10Y yield to 30Y mortgage rate to housing affordability to order flow, with ITB falling over 30% in 2022 as 30Y rates went from 3.7% to near 7%, and single-day intraday evidence confirming $60.5M in ITB outflows on one sharp mortgage rate spike day in May 2026.

The gold-to-real-yields channel was the most statistically reliable macro relationship in the 2005–2021 period (84% R-squared) and has since undergone a structural regime break. Post-2022, the correlation collapsed to 3% in 2022–2023 and 7% in 2024. Central bank purchases exceeded 1,000 tonnes per year for three consecutive years (2022–2024), more than double the prior decade average of 473 tonnes/year, and this demand is not sensitive to opportunity costs. For signal systems: the pre-2022 inverse relationship is measurable and useful in backtesting but must carry a structural-break flag for any post-2022 strategy. GDX amplifies the underlying gold move 1.5x to 2x with additional single-name risk.

The dollar transmission channel is well-quantified at the first leg: a 100 bps surprise increase in U.S. policy rate expectations appreciates the dollar 3.41% on average against advanced economies (full sample 2002–2017, rising to 4.83% post-2008). About half of the AFE dollar's 13% appreciation from September 2021 to 2023 was attributable to relative rate differentials. Goldman Sachs estimated that a 10% trade-weighted dollar appreciation reduces S&P 500 EPS by roughly 2%, given ~28% of revenues come from overseas. Monthly return correlation between EM equity and DXY is approximately -0.40 since 2000. The small-cap leverage channel is the most mechanistic: Russell 2000 carries ~45% floating-rate debt vs. 9% for S&P 500, with net debt/EBITDA leverage approximately triple the S&P 500. The 525 bps Fed hiking cycle compressed small-cap profitability faster and harder than large-cap, with the 1-2 quarter lag reflecting the next reporting cycle after rate increases hit interest expense lines.

### pitfalls
- MOVE-to-volume lag confusion: MOVE measures ANTICIPATED volatility (forward-looking), not realized vol. A spike in MOVE from policy uncertainty drives IMMEDIATE CME volume, while sustained low MOVE (2019, 2021) suppresses volumes for quarters. Do not use trailing realized vol as the driver — use implied (MOVE) which is forward-looking and same-day.
- Float income look-ahead bias: CME/ICE float income tied to fed funds is disclosed only quarterly in earnings — using it as a real-time signal creates a 45-60 day look-ahead bias. Build the rate-level-to-float income model as a lagged predictor, not an intraday trigger.
- Deposit beta heterogeneity kills aggregate NIM signals: small community banks ran deposit betas of 42–44% in 2022–2023; large money-center banks ran significantly lower betas due to diversified wholesale funding. Aggregate FDIC NIM data averages over enormous cross-sectional dispersion. Segment by bank size (<$10B vs >$100B assets) for actionable signals.
- SVB-type risk is tail-event, not systematic: the HTM unrealized loss channel only detonates when a bank MUST liquidate, requiring both large duration losses AND a deposit run trigger. FDIC now discloses aggregate AFS unrealized losses quarterly (post-SVB rule), providing a risk monitor but not a return predictor for the sector.
- Gold-TIPS regime break requires active monitoring: running gold signals against real yields POST-2022 using historical 84% R-squared parameters will generate systematic losses (correlation now 3-7%). Requires a regime classifier — WGC CB purchases trailing 4Q total as the primary regime indicator. Pre-2022 signal architecture is not forward-deployable without this flag.
- REIT drawdown asymmetry: REITs have optionality on property values that bonds do not have, so their implied duration is rate-path-dependent. In a rising rate environment driven by economic strength (vs. inflation fear), REIT cash flows actually improve (higher rents), partially offsetting discount rate pressure. Pure bond-proxy duration models overestimate REIT rate sensitivity in growth-driven tightening cycles.
- Homebuilder rate-lock nonlinearity: at very high mortgage rates (above ~7%), the inventory lock-in effect (sellers won't sell because they hold 3% mortgages) creates a supply shortage that SUPPORTS homebuilder volumes despite affordability pressure. The linear rate-beta model breaks down above 7% mortgage rates, as confirmed by 2023 homebuilder stock resilience despite elevated rates.
- Dollar transmission to EM has LOW R-squared at short horizons: monthly DXY-MSCI EM correlation of -0.40 implies only R-squared ~0.16 at monthly frequency. EM-specific political risk, commodity cycles, and China macro dominate at 3-month horizons. Dollar explains larger EM equity variance at 12-month horizons. Short-horizon signals will generate high false-positive rates.
- Gormsen-Lazarus equity duration is model-derived, not directly observable: their framework requires analyst long-term earnings growth forecasts (IBES LTG) to compute duration scores. In practice, high IBES LTG stocks are noisy proxies for theoretical duration; near-term rate sensitivity may be dominated by factor crowding (growth as a crowded trade) rather than true fundamental duration. Distinguish price-duration from cash-flow-duration.
- Small-cap floating-rate concentration: 50% of Russell 2000 floating-rate debt is held by just 10% of constituents. Aggregate IWM regressions understate true stress for the most exposed names because 33% of Russell 2000 constituents carry net cash (zero rate sensitivity). Factor-based construction should focus on high-leverage, low-interest-coverage small caps, not IWM as a whole.
- Regime instability across tightening cycles: bank NIM response flipped from negative to positive between the 2004-2006 and 2015-2018 cycles, driven by structural changes in deposit market competition and money market fund alternatives. Any NIM prediction model fitted on pre-2015 data will have the wrong sign for post-2015 periods; regime identification (money market fund rate environment) is prerequisite.
- ICE mortgage tech negative correlation is cycle-asymmetric: when rates FALL and origination volumes recover, ICE Mortgage Tech revenue lags with a 1-2 quarter delay as pipelines rebuild. This creates an asymmetric profile — faster compression on the way up (immediate origination freeze), slower recovery on the way down. Don't assume symmetric elasticity.

## Lane: self-learning-systems

### narrative
Self-improving signal systems for macro dashboards sit at the intersection of three hard problems: statistical validity under tiny event counts, adaptive weight learning under non-stationarity, and the systemic risk that the improvement mechanism itself becomes the dominant failure mode. Each of the six areas the question covers has a distinct body of rigorous literature; what follows is a synthesis for a quant architect who needs mechanisms, not vibes.

Walk-forward and small-N event validation. Macro event studies are epistemically brutal: CPI gives roughly 12 observations per year, FOMC 8. A decade of data — widely regarded as the minimum for factor research — yields N=80-120 events, and regime breaks (pre/post-2020, pre/post-QE) cut that further. The canonical practitioner response is not to wait for more data but to pool carefully. Three complementary strategies are documented. First, hierarchical pooling across semantically related event types: CPI, PPI, PCE, and FOMC rate decisions all probe inflation/rate expectations; a hierarchical Bayes model with a shared hyperprior borrows strength across event types while preserving event-specific posterior uncertainty. Feng et al. (2019/2021) demonstrate this in factor investing — assets share a common prior mean, and the James-Stein result guarantees dominated estimation whenever three or more groups are estimated simultaneously. Second, Bayesian local projections (Ferreira et al. 2023, Gonzalez-Casasas and Schorfheide 2025) shrink LP impulse-response estimates toward a VAR using a tunable forgetting penalty, directly addressing the small-T inference problem and producing better-calibrated confidence bands than Newey-West or delta-method asymptotics. Third, Lopez de Prado's combinatorial purged cross-validation (CPCV) generates multiple train/test paths, embargoes windows around the training/test boundary to kill label leakage from overlapping return windows, and produces a distribution over out-of-sample Sharpe ratios rather than a single fragile estimate. The 'purge' step is non-negotiable when labels are forward returns: adjacent rows share label computation periods and naive splits leak the future into training.

Multiple-testing control and pre-registration analogs. Harvey, Liu, and Zhu (2016) catalogued 316 published factors from 1967-2014 and showed that the conventional t > 2.0 threshold is far too permissive — by 2012 a new factor required t > 3.0, projecting toward ~3.4 by the mid-2030s, using BHY (Benjamini-Hochberg-Yekutieli) FDR control. Bailey and Lopez de Prado's Deflated Sharpe Ratio (2014) formalizes this as a penalty: DSR deflates the in-sample Sharpe by the expected maximum Sharpe of N iid strategies, penalizing both strategy selection bias and non-normality. The minimum track record length required to achieve statistical significance grows rapidly with the number of trials evaluated. White's (2000) Reality Check and Hansen's (2005) Superior Predictive Ability test extend this to strategy universes: the SPA test uses stationary bootstrap to correct p-values for the entire search space tested, re-centering the null to avoid the WRC's liberal distortion from dominated strategies. McLean and Pontiff (2016) supply the empirical base rate: 97 published anomalies earn returns 26% lower out-of-sample and 58% lower post-publication. Factor half-lives have compressed from ~60 months (2005-2010 vintage) to ~18 months (2020-2024 vintage) as AI adoption has homogenized strategies. The pre-registration analog in quant research is committing signal logic and parameter ranges in a versioned spec before seeing test-set outcomes. The base-rate problem with AND-gated confluence signals is severe: if signal A fires 15% of the time and signal B 20%, their independent conjunction fires 3%, reducing annual event counts to fewer than 1 per year for FOMC-frequency data. Honest scoring requires comparing observed conjunction hit rates against the independence baseline to isolate true interaction lift; isotonic regression on pooled calibration data is the standard nonparametric fix.

Online learning and regime-aware weight updating. EWMA with a forgetting factor lambda is the simplest principled adaptation mechanism and is a special case of the Kalman filter under Gaussian noise with unit signal/observation coefficients. For event-reaction priors, the Beta-Binomial conjugate pair provides the cleanest framework: a Beta(alpha, beta) prior encodes alpha pseudo-successes and beta pseudo-failures (ESS = alpha + beta), so a prior built from a pattern library with 20 historical confirmations and 12 rejections starts as Beta(20, 12) and updates with each new event's outcome via posterior = Beta(alpha+k, beta+n-k). The pseudo-count ESS controls how quickly new data can overturn the prior — high ESS is sticky; low ESS yields quickly. For regime-aware adaptation, Bayesian model averaging under regime switching (Shi 2016, Koop-Korobilis Dynamic Model Averaging) assigns model weights proportional to forgetting-factor-discounted predictive likelihoods, which naturally up-weights models that have been accurate in the recent past. CUSUM and ADWIN provide complementary drift detection: CUSUM monitors cumulative sum of residuals against a threshold (hyperparameter sensitive but computationally cheap), while ADWIN uses an adaptive sliding window without a pre-specified shift size. For financial time series, domain-specific detectors consistently outperform generic DDM/EDDM because financial returns have heavy tails and volatility clustering that violate the Gaussian error-rate assumption underlying DDM. PELT changepoint detection, applied to rolling signal IC or signal-outcome correlation series, provides a principled way to segment regimes and re-estimate priors segment by segment.

Forecast scoring. The correct scoring infrastructure for a macro signal system involves three layers. For distributional forecasts: CRPS is the gold standard — strictly proper, integrates pinball losses across all quantiles, and degrades gracefully to MAE for point forecasts. For interval forecasts: the Gneiting-Raftery interval score penalizes both coverage failures and excessive width. For binary or categorical outcomes: the Brier score or log-loss with Christoffersen's (1998) conditional coverage test checking both unconditional calibration and serial independence of violations — clustered failures signal regime mismatch. Baseline comparisons must include seasonal naive, consensus/survey median, and random walk. The Diebold-Mariano test with Giacomini-White conditional extension tests whether one forecast is significantly better than another conditional on current information.

Failure modes of self-improving systems. The empirical and theoretical literature converges on six catastrophic failure modes: (1) Regime-fit churn — the system rewrites weights to fit the most recent regime, analogous to the rise-then-collapse pattern in self-training LLMs. (2) Feedback onto own signals — a fund whose signals drive its positions contaminates the price series used to evaluate those signals. (3) Silent data rot — upstream data stops updating but the pipeline stays green and produces predictions from frozen inputs. (4) Alpha decay post-publication — the 58% decay rate and AI-homogenization finding (42% institutional portfolio convergence 2013-2024) mean externally-sourced pattern libraries decay at ~18-month half-lives. (5) The utility-learning tension (Wang-Dorchen 2025) — a self-modifying agent that recursively expands model capacity can transform learnable problems into unlearnable ones, destroying the statistical preconditions for reliable generalization. (6) Backtest overfitting via trial inflation — probability of overfitting rises sharply once strategy variants evaluated exceed the number of non-overlapping test periods.

Encoding prior knowledge. The principled approach separates epistemic provenance from learned weights through three mechanisms. First, pseudocount priors: encode each pattern-library entry as a Beta(alpha_0, beta_0) where ESS reflects the quality and regime-coverage of historical evidence. Second, hierarchical regime hyperparameters: a two-level model where the prior mean and variance are regime-indexed, letting a new regime shift the prior's center mass without discarding all historical data. Third, explicit provenance tagging: every weight in the signal registry carries metadata — source type (backtest vs. live ledger vs. academic paper), regime coverage, N events, and a staleness timestamp. Weights tagged as 'library-derived' with regime coverage narrow relative to current conditions receive automatic ESS deflation before being used in confluence scoring. MLflow or equivalent model registries provide the DAG lineage infrastructure; the trading-specific addition is linking each weight version to the event window and regime index under which it was estimated.

### [WEAKENED] (orig conf: strong)
CORRECTED: The James-Stein / Stein result proves that, for estimating three or more means simultaneously under Gaussian noise with squared-error loss, a shrinkage estimator achieves uniformly lower EXPECTED TOTAL (aggregate, not componentwise) squared-error risk than the raw MLE — a frequentist average-over-resamples guarantee, not a promise of lower error on any given dataset, and one that can raise the error of individual components. Via the Efron-Morris (1973) empirical-Bayes interpretation, this provides a principled rationale for hierarchical/partial pooling ACROSS SIMILAR (exchangeable) event types, but the benefit is conditional: it requires a well-chosen shrinkage target, degrades under heteroscedastic/correlated/non-Gaussian components, and can be net-harmful ("negative transfer") when pooled groups are dissimilar. Confidence should be moderate. Note: the cited Feng et al. ### [UPHELD] (orig conf: strong)
CORRECTED: The Deflated Sharpe Ratio (Bailey & Lopez de Prado 2014, SSRN:2460551) deflates a strategy's Sharpe for (1) selection bias/multiple testing — via the number of trials N and the cross-sectional variance of Sharpe ratios, which set an expected-maximum-Sharpe benchmark SR0 — (2) non-normality via return skewness (g3) and kurtosis (g4) in the denominator, and (3) short track-record length T. The companion CSCV/PBO framework (Bailey, Borwein, Lopez de Prado, Zhu 2015, SSRN:2326253; implemented in the CRAN `pbo` package) estimates the probability of backtest overfitting as the frequency that the in-sample-optimal configuration underperforms the median out-of-sample. The related Minimum Backtest Length theorem (same authors) formalizes the claim's core intuition: required sample length grows with the number of independent trials N, so overfitting probability rises rapidly when trials substantially exceed the effective sample/test length. CAVEAT for encoding as a prior: the load-bearing input is the EFFECTIVE number of independent trials — correlated or overlapping strategy variants inflate the naive trial count and cause the correction to be mis-scaled; CSCV/PBO results are also sensitive to the chosen block count. These are methodological constructs (no market-regime decay applies), so they remain valid post-2010/2020/0DTE.
EVIDENCE: Bailey, Lopez de Prado (2014) SSRN:2460551; Bailey, Borwein, Lopez de Prado, Zhu (2015) 'Probability of Backtest Overfitting'. Confirmed via Semantic Scholar and CRAN pbo package docs.

### [UPHELD] (orig conf: strong)
CORRECTED: Beta-Binomial conjugate updating is a standard, computationally trivial framework for encoding prior knowledge as pseudocounts: a Beta(alpha, beta) prior on a Bernoulli success probability encodes an effective sample size ESS = alpha + beta (alpha pseudo-successes + beta pseudo-failures), and the posterior after observing k successes in n events is Beta(alpha + k, beta + n - k). This is an exact mathematical identity (conjugacy), regime-independent and not subject to empirical decay. Two caveats for signal-system use: (1) the pseudocount interpretation is convention-dependent — 'alpha successes / beta failures' gives ESS=alpha+beta (the standard ESS measure used here), while a mode-anchored reading counts alpha-1 / beta-1; the ESS=alpha+beta result itself is unambiguous. (2) The model assumes exchangeable, i.i.d. Bernoulli trials with a single stationary success rate, so it does not natively capture non-stationarity, autocorrelated win/loss streaks, or regime shifts — for such cases a discounted/rolling-window ESS or hierarchical/state-space extension is needed. 'Cleanest' is best read as 'clean and standard,' not uniquely optimal.
EVIDENCE: Standard Bayesian statistics; ESS-as-pseudocount interpretation confirmed in multiple survey articles and Vasishth hierarchical models notes. Not a contested claim.

### [WEAKENED] (orig conf: strong)
CORRECTED: White's (2000) Reality Check (as applied in Sullivan-Timmermann-White 1999 to ~7846 trading rules) and Hansen's (2005) SPA test both control the family-wise probability that the BEST strategy in the TESTED SET beats the benchmark by chance — i.e., they correct for data-snooping over the set of strategies submitted, not a universal or per-strategy correction (per-strategy inference needs stepwise extensions: Romano-Wolf StepM, Step-SPA, or the Model Confidence Set). SPA is generally more powerful and less sensitive to poor/irrelevant models than WRC, which uses the conservative least-favorable configuration. Crucially, SPA's power gain comes from TWO modifications, not one: (1) a STUDENTIZED test statistic and (2) a sample-dependent null distribution under which very poor ("dominated") models drop out of the null centering. The claim's attribution of the power gain solely to "re-centering the null to exclude dominated strategies" is incomplete — it omits studentization, which Hansen (2005) states is co-equally responsible. Confidence: moderate, not strong.
EVIDENCE: Sullivan, Timmermann, White (1999) 'Data-Snooping, Technical Trading Rules, and the Bootstrap' (confirmed Kevinsheppard.com); Hansen (2005) 'A Test for Superior Predictive Ability', JBES 23:365-380. Rutgers econometrics paper on WRC extensions.

### measurable_constructs
- Walk-forward expanding-window Sharpe ratio distribution: compute Sharpe on each sequential OOS block (minimum 12-month test windows for macro-frequency signals); compute mean, std, and skew of the distribution; flag strategies where skew < -1 (backtest optima cluster in early blocks, indicating temporal overfitting).
- Deflated Sharpe Ratio: DSR = PSR(SR*) where SR* is the benchmark Sharpe; requires tracking T_trials (number of independent strategy variants evaluated) in the signal registry; computable from EOD return series using skewness, kurtosis, and T_trials.
- Effective N after AND-gating: for each confluence set {S1...Sk}, compute N_effective = N_base * product(base_rate(Si)); flag any configuration where N_effective < 30 for the backtest window as statistically underpowered; track empirical vs. independence-baseline firing rate ratio as 'interaction lift'.
- Signal IC rolling Z-score: for each signal, compute rolling 252-day Information Coefficient (rank correlation of signal value with forward return); Z-score the IC against its expanding-window mean and std; CUSUM on this series with k=0.5 sigma detects IC regime shifts.
- Beta-Binomial posterior update tracker: maintain (alpha_t, beta_t) for each signal where alpha_0/beta_0 encode pseudocount evidence from library; update after each event outcome via Beta(alpha+k, beta+n-k); track ESS_t = alpha_t + beta_t and posterior mean over time; flag when posterior mean crosses 0.5 for a sustained 5-event window.
- CRPS daily scoring: for each probabilistic macro forecast, compute CRPS against realized outcome; track 30/90/252-day rolling mean CRPS vs. seasonal-naive baseline; Diebold-Mariano test monthly against consensus baseline.
- Christoffersen conditional coverage test: for each 80% prediction interval, track empirical coverage on rolling 52-week window; compute Kupiec unconditional and Christoffersen independence test statistics on violation series; flag if p < 0.10 for either component.
- Freshness SLA monitor: for each upstream data feed, compute time-since-last-update; alert if any signal input exceeds its SLA by 2x; publish fraction-of-inputs-fresh as a scalar health metric to the signal scorecard; multiply signal weight by freshness scalar.
- Signal version provenance ledger: for each production signal weight, store (signal_id, version, source_type in {library|backtest|live_ledger}, regime_coverage_vector, N_events_seen, last_updated_date, ESS); compute regime_coverage_overlap between training regime and trailing 63-day regime as a staleness adjustment.
- Regime-conditional hit rate decomposition: partition historical event outcomes by macro regime label (HMM state, VIX tercile, or yield curve slope tercile); compute signal hit rate separately per regime; chi-squared test for hit rate heterogeneity; report regime with worst hit rate as the signal's 'regime vulnerability' index.
- Alpha decay monitoring: for signals sourced from published research, track post-inclusion live IC vs. backtest IC; fit McLean-Pontiff prior decay rate (58% expected decline); compare live decay rate to prior using t-test; flag if live decay is faster than prior at p < 0.20.
- Confluence interaction lift: for each signal pair (A, B), compute lift = P(Y=1|A,B) / (P(Y=1|A) * P(Y=1|B) / P(Y=1)); values near 1.0 indicate redundancy; calibrate using isotonic regression on binned signal score combinations.

### pitfalls
- Regime-fit churn: self-improving systems that re-estimate weights on expanding windows can over-index on the most recent regime if forgetting factors are too high (lambda > 0.98 for weekly data typically over-adapts); the system rewrites itself to fit the last 18 months, then fails at the next regime break. Mitigation: run parallel slow (lambda=0.99) and fast (lambda=0.95) adaptation variants; track cross-regime hit rate distribution, not just rolling-window hit rate.
- Own-signal feedback contamination: a signal system large enough to move prices — or one that shares signals with a large fund — contaminates the price series it is evaluated against. This is especially acute for pre-FOMC drift, which has decayed significantly as more funds traded it. Mitigation: evaluate on assets where the system has negligible AUM fraction; use synthetic control periods where the signal was inactive.
- AND-gate N collapse: adding confluence conditions without checking remaining N is standard practice but catastrophic. A three-condition AND-gate on monthly-frequency signals can reduce effective N below 5 per year, making any regularization nominal. Enforce a minimum N_effective gate before any signal is admitted to the confluence library.
- Look-ahead bias in pseudo-real-time event reconstruction: FOMC statement text, CPI seasonally-adjusted revisions, and consensus survey values are routinely revised after initial release. Backtests using final revised data look very different from point-in-time. Use ALFRED vintage databases exclusively; flag any feature that could carry a revision-timing bias.
- Prior pseudocount mis-calibration: setting ESS too high (e.g., 200) for a pattern sourced from a single regime makes the posterior nearly impervious to live updating. Setting it too low (e.g., 2) means the prior contributes nothing. Calibrate ESS to the effective number of independent events the source pattern was estimated from, accounting for autocorrelation (a pattern estimated from 10 years of daily data has ~120 independent monthly events, not 2500 daily observations).
- Silent data staleness: the pipeline stays green (all steps complete) but a vendor feed stopped updating 3 days ago, so all signals based on that feed are computed from frozen inputs. Mitigation: publish a freshness scalar (fraction of input feeds updated within SLA) to the signal health dashboard; treat staleness > 2x SLA as zeroing out signal weight.
- Overfitting to the multiple-testing correction itself: researchers who know BHY FDR at q=0.05 requires t > 3.0 may 'target' t-stats just above 3.0, replacing p-hacking at t=2.0 with fishing at t=3.0. Pre-registration of the exact hypothesis, parameter ranges, and evaluation window before data examination is the only structural fix.
- Isotonic calibration instability with small N: isotonic (PAVA) calibration with N<100 events in the calibration set can produce a step function wildly non-monotone in small-sample regions. Use Platt scaling (logistic calibration) for very small N or add a monotonicity-preserving regularization penalty.
- Christoffersen test low power with small N: with fewer than 100 observations, the conditional coverage test has low power to detect mild violation clustering. Augment with a runs test on violation sequences and a regime-conditional coverage breakdown.
- Catastrophic forgetting in continual learning: models retrained on recent data without replay or regularization forget patterns from distant historical regimes. Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC) penalizes weight changes important for past tasks via the Fisher information matrix, but Fisher matrix estimation is itself noisy with small macro-event datasets. Simpler alternative: blend a frozen historical-memory model with an online-updated model via BMA weights.
