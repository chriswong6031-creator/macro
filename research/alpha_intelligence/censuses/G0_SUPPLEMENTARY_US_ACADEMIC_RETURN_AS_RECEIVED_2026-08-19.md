1. Filings (10-Q/10-K vs earnings 8-K / press release)
Legal clock (PRIMARY SOURCE VERIFIED — official SEC forms opened).
Form 8-K Item 2.02 ("Results of Operations and Financial Condition") is the current-report slot for a public announcement of a completed quarter or year: the registrant must give the date, identify the release, and attach the text as an exhibit (https://www.sec.gov/about/forms/form8-k.pdf, pp. 14–15). Item 2.02 is not required if the same material is disclosed in a Form 10-Q or 10-K. A complementary oral/webcast presentation is exempt from a second 8-K if it occurs within 48 hours after a related written Item 2.02 release, is broadly accessible, posts the stats on the website (Reg FD 17 CFR 244.100), and was announced by a widely disseminated press release. General Instruction B: unless otherwise specified, an 8-K is due within four business days of the event; Item 2.02/7.01 information is generally furnished, not filed, for Exchange Act §18 liability unless the registrant elects otherwise. Form 10-Q (https://www.sec.gov/files/form10-q.pdf) is the periodic report: large accelerated/accelerated filers have 40 days after quarter-end; others 45 days. No 10-Q is required for Q4 (that is the 10-K). 10-Q Part I includes financial statements and MD&A; those items can be incorporated by reference from a published document if the document is filed as an exhibit.

So what incrementally: the 8-K/press release is the headline/full-release legal object; the 10-Q/10-K is a later, more complete, filed object (financials + MD&A) on a 40–45 day clock. Incremental information in the filing is a possible channel, not a proven one from these forms.

Academic incremental-filing papers this session: Griffin (2003), Li & Ramesh (2009), Levi (2008), Lerman & Livnat (2010) — UNKNOWN (could not open). Li (2010) JAR on MD&A FLS is opened (see §4). Wilson (1986) "accruals vs cash flows at earnings announcement and annual-report release date" appears only as a cited title in Graham et al.'s reference list — UNKNOWN as a finding.

Does not prove / PIT caveat: SEC forms prove when and what must be disclosed, not that markets leave residual information for the 10-Q. Event studies that treat 10-Q date as an independent shock must handle: (i) 8-K already furnished the earnings number; (ii) incorporation-by-reference can make the 10-Q a copy of the press release; (iii) Q4 has no 10-Q.

3. Q&A specifically (tone, evasion, analyst identity)
Hollander, Pronk & Roelofsen (2010), JAR 48(3):531–563, "Does Silence Speak? An Empirical Analysis of Disclosure Choices During Conference Calls." PRIMARY SOURCE VERIFIED abstract. Managers regularly do not answer questions. Predictors of non-answers: firm size, CEO stock-price incentives, company age, firm performance, litigation risk, and whether analysts are actively involved in Q&A. Authors report "strong support" for the maintained assumption that investors interpret silence negatively ("no news as bad news"). Channel: Q&A omission. Does not prove that unanswered questions contain a true residual cash-flow news increment vs. a selected, endogenous refusal. PIT: question selection and who is allowed to ask are endogenous (see Mayew). URL: https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/joares/v48y2010i3p531-563.html. DOI 10.1111/j.1475-679X.2010.00365.x.

Mayew (2008), JAR 46(3):627–659, "Evidence of Management Discrimination Among Analysts during Earnings Conference Calls." PRIMARY SOURCE VERIFIED abstract. Post-Reg FD transcripts: probability an analyst asks a question increases in the favorableness of the outstanding stock recommendation. Downgrades are associated with decreased access. Prestige moderates: favorable prestigious analysts have higher participation than favorable unprestigious ones; downgrade-related access loss is only for unprestigious analysts. Channel: analyst identity / access, not tone per se. Does not prove that the incremental public Q&A signal is the causal object (it may proxy private access). Identification is observational (recommendation → participation), not random assignment of questions. URL: https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/joares/v46y2008i3p627-659.html.

Mayew & Venkatachalam (2012) "The Power of Voice"; Price, Doran, Peterson & Bliss conference-call tone — UNKNOWN this session.

4. Accounting changes / non-GAAP / accruals
Bradshaw & Sloan (2002), JAR 40(1):41–66, "GAAP versus The Street." PRIMARY SOURCE VERIFIED abstract. Over ~20 years, frequency and magnitude of GAAP vs Street differences rose, with a strong bias toward Street > GAAP. Market response to Street "has displaced GAAP earnings as a primary determinant of stock prices." Earnings-release analysis: management takes a proactive role in defining and emphasizing the Street number. Channel: headline/non-GAAP definition at the press-release moment, not the 10-Q reconcilation per se. Does not prove Street is "more true," only that it became the priced construct. PIT: Street actuals and I/B/E/S definitions are themselves endogenous to what managers emphasize. URL: https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/joares/v40y2002i1p41-66.html.

Brown, Christensen, Elliott & Mergenthaler (2012), JAR 50(1):1–40, "Investor Sentiment and Pro Forma Earnings Disclosures." PRIMARY SOURCE VERIFIED abstract. Propensity to disclose an adjusted metric (especially one exceeding GAAP) rises with investor sentiment. At higher sentiment, managers exclude more recurring and nonrecurring expenses and place the pro forma figure more prominently. Authors say the association "at least partly reflects opportunistic motives"; managers' own sentiment-driven expectations also play a role. Channel: discretionary non-GAAP packaging of the headline. Does not prove incremental cash-flow information; it is about presentation choice. URL: https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/joares/v50y2012i1p1-40.html.

SEC Regulation G / Release 33-8176 (effective 28 Mar 2003). Official page opened: https://www.sec.gov/rules/final/33-8176.htm ("Conditions for Use of Non-GAAP Financial Measures"). PRIMARY SOURCE VERIFIED as the existence/date of the rule. Item-level reconciliation requirements were not extracted from the Federal Register body this session — treat the content of Reg G as UNKNOWN beyond the title/date.

Li (2008), JAE 45(2–3):221–247, "Annual report readability, current earnings, and earnings persistence." PRIMARY SOURCE VERIFIED abstract. Lower-earnings firms have harder-to-read (higher Fog, longer) annual reports; easier-to-read reports have more persistent positive earnings. Channel: filing-text fog, not the earnings 8-K. Does not prove managers obfuscate causally (performance and complexity are confounded). URL: https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jaecon/v45y2008i2-3p221-247.html.

Li (2010), JAR 48(5):1049–1102, "The Information Content of Forward-Looking Statements in Corporate Filings—A Naïve Bayesian Machine Learning Approach." PRIMARY SOURCE VERIFIED abstract. MD&A FLS tone is associated with current performance, accruals, size, MB, return vol, Fog, and firm age; average FLS tone positively associated with future earnings after controls. No systematic change in MD&A information content over time despite regulation. Tone appears to mitigate accrual mispricing when managers "warn" (tone opposite in sign to accruals). Diction/GI/LIWC tone measures do not positively predict future performance. Channel: 10-K/10-Q MD&A FLS. Does not prove the filing is incrementally informative after the 8-K/call (the tests are about future earnings/returns, not a within-event residual). PIT: tone classifier is in-sample ML; dictionary failure is a measurement result, not a market-efficiency result. URL: https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/joares/v48y2010i5p1049-1102.html.

Sloan (1996) TAR accruals; Doyle/Jennings/Soliman; Bradshaw/Christensen/Gee/Whipple — UNKNOWN (could not open).

5. Guidance (management forecasts, walk-downs, range changes)
Richardson, Teoh & Wysocki (2004), CAR 21(4):885–924, "The Walk-down to Beatable Analyst Forecasts…" PRIMARY SOURCE VERIFIED abstract. Documents the alleged "earnings-guidance game": analysts start optimistic and walk down to a beatable target. Walk-down is most pronounced when firms or insiders are net sellers after the earnings announcement (SEO / option exercise / insider sale). Channel: PRE_EVENT analyst path, capital-market incentives. Does not prove managers cause the walk-down (joint equilibrium). PIT: post-announcement selling is chosen, so reverse causality (walk-down enables selling) is the authors' hypothesis, not a randomized incentive. URL: https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/coacre/v21y2004i4p885-924.html.

**Graham, Harvey & Rajgopal (2005), JAE 40:3–73 / NBER w10550.** PRIMARY SOURCE VERIFIED NBER abstract. Survey of 401 executives + 20 interviews: EPS is the key outsider metric, more than cash flows; firms will sacrifice economic value to hit a short-run target; 78% would give up value for smooth earnings; 55% would avoid a very positive-NPV project if it meant missing this quarter's consensus. Voluntary disclosure is used to cut information risk but managers avoid precedents they cannot maintain. Channel: why HEADLINE EPS and guidance matter to managers. Does not prove that missing consensus causes the feared price drop (that is the managers' belief). Sample is self-selected executives, ~2000s. URLs: https://www.nber.org/papers/w10550, https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jaecon/v40y2005i1-3p3-73.html.

Bartov, Givoly & Hayn (2002), JAE 33(2):173–204, "The rewards to meeting or beating earnings expectations." Title/venue verified; no abstract on opened page. Finding: UNKNOWN this session. URL: https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jaecon/v33y2002i2p173-204.html.

6. Attention / information diffusion (Friday earnings, inattention, media)
DellaVigna & Pollet — published JF 2009 vs NBER WP 11683 (both opened).

Published: DellaVigna & Pollet, 2009, Journal of Finance 64(2):709–749, "Investor Inattention and Friday Earnings Announcements." PRIMARY SOURCE VERIFIED abstract. Friday announcements have a 15% lower immediate response and a 70% higher delayed response; volume 8% lower; authors say this supports PEAD-as-underreaction-from-limited-attention. URL: https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v64y2009i2p709-749.html. DOI 10.1111/j.1540-6261.2009.01447.x.
Working paper: NBER w11683 (Oct 2005), "Investor Inattention, Firm Reaction, and Friday Earnings Announcements." PRIMARY SOURCE VERIFIED. Delayed share of total response 60% Friday vs 40% other weekdays; volume 10% lower; Friday associated with 45% higher probability of a negative surprise and 50 bp lower abnormal return (strategic release of worse news). URL: https://www.nber.org/papers/w11683.
Conflict to preserve: published JF drops/changes the "firms dump bad news on Friday" magnitudes and reports different immediate/delayed splits than the WP. Crux = specification/sample revision between 2005 WP and 2009 JF, not a later contradiction study. Use JF 2009 as the published claim; treat WP numbers as superseded unless a replication re-opens them.

Hirshleifer, Lim & Teoh (2009), JF 64(5):2289–2325, "Driven to Distraction." PRIMARY SOURCE VERIFIED (IDEAS published abstract + MPRA 3110 abstract). Immediate price and volume reaction to a firm's earnings surprise is weaker, and PEAD stronger, when more other firms announce the same day. Industry-unrelated news and large surprises distract more; MPRA abstract adds that distraction is stronger for positive surprises and that a PEAD strategy is unprofitable on low-competition days. Channel: same-day attention congestion, not Friday per se. Does not prove limited attention is the only PEAD mechanism; same-day count is correlated with calendar clustering and news quality. PIT: announcement-day choice is endogenous (see DellaVigna WP). URLs: https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v64y2009i5p2289-2325.html, https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/3110/.

7. PEAD and reversal (including fade, overnight vs daytime)
Livnat & Mendenhall (2006), JAR 44(1):177–205. PRIMARY SOURCE VERIFIED abstract. PEAD is significantly larger when SUE is defined from I/B/E/S analyst forecasts/actuals than from a Compustat time-series model. Neither Compustat restatement policy nor special items explain the gap; the gap is attributed to analyst vs time-series surprise differences (or correlates). Future-announcement return patterns also differ across the two surprise types — they "may capture somewhat different forms of mispricing." Channel: how the surprise is measured (analyst-revision state vs seasonal random walk). Does not prove mispricing vs risk; does not prove PEAD is stable out of their sample. URL: https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/joares/v44y2006i1p177-205.html.
