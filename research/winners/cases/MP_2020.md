# MP 2020 winner autopsy

## 1. Bottom line

MP's 2020 alpha was not simply a rare-earth headline or Materials beta: capital was suddenly able to price a formerly private mine whose [December 9, 2020 S-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1801368/000119312520313937/d48477ds1.htm) showed acute customer concentration as a liquid U.S. strategic-minerals vehicle with EV optionality. The [July 15, 2020 merger filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1801368/000119312520192954/d949883d8k.htm) supplied a public valuation and operating model; the [November 17, 2020 closing](https://mpmaterials.com/news/press-release-11-17-2020) and $545 million capital raise removed near-term funding risk for Stage II separation; the [November 18, 2020 Defense Department agreement](https://mpmaterials.com/news/press-release-11182020/) made policy support company-specific; and [November 23, 2020 results](https://mpmaterials.com/news/press-release-11232020/) showed that higher realized prices and volume were already producing operating leverage. That stack met a scarce public-market narrative—the November 17 release put Mountain Pass at roughly 15% of global rare-earth content, while the [September 30, 2020 executive order](https://trumpwhitehouse.archives.gov/presidential-actions/executive-order-addressing-threat-domestic-supply-chain-reliance-critical-minerals-foreign-adversaries/) targeted foreign dependence—and the first clearly archived target did not appear until [December 1, 2020](https://chartexchange.com/symbol/nyse-mp/news/), letting long-duration separation and magnet economics escape the initial published model. The alpha did persist at 126 sessions and through 2021 year-end, but not smoothly: the S-1 exposed resale/warrant supply and the China/Shenghe dependency, the peak was followed by a [March 23, 2021 convertible](https://investors.mpmaterials.com/investor-news/news-details/2021/MP-Materials-Announces-Pricing-of-600-Million-Green-Convertible-Senior-Notes-Offering/default.aspx) and [same-day secondary placement](https://investors.mpmaterials.com/investor-news/news-details/2021/MP-Materials-Announces-Pricing-of-Secondary-Public-Offering-by-Selling-Stockholders/default.aspx), and MP fell 50.22% from its March 2 close to its May 13 low before recovering (`data/yahoo/MP.parquet`).

## 4. Stage anatomy

- **Compressed prior — present, atypical.** The predecessor SPAC traded near $10 before the July deal and retraced from a $15.16 summer close to $11.08 on November 2. But the tape preceding the detector date was no longer compressed: MP rose 101.2% from November 9 to December 4 (`data/baskets/ohlcv/MP.parquet`).
- **Catalyst ladder — present.** Deal economics, policy, listing/funding, a company-specific Defense Department contribution, and Q3 operating proof were independent rungs; later research and publicity amplified rather than originated the thesis.
- **Relative breakaway — present.** At the detector date, MP was at its first 63-day closing high with +89.86pp/+57.75pp trailing 21d/42d excess versus XLB; it then produced +63.34pp 63-day and +33.53pp 126-day forward excess (`data/research/winner_episodes.parquet`).
- **Liquidity confirmation — present, lagged.** Detector-date z21 was only -0.11, but the five-day/60-day dollar-volume ratio was 2.05; December 17 then printed $521.4m at z21 +3.93 and its gap remained above the prior close through ten sessions (`data/baskets/ohlcv/MP.parquet`).
- **Options convexity — unknown.** No 2020 MP options history exists in the repo; per-ticker history begins in June 2026, so neither convexity nor signed flow can be inferred for this episode.

## 5. Fundamentals arc

Point-in-time coverage is uneven because MP became public through a 2020 combination:

- The latest annual row available by `t0` in `data/edgar/fundamentals_panel.parquet` is FY2019, `asof_date` 2020-04-29: revenue **$73.4m**, operating loss **$7.6m** (margin **-10.4%**), net loss **$6.8m**, operating cash flow **-$0.4m**, assets **$101.8m**, and negative equity **-$18.0m**. Capex and debt are null in that row.
- The [November 23, 2020 release](https://mpmaterials.com/news/press-release-11232020/) is the cleanest contemporaneous during-run bridge: Q3 revenue **$41.0m**, +52% year over year; net income **$14.6m** versus $2.8m; adjusted EBITDA **$11.6m**, +159%; production **10,197 metric tons**; realized revenue **$3,393/MT**; and production cost **$1,389/MT**. Nine-month revenue was **$92.1m**, while adjusted EBITDA reached **$24.6m** from a prior-year loss. Q3 net income included an $8.9m tariff refund, so the adjusted-EBITDA trend is the cleaner operating bridge.
- FY2020 in `data/edgar/fundamentals_panel.parquet` has `asof_date` 2021-04-30—after the run peak, not available at `t0`: revenue **$134.3m**, operating loss **$34.7m** (margin **-25.8%**), net loss **$21.8m**, operating cash flow **$3.3m**, capex **$22.4m**, assets **$1.074bn**, equity **$853.9m**, and long-term debt **$1.0m**. The [March 18, 2021 release](https://investors.mpmaterials.com/investor-news/news-details/2021/MP-Materials-Reports-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-2020-Results/default.aspx) reconciled the apparent GAAP/operating split: FY adjusted EBITDA was **$42.6m**, while the GAAP loss included a **$66.6m** non-cash settlement charge; cash was **$519.7m**.
- The July transaction model expected above $100m revenue and about $30m adjusted EBITDA in 2020, as filed on [July 15, 2020](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1801368/000119312520192954/d949883d8k.htm). The March 18 actuals exceeded both, but that comparison was public only after the March 2 price peak.
- `data/edgar/statements.parquet` is a retrospective annual dataset with `as_of` 2026-07-06; it is not used as episode-timely evidence. `data/edgar/statements_quarterly.parquet` is `as_of` 2026-07-12 and its 2020 MP-labelled rows are largely blank predecessor-shell observations, so quarterly revenue/margin history there is null for this purpose. `data/edgar/eps_quarterly.parquet` provides point-in-time EPS rows, including Q3 2020 EPS of $0.20 as of 2020-11-29, but does not supply a complete margin/runway bridge.

The economic arc was therefore **balance-sheet repair plus price/volume operating leverage**, not a contemporaneous GAAP-margin turnaround. The listing cash fully funded the stated separation build, the mine produced cash economics, and later results beat the deal model; the hazards were that separated oxides and magnets remained future execution, the GAAP margin was still negative, and customer/processing concentration remained acute.

## 7. Research echo

**Consensus versus spot: null.** The repo has no analyst-target dataset, and no complete point-in-time 2020 consensus series was found externally. Individual dated actions still reveal the model-lag pattern:

- The [December 1, 2020 archive](https://chartexchange.com/symbol/nyse-mp/news/) records Deutsche Bank initiating with a **$25** target against a **$20.55** close. MP closed above that target on December 7 and reached **$32.62** by December 17—30.5% beyond the published model only 12 trading sessions after initiation (`data/yahoo/MP.parquet`).
- The same archive records Morgan Stanley initiating at **$57** on [March 2, 2021](https://chartexchange.com/symbol/nyse-mp/news/), when MP closed at **$49.44** and set the episode peak.
- It then records Deutsche Bank moving to a **$37** target on [March 9, 2021](https://chartexchange.com/symbol/nyse-mp/news/) versus a **$39.40** close, followed by Cowen initiating at **$50** on [March 10, 2021](https://chartexchange.com/symbol/nyse-mp/news/) versus a **$39.30** close.

The read is “price escaped the published model,” then research caught up and bracketed a more volatile tape. The first $25 reference was obsolete before the December liquidity climax; the later $57 reference arrived at the peak. That sequence is consistent with sparse coverage responding to listing liquidity, policy optionality, and operating proof, but it does not establish that research revisions caused the move.

## 9. Hazards & false-positive checks

| Check | Verdict | Note |
|---|---|---|
| meme_squeeze | possible | The de-SPAC structure, publicity bursts, and vertical gaps permit reflexive amplification; PIT short-interest data are absent. Independent policy, financing, and operating rungs plus persistent 126-day excess argue against this as the whole mechanism. |
| one_day_binary | ruled_out | Re-underwriting events accumulated from July 2020 through May 2021, and several major gaps held 3/5/10 sessions. |
| sector_beta | ruled_out | MP beat XLB by 63.34pp over 63 forward sessions and 33.53pp over 126; broad Materials exposure cannot explain the magnitude, though rare-earth prices and policy were meaningful thematic beta. |
| options_mirage | possible | 2020 options evidence is null, so options amplification cannot be established or excluded. Current walls/OI cannot explain a six-year-old episode. |

Episode hazards were substantial: Stage II execution had not yet produced separated oxides; the [December 9, 2020 S-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1801368/000119312520313937/d48477ds1.htm) reported more than 90% of revenue from Shenghe and an operating chain still dependent on China, while rare-earth concentrate lacked a transparent exchange price; the same filing exposed resale/warrant supply; and the March 2021 convertible plus secondary expanded supply around the unwind. The cleanest failure-to-sustain statistic is the **-50.22%** March 2-to-May 13 close drawdown, even though longer-horizon alpha recovered.

Current hazards are deliberately separated from the episode. **Extension: ruled out on simple close distance**—the 2026-07-17 $45.24 close is 54.14% below MP's 2025-10-14 all-time closing high of $98.65 (`data/yahoo/MP.parquet`). **Call wall: possible and nearby**—the current snapshot places a weak wall at $46.50, but it is descriptive, unsigned, and timestamp-specific (`site/gex/MP.json`). **Catalyst exhaustion: unknown**—this 2020 case does not reconstruct the full post-2021 public-event set, and price/options snapshots cannot answer that question.
