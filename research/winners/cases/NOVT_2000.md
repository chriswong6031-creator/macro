# NOVT 2000 — failed breakaway autopsy

## 1. Bottom line

The 2000 NOVT episode—then GSI Lumonics, ticker GSLI—was not empty promotion: a newly merged laser-equipment supplier was harvesting a semiconductor/electronics capital-spending rebound, merger efficiencies, higher-margin mix, a rising backlog, conspicuous customer orders, and portfolio pruning while investors added a telecom-optics growth narrative. The failure came from sequence and magnitude. By the 2000-07-03 mechanical onset, the mapped stock had risen 355.71% YTD and 111.26% in 42 sessions, whereas Q1 sales had risen 49% pro forma, backlog only from $83M to $87M sequentially, and operating cash was already being absorbed by receivables and inventory. The June 28 drilling orders and July 6 supply pact confirmed demand but were small relative to the valuation move; a July 6 public semiconductor-cycle warning attacked the extrapolation; Q2 later showed real 34% sales growth and $103M backlog but also sequential gross-margin compression and $16.6M of first-half operating cash use. Capital had correctly detected a cyclical/operating turn and then capitalized too much durability too early. The result was a blow-off: extreme alpha into t0, a July 20 closing peak, and then -46.59 percentage points of excess return versus XLK over the next 63 sessions.

This is a **FAILED breakaway case** (`case_type: failed_breakaway`).

## 3. Catalyst ladder

The local EDGAR event stores do not reach this episode: `data/edgar/material_8k_events.parquet` begins NOVT coverage in 2010, while `data/edgar/earnings_8k_dates.parquet` begins in 2004. SEC filing anchors below therefore come directly from contemporaneous EDGAR filings.

- **2000-01-21 — customer order:** Samsung Electro-Mechanics ordered multiple W670G laser trim systems, making electronics demand tangible ([Photonics Spectra, published 2000-01-21](https://www.photonics.com/Articles/GSI-Lumonics-Receives-Order-for-Laser-Trim-Systems/a5496)).
- **2000-04-04 — legal overhang reduced:** a district court ruled in favor of GSI subsidiaries in a patent dispute in which the claimant had sought more than $65M, although an appeal was expected ([Photonics Spectra, published 2000-04-04](https://www.photonics.com/Articles/GSI-Lumonics-Backed-on-Suit-RVSI-to-Appeal/p6/vo23/i163/a6034)).
- **2000-04-11 — order cluster:** the company announced more than $9M of semiconductor and circuit trim-system orders ([Photonics Spectra news index, published 2000-04-11](https://www.photonics.com/Issues/Photonicscom_April_2000/i163)).
- **2000-04-20 — portfolio pruning:** GSI sold its VIEW metrology operating assets, describing the line as non-core ([MetrologyWorld, published 2000-04-20](https://www.metrologyworld.com/doc/gsi-lumonics-sells-metrology-line-to-quality-0001)).
- **2000-05-05 — Q1 filing:** Q1 sales were $87.9M, up 49% pro forma; gross margin reached 42.1%, and backlog was $87M versus $83M at 1999 year-end ([SEC 10-Q, filed 2000-05-05](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1076930/000092701600001641/0000927016-00-001641.txt)).
- **2000-06-09 — further portfolio focus:** the $8.9M sale of the Xymark package-coding business directed resources toward semiconductor, electronics, and automotive markets ([Photonics Spectra, published 2000-06-09](https://www.photonics.com/Articles/GSI-Lumonics-Sells-Xymark-Laser-Coding-Unit/p5/a6452)).
- **2000-06-28 — breakaway order:** more than $3M of GS600 laser-drilling orders from Viasystems was published immediately before the high-volume break ([Photonics Spectra news index, published 2000-06-28](https://www.photonics.com/Splash.aspx?Page=1065&Tag=Latest+News)).
- **2000-07-06 — demand confirmation and macro counter-signal:** Spectra-Physics disclosed a $5M laser supply agreement for GSI systems and called demand strong; the same publication date, a public semiconductor-sector report described an analyst warning that a chip slowdown could unfold in six to nine months ([Photonics Spectra, published 2000-07-06](https://www.photonics.com/Articles/Spectra-Physics-GSI-Lumonics-Sign-365-Million/p5/a6657); [Los Angeles Times/Bloomberg, published 2000-07-06](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-2000-jul-06-fi-48412-story.html)).
- **2000-07-24 — optics acquisition intent:** the company signed a letter of intent for a precision-optics supplier with about $8M in annual sales, extending the telecom-optics narrative; the publication anchor is disclosed in the later filing ([SEC 10-Q, filed 2000-08-03](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1076930/000092701600002707/0000927016-00-002707-0001.txt)).
- **2000-08-03 — Q2 filing and reality check:** Q2 sales rose 34% to $92.8M and backlog reached $103M, but gross margin fell sequentially to 39.9% from 42.1%, and first-half operations used $16.6M as working capital expanded ([SEC 10-Q, filed 2000-08-03](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1076930/000092701600002707/0000927016-00-002707-0001.txt)).
- **2000-08-22 — Life Sciences divestiture:** GSI announced sale of a unit representing about 5% of sales for cash and stock valued near $120M; this large apparent value unlock arrived after the original break had already failed and did not restore the high ([Photonics Spectra, published 2000-08-22](https://www.photonics.com/Articles/GSI-Lumonics-Sells-Life-Sciences-Businessto/a6944)).

## 4. Stage anatomy

- **Compressed prior — absent.** The stock had already risen 340% over 126 sessions and had printed its first 252-day high three weeks before t0. The onset was vertical extension, not emergence from compression.
- **Catalyst ladder — present.** Customer orders, legal relief, divestitures, Q1 operating leverage, and telecom-optics adjacency gave repeated reasons to revise estimates. Durability was overread because many events were orders or portfolio actions inside a cyclical capital-equipment business.
- **Relative breakaway — present, then failed.** Trailing excess was +71.56pp/+107.08pp versus XLK at 21/42 days, but forward excess reversed to -32.37pp at 21 days and -46.59pp at 63 days.
- **Liquidity confirmation — present but non-durable.** T0 z21 was about +1.42 to +1.45 and 5d/60d dollar volume was 2.96×; subsequent top-volume down sessions showed distribution/volatility rather than a stable hold.
- **Options convexity — unknown.** Repository per-ticker options history begins 2026-06. No 2000 chain, open-interest migration, dealer gamma, or trade-level NBBO survives in scope.

## 5. Fundamentals arc

All four requested PIT panels are null for 2000. `data/edgar/fundamentals_panel.parquet` starts NOVT in FY2010; `data/edgar/statements_quarterly.parquet` starts in 2011; `data/edgar/eps_quarterly.parquet` starts in 2010; and `data/edgar/statements.parquet` contains only FY2020 onward. The contemporaneous figures below therefore come from filing-date-gated SEC documents, not from later restatements in the local panels.

Before the run, FY1999 sales were $274.6M and gross profit $95.8M, a 34.9% margin; backlog was $83M at 1999-12-31 versus $59M pro forma a year earlier. The 2000-03-22 filing described the semiconductor-equipment recovery and stronger semiconductor/electronics orders ([SEC 10-K, filed 2000-03-22](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1076930/000092701600000953/0000927016-00-000953.txt)). Q1, public on 2000-05-05, delivered $87.9M sales, 42.1% gross margin, and $87M backlog. Margin expansion reflected higher-margin mix, consolidated manufacturing, and lower warranty expense, while the small sequential backlog gain cautioned against extrapolating the stock's pace ([SEC 10-Q, filed 2000-05-05](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1076930/000092701600001641/0000927016-00-001641.txt)).

During the break/failure, Q2 sales were $92.8M (+34% year over year), gross profit $37.0M, operating income $7.2M, and net income $5.8M, or $0.14 diluted per share. Semiconductor sales were $14.6M versus $9.0M, electronics $30.0M versus $15.7M, and backlog rose to $103M. Yet gross margin slipped to 39.9% on less favorable mix, inventories increased to $93.9M from $72.7M at year-end, and first-half operations used $16.6M despite accounting profit because receivables, inventory, and other current assets expanded. Runway itself was not the immediate failure: an April offering at $17 generated $70.1M net and quarter-end cash was $71.7M. The weakness was quality/durability of conversion in a cyclical, working-capital-heavy model, not imminent insolvency ([SEC 10-Q, filed 2000-08-03](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1076930/000092701600002707/0000927016-00-002707-0001.txt)).

Coverage gaps: no publication-timely consensus forecast, segment operating profit, organic-versus-merger growth bridge, or normalized free-cash-flow series is available. Comparisons with 1999 are also complicated by the 1999 merger of General Scanning and Lumonics.

## 9. Hazards & false-positive checks

- **meme_squeeze — possible.** “Meme” is anachronistic, but a vertical small-cap technology move, repeated order headlines, and reflexive liquidity could have created squeeze-like amplification; 2000 short-interest evidence is unavailable.
- **one_day_binary — ruled_out.** Multiple public events accumulated from January through August, and the price formed several successive highs. No single binary event explains the whole path.
- **sector_beta — ruled_out.** NOVT exceeded XLK by +107.08pp over the trailing 42 sessions and then underperformed it by -46.59pp over 63 forward sessions; ordinary sector beta cannot explain either magnitude.
- **options_mirage — possible.** Options amplification cannot be excluded, but historical options evidence is null and no signed direction can be inferred.
- **Current/episode hazards:** `extended_after_vertical_move`, `catalyst_exhausted`, `cyclical_capex_exposure`, `working_capital_conversion`, `margin_mix_reversal`, `small_orders_overcapitalized`, and `call_wall_overhead_current_only`.
