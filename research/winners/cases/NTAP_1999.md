# NTAP 1999 — failed breakaway autopsy

## 1. Bottom line

Network Appliance's 1999 alpha came from four forces that reinforced one another: a real category shift toward network-attached storage and Internet infrastructure; a differentiated filer/NetCache/Data ONTAP product stack whose add-on software lifted average system value; rapid channel and geographic expansion; and market-structure accelerants from S&P 500 inclusion and repeated stock splits. The operating evidence was unusually strong—fiscal-1999 revenue rose 74.2% with a 59.2% gross margin ([10-K filed 1999-07-12](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1002047/000089161899003094/0000891618-99-003094.txt)), then fiscal Q2 2000 revenue and net income rose 90% and 92%, respectively, while the company announced a two-for-one split ([published 1999-11-16](https://investors.netapp.com/node/9461)); S&P 500 inclusion had already added index demand ([published 1999-06-23](https://www.thestreet.com/markets/midday06-23-99-758758)). But the supplied 1999-12-22 onset was a late re-trigger, not the birth of a base: NTAP was already +280.77% YTD and +241.07% over 126 sessions, its approximate equity value was about 32 times trailing revenue and 260 times trailing net income, and gross and operating margins had not expanded materially. The remaining mechanism therefore required continuing upside surprises and multiple persistence. It produced more fixed-horizon alpha, but not a holdable path: the stock fell 17.94% in the first ten sessions, later lost 58.09% in 25 sessions from its March peak, and eventually lost 95.60% from its October 2000 peak as growth expectations and enterprise capital spending rolled over ([growth concern published 2000-11-15](https://money.cnn.com/2000/11/15/markets/street_talk/); [order-delay warning published 2001-04-11](https://www.forbes.com/2001/04/11/0411traderam.html)). The autopsy is thus not “the fundamentals were fictitious”; it is “a genuine platform winner was detected after valuation, split mechanics, index demand, and surprise expectations had already consumed most of the margin for error.”

**This is a FAILED breakaway case (`case_type: failed_breakaway`): the run did not sustain.** More precisely, it failed the path-durability test rather than every endpoint-return test. `data/research/winner_episodes.parquet` labels the 1999-12-22 mechanical episode `blow_off`, with `clean_hold = false` and `durable_winner = false`, although its frozen `failed` field is false because 63-session forward excess remained positive. The operator case label and the mechanical outcome therefore disagree in a useful way: fixed-window alpha concealed two catastrophic path breaks.

## 9. Hazards & false-positive checks

- **meme_squeeze — ruled_out.** This predates social-media meme mechanics, and the move was accompanied by repeated revenue, earnings, channel, product, and filing evidence. Historical short interest is null, so some short-covering contribution cannot be excluded.
- **one_day_binary — ruled_out.** The visible leg ran from the August multi-window high through multiple earnings and ecosystem rungs; no single day explains the +280.77% YTD return.
- **sector_beta — possible.** XLK gained 62.65% YTD and QQQ gained strongly after its March inception, so the Internet-infrastructure regime supplied a major tailwind. Yet NTAP exceeded XLK by 218.12 pp YTD and 109.12 pp over 42 sessions at t0, so sector beta alone is insufficient. In the first March–April collapse NTAP also fell far more than XLK and QQQ.
- **options_mirage — ruled_out.** No historical options evidence is used. Options history is unavailable before 2026-06, and the mechanism rests on price, liquidity, filings, and operating results.
