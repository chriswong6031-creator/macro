# GTLB 2022 — failed-breakaway autopsy

## 1. Bottom line

GitLab's apparent August 2022 alpha was the late portion of a real but already-seasoned re-underwriting: the June 6 quarter showed 75% revenue growth, greater-than-130% dollar-based net retention, 90% non-GAAP gross margin and a raised outlook, while the market-wide summer rebound restored duration appetite to richly valued software. By the mechanical August 5 onset, however, there was no fresh company catalyst, no opening gap, only modest liquidity confirmation, and only a 63-day—not 126- or 252-day—closing high. The stock had already repriced toward published targets and a roughly 16-times forward-revenue valuation. That made the apparent alpha conditional on multiple expansion rather than on a new estimate revision cycle; a September 1 valuation downgrade broke the tape before another strong quarter could repair it. The mosaic is therefore “excellent growth, weak breakaway architecture”: fundamentals explained why GTLB bounced harder than software and SPY, while stale information, thin confirmation and valuation explained why the excess return did not persist.

**Format-contract classification: this is a FAILED breakaway case; the run did not sustain.**

## 4. Stage anatomy

- **Compressed prior — false/weak:** GTLB was 55% below its November 2021 closing high at the May 24 trough, but the “base” was highly volatile, with repeated 5–17% daily moves. Drawdown existed; compression did not.
- **Catalyst ladder — present but stale at t0:** March and June earnings repeatedly raised the fundamental floor, but August 5 was 60 calendar days after the last earnings release and had no fresh filing. September earnings came after the breakaway had already failed.
- **Relative breakaway — present, locally only:** +64.39 pp over 42 days versus SPY and a 63-day high met the supplied episode condition, but 126-/252-day highs were absent and YTD excess versus SPY was still -5.49 pp.
- **Liquidity confirmation — weak:** the supplied z21 was 1.43, enough for the detector, while dv5/60 was only 1.06 and direct z60 only 0.43. The institutional-capacity turnover occurred on June 7, not t0.
- **Options convexity — unknown:** no 2022 options history exists in the repo. Any causal options story would be invention.

[GitLab 10-K filed 2022-04-08](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1653482/000162828022008836/gtlb-20220131.htm) The vulnerability was the price paid for distant profitability, not an identified near-term financing need.
