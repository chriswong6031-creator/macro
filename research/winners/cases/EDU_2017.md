# EDU 2017 winner autopsy

## 1. Bottom line

EDU's 2017 alpha was a durable re-rating of a cash-rich, increasingly K-12-led education platform, but it was not clean narrow-cohort leadership: January showed that 56% enrollment growth and roughly 45% K-12 revenue growth could turn the seasonally weak quarter's operating loss positive; April converted that demand into a revenue beat, 36% operating-income growth, and margin expansion; July added another quarter of leverage, a 34% deferred-revenue increase, a special dividend, and evidence that the O2O teaching system was improving retention and acquisition while the physical network expanded. [Company releases published 2017-01-17](https://investor.neworiental.org/news-releases/news-release-details/new-oriental-announces-results-second-fiscal-quarter-ended-8/), [2017-04-24](https://investor.neworiental.org/static-files/500c0b0e-7249-4f8b-8ed6-37c7fe83e41a), and [2017-07-25](https://investor.neworiental.org/news-releases/news-release-details/new-oriental-announces-results-fourth-fiscal-quarter-and-1/). That evidence let capital extend the runway well beyond SPY, XLY, and broad China-growth proxies. The qualification is important: TAL was stronger at every trailing horizon at the April onset and over calendar 2017, so common China-tutoring enthusiasm was material. EDU's own October release then showed the cost of the expansion—23.8% revenue growth but a 420 bp operating-margin decline—and TAL's weaker profit conversion two days later coincided with EDU's largest dollar-volume decline of the year. [EDU release published 2017-10-24](https://investor.neworiental.org/news-releases/news-release-details/new-oriental-announces-results-first-fiscal-quarter-ended-6/); [TAL release published 2017-10-26](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/tal-education-group-announces-unaudited-financial-results-for-the-second-fiscal-quarter-ended-august-31-2017-300543755.html). EDU nevertheless recovered to finish at a new adjusted closing high, making the mechanism a platform re-rating with real cohort beta and an explicitly visible margin hazard, rather than a one-print event.

## 3. Catalyst ladder

- **2017-01-17 — Q2 FY2017 results (published 3:00 a.m. EST).** Revenue rose 22.7% to $341.2m, enrollment 56.0%, and K-12 revenue roughly 45%; operating income improved to $0.2m from a $10.4m loss and deferred revenue rose 30.4%. Management guided Q3 revenue to $408.7m-$421.8m. This established the pre-onset demand and leverage pattern. [Company release, published 2017-01-17](https://investor.neworiental.org/news-releases/news-release-details/new-oriental-announces-results-second-fiscal-quarter-ended-8/).
- **2017-04-24 — Q3 FY2017 results (published 4:00 a.m. ET).** Revenue reached $437.8m, 26.2% growth and 3.8% above the top of January's range; operating income rose 36.0%, net income 39.6%, and operating margin expanded to 13.1% from 12.2%. The apparently low 5.9% quarterly enrollment growth was a timing artifact: combined Q2-Q3 enrollment grew 32%. This was the estimate reset and the mechanical t0. [Company release, published 2017-04-24](https://investor.neworiental.org/static-files/500c0b0e-7249-4f8b-8ed6-37c7fe83e41a).
- **2017-05-17 — J.P. Morgan rating downgrade (published 9:25 a.m. ET).** A contemporaneous roundup reported a move from Overweight to Neutral; EDU closed down 9.06% on $206.6m dollar volume. The downgrade tested valuation support without terminating the run. [Benzinga report, published 2017-05-17](https://www.benzinga.com/analyst-ratings/upgrades/17/05/9479294/benzingas-top-upgrades-downgrades-for-may-17-2017).
- **2017-06-30 — Macquarie model revision.** The report lifted its DCF target 17% to $88 from a $71.60 reference price and raised FY2018/FY2019 earnings estimates by 1%/2%, citing K-12 share gains, utilization, dual-teacher expansion, and O2O reach. It forecast $480m Q4 revenue versus $477m consensus. [Macquarie report, published 2017-06-30](https://pdf.dfcfw.com/pdf/H3_AP201706300679879076_01.pdf).
- **2017-07-25 — Q4/FY2017 results and special dividend (published 4:00 a.m. EDT).** Quarterly revenue grew 23.2%, enrollment 36.9%, operating income 39.7%, and operating margin expanded to 10.7% from 9.4%; actual revenue of $486.4m exceeded both the Macquarie and cited consensus estimates. FY2017 revenue grew 21.7%, operating income 31.8%, and K-12 revenue in renminbi 44.2%, reaching 55% of revenue. EDU also declared a $0.45-per-ADS special dividend funded from surplus cash. [Company release, published 2017-07-25](https://investor.neworiental.org/news-releases/news-release-details/new-oriental-announces-results-fourth-fiscal-quarter-and-1/).
- **2017-09-27 — FY2017 Form 20-F filed.** The audited filing put the annual growth, cash resources, VIE structure, and policy risks into the public record; it confirmed $1.800bn revenue, $262.1m operating income, and $1.721bn total equity. [SEC Form 20-F, filed 2017-09-27](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1372920/000119312517295945/d427302d20f.htm).
- **2017-10-24 — Q1 FY2018 results (published 4:13 a.m. EDT).** Revenue of $661.2m grew 23.8% and exceeded the prior $626.5m-$647.3m range; K-12 revenue grew 35.3%, deferred revenue 41.5%, and Q2 guidance called for 31%-35% growth. The counterevidence was equally public: operating margin fell to 24.4% from 28.6% as classroom area expanded 31.4% and a large summer promotion raised costs. [Company release, published 2017-10-24](https://investor.neworiental.org/news-releases/news-release-details/new-oriental-announces-results-first-fiscal-quarter-ended-6/).
- **2017-10-26 — TAL peer results supplied a negative cohort read-through.** TAL reported 68.1% revenue growth but cost of revenue grew 85.7%, operating income only 32.6%, and attributable net income 6.7%. EDU fell 6.95% on its largest 2017 dollar-volume day, showing that investors were underwriting shared tutoring economics, not EDU in isolation. [TAL release, published 2017-10-26](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/tal-education-group-announces-unaudited-financial-results-for-the-second-fiscal-quarter-ended-august-31-2017-300543755.html).

## 4. Stage anatomy

- **Compressed prior — absent.** EDU had already gained 44.92% YTD at t0, and strict 63/126/252-day highs first appeared in March. The 21-day gain had slowed to 8.11%, but this was digestion inside an established advance, not a compressed pre-break base.
- **Catalyst ladder — present.** January demand/leverage evidence, the April beat, July annual confirmation and dividend, and October's growth-versus-margin trade-off gave capital several publication-dated revisions rather than one binary event.
- **Relative breakaway — present.** At t0, 42-day excess was +24.31 pp versus XLY, +25.77 pp versus SPY, and +23.59 pp versus CQQQ, with a 63-day high. EDU lagged TAL by 7.80 pp over 42 days, limiting the company-specific interpretation.
- **Liquidity confirmation — present.** T0 dollar volume was 2.99 standard deviations above its 21-day mean and 2.64 above its 60-day mean; July and October produced later 3.3-3.7 sigma company-report sessions.
- **Options convexity — unknown.** There is no 2017 per-ticker options history and no trade-level NBBO evidence; equity price/volume cannot establish historical options convexity.

During the run, Q3 revenue/operating income grew 26.2%/36.0% and margin expanded 90 bp; Q4 revenue/operating income grew 23.2%/39.7% and margin expanded 130 bp. FY2017 then showed revenue of $1.800bn (+21.7%), operating income of $262.1m (+31.8%), and operating margin of 14.6% versus 13.4%. [Company releases published 2017-04-24](https://investor.neworiental.org/static-files/500c0b0e-7249-4f8b-8ed6-37c7fe83e41a) and [2017-07-25](https://investor.neworiental.org/news-releases/news-release-details/new-oriental-announces-results-fourth-fiscal-quarter-and-1/).

The late-run trade-off was capacity ahead of utilization. Q1 FY2018 revenue grew 23.8% and K-12 revenue 35.3%, but operating income grew only 5.6% and operating margin contracted 420 bp as classroom area rose 31.4% and promotion costs increased. Runway still expanded: at 2017-08-31 cash, term deposits, short-term investments, and near-term held investments totaled about $2.379bn; quarterly operating cash flow was $204.4m versus $54.1m capital expenditure, and deferred revenue reached $930.0m (+41.5%). The market could continue underwriting demand, but the easy margin-expansion phase had ended. [Company release, published 2017-10-24](https://investor.neworiental.org/news-releases/news-release-details/new-oriental-announces-results-first-fiscal-quarter-ended-6/).

## 9. Hazards & false-positive checks

- **meme_squeeze — ruled_out.** The move accumulated across a January-April-July operating ladder, survived a May downgrade, and retained broad-benchmark excess through 252 sessions. Historical short-interest coverage is null, so this does not claim anything about short positioning.
- **one_day_binary — ruled_out.** EDU's forward SPY excess expanded from +15.34 pp at 21 sessions to +39.01 pp at 126 sessions, with later liquidity renewal and a year-end new high.
- **sector_beta — possible.** EDU beat XLY, SPY, and CQQQ across the episode, but TAL led EDU at every trailing horizon, over calendar 2017, and by 52.73 pp over the 252 sessions after t0. The October TAL read-through also generated EDU's largest volume day.
- **options_mirage — ruled_out.** No historical options evidence enters the mechanism; the options-convexity stage is explicitly unknown.
- **Current hazards as of the latest local bar, 2026-07-17:** extension is not present on a simple price basis (EDU at $49.15 was 16.05% below its trailing 63-session high and 21.70% below its 126/252-session high); call-wall overhead is null because there is no EDU GEX snapshot; current catalyst exhaustion is unknown because this case's publication study is bounded to the 2017 episode. Historically, margin conversion and cohort contagion became live hazards in October, but the 2017 price recovery means catalyst exhaustion was possible rather than established.
