# TAL — 2017 winner autopsy

## Bottom line

TAL's 2017 alpha was a rolling re-underwriting of a scarce, liquid China K–12 growth franchise, not one headline: the January print established roughly 40% revenue growth despite a near-term profit decline, the April print abruptly reset the slope to 81% quarterly revenue growth, 70% enrollment growth and 161% operating-income growth, and July sustained 65% revenue growth while management kept adding learning centers and online capacity. China growth beta helped—EDU and CQQQ were also strong—but TAL's 21-day excess was 21.26 percentage points versus SPY and 20.16 points versus CQQQ at the March 21 breakaway. Rising enrollment, geographic/capacity expansion, operating leverage in the April quarter, a special dividend and later a six-for-one-equivalent ADS ratio change widened the investable audience and kept estimates chasing price. The move did not sustain its October verticality: on October 26, revenue still grew 68%, but costs grew faster, operating income only 33%, and the shares fell 14.3%; the market had shifted from rewarding land-grab growth to demanding profit conversion.

## Catalyst ladder

- **2017-01-19 — Q3 FY2017 results:** revenue rose 39.0% to $260.6 million and enrollment rose 55.2%, while operating income fell 11.4%; next-quarter revenue guidance of $278.3–$283.8 million implied 59%–62% growth. This was a growth-duration signal with an explicit margin caveat. [Company release, published 2017-01-19](https://media.corporate-ir.net/media_files/IROL/24/240640/TAL-Q3-FY2017-Earnings-Release.pdf).
- **2017-04-27 — Q4/FY2017 results and special dividend:** quarterly revenue rose 80.7% to $316.3 million, enrollment 69.6%, and operating income 160.9%; FY revenue rose 68.3% to $1.043 billion. TAL also declared a $0.25-per-ADS special dividend and guided Q1 FY2018 revenue to $305.6–$309.4 million, 56.6%–58.5% growth. [Company release, published 2017-04-27](https://filecache.investorroom.com/mr5irasia_100tal/161/20170427%20Fourth%20Quarter%20Fiscal%20Year%202017%20Earnings%20Release.pdf).
- **2017-05-15 — convertible-note put completed:** holders put $206.5 million principal back to TAL; the company paid from cash and retained $23.5 million outstanding, removing a known refinancing event without issuing equity. [Company IR archive, published 2017-05-15](https://ir.tal.com/index.php?l=100&o=100&s=43).
- **2017-06-29 — FY2017 20-F filed:** the filing made the annual economics and risks public in audited form, including $1.043 billion revenue, $134.6 million operating income, $116.9 million net income, $1.829 billion assets, $680.9 million equity, and $450.1 million combined bond/long-term debt. [SEC filing, filed 2017-06-29](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1499620/000114420417034624/v464791_20f.htm).
- **2017-07-27 — Q1 FY2018 results:** revenue rose 65.0% to $321.9 million, enrollment 62.2%, operating income 63.9%, and net income 117.5%; management guided Q2 revenue to $435.8–$439.4 million, 60.8%–62.1% growth. Cost growth and promotion spending showed the capacity investment required to sustain the pace. [Company release, published 2017-07-27](https://filecache.investorroom.com/mr5irasia_100tal/162/20170727%20First%20Quarter%20Fiscal%20Year%202018%20Earnings%20Release.pdf).
- **2017-07-27 — ADS ratio change announced:** the change would have the same effect as a six-for-one ADS split, with five additional ADSs per ADS held at the August 8 record date and an anticipated August 16 effective date. It changed trading denomination, not enterprise value. [Company release, published 2017-07-27](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/tal-education-group-announces-ads-ratio-change-300495103.html).
- **2017-10-26 — Q2 FY2018 results broke the vertical move:** revenue rose 68.1% and enrollment 100.6%, but cost of revenue rose 85.7%, operating income only 32.6%, and net income attributable to TAL only 6.7%. The shares closed down 14.3% on the publication day, the year's largest dollar-volume session. [Company release, published 2017-10-26](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/tal-education-group-announces-unaudited-financial-results-for-the-second-fiscal-quarter-ended-august-31-2017-300543755.html); [Yicai report, published 2017-10-27](https://www.yicaiglobal.com/news/tutoring-service-tal-education-share-price-plunges-14-on-worse-than-expected-financial-results).

## Stage anatomy

- **Compressed prior — present.** The price was not deeply depressed, but the 42-day gain (25.79%) was far below the 63-day gain (49.64%), and first long-window highs arrived in early February before the March 21 acceleration. This was a stair-step base/advance, not a fresh rebound from distress.
- **Catalyst ladder — present.** January guidance, April's sharp growth-and-leverage reset, July's continuation and the ADS ratio change repeatedly extended the duration investors could underwrite.
- **Relative breakaway — present.** At March 21 TAL exceeded SPY by 21.26 pp over 21 days and 21.88 pp over 42 days; it also exceeded CQQQ by 20.16 pp over 21 days.
- **Liquidity confirmation — present.** `t0` dollar volume was 1.76 z21 and 1.69 z60, with the five-day/60-day ratio at 1.464; May earnings digestion and the October failure printed still larger volume.
- **Options convexity — unknown.** No 2017 option history exists in the repo, and no trade-level NBBO dataset supports a signed-flow claim.

## Fundamentals arc

The repo's PIT-gated `data/edgar/fundamentals_panel.parquet`, `data/edgar/statements.parquet`, `data/edgar/statements_quarterly.parquet`, and `data/edgar/eps_quarterly.parquet` contain zero TAL rows. Therefore the following arc comes from contemporaneous company releases/SEC filings and is not represented as a repo-derived standardized panel.

Before the breakaway, the January 19 release showed Q3 FY2017 revenue of $260.6 million (+39.0%) and enrollment +55.2%, but operating income down 11.4% and net income down 10.2%: demand was accelerating faster than near-term profit conversion. The April 27 release changed the model: Q4 revenue reached $316.3 million (+80.7%), gross profit $160.8 million (+88.0%), operating income $43.4 million (+160.9%), and enrollment +69.6%. FY2017 revenue was $1.043 billion (+68.3%), gross profit $521.5 million (+65.7%), operating income $134.6 million (+58.2%), and net income $116.9 million (+32.0%). Calculated operating margin was 13.7% in Q4 versus 9.5% a year earlier and 12.9% for FY2017 versus 13.8% in FY2016: the quarter showed leverage, while the full year showed expansion costs still absorbing it. [Company releases, published 2017-01-19 and 2017-04-27](https://media.corporate-ir.net/media_files/IROL/24/240640/TAL-Q3-FY2017-Earnings-Release.pdf), [Q4 release](https://filecache.investorroom.com/mr5irasia_100tal/161/20170427%20Fourth%20Quarter%20Fiscal%20Year%202017%20Earnings%20Release.pdf).

During the run, Q1 FY2018 revenue was $321.9 million (+65.0%), gross profit $152.3 million (+61.0%), and operating income $28.8 million (+63.9%); calculated operating margin was essentially flat at 8.9% versus 9.0%. Q2 revenue then reached $455.7 million (+68.1%) and enrollment doubled, but gross profit grew only 51.4%, cost of revenue 85.7%, and operating income 32.6%; calculated operating margin compressed to 15.0% from 19.0%. The runway was funded: at February 28, 2017 the audited balance sheet showed $1.829 billion of assets and $680.9 million of equity against $450.1 million of bond plus long-term debt, and the May note put was paid from cash. The trade-off was explicit—new facilities, teacher compensation, promotions and technology enlarged future capacity while eroding incremental margin in the October print. [Q1 release, published 2017-07-27](https://filecache.investorroom.com/mr5irasia_100tal/162/20170727%20First%20Quarter%20Fiscal%20Year%202018%20Earnings%20Release.pdf); [Q2 release, published 2017-10-26](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/tal-education-group-announces-unaudited-financial-results-for-the-second-fiscal-quarter-ended-august-31-2017-300543755.html); [20-F, filed 2017-06-29](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1499620/000114420417034624/v464791_20f.htm).

## Research echo

The repo has no analyst-target history. Searchable, publication-dated 2017 target-revision coverage was too incomplete to reconstruct a defensible consensus-versus-spot series, so consensus and individual target values are **null**. The observable echo is nevertheless clear: each earnings release gave a higher public operating base, while price repeatedly moved ahead of the next published model. At March 21, the stock had already gained 40.81% YTD before the April 27 report disclosed 80.7% quarterly growth; after that reset, it climbed to a 2017 peak of $36.10 on October 16, then fell to $27.73 when the October 26 release exposed weaker profit conversion. This is the “price escaped the published model” read: price capitalized continued 60%+ growth before each confirmation, then repriced when margins stopped matching the extrapolation. ## Hazards & false-positive checks

- **meme_squeeze — ruled_out:** no meme evidence is present; the advance unfolded across multiple earnings publications and alongside measurable revenue/enrollment growth. Historical short-interest coverage is absent, so this is not a claim about short positioning.
- **one_day_binary — ruled_out:** the 21-, 42-, 63- and 126-day ladders and repeated new highs rule out a single-session origin. The October reversal was one-day abrupt, but it ended rather than created the episode.
- **sector_beta — possible:** EDU gained 16.31% over the trailing 21 days and CQQQ was positive; China education/growth beta contributed. TAL's 20.16 pp excess over CQQQ and 4.80 pp over EDU still require name-specific explanation.
- **options_mirage — ruled_out:** the thesis does not rely on options; historical options evidence is null.
- **Current hazards (as of 2026-07-17):** extension is unknown without a current study; call walls are null because no TAL GEX snapshot exists; catalyst exhaustion is possible in the historical template because the October 2017 print showed that enrollment growth without commensurate margin conversion could terminate the vertical phase.
