# A 2003 — winner autopsy

## 1. Bottom line

Agilent's 2003 alpha was a depressed-cycle **loss-to-profit turnaround**, not a new-product binary and not life-science-sector beta: the May 19 quarter still showed a $72 million adjusted loss and no material end-market improvement, but semiconductor-test orders had reached a two-and-a-half-year high and management kept a concrete $1.45 billion quarterly breakeven-cost target ([published and filed 2003-05-19](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1090872/000089161803002644/f90389exv99w1.htm)); the June 2 tape then broke away while A was still behind XLV, SPY, XLK and QQQ YTD, making the move an early recovery catch-up rather than late-cycle extrapolation. The thesis acquired evidence in sequence: August reduced the adjusted operating loss to $27 million, put semiconductor-equipment orders at a three-year high and guided to fourth-quarter profitability ([published and filed 2003-08-18](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1090872/000089161803004477/f92573exv99w1.htm)); November delivered $122 million of adjusted operating income, $71 million of adjusted net income and about $170 million of free cash flow as orders rose 16% year over year ([published and filed 2003-11-17](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1090872/000119312503082720/dex991.htm)). That operating leverage, helped by semiconductor and automated-test recovery, sustained the stock to a roughly 100% gain from t0 by February 2004. The required XLV comparison overstates cohort alpha because Agilent said about 40% of revenue was tied to consumer electronics and still reported large test, automated-test and semiconductor businesses ([published 2003-11-17](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1090872/000119312503082720/dex991.htm)); nevertheless, forward excess remained large versus XLK, QQQ and SPY. The run ended when the published model caught up: guidance was raised sharply on January 27, 2004 ([published 2004-01-27](https://www.edn.com/agilent-ups-guidance/)), then adjusted EPS missed the February 17 First Call consensus by $0.02 and the shares fell after hours ([published 2004-02-17](https://www.thestreet.com/technology/agilent-sinks-on-soft-quarter-10144011)).
