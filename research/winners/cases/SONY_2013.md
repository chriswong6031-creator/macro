# SONY 2013 winner autopsy

## Bottom line

SONY's 2013 alpha was a layered rerating of a distressed conglomerate, not a single earnings surprise: a sharply weaker yen and highly visible property disposals reduced financing/failure risk; Kazuo Hirai's cost and television-loss work made a return to reported profit plausible; PS4 disclosure created a year-end product-cycle runway; and Third Point's May proposal put a public sum-of-the-parts frame around entertainment and imaging assets that the market had not been capitalizing. The tape confirms that distinction. The January breakaway occurred on only moderate one-day dollar-volume confirmation but after a rapid series of closing gains; the truly exceptional liquidity arrived on May 14, alongside the activist proposal, and the ADR ultimately peaked 71.71% above the onset close on July 23. FY2012's reported operating swing was real but low quality at the consolidated level—large asset gains did much of the work while Game and several electronics segments weakened—so the durable driver was falling left-tail risk plus newly legible optionality, amplified by Japan/yen beta, rather than broad organic margin acceleration.

## Catalyst ladder

- **2013-01-17 — balance-sheet repair:** Sony announced a $1.1 billion contract to dispose of its U.S. headquarters, expecting about $770 million net cash and a $685 million operating gain. This was public one week before t0 and directly reduced balance-sheet anxiety ([Sony, published 2013-01-17](https://www.sony.com/en/SonyInfo/IR/news/20130118E.pdf)).
- **2013-01-31 — product-cycle tease:** the official PlayStation Blog published “See the Future,” establishing February 20 as a public date for the next PlayStation reveal ([PlayStation Blog, published 2013-01-31](https://blog.playstation.com/2013/01/31/see-the-future/comment-page-5/)).
- **2013-02-07 — earnings/forecast bridge:** fiscal Q3 produced ¥46.4 billion of operating income versus a year-earlier loss while Sony retained its ¥130 billion full-year operating-profit forecast; the release also said weak electronics would be offset by Financial Services and planned asset disposals, making the mixed quality explicit ([Sony Q3 release, published 2013-02-07](https://www.sony.com/en/SonyInfo/IR/library/presen/er/12q3_sony.pdf)).
- **2013-02-20 — PS4 reveal:** Sony introduced PS4 for the 2013 holiday season, making the next console cycle concrete even before price and industrial design were public ([Sony Computer Entertainment, published 2013-02-20](https://sony.mediaroom.com/2013-02-20-Sony-Computer-Entertainment-Inc.-Introduces-PlayStation-4-PS4)).
- **2013-04-24 — profit forecast revision:** Sony doubled forecast net income for FY2012 from ¥20 billion to ¥40 billion, principally reflecting asset transactions and a weaker yen, rather than a broad electronics demand recovery ([Sony, published 2013-04-24](https://www.sony.com/en/SonyInfo/IR/library/presen/er/12revision_sony.pdf)).
- **2013-05-09 — return to reported profit:** Sony reported FY2012 revenue of ¥6,800.9 billion (+4.7%), operating income of ¥230.1 billion versus a ¥67.3 billion loss, and net income of ¥43.0 billion versus a ¥456.7 billion loss; it simultaneously disclosed that large asset gains drove much of the swing and that Game sales and operating income fell ([Sony FY2012 release, published 2013-05-09](https://www.sony.com/en/SonyInfo/IR/library/presen/er/12q4_sony.pdf)).
- **2013-05-14 — sum-of-the-parts catalyst:** Third Point publicly disclosed a 6.5% stake and proposed listing 15%–20% of Sony Entertainment, making hidden entertainment value and capital allocation a live public debate; the ADR rose 9.9% that day ([Los Angeles Times, published 2013-05-14](https://www.latimes.com/business/la-fi-ct-sony-stock-rises-third-point-entertainment-public-20130514-story.html)).
- **2013-06-11 — PS4 commercial terms:** Sony published a $399 U.S. price, $100 below Xbox One, no new used-disc restriction, and a developer base that had expanded from 126 in February to 505; this converted product interest into a more underwritable launch proposition ([Sony Interactive Entertainment, published 2013-06-11](https://sonyinteractive.com/en/press-releases/2013/playstation4-ps4-design-and-price-unveiled-available-at-399-in-us-and-at-399-in-europe/); [The Guardian, published 2013-06-11](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2013/jun/11/ps4-sony-price-used-games)).
- **2013-06-18 — activist escalation:** Third Point disclosed that its exposure had increased to 70 million shares, about 7%, and reiterated the entertainment-listing case, sustaining the public valuation debate ([AllThingsD, published 2013-06-18](https://allthingsd.com/20130618/loeb-raises-his-sony-stake-drive-for-entertainment-ipo/)).
- **2013-06-20 — board consideration remained open:** at the annual meeting, Hirai said Sony needed more time to consider the proposal, preserving optionality rather than resolving it immediately ([The Guardian/Reuters, published 2013-06-20](https://www.theguardian.com/business/2013/jun/20/sony-chief-time-daniel-loeb-entertainment)).

## Stage anatomy

- **Compressed prior — present.** The 126-session return into t0 was only 16.99% and lagged XLY by 1.01 pp, while the 21-session return accelerated to 23.70%; that is a long-window lag turning into a short-window breakaway. The ADR had also traded below $10 in late 2012 after years of losses ([TIME, published 2015-05-21, retrospective context](https://time.com/3892591/sony-turnaround/)).
- **Catalyst ladder — present.** Headquarters monetization, the PS4 reveal, forecast revision, FY results, activist value-unlocking proposal, and E3 pricing arrived sequentially from January through June.
- **Relative breakaway — present.** At t0, excess return was +19.06 pp over 21 sessions and +26.22 pp over 42 sessions versus SPY; the same-date 63- and 126-session closing-high tests were true.
- **Liquidity confirmation — present.** T0 z21 was only 0.36, but five-day/60-day dollar volume was 2.59; on May 14 turnover reached $733.50 million and z60 7.33, confirming a second, much stronger re-underwriting wave.

## Fundamentals arc

Repo PIT coverage is **null** for SONY: `data/edgar/fundamentals_panel.parquet`, `data/edgar/statements.parquet`, `data/edgar/statements_quarterly.parquet`, and `data/edgar/eps_quarterly.parquet` all contain zero rows. Accordingly, the arc below uses only Sony's dated releases and does not pretend the later-local tables covered the episode.

Before the run, FY2011 revenue was ¥6,493.2 billion, operating loss ¥67.3 billion (about -1.04% operating margin), and net loss ¥456.7 billion. By FY2012, published 2013-05-09, reported revenue had risen 4.7% to ¥6,800.9 billion, operating income reached ¥230.1 billion (about 3.38% margin), and net income reached ¥43.0 billion ([Sony FY2012 release, published 2013-05-09](https://www.sony.com/en/SonyInfo/IR/library/presen/er/12q4_sony.pdf)). The quality caveat is central: Sony identified a ¥122.2 billion M3 gain, ¥65.5 billion U.S.-headquarters gain, ¥42.3 billion Osaki-property gain, and ¥9.1 billion chemical-business gain within operating income. Organic evidence was mixed. Television losses improved by ¥137.9 billion through panel/expense reductions; Devices swung to ¥43.9 billion operating profit and image-sensor demand increased; Pictures revenue rose 11.4% and operating income 40.1%. Conversely, Game revenue fell 12.2% and operating income fell 94.1% to ¥1.7 billion, IP&S profit fell 92.3%, and Mobile recorded a ¥97.2 billion operating loss. The runway was therefore PS4 commercialization, smartphone mix, image sensors, continued TV cost repair, yen translation, and possible conglomerate simplification—not an already-demonstrated groupwide organic margin trend.

## Research echo

The repo has no analyst-target history, and accessible dated sources did not yield a reliable contemporaneous consensus target series; consensus-vs-spot and numeric target revisions are therefore **null**. The qualitative echo did change. On 2013-02-07, the retained ¥130 billion operating-profit forecast was above the cited ¥119 billion consensus of 19 analysts, but reporting still emphasized weak televisions and devices ([Reuters via Business Standard, published 2013-02-07](https://www.business-standard.com/article/reuters/sony-returns-to-black-in-q3-on-asset-product-sales-113020700501_1.html)). On 2013-05-17, CLSA estimated 28% separation value and Macquarie judged roughly 30% potential value accretion from an entertainment separation plausible—below Third Point's 60% framing but a public move toward sum-of-the-parts analysis ([Washington Post/Bloomberg, published 2013-05-17](https://www.washingtonpost.com/business/sony-urged-to-cut-entertainment-stake/2013/05/17/ecb26d86-be6a-11e2-97d4-a479289a31f9_story.html)). Ahead of E3, published analyst estimates clustered around a sub-$400 PS4; Sony then printed $399 while undercutting Xbox One by $100 ([GameSpot, published 2013-06-07](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/ps4-will-debut-below-400-says-analyst/1100-6409442/); [Sony, published 2013-06-11](https://sonyinteractive.com/en/press-releases/2013/playstation4-ps4-design-and-price-unveiled-available-at-399-in-us-and-at-399-in-europe/)). “Price escaped the published model” is best read as **possible but not numerically demonstrable**: by May the tape was repricing break-up optionality and a console-cycle outcome that conventional earnings targets did not yet isolate, but the missing target series prevents a precise spot-versus-consensus claim.
