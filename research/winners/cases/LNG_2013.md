# LNG 2013 — winner autopsy

## 1. Bottom line

LNG's 2013 alpha was a probability-weighted re-underwriting of future liquefaction cash flows, not an improvement in the operating income statement and not an Energy-sector beta trade. The mosaic was cumulative: Cheniere repeatedly funded Sabine Pass, issued notices to proceed, advanced construction, filled long-dated fixed-fee capacity, and then made the Corpus Christi follow-on project more concrete through its first customer and fixed-price engineering contracts. By the 2013-10-15 public presentation, trains 1–4 were fully contracted and under construction and approximately 20 mtpa of take-or-pay-style agreements represented an estimated $2.9 billion of annual fixed fees for 20 years ([company presentation filed 2013-10-15](https://lngir.cheniere.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000003570-13-000198/october2013corporatepres.htm)); subsequent Pertamina and Bechtel announcements expanded the option set ([published 2013-12-04](https://lngir.cheniere.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/25/cheniere-and-pertamina-sign-20-year-lng-sale-and-purchase), [published 2013-12-09](https://lngir.cheniere.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/115/cheniere-enters-into-lump-sum-turnkey-contracts-with)). Revenue was nearly flat and losses widened, so the price was discounting a funded, contracted infrastructure asset years before startup. The tape separated from both XLE and SPY, then sustained that separation through April 2014 as Corpus contracted its first full train.

## 4. Stage anatomy

- **Compressed prior — absent.** LNG had risen 91.48% YTD and 27.47% over the trailing 42 sessions by `t0`. There was only a short digestion before the simultaneous 63/126/252-day high; treating this as a long quiet base would misdescribe the tape.
- **Catalyst ladder — present.** Financing, construction notices, regulatory orders, contract coverage, Corpus customer commitments, and fixed-price engineering contracts reduced different project-risk terms over many months.
- **Relative breakaway — present.** At `t0`, trailing 42-session excess was +25.60pp versus XLE and +26.95pp versus SPY. From `t0` through 126 sessions, excess expanded to +48.50pp and +47.24pp, respectively.
- **Liquidity confirmation — present.** The initial session was modest at +0.23 z21, but the five-to-60-day dollar-volume ratio was 1.54 and 2013-10-16 printed +3.51 z21; multiple later sessions exceeded $300 million.
- **Options convexity — unknown.** The repo's per-ticker options history begins in June 2026. There is no 2013 trade-level NBBO record with which to infer opening direction, customer side, or dealer hedging.

## 5. Fundamentals arc

Before the breakaway, the public income statement looked like a loss-making import-terminal operator funding a multiyear build. The 2012 Form 10-K reported revenue of $266.2 million, operating loss of $75.8 million (−28.5% operating margin), net loss attributable to common holders of $332.8 million, and cash used in operations of $107.8 million; year-end cash was $201.7 million, restricted cash was $793.2 million, and long-term debt was $2.17 billion. [Form 10-K filed 2013-02-22](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/3570/000000357013000036/cei2012form10k.htm).

During the run, revenue did not explain the equity move. The 2013-11-08 disclosure showed Q3 revenue of $67.7 million versus $66.0 million a year earlier, an operating loss of $45.9 million versus $54.5 million, and a $100.8 million attributable net loss. For the first nine months, revenue was $200.8 million versus $198.8 million while operating loss widened to $249.6 million from $59.9 million; cash was $374.2 million, restricted cash $1.41 billion, and debt $5.57 billion. [Quarterly release published 2013-11-08](https://lngir.cheniere.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/113/cheniere-energy-reports-third-quarter-2013-results); [Form 10-Q filed 2013-11-08](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/3570/000000357013000213/cei2013form10q3rdqtr.htm).

The full-year filing made the divergence starker: 2013 revenue was $267.2 million, only 0.4% above 2012, while operating loss widened to $329.0 million (−123.1% margin) and attributable net loss reached $507.9 million. General and administrative expense included $252.1 million of share-based compensation. Cash had risen to $960.8 million and restricted cash to $1.63 billion, but debt had also risen to $6.58 billion. The runway was project-finance and capital-markets runway, not self-funded operating cash flow. [Results release published 2014-02-21](https://lngir.cheniere.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/117/cheniere-energy-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2013); [Form 10-K filed 2014-02-21](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/3570/000000357014000031/cei2013form10k.htm).

Gross margin is **null** for this comparison: the reviewed filing presentation and local normalized stores do not supply a consistent gross-profit series for the terminal business. Local PIT coverage is also **null**—`data/edgar/fundamentals_panel.parquet`, `data/edgar/statements.parquet`, `data/edgar/statements_quarterly.parquet`, and `data/edgar/eps_quarterly.parquet` contain zero LNG/CIK 3570 rows. The fundamentals above therefore come from publication-dated filings, not from backfilled local panel values. The business runway evidence was the construction percentages, financing closings, and 20-year capacity contracts; first LNG production was still future execution.

## 9. Hazards & false-positive checks

- **meme_squeeze — ruled_out.** A 2013-11-30 secondary report put short interest at 13.5 million shares, or 7.3% of float ([published 2013-11-30](https://www.forbes.com/sites/greatspeculations/2013/11/30/bearish-sentiment-adds-to-bullish-case-for-cheniere-energy/)); covering could have amplified sessions, but the multi-month funding, contracting, construction, and regulatory ladder supplies an independent mechanism.
- **one_day_binary — ruled_out.** There was no single binary publication at `t0`; excess return and dollar-volume waves persisted through the December Corpus events and April 2014 Endesa contracts.
- **sector_beta — ruled_out.** LNG exceeded XLE by 48.50pp and SPY by 47.24pp over 126 sessions from `t0`; XLE itself returned only 7.09%.
- **options_mirage — possible.** Historical options coverage is absent, so options amplification cannot be measured or excluded. It is not needed to explain the company-specific catalyst ladder.

Episode hazards were `extended_after_vertical_move`, `construction_execution`, `regulatory_non_fta_pending`, `leveraged_project_financing`, `holding_company_complexity`, `contract_conditions_and_counterparty`, and `management_compensation_dilution`. The stock was already up 91.48% YTD at `t0`, construction would consume years and billions of dollars, some contracts retained project/regulatory conditions, and 2013 debt and share-based compensation both increased materially.

Current hazards are context only. At 2026-07-16, LNG was +10.10% over 21 sessions but 12.57% below its prior 252-session closing high (`data/yahoo/LNG.parquet`), so acute extension is not evident from that simple test.
