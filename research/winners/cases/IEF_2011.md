# IEF 2011 — winner autopsy

## 1. Bottom line

IEF did not produce security-specific alpha in 2011; it produced a liquid, intermediate-duration expression of a broad Treasury/risk-off regime that the episode census measured against SPY because IEF had no sector mapping. By the mechanical 2011-08-08 onset, IEF was +21.58 percentage points versus SPY over 21 trading days, but it lagged the available Treasury-sector proxy TLT by 5.93 points over the same window and by 4.31–8.60 points across every requested trailing horizon. The move was built by a mosaic of slowing U.S. growth, debt-ceiling and rating stress, euro-area contagion, and then explicit Fed duration support—most importantly the [2011-08-09 mid-2013 rate pledge](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20110809a.htm) and [2011-09-21 maturity-extension program](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20110921a.htm)—not by issuer fundamentals. Liquidity and new-high evidence were real, and the absolute-price path qualified as a winner-track `clean_hold`: IEF remained positive after t0 and through year-end. The central caveat is benchmark mismatch rather than episode failure: longer-duration TLT captured the same macro mechanism with greater convexity, while SPY's later recovery erased IEF's fallback-benchmark alpha.

## 3. Catalyst ladder

The local filing check is null by instrument design: `data/edgar/material_8k_events.parquet` and `data/edgar/earnings_8k_dates.parquet` contain zero IEF rows. The public ladder is therefore macro/policy. Dates below are publication dates, not later retrospective labels.

- 2011-03-15 — macro_policy — The FOMC continued reinvestment and its already-announced $600 billion of longer-term Treasury purchases through the second quarter. This coincided with IEF's first 63-day closing high. Durability: medium. [Federal Reserve publication, 2011-03-15](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20110315a.htm).
- 2011-06-22 — macro_policy — The FOMC said recovery and labor conditions were weaker than expected, completed the $600 billion purchase program at month-end, but retained principal reinvestment and an extended-period low-rate stance. The growth read was supportive; the end of net purchases was a counterweight. Durability: medium. [Federal Reserve publication, 2011-06-22](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20110622a.htm).
- 2011-07-08 — other — BLS reported only 18,000 June payroll additions, 9.2% unemployment, and downward revisions to April and May payrolls. This strengthened the slower-growth/lower-yield prior. Durability: medium. [BLS publication, 2011-07-08](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/empsit_07082011.htm).
- 2011-07-14 — other — S&P placed the U.S. AAA/A-1+ sovereign ratings on CreditWatch negative, citing at least a one-in-two likelihood of a long-term downgrade within 90 days amid debt-ceiling stalemate. The signal increased system stress even though Treasury-credit risk and Treasury safe-haven demand pulled in opposite directions. Durability: medium. [S&P release distributed by PR Newswire, 2011-07-14 08:55 ET](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/united-states-of-america-aaaa-1-ratings-placed-on-creditwatch-negative-on-rising-risk-of-policy-stalemate-125607738.html).
- 2011-07-15 — macro_policy — Treasury deployed its final extraordinary measure and repeated that borrowing authority would be exhausted on August 2 absent legislation. Durability: medium. [U.S. Treasury publication, 2011-07-15](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/tg1243).
- 2011-07-29 — other — BEA's advance release put second-quarter real GDP growth at 1.3% and revised first-quarter growth to 0.4%; the annual revision also lowered the reported depth of the 2008–09 contraction. This was the cleanest pre-t0 re-underwriting of growth. Durability: high. [BEA publication, 2011-07-29 08:30 ET](https://www.bea.gov/news/2011/gross-domestic-product-2nd-quarter-2011-advance-estimate).
- 2011-08-03 — macro_policy — Treasury confirmed enactment of the Budget Control Act, an immediate $400 billion debt-limit increase, and expected modest reductions in nominal coupon issuance. Default-tail risk fell, while lower expected issuance reduced prospective supply. Durability: medium. [U.S. Treasury publication, 2011-08-03](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/tg1267).
- 2011-08-05 — other — Federal banking agencies confirmed that S&P lowered the long-term U.S. government rating from AAA to AA+, while keeping Treasury securities' regulatory risk weights unchanged. The downgrade intensified broad risk aversion without impairing regulated-bank treatment of Treasuries. Durability: medium. [OCC/Federal banking agencies publication, 2011-08-05](https://www.occ.treas.gov/news-issuances/news-releases/2011/nr-ia-2011-104.html).
- 2011-08-07 — macro_policy — The ECB said it would actively implement its Securities Markets Programme amid exceptional euro-area market circumstances. This was a public marker of sovereign-contagion stress immediately before t0. Durability: medium. [ECB publication, 2011-08-07](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2011/html/pr110807.en.html).
- 2011-08-09 — macro_policy — The FOMC said growth was considerably slower, downside risks had increased, and exceptionally low policy rates were likely through at least mid-2013. This converted vague duration support into date-based forward guidance. Durability: high. [Federal Reserve publication, 2011-08-09](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20110809a.htm).
- 2011-09-21 — macro_policy — The FOMC announced $400 billion of purchases in Treasuries with 6–30 years remaining, funded by equal sales of securities with three years or less remaining, explicitly seeking downward pressure on longer rates. This directly supported IEF but favored longer-duration TLT more. Durability: high. [Federal Reserve publication, 2011-09-21](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20110921a.htm).
- 2011-10-26 — macro_policy — Euro-area leaders announced a proposed 50% nominal discount on privately held Greek debt, up to €100 billion of additional program financing, bank recapitalization, and an operational EFSF framework. This was a counter-catalyst: partial risk repair reduced the scarcity premium of U.S. Treasuries relative to equities. Durability: medium. [Euro Summit publication, 2011-10-26](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/media/41526/20111026-eurosummit-statement.pdf).
- 2011-11-30 — macro_policy — Six central banks cut the price of existing U.S. dollar liquidity swaps by 50 basis points and added temporary bilateral arrangements. This further attacked the funding-stress channel behind safe-haven demand and helped exhaust IEF's SPY-relative edge. Durability: medium. [Federal Reserve publication, 2011-11-30 08:00 ET](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20111130a.htm).

## 4. Stage anatomy

- Compressed prior — absent. IEF printed its first 63-day closing high on 2011-03-15, its first 126-day high on 2011-05-26, and its first 252-day high on 2011-07-19; the setup was an advancing duration trend, not a coiled base.
- Catalyst ladder — present. Weak labor/GDP releases, debt-limit and rating stress, euro-area contagion, date-based Fed forward guidance, and the maturity-extension program successively lowered the market's growth/rate path.
- Relative breakaway — absent on the proper cohort test. It was present only against fallback SPY (+21.58pp/21d at t0); IEF lagged TLT by 5.93pp/21d and by 4.31–8.60pp over every requested trailing horizon.
- Liquidity confirmation — present. T0 dollar volume was $525.63 million, z=+3.35/21d and +4.78/60d, with a 2.22 five-day/60-day ratio; several later high-turnover sessions confirm a liquid macro repricing, not who initiated it.
- Options convexity — unknown. No 2011 options history exists, and all three current IEF snapshot surfaces requested are absent.

Revenue, gross margin, operating margin, EPS, cash burn, and runway are all `null`; inventing an issuer-style arc would confuse the fund wrapper with the bonds it holds. The economically relevant arc is the local yield path. The seven-year/10-year constant-maturity yields fell from 2.74%/3.36% on 2011-01-03 to 2.09%/2.82% on the 2011-07-29 GDP release, 1.75%/2.40% at t0, and 1.24%/1.72% on 2011-09-22. They ended 2011 at 1.35%/1.89%. That is the ETF's real “fundamental” re-underwriting: lower expected policy rates, lower term yields, and a global demand shock for Treasury duration. Duration, convexity, portfolio turnover, assets under management, and fund flows for 2011 are not available in the specified repo surfaces, so those fields remain `null`. Yield sources: `data/fred/DGS7.parquet` and `data/fred/DGS10.parquet`.

## 7. Research echo

There is no analyst-target series for IEF, and the repo has no analyst-target data. The closest point-in-time “consensus versus spot” analogue is the Philadelphia Fed's Survey of Professional Forecasters for the 10-year Treasury yield. On [2011-05-13](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/-/media/frbp/assets/surveys-and-data/survey-of-professional-forecasters/2011/spfq211.pdf?la=en), the median forecast was 3.7% for 2011 Q3 and 3.8% for Q4. By t0, the local DGS10 spot was already 2.40%, meaning price had escaped the published rate model before the most visible catalyst day. The next survey, published [2011-08-12](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/-/media/frbp/assets/surveys-and-data/survey-of-professional-forecasters/2011/spfq311.pdf?la=en), cut Q3/Q4 medians to 2.9%/3.1%, yet spot was 2.24% that day. The [2011-11-14](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/-/media/frbp/assets/surveys-and-data/survey-of-professional-forecasters/2011/spfq411.pdf?la=en) release finally cut the Q4 median to 2.3%. The notable revision sequence was therefore 3.7%→2.9% for Q3 and 3.8%→3.1%→2.3% for Q4: research chased a yield collapse the tape had already priced. This echo supports a macro rerating, not IEF-specific research alpha.

## 9. Hazards & false-positive checks

| Check | Verdict | Note |
|---|---|---|
| meme_squeeze | ruled_out | Broad Treasury ETF, multi-month high ladder, and official macro catalysts; no issuer-float or social-coordination mechanism is needed. |
| one_day_binary | ruled_out | First 63d/126d/252d highs arrived across March, May, and July, and the macro ladder continued through September. |
| sector_beta | likely | This is the core false positive: the detector fell back to SPY, while IEF lagged TLT at every requested trailing horizon and every measured forward horizon. |
| options_mirage | possible | Historical and current IEF options surfaces are absent, so options cannot be credited or fully excluded; no flow direction is inferred. |

Episode-end hazards were extension after a vertical duration move, catalyst exhaustion after the 2011-09-21 maturity-extension announcement, equity/risk normalization, and benchmark mismatch. At the 2011-09-22 peak, the 10-year yield was 1.72% and IEF had gained 5.06% from t0, but its excess versus TLT was already -11.94pp. The later Euro Summit and coordinated swap actions reduced systemic-stress demand, while SPY recovery turned IEF's year-end excess negative. Call-wall risk is `null` because the requested options surfaces do not cover IEF. The machine lesson is not “large volume plus new high failed”; it is “large volume plus new high against the wrong benchmark identified sector beta as alpha.”
