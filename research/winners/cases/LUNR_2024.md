# LUNR 2024 — failed-breakaway autopsy

## 1. Bottom line

LUNR's 2024 alpha came from a sequence, not a single moonshot headline: a depressed post-SPAC base and scarce cash were first re-underwritten by an actual lunar landing, then by a $30 million lunar-vehicle study, a $116.9 million fourth lunar-delivery award, and—most importantly—the September Near Space Network award that made Intuitive Machines legible as a communications-and-infrastructure platform rather than a one-product lander company. Revenue, backlog and cash then rose enough to give that story operating support, while December direct-to-Earth task orders and the approaching IM-2 mission extended the reflexive loop. The failure was equally mosaic: investors capitalized multiyear IDIQ ceilings faster than funded task orders and margins, liquidity at the December 24 detector onset was only marginally fresh, the company used equity and warrant exercises to finance its runway, and price soon exceeded the published target range. The January 24, 2025 peak was therefore carrying both recurring-services hope and a binary demand for another clean landing; IM-2's March 7 early end on its side removed that proof point, exposing lumpy low-margin economics beneath a well-funded but still execution-dependent platform. [NASA NSN award, published 2024-09-17](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-selects-lunar-relay-contractor-for-near-space-network-services/); [Q3 results, published 2024-11-14](https://investors.intuitivemachines.com/news-releases/news-release-details/intuitive-machines-reports-third-quarter-2024-results); [NASA IM-2 conclusion, published 2025-03-07](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-receives-some-data-before-intuitive-machines-ends-lunar-mission/).

**FAILED BREAKAWAY:** this case is `case_type: failed_breakaway`; the run did not sustain, and the failure—not the temporary alpha—is the object of the study.
