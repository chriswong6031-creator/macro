# GDX 2016 winner autopsy

## 1. Bottom line

GDX's 2016 move was principally a gold-miner cycle upswing, not durable ETF-specific alpha: a deeply impaired mining complex met a rapid repricing of the Fed path, negative-rate policy abroad, safe-haven demand, and then Brexit ([Fed, published 2016-03-16](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomcprojtabl20160316.htm); [BOJ, published 2016-01-29](https://www.boj.or.jp/en/mopo/mpmdeci/mpr_2016/k160129a.pdf); [Bank of England, published 2016-06-24](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/news/2016/june/statement-from-the-governor-of-the-boe-following-the-eu-referendum-result)); gold rose, ETF demand made the allocation shift observable ([World Gold Council, published 2016-05-11](https://www.gold.org/news-and-events/press-releases/gold-demand-record-levels-q1-2016-21-investors-surged-etfs)), and prior cost and debt work at major miners converted a modest bullion move into a much larger change in expected equity cash flow ([Barrick, published 2016-02-17](https://www.barrick.com/English/news/news-details/2016/Barrick-Reports-2015-Full-Year-and-Fourth-Quarter-Results/default.aspx)). The tape confirms genuine alpha against SPY and strong amplification over GLD, but not a lasting edge over the closest traded miner cohort: GDX was ahead of GDXJ at the 2016-02-04 breakaway, then GDXJ's smaller-company beta overtook it by the August crest and through year-end. The run failed to sustain after the macro ladder became crowded and then reversed—stronger U.S. data, a firmer dollar and yields, post-election reflation expectations ([Reuters, published 2016-11-14](https://www.business-standard.com/article/reuters/gold-hits-5-1-2-month-low-on-strong-dollar-treasury-yields-116111401566_1.html)), and the December rate increase ([Fed, published 2016-12-14](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20161214a.htm)) compressed gold's valuation support—leaving GDX down 33.01% from its 2016-08-02 peak to year-end.

## 4. Stage anatomy

1. **Compressed prior — present.** GDX fell 24.71% in 2015 and was 80.57% below its 2011-09-08 adjusted closing high at the 2016-01-19 trough. This was a violent V-bottom rather than a quiet base: t0 was already 17.71% above year-end and 4.09% above the 200-session average, yet still 27.86% below its prior 252-session high.

2. **Catalyst ladder — present.** Fed-path repricing ([published 2016-03-16](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomcprojtabl20160316.htm)), BOJ negative rates ([published 2016-01-29](https://www.boj.or.jp/en/mopo/mpmdeci/mpr_2016/k160129a.pdf)), miner cost/debt disclosures ([published 2016-02-17](https://www.barrick.com/English/news/news-details/2016/Barrick-Reports-2015-Full-Year-and-Fourth-Quarter-Results/default.aspx)), record investment demand ([published 2016-05-11](https://www.gold.org/news-and-events/press-releases/gold-demand-record-levels-q1-2016-21-investors-surged-etfs)), weak U.S. payrolls ([published 2016-06-03](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/empsit_06032016.htm)), and Brexit ([published 2016-06-24](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/news/2016/june/statement-from-the-governor-of-the-boe-following-the-eu-referendum-result)) supplied distinct public rungs from January through July; stronger data, yields, and the dollar then formed a visible reversal ladder ([Reuters, published 2016-10-04](https://in.investing.com/news/commodities-news/precious-gold-slips-for-sixth-day-after-upbeat-u.s.-data-75394)).

3. **Relative breakaway — present.** At t0, GDX led GDXJ by 10.35 points and SPY by 20.04 points over 21 sessions, and it progressed from 63- to 126- to 252-session highs by 2016-03-16. The breakaway was real, although later GDXJ outperformance shows it was miner-cycle breadth rather than durable large-miner selection alpha.

4. **Liquidity confirmation — present.** T0 dollar volume was z21 2.75 and z60 3.77, the five-session/60-session ratio was 1.59, and elevated volume recurred on 2016-02-05, 2016-02-11, and later macro dates. The year's largest sessions were two-sided, so the evidence confirms participation, not direction.

5. **Options convexity — unknown.** There is no 2016 per-ticker options archive and no current GDX options snapshot in the requested files. Historical gamma, implied volatility, open interest, and call-wall effects are null rather than inferred.
