# XME — 2009 winner autopsy

## 1. Bottom line

XME's spring-2009 move was a high-beta re-pricing of a distressed metals-and-mining basket, not an issuer-specific earnings surprise: the March low followed a 77.0% drawdown from the prior 252-session high, then Chinese infrastructure stimulus began to show up in an expansionary March manufacturing survey ([Xinhua/China Daily, 2009-04-03](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/bizchina/2009-04/03/content_7646488.htm)), copper inventories/imports and metals prices confirmed the demand impulse ([World Bank, May 2009](https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/214541462307047642-0050022016/original/CMO2009May.pdf)), and coordinated Fed/G20 measures lowered the market's depression and funding-risk discount ([Federal Reserve, 2009-03-18](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20090318a.htm); [G20/IMF, 2009-04-02](https://www.imf.org/external/np/sec/pr/2009/pdf/g20_040209.pdf)). The local tape confirms a violent and liquid escape—XME gained 53.0% in the 42 sessions through 2009-04-30 and another 27.6% over the next 21—but the attribution is ruler-dependent: excess return was 28.24 percentage points versus SPY yet only 10.11 points versus broad-materials XLB over the trailing 42 sessions. Thus the large index alpha held, while much of the apparent edge versus a sector cohort was cyclical metals beta plus operating leverage to commodity prices; the later clean hold versus SPY says the re-underwriting persisted, not that XME possessed a company-level moat.

## 3. Catalyst ladder

- **2008-11-11 — macro policy:** China publicly detailed a RMB4 trillion domestic-demand package emphasizing infrastructure and looser credit, establishing the latent metals-demand mechanism months before the breakaway ([Chinese government/consular publication, 2008-11-11](https://chicago.china-consulate.gov.cn/eng/lmbf/ywzn/sw/200811/t20081111_5496569.htm)). Durability: high.
- **2009-03-18 — macro policy:** the FOMC announced up to $300bn of longer-term Treasury purchases, another $750bn of agency MBS purchases, and extended near-zero rates, materially changing the discount-rate and credit-tail backdrop ([Federal Reserve, 2009-03-18](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20090318a.htm)). Durability: high.
- **2009-04-02 — macro policy:** G20 leaders published a $1.1tn support program for credit, growth, jobs, and trade finance, reinforcing a coordinated global reflation regime ([IMF-hosted leaders' statement, 2009-04-02](https://www.imf.org/external/np/sec/pr/2009/pdf/g20_040209.pdf)). Durability: high.
- **2009-04-03 — other/data:** China's March manufacturing PMI was published at 52.4, its first reading above 50 since July 2008; new orders and output were also above 50, turning stimulus from promise into observable activity ([Xinhua/China Daily, 2009-04-03](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/bizchina/2009-04/03/content_7646488.htm)). Durability: medium.
- **2009-04-15 — other/commodity confirmation:** contemporary reporting said copper reached a six-month high as LME-tracked inventories fell, while a mining equity received an analyst upgrade; this was a public price/inventory confirmation rather than a new policy action ([The Independent, 2009-04-15](https://www.independent.co.uk/news/business/sharewatch/market-report-copperbottomed-rally-lifts-mining-sector-1668886.html)). Durability: medium.
- **2009-05-07 — macro/financial-system:** U.S. authorities published bank stress-test results that Treasury described as less acute than some expected, reducing a major left-tail risk for cyclical capital ([U.S. Treasury, 2009-05-07](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/tg123)). Durability: medium.
- **2009-05-28 — research echo:** S&P raised 2009 assumptions for copper, nickel, and gold after the tape had already moved, while retaining caution on weak end demand and high inventories ([S&P Global Ratings, 2009-05-28](https://www.spglobal.com/ratings/en/regulatory/article/-/view/sourceId/5419977)). Durability: medium.

Local filing anchors are null: `data/edgar/material_8k_events.parquet` and `data/edgar/earnings_8k_dates.parquet` contain zero XME rows, as expected for an ETF rather than an operating issuer.

## 4. Stage anatomy

- **Compressed prior — present.** XME closed 2009-03-09 about 77.0% below its prior 252-session high; this was crisis compression/distress, not a quiet low-volatility base (`data/yahoo/XME.parquet`).
- **Catalyst ladder — present.** China stimulus preceded the run; Fed balance-sheet expansion, G20 coordination, Chinese PMI, falling copper inventories, and stress-test disclosure successively reduced demand and funding tail risks.
- **Relative breakaway — present.** At t0, trailing excess was +16.32 pp/21d and +28.24 pp/42d versus SPY with a 63-day high; versus XLB it was only +10.68/+10.11 pp, so the breakaway was much clearer against the index.
- **Liquidity confirmation — present.** T0 dollar volume was +1.69 z on 21 days and +1.51 z on 60 days; five-day mean dollar volume was 1.425× the 60-day mean (`data/yahoo/XME.parquet`).
- **Options convexity — unknown.** No 2009 options history exists locally; per-ticker history begins only in 2026-06 and no current XME surface is present.

## 7. Research echo

There is no repo analyst-target dataset, and an ETF has no single-company consensus target. The closest contemporaneous published-model test was cautious: on 2009-04-24 the World Bank warned that selected metals prices could recede once Chinese restocking ended even as metal consumption remained weak ([World Bank Global Monthly, 2009-04-24](https://pubdocs.worldbank.org/en/427871474398941504/Global-Monthly-Apr09.pdf)). By 2009-05-28—after XME had already risen 53.0% in 42 sessions through t0 and continued higher—S&P raised its 2009 copper, nickel, and gold assumptions, but still described industrial end demand as depressed and inventories as high ([S&P Global Ratings, 2009-05-28](https://www.spglobal.com/ratings/en/regulatory/article/-/view/sourceId/5419977)). The “price escaped the published model” read is therefore literal but qualified: market prices and mining equities front-ran upward estimate revisions, while published forecasters continued to distinguish Chinese stockpiling/cutbacks from durable final demand. Notable ETF target revisions: null.

## 9. Hazards & false-positive checks

- **meme_squeeze — ruled_out:** the episode predates modern social-meme mechanics; the broad macro ladder, XLB participation, multi-month continuation, and clean-hold label fit cyclical repricing better. Historical short-interest evidence is null, so this does not rule out ordinary short covering.
- **one_day_binary — ruled_out:** the move accumulated across multiple policy/data releases; XME gained 88.5% from 2009-03-09 through 2009-06-01 and the largest dollar-volume days had mixed signs.
- **sector_beta — likely:** XLB gained 42.84% over the same trailing 42 sessions, leaving XME only +10.11 pp ahead; XME lagged XLB over 5, 63, and YTD windows at t0.
- **options_mirage — ruled_out:** no options evidence is used. Historical and current XME options surfaces are null.

Current hazards as of the last local close, 2026-07-17: **extension is not present** by simple moving-average geometry—XME is 14.2% below its 50-day average, 10.8% below its 200-day average, and 25.9% below its trailing 252-day high (`data/yahoo/XME.parquet`). **Call walls: null** because no XME GEX snapshot exists. **Catalyst exhaustion: unknown** because this case researched the 2009 ladder, not a complete 2026 public-event set. The standing historical hazard remains commodity/inventory reflexivity: Chinese stockpiling and producer cutbacks can lift spot prices before end demand is durable.
