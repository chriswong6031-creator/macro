# INSP 2020 winner autopsy

## Bottom line

INSP's 2020 alpha was a reopening-and-access re-underwriting of an already fast-growing, high-gross-margin medical-device platform, not simply an earnings surprise or health-care beta. COVID stopped elective implants in March and drove Q2 revenue down 32% year over year, but the company kept adding commercial capacity, raised $124.7 million, completed nationwide Medicare coverage, expanded commercial covered lives, and accumulated approved patients. The November 2 publication then showed that this latent demand had converted: Q3 revenue rebounded 72% year over year to $35.8 million, gross margin reached 85.5%, 42 centers opened, prior-authorization submissions and approvals rose sharply, Humana added coverage, and a Category I CPT code had been approved. The market therefore repriced both the near-term recovery and the durability of the distribution/reimbursement flywheel. The November 3 gap held and extended, producing large excess return versus both XLV and SPY; the evidence does not support sector beta, a meme episode, or a one-session binary as the primary mechanism.

## Catalyst ladder

- **2020-02-25 — growth, reimbursement, and distribution plan.** The filed release reported Q4 2019 revenue growth of 62%, guided 2020 revenue to $115-$119 million (40%-45% growth), raised the planned pace of center and territory additions, and said all seven Medicare contractors had issued draft coverage determinations. Publication/filing date: 2020-02-25. [SEC-filed release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1609550/000160955020000009/insp2019-q4pressreleas.htm).
- **2020-04-13 — COVID interruption quantified, but backlog and access kept building.** The company withdrew guidance after substantially all procedures were postponed beginning in the second week of March, while reporting 929 prior-authorization submissions, 761 approvals, 28 new centers, five final Medicare coverage determinations, and approximately $142 million of liquidity. Publication/filing date: 2020-04-13. [SEC-filed release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1609550/000110465920045950/tm2012413d4_ex99-1.htm).
- **2020-05-05 — reopening optionality and runway.** The Q1 release reported 31% revenue growth despite the March shutdown, the first resumed implants in Germany and the United States, an FDA age-range expansion to ages 18-21, a $124.7 million equity raise, and Medicare coverage live in 37 states. Publication/filing date: 2020-05-05. [SEC-filed release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1609550/000160955020000021/insp2020-q1pressreleas.htm).
- **2020-08-04 — procedure recovery and nationwide Medicare coverage.** The Q2 release disclosed a 32% revenue decline but steady procedure recovery through May and June, all seven Medicare contractor policies effective, Cigna coverage, 182 million commercially covered lives, a patient app launch, and reinstated 2020 revenue guidance of $88-$92 million. Publication/filing date: 2020-08-04. [SEC-filed release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1609550/000160955020000034/insp2020-q2pressreleas.htm).
- **2020-11-02 — decisive re-underwriting event.** Q3 revenue grew 72% to $35.8 million, gross margin reached 85.5%, 42 centers opened, commercial covered lives reached 207 million, prior-authorization submissions/approvals reached 1,233/1,039, Humana added coverage, and a Category I CPT code had been approved. Management raised full-year revenue guidance to $110-$112 million from $88-$92 million. Publication/filing date: 2020-11-02. [SEC-filed release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1609550/000160955020000046/insp2020-q3pressreleas.htm).

## Stage anatomy

- **Compressed prior — present.** From 2020-10-05 through 2020-11-02, INSP closed between $119.35 and $133.79 and declined 6.11%; the 21-session coefficient of variation of closes was 3.01%. The longer 2020 structure was not compressed: the stock had already more than doubled YTD.
- **Catalyst ladder — present.** Medicare and commercial access, center/territory capacity, approved-patient backlog, reopening, and CPT progress accumulated before the Q3 revenue/guidance reset.
- **Relative breakaway — present.** At t0, INSP was ahead of XLV by 25.00 pp over 21 sessions and 35.21 pp over 42 sessions, while setting 63-/126-/252-session closing highs.
- **Liquidity confirmation — present.** T0 dollar volume was $243.96 million, z21 4.31, and the five-to-60-session dollar-volume ratio was 1.89.
- **Options convexity — unknown.** No episode-era options history exists; current repo per-ticker options history begins only in 2026-06.

## Fundamentals arc

The PIT-usable picture before t0 comes primarily from `data/edgar/statements_quarterly.parquet`, gated by its `filed` date, and the dated earnings 8-Ks in `data/edgar/earnings_8k_dates.parquet`. Q4 2019 revenue was $26.9 million (+62% year over year) with 84.2% gross margin; Q1 2020 revenue was $21.3 million (+31%) with 84.6% gross margin; Q2 fell to $12.2 million (-32%) while gross margin held at 84.0%; and Q3 rebounded to $35.8 million (+72%) with 85.5% gross margin. Quarterly operating loss moved from $16.4 million in Q1 to $22.8 million in Q2 and narrowed to $9.9 million in Q3. Thus the run combined accelerating revenue and stable-to-improving unit economics, but not operating profitability.

Runway was materially extended: the April offering produced $124.7 million net proceeds, and the Q3 publication reported $234.6 million of cash, cash equivalents, and investments at 2020-09-30. `data/edgar/statements_quarterly.parquet` contains $178.7 million cash for Q3 because its concept coverage differs from the release's broader cash-plus-investments measure; the release is used for the broader liquidity figure. The same quarterly parquet shows 2020 Q1/Q2/Q3 revenue of $21.347/$12.183/$35.842 million, gross profit of $18.050/$10.229/$30.631 million, and operating loss of $16.440/$22.814/$9.892 million.

`data/edgar/fundamentals_panel.parquet` is annual and PIT-gates FY2019 to `asof_date: 2020-04-29`; it reports FY2019 revenue of $82.05 million, gross profit of $68.41 million (83.37% margin), operating loss of $34.90 million, cash flow from operations of -$32.85 million, and net loss of $33.24 million. FY2020 is gated only to 2021-04-30 and therefore cannot be used at t0; ex post it reports $115.38 million revenue, $97.76 million gross profit (84.73% margin), operating loss of $56.21 million, and CFO of -$53.05 million. `data/edgar/statements.parquet` has an `as_of` ingestion timestamp in 2026 rather than contemporaneous PIT availability, so it is used only as a present-day cross-check. `data/edgar/eps_quarterly.parquet` is not usable for the 2020 episode: its Q2 and Q3 2020 rows have `asof_date` values in 2021. These gaps are printed rather than backfilled with hindsight.

## Hazards & false-positive checks

- **meme_squeeze — ruled_out.** The public ladder, multi-horizon relative strength, and durable post-gap hold explain the episode without a social-trading mechanism; historical short-interest data are unavailable, so this is not a claim that short positioning was zero.
- **one_day_binary — ruled_out.** The 19.1% opening gap held above both the prior close and the gap-day open at 3/5/10 sessions; the stock added 20.62% over the following 21 sessions.
- **sector_beta — ruled_out.** INSP exceeded XLV by 25.00 pp over 21 sessions, 35.21 pp over 42, and 111.31 pp YTD at t0; SPY comparisons were similarly large.
- **options_mirage — possible.** Options history is structurally absent, so convexity cannot be tested. It cannot be credited as the mechanism or completely excluded.
- **Current hazards (2026-07-18 snapshot) — extension: null; call walls: null; catalyst exhaustion: possible but unmeasured.** Current price-extension and options-wall evidence was not requested from a live external feed and the local INSP options surfaces are absent. The 2020 catalyst ladder was substantially reflected after the Q3 reset, creating a contemporaneous exhaustion hazard even though the tape continued higher.
