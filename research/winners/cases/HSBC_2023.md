# HSBC 2023 winner autopsy

## 1. Bottom line

HSBC's 2023 alpha was a layered re-underwriting of earnings quality and distributable capital, not simply "China reopened" or "rates rose." By late 2022, higher policy rates were already lifting net interest income across all three businesses, the Canada sale had made roughly $10.1bn of cash consideration and an estimated $5.7bn pre-tax gain visible, and the bank had promised a return to quarterly dividends. The January 5 breakaway came when a public analyst revision joined those pieces—China/Hong Kong reopening plus capital returns—into a more aggressive valuation model; the tape then received harder confirmation from 2022 results, a 50% payout framework, the first quarterly dividend since 2019, and successive repurchases. The bank-stress selloff in March showed the limits: the largest dollar-volume days were defensive, and HSBC never became a clean one-way compounder. Still, cheap balance-sheet optionality became observable cash returns while Asia reopening reduced the discount, allowing the ADR to finish 2023 about 28.7% above the January 5 close even though the census records only +4.5 percentage points of 252-day excess versus SPY.

## 3. Catalyst ladder

Dates below are publication dates. The local SEC event stores were checked first: [`data/edgar/material_8k_events.parquet`](../../../data/edgar/material_8k_events.parquet) and [`data/edgar/earnings_8k_dates.parquet`](../../../data/edgar/earnings_8k_dates.parquet) contain **zero HSBC rows**, consistent with a foreign private issuer whose material reporting is not captured by these 8-K surfaces.

1. **2022-10-25 — 3Q22 results upgraded the earnings-power model.** HSBC reported 28% adjusted-revenue growth to $14.3bn, a 38 bp year-over-year rise in net interest margin to 1.57%, and raised 2022 net-interest-income guidance to about $32bn while retaining at least $36bn for 2023. This made rate leverage and eventual higher distributions explicit. [HSBC 3Q22 release, published 2022-10-25](https://www.hsbc.com/news-and-views/news/media-releases/2022/hsbc-holdings-plc-3q-2022-earnings-release).
2. **2022-11-29 — Canada disposal crystallized excess-capital optionality.** RBC agreed to pay CA$13.5bn (US$10.1bn); HSBC estimated a $5.7bn pre-tax gain and about 130 bp of CET1 uplift. [HSBC sale announcement, published 2022-11-29](https://www.hsbc.com/news-and-views/news/media-releases/2022/hsbc-agrees-to-sell-its-business-in-canada-to-royal-bank-of-canada).
3. **2023-01-05 — the public model caught up to the mosaic.** Jefferies raised its HSBC target from 574p to 770p and moved its rating positively, citing underappreciated China/Hong Kong reopening and prospective capital returns. The ADR gained 3.30% on $153.6m of dollar volume that day and printed its first 126-day closing high. [Onvista/dpa-AFX broker note report, published 2023-01-05](https://www.onvista.de/news/2023/01-05-analyse-flash-jefferies-hebt-hsbc-auf-buy-ziel-770-pence-10-26082877).
4. **2023-02-21 — annual results converted the setup into a payout framework.** Reported revenue rose 4% to $51.7bn; management established a 50% dividend payout ratio for 2023–24, restored quarterly dividends from 1Q23, prioritized a possible $0.21 special dividend after the Canada sale, and brought repurchase consideration forward to May. [HSBC FY22 release, published 2023-02-21](https://www.hsbc.com/news-and-views/news/media-releases/2023/hsbc-holdings-plc-annual-results-2022).
5. **2023-03-13 — bank stress created a low-cost franchise acquisition, not only contagion risk.** HSBC UK acquired SVB UK for £1, including approximately £5.5bn of loans, £6.7bn of deposits, and expected tangible equity near £1.4bn. [HSBC acquisition release, published 2023-03-13](https://www.hsbc.com/news-and-views/news/media-releases/2023/hsbc-acquires-silicon-valley-bank-uk-limited).
6. **2023-05-02 — 1Q23 supplied the hard distribution confirmation.** HSBC reported annualized RoTE of 19.3% excluding strategic transactions, announced its first quarterly dividend since 2019 at $0.10 per share, and announced up to $2bn of repurchases. Reported revenue was $20.2bn, though $3.6bn of notable gains inflated the quarter. [HSBC 1Q23 release, published 2023-05-02](https://www.hsbc.com/news-and-views/news/media-releases/2023/hsbc-holdings-plc-1q-2023-earnings-release).
7. **2023-05-05 — the breakup overhang receded.** More than 80% of votes cast followed the board's recommendation against shareholder resolutions seeking an Asia separation and fixed annual dividends, allowing the market to focus on execution of the integrated model. [HSBC AGM report, published 2023-05-05](https://www.hsbc.com/news-and-views/news/hsbc-news-archive/hsbc-shareholders-reject-break-up-calls).
8. **2023-08-01 — interim results extended the cash-return ladder but marked the run peak.** First-half profit before tax rose to $21.7bn, annualized RoTE reached 22.4% (18.5% excluding the France/SVB UK effects), 2023 NII guidance rose above $35bn, and another repurchase of up to $2bn was announced. The ADR set its 2023 adjusted-close high that day. [HSBC interim release, published 2023-08-01](https://www.hsbc.com/news-and-views/news/media-releases/2023/hsbc-holdings-plc-interim-results-2023).
9. **2023-10-30 — a final 2023 distribution step did not restart the vertical phase.** HSBC announced a third $0.10 interim dividend and a further repurchase of up to $3bn, taking announced 2023 repurchases to as much as $7bn; 3Q net interest income was $9.2bn versus $8.0bn a year earlier. [HSBC 3Q23 release, published 2023-10-30](https://www.hsbc.com/news-and-views/news/media-releases/2023/hsbc-holdings-plc-3q-2023-earnings-release).

## 4. Stage anatomy

- **Compressed prior — present, but late.** HSBC fell 31.8% from its February 2022 adjusted-close high to the October low. Compression emerged only immediately before onset: 21-day annualized realized volatility fell to 17.8% by January 4 from 40.0% on October 24; the 63-day price range was still a broad 30.7%, so this was not a textbook tight base.
- **Catalyst ladder — present.** Higher NII, the Canada sale, reopening, an explicit payout framework, the SVB UK acquisition, quarterly dividends, and repeated repurchases successively reduced uncertainty.
- **Relative breakaway — present.** At January 5, HSBC had +15.67 pp 21-day and +27.67 pp 42-day excess versus SPY and made a 126-day closing high; versus XLF the corresponding excess was +11.66 pp and +25.81 pp.
- **Liquidity confirmation — present, episodic.** Onset dollar volume was 3.21 standard deviations above its inclusive trailing 21-day mean, but the five-day/60-day ratio was only 0.99. Later peak-volume days were primarily March stress, not persistent upside participation.
- **Options convexity — unknown.** No 2023 options history exists in the repo; no inference is made from a 2026-era absence.

## 5. Fundamentals arc

The requested PIT-gated local surfaces—[`data/edgar/fundamentals_panel.parquet`](../../../data/edgar/fundamentals_panel.parquet), [`data/edgar/statements.parquet`](../../../data/edgar/statements.parquet), [`data/edgar/statements_quarterly.parquet`](../../../data/edgar/statements_quarterly.parquet), and [`data/edgar/eps_quarterly.parquet`](../../../data/edgar/eps_quarterly.parquet)—contain **zero HSBC rows**. Local revenue, margin, cash-flow, and EPS history is therefore null; the following arc uses dated company disclosures and is not a substitute for those missing PIT panels.

Before onset, 3Q22 supplied the cleanest available rate-cycle comparison: NIM rose 38 bp year over year to 1.57%, adjusted revenue rose 28% to $14.3bn, and adjusted profit before tax rose $1.0bn to $6.5bn. Reported profit was depressed by a $2.4bn France-sale impairment and credit costs, showing why the equity still carried a discount despite better core earnings. [HSBC 3Q22 release, published 2022-10-25](https://www.hsbc.com/news-and-views/news/media-releases/2022/hsbc-holdings-plc-3q-2022-earnings-release).

During the run, 1Q23 reported revenue reached $20.2bn and annualized RoTE was 27.4%, but the cleaner RoTE excluding the SVB UK provisional gain and France impairment reversal was 19.3%. That distinction matters: the re-underwriting rested on stronger recurring NII and distribution capacity, while $3.6bn of transaction/accounting gains made the headline quarter look better than the run-rate. [HSBC 1Q23 release, published 2023-05-02](https://www.hsbc.com/news-and-views/news/media-releases/2023/hsbc-holdings-plc-1q-2023-earnings-release).

By 1H23, revenue was $36.9bn versus $24.5bn in 1H22, NII was $18.3bn versus $13.4bn, and annualized RoTE was 22.4% versus 10.6%; excluding the France/SVB effects, RoTE was 18.5%. Management raised 2023 NII guidance above $35bn but disclosed falling underlying deposits and weak wholesale loan demand, limiting runway as deposit migration and eventual rate normalization threatened margins. [HSBC interim results, published 2023-08-01](https://www.hsbc.com/investors/results-and-announcements/all-reporting/interim-results-2023-quick-read).

The full-year outcome, published after the episode, confirms what the market had begun to price: 2023 revenue rose 30% to $66.1bn, NII rose $5.4bn, reported RoTE was 14.6%, and dividends plus announced repurchases became substantial. This is outcome evidence, not a pre-onset input. [HSBC FY23 release, published 2024-02-21](https://www.hsbc.com/news-and-views/news/media-releases/2024/hsbc-holdings-plc-annual-results-2023).

## 7. Research echo

At onset, the research echo was a catch-up signal: Jefferies' January 5 target increase from 574p to 770p was a 34% model revision tied to reopening and capital returns, arriving only after HSBC had already gained 30.4% over 42 trading days. The price had escaped the old published model; the revision explained the new one. [Onvista/dpa-AFX report, published 2023-01-05](https://www.onvista.de/news/2023/01-05-analyse-flash-jefferies-hebt-hsbc-auf-buy-ziel-770-pence-10-26082877).

The echo then broadened rather than leading. Barclays raised its target to 840p from 780p after FY22 results on February 22, while retaining its positive stance. [Morningstar/Alliance News broker roundup, published 2023-02-22](https://www.morningstar.co.uk/uk/news/AN_1677059466265330500/london-broker-ratings-credit-suisse-raises-wpp%3B-db-cuts-ihg-to-hold.aspx). After 1Q23, at least nine target upgrades were reported; Goldman Sachs moved to HK$81 and Morgan Stanley to HK$67.50. [South China Morning Post, published 2023-05-04](https://www.scmp.com/business/china-business/article/3219248/analysts-charmed-hsbcs-earnings-push-stock-price-outlook-most-bullish-july-2019). This supports a lagging-consensus interpretation: targets rose as the payout mechanism became explicit, but external consensus-history coverage is incomplete and the repo has no analyst-target data.

Current hazards are **extension after a vertical move** (the ADR was already +30.4% over 42 days at onset), **catalyst exhaustion** (successive distributions were increasingly expected by August), **rate/deposit sensitivity** (deposit migration and eventual rate normalization could compress NII), and **China/Hong Kong credit and macro exposure**. The March 2023 dollar-volume spike also demonstrates that a global-bank risk event could temporarily dominate the company-specific thesis.
