# CIFR 2024 — failed breakaway autopsy

## Bottom line

CIFR's November breakaway was a mosaic of three mutually reinforcing—but differently durable—inputs: a pre-existing data-center expansion/HPC-optionality story, a fresh Q3 update that enlarged the power-site pipeline, and the 2024 election's immediate repricing of bitcoin and crypto equities. The tape confirmed that capital arrived: on 2024-11-06 CIFR gained 31.0%, printed its largest 2024 dollar-volume day, cleared 126- and 252-day closing highs, and led SPY by 72.89 percentage points over the preceding 21 sessions. What failed was the bridge from optionality to current economics. The 2024-10-31 filing showed Q3 mining revenue down 20.5% year over year, a $91.4 million operating loss distorted but not wholly explained by a derivative mark, $58.5 million of nine-month operating cash use, $227.6 million of equipment deposits plus property purchases, and $225.2 million of stock issuance. The election shock therefore accelerated a high-beta bitcoin miner whose published expansion model still required time and external capital; it did not create a contemporaneous earnings inflection. The result was a one-session gap that held initially, peaked three sessions later, then delivered -17.17 points of 63-day excess return and -54.07 points at 126 days before a separate 2025 recovery.

**This is a FAILED breakaway case (`case_type: failed_breakaway`); the run did not sustain.**

## Stage anatomy

- **Compressed prior — absent.** CIFR was not emerging from a quiet, tight base: it had already triggered failed/blow-off census episodes on 2024-03-27 and 2024-07-15, and the 42-day return at the November onset was +136.27% (`data/research/winner_episodes.parquet`).
- **Catalyst ladder — present.** Site acquisitions and pipeline expansion culminated in the 2024-10-31 update, followed six days later by the election/bitcoin shock; the sequence let capital re-underwrite both long-duration power optionality and near-term bitcoin beta.
- **Relative breakaway — present.** CIFR led SPY by 72.89 pp over 21 sessions and 127.77 pp over 42 sessions at t0, while closing at a 252-day high (`data/research/winner_episodes.parquet`; `data/baskets/ohlcv/CIFR.parquet`).
- **Liquidity confirmation — present.** T0 was the year's largest dollar-volume day through that date; z21 was 3.97 and the 5d/60d ratio 2.03 (`data/research/winner_episodes.parquet`).
- **Options convexity — unknown.** Per-ticker options history begins in 2026-06. No 2024 contract-level positioning or trade-level NBBO exists locally, so historical convexity cannot be inferred.

## Fundamentals arc

The four requested structured surfaces—`data/edgar/fundamentals_panel.parquet`, `data/edgar/statements.parquet`, `data/edgar/statements_quarterly.parquet`, and `data/edgar/eps_quarterly.parquet`—contain **zero CIFR rows**. Accordingly, PIT-gated repo fundamentals are null; the following contemporaneous figures come directly from the 2024-10-31 SEC exhibit and are dated to that publication.

Before/during the run, the arc was mixed rather than a clean earnings inflection. Q3 2024 bitcoin-mining revenue was $24.1 million versus $30.3 million in Q3 2023 (-20.5%), while nine-month revenue was $109.0 million versus $83.4 million (+30.7%). Revenue less cost of revenue was $9.0 million in Q3 2024 (37.5% of revenue) versus $17.3 million (57.1%) a year earlier; this is a simple contribution calculation, not a company-defined margin. GAAP operating loss widened to $91.4 million, including a $48.5 million adverse derivative fair-value change and $28.6 million depreciation/amortization; adjusted loss was reported as roughly $3 million. Publication date: 2024-10-31. [SEC earnings exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1819989/000181998924000026/cifr-20241031xex991.htm).

Runway existed, but it depended on monetizable bitcoin and capital markets. At 2024-09-30 Cipher had $25.3 million cash, $14.4 million restricted cash, and $95.5 million bitcoin, against $59.1 million current liabilities. Nine-month operating cash use was $58.5 million; equipment deposits and property purchases consumed $135.3 million and $92.4 million, respectively. Financing supplied $225.2 million from common-stock issuance, while shares outstanding rose to 347.8 million from 291.0 million at 2023 year-end (+19.5%). Publication date: 2024-10-31. [SEC earnings exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1819989/000181998924000026/cifr-20241031xex991.htm). This capital-intensity/dilution bridge is the central fundamental distinction from a durable self-funded winner.

## Hazards & false-positive checks

- **meme_squeeze — possible.** A 31% t0 jump, repeated earlier blow-off episodes, and a retail-accessible crypto narrative are squeeze-like, but local short-interest history is unavailable and daily volume cannot establish a short squeeze.
- **one_day_binary — possible.** The election was a discrete macro binary and produced the largest dollar-volume day, but the pre-t0 21- and 42-day gains and October catalyst ladder show the entire move was not confined to one day.
- **sector_beta — likely.** The decisive t0 shock lifted bitcoin and crypto equities broadly; CIFR's operating exposure made it a levered expression. A miner ETF series is unavailable locally, preventing exact cohort attribution.
- **options_mirage — ruled_out.** There is no 2024 options history, so options cannot be credited as a causal confirmation. Absence of data is not evidence of absence of options activity.

Current hazards are temporally separate from the 2024 case. As of 2026-07-17/18 the stock has IV30 around 123%, so **extension/volatility** is likely; a **call wall** is null in `site/gex/CIFR.json`; **catalyst exhaustion** is unknown; and the $18 largest-OI strike plus $17 put wall are current positioning landmarks, not historical explanations. The 2024-specific hazards were catalyst exhaustion after the election, high bitcoin beta, capital intensity, dilution dependence, and a price/model gap unsupported by current earnings.
