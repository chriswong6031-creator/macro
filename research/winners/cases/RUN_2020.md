# RUN 2020 — failed-breakaway autopsy

## 1. Bottom line

Sunrun's 2020 alpha was a real but increasingly reflexive re-underwriting of scale, policy optionality, and very long-dated contracted cash flows: pandemic-era digital selling and grid-resilience demand stabilized the operating story; the [Vivint Solar agreement, published 2020-07-06](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2020/07/07/2058261/0/en/sunrun-announces-definitive-agreement-to-acquire-vivint-solar-for-an-enterprise-value-of-3-2-billion.html), reframed RUN as a roughly 500,000-customer, 3-GW home-energy platform with $90 million of projected annual synergies; and the [Biden clean-energy plan, published 2020-07-14](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/biden-campaign-press-release-the-biden-plan-build-modern-sustainable-infrastructure-and), [FERC Order 2222, published 2020-09-17](https://www.ferc.gov/news-events/news/ferc-opens-wholesale-markets-distributed-resources-landmark-action-breaks-down), and [California's zero-emission vehicle order, published 2020-09-23](https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/news/governor-newsom-announces-california-will-phase-out-gasoline-powered-cars-drastically-reduce/printable/print), made the stock a liquid proxy for a policy regime change. The failure was in the timing and economics of the 2020-09-28 breakaway, not in the absence of a story: by t0 RUN was already +421.22% YTD and had advanced 633.74% in 126 sessions, while the latest results still showed revenue down 11%, deployed megawatts down 24%, creation cost per watt up 12%, and negative $30 million of cash generation ([Q2 release, published 2020-08-10](https://investors.sunrun.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/211/sunrun-reports-second-quarter-2020-financial-results)). [Merger completion, published 2020-10-08](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2020/10/08/2105614/0/en/sunrun-completes-acquisition-of-vivint-solar-to-accelerate-clean-energy-adoption-and-enhance-customer-value.html), removed an uncertainty but did not create another earnings inflection; liquidity then climaxed around the October top and on large down days. A January policy-sweep rebound briefly carried RUN to $96.50, but the [results published 2021-02-25](https://investors.sunrun.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/230/sunrun-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2020-financial) exposed the mismatch between strong subscriber-value guidance and GAAP economics—Q4 revenue rose 31%, but cost of revenue rose 50%, total operating expenses rose 97%, and common net loss was $169.3 million—just as rising-rate pressure was [reported 2021-03-03](https://www.fool.com/investing/2021/03/03/why-sunruns-shares-plunged-135-today/) against a model whose own earning-asset values discounted 20- to 25-year cash flows. The move was therefore merger scale plus policy beta plus low-rate duration; it failed because the detector fired after those expectations had been capitalized, without a compressed prior or a second operating surprise large enough to support the valuation.

**Format-contract status: this is a FAILED breakaway case (`case_type: failed_breakaway`). The run did NOT sustain.**

## 4. Stage anatomy

- **Compressed prior — absent.** The March 20 close was $8.36; t0 was $71.98, +761%. RUN had already printed a post-trough 63-day high on June 8 and 126/252-day highs on July 7. The September trigger was a vertical continuation after two earlier 2020 detector onsets, not a quiet base.
- **Catalyst ladder — present.** Operating adaptation, VPP proof, Vivint scale, strategic capital, project partnerships, merger clearance, and policy changes repeatedly expanded the model. The defect was maturity: most major rungs preceded t0, and the merger close culminated rather than renewed the narrative.
- **Relative breakaway — present, then failed.** At t0 trailing excess was +38.00 pp/21d and +89.16 pp/42d versus XLI, with a simultaneous 63/126/252-day high. Forward excess reversed to -23.47 pp at 21d and -47.29 pp at 126d.
- **Liquidity confirmation — present but late/distributive.** T0 barely met the z21 threshold at 1.00 and the five-day/60-day ratio was only 1.26. Turnover exceeded $1 billion near the October and January peaks, while $704-$710 million also printed on three large October decline days.
- **Options convexity — unknown.** No episode-timely options surface exists. Current per-ticker history begins in 2026-06, and RUN is absent even from the current local GEX/state surfaces, so no historical convexity claim is supportable.

Before t0, the arc was recovery in demand and financing access, not a GAAP margin breakout. Q1 gross margin was approximately 19.4% and Q2 approximately 18.8%; Q2 revenue fell 11% year over year and unlevered NPV per watt fell to $0.51 from $1.11, while cash generation was negative $30 million ([Q1, published 2020-05-06](https://investors.sunrun.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/201/sunrun-reports-first-quarter-2020-financial-results); [Q2, published 2020-08-10](https://investors.sunrun.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/211/sunrun-reports-second-quarter-2020-financial-results)). The positive counterweight was runway: Q2 ended with $354.2 million of cash and restricted cash and financing capacity for about 200 MW beyond deployed projects. This was not an imminent liquidity failure, but it was a capital-dependent model with negative operating cash flow.

During the failed run, Q3 improved sequentially: revenue returned to $209.8 million, gross margin rose to approximately 27.1%, deployed MW rose 40% from Q2, cash and restricted cash reached $381.5 million, and expected project-finance capacity exceeded 275 MW. Yet revenue was still 3% below the prior year, operating margin remained -29.7%, and the economics disclosed on 2020-11-05 did not match a stock that had recently been more than six times its 2019 year-end close ([Q3 release, published 2020-11-05](https://investors.sunrun.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/220/sunrun-reports-third-quarter-2020-financial-results)).

The preliminary publication on 2021-01-25 confirmed the scale step but also quantified its balance-sheet load: approximately 171.6 MW deployed in Q4, more than 550,000 customers, and $708 million of cash including restricted cash sat against $5.1 billion of total debt and financing obligations; estimated Vivint transaction costs were $60-$70 million ([Sunrun release, published 2021-01-25](https://investors.sunrun.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/224/sunrun-announces-preliminary-fourth-quarter-2020-results)). That was ample liquidity and much larger earning assets, not a clean transition to self-funded economics.

The 2021-02-25 publication made both sides explicit. Pro-forma customer count reached 550,078, annual recurring revenue from subscribers was $668 million, Q4 net subscriber value was $9,051, 2021 installed-capacity growth guidance was 20%-25%, and available/term-sheet project capital covered more than 500 MW. But Q4 gross margin fell to approximately 14.6%, common net loss was $169.3 million, and FY2020 operating cash flow was -$318.0 million. Management also changed the earning-asset discount rate from 6% to 5%, explicitly reflecting the lower cost-of-capital environment; that accounting-model choice made the equity's sensitivity to the subsequent rate reversal unusually visible ([FY2020 release, published 2021-02-25](https://investors.sunrun.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/230/sunrun-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2020-financial)). The failure was therefore not “no runway”; it was abundant project funding and genuine scale paired with weak GAAP margins, negative operating cash, integration complexity, and a valuation dependent on distant cash flows.
