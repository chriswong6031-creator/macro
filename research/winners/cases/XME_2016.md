# XME 2016 winner autopsy

## Bottom line

XME's 2016 alpha was the equity convexity of a metals-and-mining cycle turn, not an ETF-specific fundamental surprise: after a 50.53% loss in 2015 and an 82.80% decline from its 2011 high to the January 19, 2016 trough, China simultaneously revived old-economy demand and constrained steel/coal supply ([State Council, published 2016-01-24](https://english.www.gov.cn/premier/news/2016/01/24/content_281475277746575.htm)), U.S. trade cases improved the domestic steel price umbrella ([USITC, published 2016-06-22](https://www.usitc.gov/press_room/news_release/2016/er0622ll621.htm)), easier global policy and Brexit supported the precious-metals sleeve ([Bank of England, published 2016-06-24](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/news/2016/june/statement-from-the-governor-of-the-boe-following-the-eu-referendum-result)), and the U.S. election added an infrastructure/trade-policy leg ([Reuters, published 2016-11-09](https://www.business-standard.com/article/reuters/copper-hits-15-month-high-after-trump-wins-white-house-116110901045_1.html)). Composition did the rest. XLB was 74.0% chemicals at December 31, 2015 ([SEC filing, published 2016-02-29](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1064641/000119312516485700/d219563dnq.htm)), whereas XME was 49.9% steel at June 30, with the balance spread across high-beta ore, coal, aluminum, silver and gold miners ([XME annual report, filed 2016-09-08](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1064642/000119312516705345/d172314dncsr.htm)). That mismatch turned a broad commodity reflation into 89.24 percentage points of full-year excess return over XLB and 94.04 points over SPY. The move sustained—XME was still 68.23% above t0 at year-end—but it was sector beta in a particularly convex wrapper, and the largest liquidity days were two-sided. Price anticipated the operating turn; July–August filings later showed much better margins on flat-to-mixed revenue in representative steel, ore and precious-metals constituents.

## Stage anatomy

- **Compressed prior — present.** XME lost 50.53% in 2015, fell another 19.93% from year-end to January 19, and reached that trough 82.80% below its April 2011 close. All preceding 63, 126 and 252 sessions were below the 200-day average at t0; the fund's later SEC report showed three- and five-year cumulative returns of -22.41% and -61.84% through June 30 ([filed 2016-09-08](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1064642/000119312516705345/d172314dncsr.htm)).

- **Catalyst ladder — present.** Urbanization/capacity policy, the February 29 RRR cut, U.S. steel duties, better property/investment data, coal operating-day restrictions, and later constituent results were separate public rungs, not one recycled headline. The State Council's [January 24 publication](https://english.www.gov.cn/premier/news/2016/01/24/content_281475277746575.htm) and [February 4 publication](https://app.www.gov.cn/govdata/gov/201602/04/368412/article.html) both preceded t0.

- **Relative breakaway — present.** At t0 XME was +32.78% over 21 sessions and +30.01 points versus SPY; it was +23.09 points versus XLB. The 63-day high arrived February 29, followed by a 126-day high March 7 and a 252-day high July 1.

- **Liquidity confirmation — present.** T0 dollar-volume z21 was +1.68 and z60 +2.90; z21 rose to +3.56 on March 4 and remained +2.81 on March 7. Confirmation was two-sided, because March 8 and May 9 were among the largest dollar-volume days and both fell more than 7%.

- **Options convexity — unknown.** Per-ticker options history starts in 2026-06, and all three requested current XME options locations are null. No 2016 convexity claim is supportable.

## Research echo

There is no analyst-target history for ETFs in the repo, and a conventional company target is a poor fit for a rules-based basket. The public echo is therefore commodity/sector-model versus spot:

- **Consensus versus spot.** Goldman raised its 2016 iron-ore forecast 19% to $47/tonne on May 6 while spot was already $59.50; only one month earlier it had expected $35 by year-end ([Reuters, published 2016-05-06](https://au.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/goldman-lifts-2016-iron-ore-price-outlook%2C-cites-firmer-china-steel-29691)). The revision recognized firmer Chinese steel demand but still modeled mean reversion.

- **Notable revisions.** The same May 6 publication lifted Q2–Q4 iron-ore estimates 14%–47% and the 2016 metallurgical-coal estimate 10% to $82/tonne, while leaving 2017–2018 iron ore at $35. The direction changed; the durability assumption did not.

- **Price escaped the published model.** The IMF's retrospective January 25, 2017 monthly report recorded iron ore at $80/tonne on December 30, up 82.6% in 2016 ([IMF, published 2017-01-25](https://www.imf.org/external/np/res/commod/pdf/monthly/012617.pdf)). That post-episode source is audit evidence only, not a catalyst. It shows the actual year-end price was 70% above Goldman's revised 2016 average and 129% above its earlier year-end view.

- **Skepticism persisted near the peak.** Wood Mackenzie said on December 5 that the market had rallied on the president-elect's infrastructure promise but estimated the overall steel-demand impact would be small ([published 2016-12-05](https://www.woodmac.com/reports/metals-can-president-elect-trumps-infrastructure-plan-make-steel-and-iron-ore-great-again-43981462/)). Moody's said on December 14 that recent base-metals gains were driven by Chinese stimulus more than underlying supply/demand and expected the met-coal spike above $200/tonne to retreat toward $90–$120 over 18 months ([Engineering News report, published 2016-12-14](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/global-base-metals-us-coal-and-steel-prices-stabilising-moodys-2016-12-14)).

- **The tape forced public recognition.** ETF.com ranked XME the second-best nonleveraged/noninverse ETF of 2016 at +114.49% through December 21, attributing the move to China production cuts/demand plus the post-election U.S. infrastructure/trade narrative ([published 2016-12-23](https://www.etf.com/sections/news/top-performing-etfs-2016)). This was late recognition, after most of the repricing.

The echo is consistent with a winner whose price escaped the published model: forecasters repeatedly raised near-term inputs but kept expecting the cycle to fade, while the basket's operating leverage and policy-driven scarcity made the equity response much larger than the commodity revision.
