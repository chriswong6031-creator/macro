# FUTU 2024 winner autopsy

## 1. Bottom line

FUTU produced large 2024 alpha because two things stacked: the company had already shown that its brokerage/wealth platform could convert better Hong Kong and overseas market activity into paying-client growth, client-asset growth, margin balances, and high operating margins, and then China abruptly changed the discount rate applied to the whole Greater China risk complex in late September 2024. The move was not a single earnings surprise. It was a macro-policy re-underwriting of Chinese equities hitting a high-beta brokerage whose own Q2 2024 report, published August 20, 2024, had already raised 2024 new-paying-client guidance and reported 69.0% YoY trading-volume growth. The large alpha was real, but the vertical part was not fully sustained: from the September 25, 2024 mechanical t0 FUTU was +49.4% in five trading days, then +16.7% by 21 trading days as the October 8, 2024 China-stimulus disappointment took air out of the first leg.

## 5. Fundamentals arc

Repo coverage gap: `data/edgar/fundamentals_panel.parquet`, `data/edgar/statements.parquet`, `data/edgar/statements_quarterly.parquet`, and `data/edgar/eps_quarterly.parquet` have zero FUTU rows in this checkout, so no PIT-gated local fundamentals panel is available. Futu is a foreign private issuer; public fundamentals for 2024 come from Form 6-K/20-F and company IR releases rather than the local U.S.-issuer statement panels.

Before the run, the public company arc was not purely cyclical hope. On March 14, 2024, Futu reported FY 2023 revenue of HK$10.0b, +31.4% YoY, gross profit +28.0% YoY, net income +46.2% YoY, and operating margin improving to 50.0% from 46.9%. The same release reported Q4 2023 total trading volume down 12.5% YoY, which is why the early-2024 thesis still needed market-activity reacceleration.

Q1 2024, published May 28, was mixed: paying clients rose 23.5% YoY and client assets rose 11.2% YoY, but revenue rose only 3.7% YoY and net income fell 13.1% YoY. This kept the re-underwriting incomplete.

Q2 2024, published August 20 and therefore known before the September 25 t0, was the turn: paying clients rose 28.8% YoY to 2.04m, client assets rose 24.3% YoY to HK$579.3b, total trading volume rose 69.0% YoY to HK$1.62t, revenue rose 25.9% YoY to HK$3.13b, and management raised 2024 new-paying-client guidance to 550k. Operating margin was 47.3%, lower YoY but still high for a growth brokerage platform.

During/after the breakaway, Q3 2024 was published November 19: revenue rose 29.6% YoY to HK$3.44b, brokerage commission and handling charge income rose 51.5% YoY, trading volume rose 74.7% YoY, and non-GAAP adjusted net income rose 20.8% YoY. This confirmed the direction of the Q2 arc, but it did not cause the September/early-October breakaway because it was published later.

Runway read: the business entered the run with net cash-like balance-sheet characteristics in company filings, high gross margins, high net income margins, and a capital-return authorization. The missing local PIT statements mean this section should be treated as cited public-disclosure narrative, not a repo-derived fundamentals factor.

## 7. Research echo

The research echo lagged and then chased. On May 28, 2024, JPMorgan raised its target to $92 from $62 after strong client-growth/asset-flow evidence. On May 29, 2024, BofA raised its target to $85.80 from $81.00 after Q1. By October 2, 2024, FUTU closed at $108.51 in the local tape, already above those May target levels. That same day, JPMorgan raised its target to $160 from $88, explicitly tying the new outlook to improving retail sentiment in Hong Kong and mainland China. The read is that the price escaped the pre-policy published model first, then the published model reset upward after the China policy shock.
