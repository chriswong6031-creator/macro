# TCOM 2015 — winner autopsy

## 1. Bottom line

TCOM's 2015 winner was Ctrip (`CTRP` at the time), and it was a two-phase platform rerating rather than one durable March earnings gap. The 2015-03-19 Q4 release showed transaction volume far ahead of guidance and guided 40%–50% revenue growth even while a `-21%` GAAP operating margin exposed the cost of the mobile and market-share campaign; the next session's `+24.79%` move began the mechanical episode. Ctrip then proved that volume could become revenue and operating leverage—net-revenue growth moved from 33% in Q4 2014 to 46%/47%/49% in Q1/Q2/Q3 2015 while GAAP operating margin recovered from `-21%` to `-8%`, `+2%`, and `+13%`—and converted competitive intensity into consolidation through eLong, Priceline, and finally a 45% voting interest in Qunar. The distinction matters: Ctrip lagged KWEB over the first 21 and 63 forward sessions, so the initial leg was partly China-internet beta; cohort alpha emerged at 126 sessions and exploded after Qunar as Ctrip held while KWEB fell. The November Q3 headline profit was flattered by a one-time Tujia deconsolidation gain, but the operating-margin recovery was real. The shares peaked 93.94% above `t0` on 2015-11-20 and then retraced 17.02% by year-end, failing to sustain the vertical pace while still finishing 2015 up 103.65%, 85.27 percentage points ahead of KWEB and 102.41 points ahead of SPY. Primary anchors: Ctrip's [Q4 release, published 2015-03-19 and filed 2015-03-20](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1269238/000110465915021180/a15-7370_1ex99d1.htm), [Q3 release, published 2015-11-18 and filed 2015-11-19](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1269238/000110465915080054/a15-23753_1ex99d1.htm), and [Qunar transaction release, published and filed 2015-10-26](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1269238/000110465915072624/a15-21702_1ex99d1.htm).

## 3. Catalyst ladder

The local 8-K anchor files `data/edgar/material_8k_events.parquet` and `data/edgar/earnings_8k_dates.parquet` contain zero `TCOM`/`CTRP` rows. Ctrip was a foreign private issuer reporting on Form 6-K, so the missing 8-K rows are a coverage boundary, not evidence that no catalysts occurred. Dates below are first public release dates; later filing dates are stated rather than substituted backward.

- **2015-01-05 — international content acquisition:** Ctrip disclosed that it had acquired a majority stake in UK-based Travelfusion, whose platform aggregated more than 200 low-cost and full-service carriers, rail operators, and more than 30 hotel consolidators. [Ctrip 6-K exhibit, published 2015-01-05 and filed 2015-01-06](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1269238/000110465915000473/a15-1263_1ex99d1.htm).
- **2015-03-19 — Q4/FY2014 earnings and forward guide:** Q4 net revenue grew 33%, above 30% guidance; accommodation volume grew 53% versus 40%–50% guidance and transportation-ticketing volume grew 102% versus 55%–65% guidance. Gross margin was 69%, GAAP operating margin was `-21%`, and Q1 revenue-growth guidance was 40%–50%. [Ctrip 6-K exhibit, published 2015-03-19 and filed 2015-03-20](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1269238/000110465915021180/a15-7370_1ex99d1.htm).
- **2015-05-13 — Q1 earnings:** Net revenue grew 46%; accommodation volume grew 60%; transportation volume grew 104%; mobile represented roughly 70% of online transactions; and Q2 revenue-growth guidance rose to 45%–50%. Gross margin improved sequentially to 70%, although GAAP operating margin remained `-8%`. [Ctrip 6-K exhibit, published 2015-05-13 and filed 2015-05-14](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1269238/000110465915037854/a15-11631_1ex99d1.htm).
- **2015-05-22 — eLong stake and Expedia cooperation:** Ctrip acquired 37.6% of eLong for approximately `$400M` and agreed with Expedia to cooperate on travel-product offerings in specified geographies. [Ctrip 6-K exhibit, published and filed 2015-05-22](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1269238/000110465915040113/a15-12634_1ex99d1.htm).
- **2015-05-26 — Priceline capital and commercial reinforcement:** Priceline committed another `$250M` via a convertible bond, could reach up to 15% ownership when combined with conversion and permitted open-market accumulation, and continued cross-promotion of accommodation inventory. [Ctrip 6-K exhibit, published 2015-05-26 and filed 2015-05-27](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1269238/000110465915040928/a15-12816_1ex99d1.htm).
- **2015-06-01 — Qunar rejection made consolidation intent public:** Qunar disclosed that it had received Ctrip's unsolicited proposal on 2015-05-08, declined it on 2015-06-01, but remained open to discussions with Ctrip and other strategic parties. The ladder date is the public disclosure, not the earlier non-public proposal date. [Qunar SEC exhibit, published and filed 2015-06-01](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1551060/000110465915042636/a15-9407_8ex99d1.htm).
- **2015-06-18 — acquisition runway with dilution risk:** Ctrip priced `$700M` of 1.00% convertible senior notes due 2020 and `$400M` of 1.99% notes due 2025, before over-allotment options. This expanded strategic capacity but also raised conversion and leverage hazards. [Ctrip 6-K exhibit, published 2015-06-18 and filed 2015-06-19](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1269238/000110465915046267/a15-14147_4ex99d1.htm).
- **2015-08-03 — Q2 earnings and first GAAP operating profit in the ladder:** Net revenue grew 47%, accommodation and transportation volume grew 55% and 106%, gross margin reached 71%, GAAP operating margin improved to `+2%` from `-8%` in Q1, and Q3 revenue-growth guidance remained 45%–50%. [Ctrip 6-K exhibit, published 2015-08-03 and filed 2015-08-04](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1269238/000110465915055533/a15-16908_1ex99d1.htm).
- **2015-10-26 — Qunar share exchange:** Ctrip completed a share exchange with Baidu that gave Ctrip approximately 45% of Qunar's voting interest, gave Baidu approximately 25% of Ctrip's voting interest, added cross-board representation, and established broad business cooperation. [Ctrip 6-K exhibit, published and filed 2015-10-26](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1269238/000110465915072624/a15-21702_1ex99d1.htm).
- **2015-11-18 — Q3 earnings and margin confirmation:** Net revenue grew 49%; accommodation and transportation volume grew 50% and 150%; gross margin reached 73%; GAAP operating margin reached 13%; and Q4 revenue-growth guidance stayed at 45%–50%. Reported net income included a large non-operating gain from deconsolidating Tujia, so the cleaner confirmation was operating income and margin. [Ctrip 6-K exhibit, published 2015-11-18 and filed 2015-11-19](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1269238/000110465915080054/a15-23753_1ex99d1.htm).
- **2015-12-10 — post-peak capital confirmation:** Priceline and a long-term equity investment firm each committed `$500M` through convertible bonds; Priceline's commercial partnership and permission to reach up to 15% ownership remained in place. This reinforced runway after the visible peak but added further conversion exposure. [Ctrip 6-K exhibit, published 2015-12-10 and filed 2015-12-11](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1269238/000110465915084158/a15-24884_1ex99d1.htm).

## 4. Stage anatomy

- **Compressed prior — present.** Five sessions before `t0`, the split-adjusted close was 36.17% below its prior 252-session high. The Q4 release paired rapid volume with a `-21%` operating margin, leaving the market to choose between a structurally stronger mobile platform and an uneconomic share campaign.
- **Catalyst ladder — present.** Q4 and Q1 established growth, eLong/Priceline added distribution and capital, the public Qunar rejection preserved consolidation optionality, Q2 showed an operating-profit inflection, and the October Qunar exchange plus Q3 margin confirmation completed the rerating.
- **Relative breakaway — present, but delayed versus cohort.** The onset was already far ahead of SPY on trailing returns, yet Ctrip lagged KWEB at 21 and 63 forward sessions. Distinct cohort alpha appeared by 126 sessions (`+26.86pp`) and reached `+85.27pp` for calendar 2015.
- **Liquidity confirmation — present.** The March onset carried `$1.279B` of turnover and `z21=4.27`; the October Qunar session was the year's largest run-window dollar-volume day at `$2.204B`, with `z21=4.33`. Both catalyst gaps held after 3/5/10 sessions.
- **Options convexity — unknown.** No current TCOM snapshot exists in the approved local surfaces, and per-ticker history begins only in 2026-06. Historical dealer positioning, call walls, and signed flow are null.

The operating mosaic was stronger than the EPS headline. Accommodation volume stayed near 50%–60% growth across the four public quarters, while transportation-ticketing volume ran from 102% in Q4 2014 to 150% in Q3 2015. Revenue growth accelerated as gross margin recovered four points and operating margin improved 34 points from Q4 2014 to Q3 2015. Q1 product-development and sales/marketing expenses were still 32% and 30% of net revenue on the company's non-GAAP presentation, evidence that the model had not simply stopped investing. By Q3, GAAP operating income was RMB405M and operating margin 13%; the RMB2.4B reported net income was heavily influenced by the Tujia deconsolidation gain. Runway was ample in the disclosed liquidity balances, but the `$1.1B` June notes and later strategic convertibles mean it was partly financed runway, not proof of self-funding. Quarterly comparable cash-flow coverage is **unknown** in the approved local surfaces.

Individual dated models nevertheless show price repeatedly escaping the published frame. On 2015-04-24, Guotai Junan raised its target from `$36` to `$52` while reporting spot at `$65.62`, leaving its revised target 20.8% below the market even though it forecast roughly 46% 2015 revenue growth; the report still expected an operating loss and focused on competitive spending ([Guotai Junan report, published 2015-04-24](https://www.gtjai.com/UploadFiles/gtja_Report/2015/04/CTRP_CR_20150424.pdf)). Thus the published model sequence moved from materially below spot in April to only modestly above spot after the operating and consolidation evidence had already been capitalized. ## 9. Hazards & false-positive checks

- **meme_squeeze — ruled_out.** The episode predates the modern meme channel and persisted across four operating releases, multiple strategic transactions, a summer drawdown, and a second breakaway. Short-cover amplification is unknown, but it is not needed to explain the full-year path.
- **one_day_binary — ruled_out.** The March, Q1, eLong, Qunar, and Q3 gaps each held after 3/5/10 sessions, and the stock finished the year 103.65% above its 2014 close despite a 17.02% post-peak retracement.
- **sector_beta — possible.** Ctrip lagged KWEB by 2.45pp at 21 sessions and 4.37pp at 63 sessions, so early follow-through was not cohort alpha. The check weakens only at 126 sessions and YTD, when the excess widened to 26.86pp and 85.27pp.
- **options_mirage — possible.** Approved options history is absent for 2015, so an amplifying convexity channel cannot be tested. The fundamental/consolidation mechanism does not require it.
- **Extension:** `extended_after_vertical_move` was likely at the 2015-11-20 peak: +93.94% from `t0` and +145.43% from the prior year-end. The subsequent 17.02% retracement shows that hazard partially realized without erasing the winner.
- **Call walls:** **unknown**. No approved current TCOM GEX snapshot or historical 2015 options surface exists.
- **Catalyst exhaustion:** **possible** after Qunar and Q3. By 2015-11-19 the major competitive, strategic-capital, growth, and margin disclosures were public; the price peaked one session later and ended the year below that high.
- **Other hazards:** acquisition integration across eLong/Qunar/Travelfusion, convertible dilution and leverage, VIE/China policy risk, and over-reading Q3 net income despite the one-time Tujia gain.
