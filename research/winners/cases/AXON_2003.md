# AXON 2003 — winner autopsy

## 1. Bottom line

AXON's 2003–04 alpha was the combination of a genuine new-product adoption curve and a reflexive market structure, not either one in isolation. Then known as TASER International and traded as TASR, the company moved from a niche M26 vendor to an X26-led growth story: quarterly revenue accelerated from $3.4 million in Q1 2003 to $6.1 million in Q3 and $10.8 million in Q4, gross margin expanded, police-agency deployments broadened, and cash generation funded capacity; the decisive public reset was the 2003-10-20 Q3 release, which reported 216% year-over-year quarterly growth and raised full-year revenue guidance ([Q3 release, published and filed 2003-10-20](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1069183/000095015303002019/p68350exv99w1.htm)). But the magnitude after the 2003-11-20 mechanical anchor also depended on a small float, heavy short positioning, two split announcements and speculative turnover ([The Motley Fool, published 2004-01-16](https://www.fool.com/investing/general/2004/01/16/taser-on-a-tear.aspx)). AXON gained 418.34% from that anchor to its 2004-04-19 peak and still retained 132.18 percentage points of excess return over XLI at 126 sessions, yet the peak did not hold: a 56.58% drawdown followed by 2004-05-10 as new-adoption breadth slowed ([Q1 release, published and filed 2004-04-20](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1069183/000095012404001687/p69051exv99w1.htm)) and product-safety headlines challenged a valuation already far ahead of published models ([The Washington Post, published 2004-05-02](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/business/2004/05/03/bad-news-makes-taser-stock-price-even-more-volatile/616023ca-2c51-4a6e-aeb4-6010a67b050a/)). The business re-underwriting was real; the terminal vertical leg was not durable.

## 3. Catalyst ladder

Local filing-anchor coverage is null for the episode. `data/edgar/material_8k_events.parquet` begins AXON coverage on 2015-07-20, and the first AXON row in `data/edgar/earnings_8k_dates.parquet` is 2005-04-01. The dates below therefore come from contemporaneous public releases and SEC exhibits; where a release and filing date differ, both are stated.

- **2003-04-21 — earnings:** Q1 revenue reached a record $3.4 million, up 44% year over year, while 340 new departments ordered and 43 committed to full patrol deployment ([company release, published 2003-04-21; filed 2003-04-22](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1069183/000095015303000758/p67712exv99w1.htm)). This established the early adoption slope.
- **2003-05-06 — product launch:** a contemporaneous trade publication described the smaller, lighter X26 and its planned 2003-05-30 introduction ([DefenseReview, published 2003-05-06](https://defensereview.com/taser-international-introduces-new-x26-taser/)). X26 became the incremental product that later drove the revenue inflection.
- **2003-06-30 — M&A activity:** TASER announced the acquisition of Tasertron's principal assets, including the TASER trademark, six patents, two pending applications, customer contacts and an end to existing litigation ([company release, published and filed 2003-06-30](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1069183/000095015303001234/p67967exv99w1.htm)). This consolidated brand and intellectual-property control.
- **2003-07-21 — earnings:** Q2 revenue rose 53% to $4.2 million; the company disclosed $1.7 million of orders expected to ship in Q3 and another $950,000 received in the first two weeks of July ([company release, published and filed 2003-07-21](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1069183/000095015303001336/p68037exv99w1.htm)).
- **2003-10-20 — earnings/guidance:** Q3 revenue rose 216% to $6.1 million, net income reached $1.1 million from a prior-year loss, deployments exceeded 3,500 agencies, and full-year revenue guidance rose to $20 million from the original $13–15 million range; the release also disclosed a $1.0 million U.S. Army appropriation ([company release, published and filed 2003-10-20](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1069183/000095015303002019/p68350exv99w1.htm)). This was the principal fundamental breakaway rung.
- **2004-01-14 — other:** the board announced a three-for-one stock split to lower the per-share price and expand market liquidity ([company release dated 2004-01-14; filed 2004-01-23](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1069183/000095015304000145/p68698exv99.htm)). It changed accessibility, not enterprise value, and contributed to reflexive acceleration.
- **2004-02-02 — earnings/guidance:** Q4 revenue rose 283% to $10.8 million, full-year revenue rose 148% to $24.5 million, year-end cash reached $15.9 million, and management guided to approximately 100% revenue growth in 2004 ([company release, published and filed 2004-02-02](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1069183/000095015304000228/p68732exv99w1.htm)).
- **2004-02-02 — other:** a separate release disclosed approximately $4.9 million of January orders, about $7.4 million of booked orders including year-end backlog, and redemption of the remaining public warrants while rebutting a saturation critique ([company release published 2004-02-02; filed 2004-02-06](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1069183/000095015304000279/p68732bexv99w1.htm)). This simultaneously reinforced demand and highlighted short-position tension.
- **2004-02-18 — contract/market expansion:** a contemporaneous report described U.S. military deployments in Iraq and framed defense adoption as another addressable market ([Forbes, published 2004-02-18](https://www.forbes.com/2004/02/18/cx_ah_0218tasr.html)).
- **2004-04-06 — other:** TASER announced a second, two-for-one split and responded publicly to a CBS report about deaths following TASER use ([company release published 2004-04-06; filed 2004-04-16](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1069183/000095015304000876/p69045exv99.htm)). The split added another low-durability liquidity impulse; the safety response made the emerging headline risk explicit.
- **2004-04-20 — earnings/negative inflection:** Q1 revenue still rose 286% to $13.1 million and net income reached $3.6 million, but only 92 new departments were added—explicitly lower than prior quarters—while execution and safety-publicity risks were prominent ([company release, published and filed 2004-04-20](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1069183/000095012404001687/p69051exv99w1.htm)). AXON fell 28.90% that session (`data/yahoo/AXON.parquet`), showing that excellent absolute results no longer cleared embedded expectations.
- **2004-05-02 — other/negative:** a contemporaneous report documented another post-use death headline, extreme valuation, high turnover relative to public float and a thin analyst bench ([The Washington Post, published 2004-05-02](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/business/2004/05/03/bad-news-makes-taser-stock-price-even-more-volatile/616023ca-2c51-4a6e-aeb4-6010a67b050a/)). This was part of the 56.58% post-peak drawdown, not a refutation of the already reported revenue growth.

## 4. Stage anatomy

- **Compressed prior — absent.** The 63 sessions before `t0` delivered +183.47%, the prior 21-session range was 27.11%, and annualized 21-session close-to-close volatility was about 92%; this was already a vertical, volatile leader (`data/yahoo/AXON.parquet`).
- **Catalyst ladder — present.** X26 launch, trademark/IP consolidation, sequential quarterly acceleration, Army optionality, repeated guidance expansion and order updates supplied multiple public rungs from April 2003 through February 2004 ([Q3 release, published 2003-10-20](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1069183/000095015303002019/p68350exv99w1.htm); [FY release, published 2004-02-02](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1069183/000095015304000228/p68732exv99w1.htm)); the April 2004 safety/expectations reset was the first clear negative rung ([Q1 release, published 2004-04-20](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1069183/000095012404001687/p69051exv99w1.htm)).
- **Relative breakaway — present.** At `t0`, trailing excess return was +27.66pp over XLI at 21 sessions and +133.61pp at 42 sessions; forward excess reached +124.22pp at 63 sessions and +132.18pp at 126 sessions (`data/yahoo/{AXON,XLI}.parquet`).
- **Liquidity confirmation — present, then adverse.** `t0` dollar-volume z-scores were 1.50/2.57 versus 21/60 sessions, but the $2.734 billion 2004-04-20 collapse and $2.823 billion follow-on session show that later liquidity confirmed exit pressure as strongly as earlier re-underwriting (`data/yahoo/AXON.parquet`).
- **Options convexity — unknown.** No 2003–04 per-ticker options history exists in the repository. Current options surfaces begin in 2026-06 and cannot be projected backward.

During the run, Q4 revenue reached $10.78 million, gross margin approximately 67.4%, net income $2.80 million and net margin approximately 26.0%; full-year revenue was $24.46 million, up 148%, with $4.45 million of net income and $15.88 million of year-end cash ([Q4/FY release, published 2004-02-02](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1069183/000095015304000228/p68732exv99w1.htm)). Runway was expanding rather than constraining growth. The late-stage problem was expectations and adoption breadth: revenue still rose 22% sequentially in Q1 2004, but new-department additions slowed markedly and backlog remained approximately flat ([Feltl research note, published 2004-04-21](https://www.feltl.com/articles/TASR042104.pdf)). Product-level unit economics, order cancellation history and independently normalized 2003 quarterly EPS are local coverage gaps.

## 9. Hazards & false-positive checks

- **meme_squeeze — possible:** this predated modern social-media meme mechanics, and the product/earnings ladder was substantive; however, more than half the float was reported short by 2003-12-15 ([The Motley Fool, published 2004-01-16](https://www.fool.com/investing/general/2004/01/16/taser-on-a-tear.aspx)), while a contemporaneous account described bulletin-board attention, day trading and volume often exceeding public float ([The Washington Post, published 2004-05-02](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/business/2004/05/03/bad-news-makes-taser-stock-price-even-more-volatile/616023ca-2c51-4a6e-aeb4-6010a67b050a/)). Squeeze/reflexivity plausibly amplified the move.
- **one_day_binary — ruled_out:** the re-underwriting unfolded across successive Q1/Q2/Q3/Q4/Q1 publications, product launch, IP consolidation and military-market evidence over more than a year.
- **sector_beta — ruled_out:** forward excess versus XLI was +124.22pp at 63 sessions and +132.18pp at 126 sessions; SPY-relative results were similar (`data/yahoo/{AXON,XLI,SPY}.parquet`).
- **options_mirage — possible:** no episode options history exists, so derivatives amplification is unknowable. Cash-market turnover and multi-month earnings evidence independently establish that the move was not merely an options-surface artifact.

Episode hazards were `extended_after_vertical_move`, `low_float_short_squeeze`, `stock_split_reflexivity`, `catalyst_exhausted`, `valuation_ahead_of_published_model`, and `product_safety_reputational_risk`. Extension and catalyst exhaustion became acute at the 2004-04-19 peak: even a 286% revenue-growth print could not sustain price. Current-only options hazards are `call_wall_overhead` and `elevated_iv`; the 2026-07-16 $600 call wall sits 10.75% above spot, but it has no bearing on the historical causal finding.
