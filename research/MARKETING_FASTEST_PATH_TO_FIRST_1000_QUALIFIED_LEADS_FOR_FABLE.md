# Marketing: Fastest Path to the First 1,000 Qualified Leads

## Distribution-first growth strategy for Fable

**Canonical deliverable:** this file is the strategy for reaching Mastermind's first 1,000 qualified leads as quickly as the product can serve them well, without pretending an arbitrary seven-day deadline is a forecast.
**Prepared:** 2026-07-19
**Prepared for:** Fable, Command, Allies, Funnel, Radar, Studio, Broadcast, Workshop, Lab, Sentinel, and Engine Room
**Scope:** Mastermind / Macro Dashboard / Neural Web product family
**Status:** strategy and execution docket; no outreach, agreement, spend, or offer change has been activated
**Companion target book:** research/marketing_dockets/MKT_FIRST_1000_DISTRIBUTION_TARGETS.md contains 50 named current candidate distributors, published routes or route-validation hypotheses, recent evidence, pitch angles, owners, and an empty booked-inventory ledger.

---

## 1. The correction

The user's frustration was directionally right: the urgency framing was a thought experiment, not a literal operating deadline. The important signal was that distribution had been underdeveloped.

The earlier plan spent too much energy inventing things Mastermind could build and too little answering the real question:

> **Who already has the audience, where are those people paying attention, and what would make that distributor voluntarily put Mastermind in front of them?**

Mastermind does not need to bet the company on a one-week stunt. It does need to stop confusing content production with distribution.

The fastest credible path to the first 1,000 qualified leads is:

1. choose one urgent, genuinely useful research service;
2. deliver enough value before signup that the offer can survive scrutiny;
3. build a finished version for a creator, newsletter, community, or complementary product before asking for a meeting;
4. let that partner improve its own audience experience by distributing the service;
5. attribute every lead, activation, second-use event, subscription, refund, and payout to the source;
6. repeat with the audience cells that produce retained users;
7. turn the best event campaign into an evergreen “what changed for my tickers?” relationship.

This is not “build an audience from scratch.” It is **borrow, earn, and gradually own distribution**.

The core strategy is:

> **Become the free intelligence layer behind people and products that already aggregate investors.**

## 2. What the actual product state says

Mastermind already has substantial public value:

- approximately 1,595 ticker dossiers;
- advanced charts, fundamentals, options context, valuation, ownership, news, themes, peers, and event data;
- a current Mag-7 / AI-capex earnings research memo;
- Content Studio, chart rendering, six editorial identities, earnings cards, movers, and share cards;
- public confluence and movers tools;
- UTM and short-link helpers;
- a Growth OS, attribution schema, partner scaffold, and measurement architecture.

The shortage is not research supply.

The shortage is:

- a proven lead form and verification path;
- a reliable email-delivery and second-value event;
- a usable creator and community acquisition ledger;
- accepted partner economics;
- source-level activation and paid attribution;
- repeatable partner provisioning;
- live distribution relationships.

The current Allies target ledger makes the mismatch obvious. It contains:

- 51 fund managers;
- 5 newsletters;
- 1 creator;
- 11 communities.

That is a useful research-intelligence list. It is not a serious distribution book.

Several large subreddits in the existing registry prohibit self-promotion. The current live publisher and email fulfillment are not production-ready. D07 does not yet join campaign source through the full signup and paid path. These are not reasons to retreat into more feature building; they identify the exact small bridges that must be completed.

The next marginal unit of effort should therefore go to:

1. a lead-worthy offer;
2. reachable distributors;
3. capture and delivery;
4. source-level measurement;
5. repeatable partner success.

## 3. Define the first 1,000 properly

The target is not 1,000 email strings.

Use five states:

1. **Visitor:** opens the campaign object.
2. **Submitted lead:** supplies an email, explicit consent, at least one ticker, and a source or partner code.
3. **Verified lead:** completes confirmation and survives dedupe and fraud checks.
4. **Qualified activated lead:** is verified and views a personalized result tied to the same session.
5. **High-intent lead:** selects at least three tickers and opens a later update, returns to a dossier, or starts a trial.

The first-1,000 goal counts **qualified activated leads**.

Do not count:

- impressions;
- followers;
- raw addresses;
- unverified forms;
- generic giveaway entrants;
- duplicate users;
- users who never see a result;
- traffic that cannot be attributed.

### Milestone ladder

The goal should be staged by evidence rather than a theatrical deadline:

| Milestone | What must be true | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| First 25 | form, verification, result, delivery, and attribution work end to end | plumbing proof |
| First 100 | at least 3 independent sources produce activated leads | offer proof |
| First 300 | at least 2 sources repeat and second-use rate is visible | channel proof |
| First 1,000 | roughly 10–25 active repeat distributors, or fewer high-volume partners, backed by a replenished prospect pool | scalable acquisition proof |

A fast case may reach 1,000 in several weeks if a few partners break out. A slower organic case may take a few months. The operating target is to compress the time through distribution quality—not lower the lead definition.

## 4. The first offer: a real service, not a lead magnet

### 4.1 Earnings Week Risk Scan

Use current earnings as the first pilot because the urgency and second-value event are real.

The initial launch supports a curated 30–50-ticker universe drawn from the current Mag-7 / AI-capex research, directly reporting companies, and the best-supported adjacent exposures.

A user enters one to ten tickers and receives:

- which supported names report and when;
- direct event exposure;
- second-order exposure only where a deterministic mapping or cited research relationship exists;
- the two or three variables that can change the interpretation;
- earnings, technical, valuation, ownership, options, and expected-move context where present;
- labeled bull, base, and bear scenarios;
- named invalidation conditions;
- one post-result update;
- one next-morning “what changed” autopsy;
- links to the relevant Mastermind dossiers.

The first useful result appears before email. Email is requested only for the promised live update. That makes the opt-in part of the service rather than an artificial gate.

If a ticker is outside the supported universe, the user gets its existing dossier and an honest notice that a custom live event update is unavailable. Do not pretend the current product can build arbitrary second-order exposure graphs for every stock.

The scan remains descriptive research, not individualized buy, sell, or sizing advice. Missing mappings print unknown; an LLM does not invent exposure edges.

Tesla has confirmed Q2 2026 results after the close on Wednesday, July 22, with a 5:30 p.m. ET webcast. IBM has confirmed its call for the same date at 5:00 p.m. ET. Those are legitimate first anchors, not manufactured scarcity. See [Tesla Investor Relations](https://ir.tesla.com/press-release/tesla-second-quarter-2026-production-deliveries-and-deployments) and [IBM Newsroom](https://newsroom.ibm.com/2026-07-08-IBM-to-Announce-Second-Quarter-2026-Financial-Results).

Those dates do not create a launch deadline. If capture, verification, delivery, and attribution have not passed the first-25 plumbing proof, use the July 22 events as an internal alpha and content rehearsal. The first public partner pilot moves to the next relevant event after the service works end to end.

### 4.2 The evergreen continuation

The event campaign should teach Mastermind how to sell a durable job:

> **Tell me when the read on the tickers I care about materially changes.**

After the event:

- let a free user monitor one supported ticker;
- send only named material changes;
- reopen the prior state and show what changed;
- preserve a shareable receipt;
- charge for broader watchlists, portfolios, faster freshness, deeper history, and more expensive intelligence.

The event is the acquisition wedge. “What changed?” is the retained product.

## 5. The real channel stack

The channels are not equal. Use them for different jobs.

| Channel | How Mastermind gets in front of people | Near-term role | Long-term value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Niche creators and newsletters | finished co-branded research object | primary lead source | repeat distributor network |
| Complementary finance products | portfolio/event utility for their users | primary partnership source | embedded distribution |
| Private communities and clubs | admin-approved clinic or member utility | primary trust channel | recurring event program |
| Data PR and independent writers | exclusive chart, table, and methodology | credibility and burst reach | citation and link network |
| X | partner Spaces, reply graph, live evidence | amplification | owned audience |
| Stocktwits | full-value ticker-native analysis | credibility and ticker discovery | recurring branded desk |
| TradingView | durable pre/post-event ideas | ticker-page discovery | public track record |
| Approved Reddit routes | complete native analysis | occasional high-reach proof | community legitimacy |
| Product Hunt / Show HN | usable public tool and engineering story | bonus discovery | tech/product backlinks |
| Ticker SEO | existing dossiers and genuinely updated pages | compounding background | owned search demand |

### 5.1 Niche creators and newsletters

This is the fastest starting channel because the audience is already segmented by ticker, style, and trust.

Do not chase celebrities first. Target operators with:

- approximately 5,000–100,000 followers;
- approximately 1,000–50,000 newsletter subscribers;
- current coverage of the relevant ticker or theme;
- visible engagement;
- a public professional contact or partner route;
- evidence that collaborations, sponsorships, guests, or tools are accepted.

The 50-name companion target book starts with active fundamental, semiconductor, Tesla, options, podcast, community, tool, and student-club operators. It is a real first wave, not a category brainstorm.

### 5.2 Complementary product alliances

This is the most underused channel.

Partner with products that already know the user's holdings or upcoming events but do not provide Mastermind's intelligence layer:

- portfolio trackers;
- trade journals;
- options calculators;
- stock-event apps;
- investing education communities;
- newsletter platforms;
- Discord utilities;
- niche screeners.

The offer is not “exchange logos.” It is:

> **Let your users run a useful earnings or change-risk scan without your team building the research engine.**

The route must genuinely put Mastermind in front of the other product's users. Sharesight publishes a two-way technology-partner route. Several other products in the companion book publish affiliate programs that primarily recruit people to promote *their* product; that is the reverse of the distribution Mastermind needs. Stock Events, Simply Wall St, Koyfin, OptionStrat, TraderSync, and Tradervue therefore remain unvalidated alliance hypotheses until a real business-development, integration, educational-event, or reciprocal-distribution route is confirmed. An affiliate application alone is not a partnership win.

### 5.3 Private communities

Contact owners and moderators, not members.

Offer:

- a free co-branded clinic;
- 20 bounded member scans;
- a native recap the admin can publish;
- free Pro for moderators;
- no unsolicited member messages;
- no sales pitch inside the room;
- a disclosed share of later subscription revenue.

University clubs are particularly interesting: they have organized distribution, recurring programming needs, and members willing to test tools. The companion ledger includes current official routes for clubs at Auckland, Berkeley, Wharton, HKU, and UT Austin.

### 5.4 Data PR

Do not pitch “our AI stock website.”

Pitch one real finding:

- how many stocks share a hidden earnings exposure;
- where nominal AI capex and free-cash-flow pressure diverge;
- which portfolios have clustered earnings risk;
- which prior scenarios changed after the call;
- which good news failed to lift the tape.

Give the writer:

- three charts;
- a structured table or CSV;
- methodology;
- five defensible observations;
- a quick custom sector cut;
- permission to cite and embed within data rights.

Reporters receive data, not affiliate commissions.

### 5.5 Native public platforms

Owned accounts are support lanes until they have audiences.

On X:

- co-host Spaces with people who bring listeners;
- publish distinct setup, result, and what-changed objects;
- make manual replies genuinely useful and mostly link-free;
- use explicit opt-in for “reply with a ticker.”

X permits authorized informational broadcasting but prohibits unsolicited automated replies; AI reply bots require prior written approval. See [X automation rules](https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/x-automation).

On Stocktwits:

- give the full observation in the ticker stream;
- keep promotion below one post in ten;
- use established Stocktwits authors for borrowed reach;
- avoid duplicate posts and unrelated cashtags.

Stocktwits caps product promotion at 10%, restricts promotional tagging during the first 50 posts, and welcomes automated feeds only when they add information rather than promotional noise. See [Stocktwits Community Guidelines](https://stocktwits.com/about/rules/).

On TradingView:

- publish rigorous pre-event ideas;
- update the original idea after the result;
- keep the content ad-free;
- put a contact link only in an eligible signature.

TradingView says ideas can reach ticker followers and strong ideas can surface on ticker and front pages, but publications cannot be ads. See [publishing ideas](https://www.tradingview.com/support/solutions/43000591338-publishing-and-updating-ideas/) and [house rules](https://www.tradingview.com/support/solutions/43000591638-our-house-rules/).

On Reddit:

- use modmail where a rule permits pre-approval;
- publish the full analysis natively;
- disclose the affiliation;
- include a source link only if permitted;
- never mass-post or mass-DM.

Reddit prohibits repeated or unsolicited mass engagement and advises business owners to be thoughtful when most contributions link back to their business. See [Reddit's spam policy](https://support.reddithelp.com/hc/en-us/articles/360043504051-Spam).

Product Hunt and Show HN remain supplemental. Product Hunt permits makers to post a usable product directly, but a launch is not guaranteed distribution. See [Product Hunt's posting guide](https://help.producthunt.com/en/articles/479557-how-to-post-a-product).

## 6. The partner offer

### 6.1 Reverse demo

Before contacting a partner, build a private preview around three tickers or themes they covered recently.

Give them:

- a complete co-branded page;
- one audience-specific chart;
- a ready-to-send email;
- native post copy;
- a short video or podcast segment outline;
- partner attribution;
- seven-day event access for the audience;
- no integration or meeting requirement.

The first ask is:

> **I built this for your audience. Should I turn it on?**

That is qualitatively different from “Can I show you our product?”

### 6.2 Founding partner economics

For the first 20 useful distributors, test one fixed offer:

- 100% of net month-one subscription revenue;
- 40% of net revenue for months 2–12;
- no lifetime tail;
- free Pro access while the partner remains active;
- optional performance bounty only after the channel demonstrates qualified activation.

Margins are less valuable than acquiring a retained customer and a repeat distributor. The terms are intentionally generous but bounded.

Communities receive a disclosed 40% net share for 12 months. Reporters receive no commissions.

Root should ratify one standard clickwrap affiliate agreement. Fable can then issue routine invitations inside it; bespoke terms, signatures, regulated referral structures, or unusual institutions remain exceptions.

Every partner receiving money, free access, or another material benefit must disclose the relationship clearly in the actual post, email, video, or live segment. The FTC says money and free benefits are material connections and disclosures should be hard to miss and placed with the endorsement. See [FTC Disclosures 101](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers).

### 6.3 Copy-ready approaches

Creator or newsletter:

> **Subject:** Built a live [TSLA / semis / options] earnings desk for your audience
>
> [Name] — you have been covering [specific ticker or theme], so I built a private earnings desk around three names your audience is watching: [preview].
>
> It shows what matters before the report and updates after it. Your readers get the event pass free, with no card. The chart, short intro, and tracking are already done. No call needed.
>
> Want me to activate the [Partner] version?

Community admin:

> I can run a free, no-pitch earnings clinic for [Community]. Members submit supported tickers and receive a risk scan plus a post-result autopsy. I provide the co-branded page, native recap, and 20 bounded scans. No member DMs.
>
> Private preview: [link]. Would this be useful for the next event?

Reporter or writer:

> **Subject:** Data: hidden exposure to this earnings cycle
>
> Attached are three publication-ready charts, the table, and methodology. The most interesting finding is [real finding]. You may cite or embed them within the stated data terms.
>
> I can cut the same map for [their sector] by [time]. No call required.

## 7. From first pilot to 1,000

### Phase 0 — make the service trustworthy

Ship only:

- the public sample;
- bounded ticker input;
- verified email and consent;
- partner/referral/UTM capture;
- instant result;
- reliable update delivery;
- co-brand layer;
- source-level scorecard;
- manual fallback bounded by precompiled ticker modules.

The repo does not currently have a proven campaign email-verification and fulfillment path. The free earnings feed can fail soft, the paid source is unprovisioned, and live attribution remains incomplete. If an already-proven external form and transactional-email stack exists, configuration may be fast. Otherwise budget 24–48 hours or narrow the pilot further.

Do not manually write bespoke updates for hundreds of users. Precompile one verified event module per supported ticker and assemble bounded reports.

### Phase 1 — first 100 activated leads

Use the 50 named candidates as a research and validation cohort. Prioritize the best 20–25 for reverse demos, but do not assume that this small book can supply the whole channel. Expand toward 250–500 researched prospects as actual reply and placement rates become visible.

Operating cadence:

1. create five reverse demos;
2. send five to ten deeply personalized approaches per day;
3. book the first one or two pilot placements;
4. run one earnings or event desk;
5. inspect every funnel step;
6. interview users and partners;
7. improve the offer, then continue until at least three independent sources have placed it.

The first 25 users prove plumbing. The first 100 prove that at least three independent sources can create activated leads.

### Phase 2 — 100 to 300

- repeat with the best two source types;
- add roughly 100 researched prospects and pursue the best-fit next wave;
- run a second event or “what changed” campaign;
- give winning partners a second finished object immediately;
- stop serving sources that produce low activation or high complaints;
- connect the event user to an evergreen ticker-monitoring job.

### Phase 3 — 300 to 1,000

- maintain roughly 10–25 active repeat distributors, or fewer partners with proven high-volume placements;
- replenish a living pool of 250–500 researched prospects instead of treating the initial 50 as the whole market;
- publish a recurring partner event calendar;
- let partners request or clone bounded research objects;
- add complementary-product integrations;
- build a public data and citation program;
- use referrals only after users receive a second useful event;
- start paid tests only when warm-source activation and retention are understood.

One large placement can accelerate this sequence. It should not be the operating assumption.

### Distributor acquisition capacity model

This is a capacity model, not a forecast. It deliberately excludes repeat sends, large editorial wins, virality, and partner referrals so the base acquisition arithmetic remains visible.

| Input | Conservative | Working | Strong |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Minimum live researched pool, continuously replenished | 250 | 350 | 500 |
| Personalized approaches per week | 40 | 60 | 80 |
| Reply rate | 5% | 8% | 12% |
| Reply to placement | 35% | 50% | 60% |
| New placements per week | 0.7 | 2.4 | 5.8 |
| Activated leads per first placement | 10 | 20 | 35 |
| First-placement activated leads per week | 7 | 48 | about 200 |
| Total approaches to 1,000 without repeat sends | about 5,720 | about 1,250 | about 400 |
| Approximate weeks to 1,000 without repeat sends | 143 | 21 | 5 |

The table exposes why the opening 50-name ledger cannot support a claim of three to five immediate placements. In the working case, 60 strong approaches produce only about 2.4 placements, and the live prospect pool must be replenished continuously. Faster growth must come from better offer fit, repeat sends from proven partners, or occasional high-volume placements—not imaginary conversion rates.

## 8. Measurement that prevents self-deception

Track by source:

- delivered unique clicks;
- click to submitted lead;
- submitted to verified;
- verified to activated;
- first to second useful event;
- high-intent rate;
- trial and paid conversion;
- 30- and 90-day retention;
- refunds and chargebacks;
- partner payout;
- contribution after data, inference, delivery, and support cost;
- complaints and unsubscribes.

Use follower counts and opens only for provisional planning. Ask partners for recent median unique clicks where possible.

Early decision bands:

| Signal | Weak | Promising | Strong |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Personalized outreach reply | under 5% after 50 good approaches | 5–12% | over 12% |
| Click to activated, after 200 partner clicks | under 20% | 20–25% | over 25% |
| Activated to second useful event | under 25% | 25–40% | over 40% |
| Partner's first placement | under 10 activated leads | 10–25 | over 25 |

Thirty to fifty friendly testers can catch broken forms and copy. They cannot validate conversion. Use real partner traffic and rolling confidence intervals before declaring a winner.

The first 1,000 are valuable only if they teach:

- which audiences activate;
- which return;
- which pay;
- which partners repeat;
- which value object creates the second session.

## 9. Small builds that unlock distribution

Fable should prioritize five bounded packets:

### MKT-FIRST1000-00 — Capture and fulfillment

Verified email, consent, source capture, instant result, update delivery, and unsubscribe.

### MKT-FIRST1000-01 — Distributor ledger

Expand the 50 named candidates into a living book of 250–500 creators, newsletters, communities, editorial operators, and complementary products. A target is not inventory until a placement is scheduled.

### MKT-FIRST1000-02 — Reverse-demo assembler

Given a partner and three supported tickers, compile a private co-branded page, chart, email, native copy, and source code.

### MKT-FIRST1000-03 — Source economics

Join click, submitted, verified, activated, second-use, paid, refund, and partner payout state.

### MKT-FIRST1000-04 — Event-to-evergreen bridge

Turn a successful event scan into one free monitored ticker and a clear paid expansion.

These are distribution bridges. They are not invitations to build another generalized analytics layer.

## 10. What to stop doing

Stop:

1. treating more content formats as the main constraint;
2. calling famous fund managers a distribution pipeline;
3. waiting for zero-follower accounts to grow before approaching partners;
4. chasing celebrities before reachable niche operators;
5. sending generic collaboration pitches;
6. gating generic PDFs;
7. assuming a subreddit, Product Hunt, or one viral post is a strategy;
8. mass-posting across communities;
9. automating unsolicited replies or DMs;
10. paying for impressions before activated-lead economics are visible;
11. launching multiple offers at once;
12. counting raw emails as qualified leads;
13. asking for a subscription before the promised second value event;
14. building universal exposure mapping before a bounded pilot proves demand.

## 11. Outreach and partner operating rails

These are execution requirements, not the strategy's center.

Because outreach originates in Canada, commercial electronic messages generally require consent or a valid basis, identification, and an unsubscribe mechanism. A conspicuously published professional address is not a blanket license: the message must be relevant to the role, the publication must not reject unsolicited messages, and the source and date should be recorded.

Include a valid contact mailing address and a simple unsubscribe mechanism that remain valid for at least 60 days; process unsubscribe requests without delay and no later than 10 business days. Start cold outreach only where the applicable template covers the recipient jurisdiction. See the [CRTC CASL FAQ](https://www.crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm). This is an operating guard, not legal advice.

On platforms, use the sanctioned route:

- moderator approval where required;
- full native value where promotion is constrained;
- official APIs or permitted manual posting;
- clear material-connection disclosure;
- no fake accounts, self-amplification, or reputation gaming.

## 13. Final verdict

There is no magical free platform that will donate 1,000 qualified users to an unknown finance product.

The closest thing to a free organic acquisition engine is a network of people and products that already aggregate the exact users Mastermind wants—and a research service valuable enough that distributing it makes them look better.

So the practical answer to “where does it start?” is:

1. start with the 50 named targets in the companion ledger;
2. build five audience-specific reverse demos;
3. approach five to ten reachable operators per day;
4. run one bounded earnings pilot;
5. prove 100 activated leads from at least three sources;
6. repeat with the sources that create a second useful event;
7. grow from event distribution into evergreen ticker monitoring;
8. reach 1,000 through roughly 10–25 active repeat distributors—or fewer proven high-volume partners—not one lucky viral post.

That is fast without being fake, creative without being gimmicky, and aggressive without letting a rhetorical deadline force bad decisions.
