## How the calculation treats conviction

Three different quantities are kept separate:

- **Shares added %** is the reported change in share count from Q4 2025 to Q1 2026. It says how aggressively the manager changed an existing holding.
- **Final book %** is the Q1 position's percentage of the reported 13F equity book. It includes the legacy shares already owned, so it can overstate the capital represented by the new decision.
- **Estimated incremental book %** isolates the newly bought portion. For a New position it equals final book %. For an Add it is:

  `final book % × shares added % / (100 + shares added %)`

Fund returns below are weighted by estimated incremental book %. This is more decision-faithful than weighting an Add by the whole final position. The estimate still uses quarter-end market value, not the manager's undisclosed transaction prices.

The 60-day **estimated book P&L** is `sum(incremental book % × stock return)`. It is a percentage-point attribution to the reported 13F book, not an estimate of total fund NAV.

## Fund-by-fund assessment

### 1. Casdin Capital — exceptional and internally consistent

Casdin bought 15 priced common equities. The final Q1 positions represented 24.1% of the reported book; the estimated newly deployed portion was 9.9%. The basket returned +43.7%, beat SPY by 41.7 points, and added an estimated +4.32 percentage points to the 13F book.

The largest decisive Add was **TXG**: shares +62.5%, final position 3.66%, estimated incremental 1.41%, return +116.0%. **RLAY** was already a 10.86% position, but only about 1.07% of book was incremental from a +10.9% share Add; it returned +56.4%. New **PSNL** was only 0.77% of book but returned +136.3%. New QGEN, GRAL, and AGMB were also positive. ANL (-31.9%), TSHA (-4.4%), and NTLA (-2.7%) were the main failures and were too small to impair the basket.

**Verdict:** the best fund in this release. Both Adds and New positions worked, performance broadened after the 30-day checkpoint, and gains were not dependent on a single holding. Historical page coverage is only 28.1%, so the current A grade still deserves caution; the Q1 outcome itself does not.

### 2. Starboard Value — exceptional New selection, ordinary Adds

Starboard made eight priced common-equity buys. The final positions represented 28.62% of book; estimated incremental capital was 15.32%. The basket returned +23.6%, beat SPY by 21.7 points, and added an estimated +3.62 percentage points to the reported book. Seven of eight stocks were positive.

The result came from four New positions totaling 13.23% of book. **KMX** was a 5.64% New position and returned +50.9%, contributing about +2.87 points by itself. New GPGI returned +21.1%; New LW returned +5.5%; the small HYG position returned +1.3%. The four Adds were far weaker: +1.9% as a sleeve. RIOT shares rose +28.2% (4.22% final, 0.93% incremental) but the stock lost 14.0%; gains in Added TRIP (+48.0%), IJH (+4.9%), and IWM (+6.3%) just offset it.

**Verdict:** the second-best release overall and a clear fresh-idea success. The 100% New-position hit rate, large KMX win, and +3.62-point estimated book contribution are strong. But Adds merely matched SPY, so the evidence supports Starboard's new-name selection in this release—not a blanket follow-on edge. That distinction matters because Starboard ranks last in the current Grade A dossier on historical median buy excess.

**Verdict:** a clear success, especially in follow-on sizing. Adds returned +23.7% with an 89% hit rate; New positions returned +9.0% but only half were positive. The fund deserves credit for portfolio impact more than breadth of initial selection.

Soros had 141 resolved common-equity buys, of which 121 had complete 60-day paths. Estimated incremental book was 45.6%; 86.4% of that weight was priced. The scored basket returned about +2.0%, essentially equal to SPY. Adds returned +0.4% and lagged SPY; New positions returned +4.1%.

The largest scored decision was a +316% increase in SPY itself: 10.73% final weight and 8.15% estimated incremental weight, pulling the aggregate toward the benchmark by construction. TSM and Sealed Air/SEE were meaningful Adds, but SEE lacked a complete public price path after a corporate event and is excluded from the 60-day score. CRWV was the largest scored detractor (-25.5%). The high 65% stock hit rate did not translate into excess return because gains were small and larger losers/benchmark exposure absorbed them.

**Verdict:** neutral. This looks like a broad trading/rotation book, not a concentrated stock-picking call. Incomplete 60-day paths and 86% incremental-weight coverage lower confidence; the measured portion produced no 60-day edge.

### 16. Corvex — clear failure despite two winning tickers

Corvex initiated only three common-equity positions, totaling 2.03% of book. CSGP was 1.37% and lost 15.3%. LION was 0.62% and returned +5.1%; IWM was only 0.05% and returned +6.3%. The basket lost 8.6%, lagged SPY by 10.5 points, and cost an estimated 0.17 points of book.

**Verdict:** failed. The nominal 67% hit rate is misleading because the largest position was the loser and the two winners were too small to compensate.

## Cross-fund conclusions

### The best managers increased existing winners well

The strongest action-class results were Casdin Adds (+61.5%), Himalaya's CROX Add (+38.2%), Elliott Adds (+24.5%), Duquesne Adds (+23.7%), and RA Capital Adds (+15.8%). In this sample, correct follow-on sizing was at least as important as discovering New names.

### New-position success was uneven

Casdin, Starboard, Altimeter, Basswood, Light Street, Himalaya, and Baupost had strong New sleeves. Starboard's New sleeve returned +27.1% and produced nearly all of its +3.62-point book impact. Altimeter is the most extreme split: New ARM/AXON created all the positive outcome while Adds lost money. Sachem was the opposite: its large New rotation barely worked, and Adds rescued it. Baupost and Whale Rock also had positive New selection and negative Adds.

### Historical Grade A and latest-release success are related but not equivalent

Casdin and Himalaya look strong under both the page dossier and Q1 attribution. Starboard is the clearest positive contradiction: it has the weakest historical median buy excess in Grade A (-5.9%) but the second-best Q1 release (+23.6%). Corvex is the clearest negative contradiction. Sachem, Light Street, RA Capital, Corvex, Soros, Altimeter, and Starboard are Grade A despite negative historical median buy excess because the grade is relative to the current manager universe. The correct interpretation is “upper cross-sectional tier in this tracker,” not “absolute proven alpha.”

### Economic impact changes the ranking

Fairholme had a better percentage return than Light Street, but its estimated new capital was only 0.76% of book, so the implied contribution was +0.09 points versus Light Street's +2.49. Casdin, Duquesne, and Starboard combined good returns with enough weight to add roughly +4.3, +4.0, and +3.6 points to their reported books. That is why they rank above smaller successful probes.

## Filing-time sensitivity

Fifteen of the 16 Q1 filings have a May 15 filing date. Baupost filed after the close on May 14, making the requested May 15 close its first investable close. Of the May 15 filings, only Whale Rock, Basswood, Duquesne, and Light Street were accepted before 4:00 p.m. ET; the other eleven were accepted at or after the close. The requested May 15 close-to-close path is therefore a clean common anchor but not always an investable one.

Using the first available close after each filing when it was accepted after the market close changes selected 60-day basket returns as follows:

- Casdin +44.7% versus +43.7% common-anchor
- Starboard +23.9% versus +23.6%
- Himalaya +8.7% versus +13.3%
- Fairholme +10.1% versus +12.3%
- Baker Bros. +13.2% versus +8.6%
- RA Capital +12.3% versus +7.9%
- Altimeter +7.6% versus +8.1%
- Soros +1.8% versus +2.0%
- Corvex -9.2% versus -8.6%

The broad ranking is stable: Casdin remains first, Corvex last, and the same middle-tier qualifications remain. The sensitivity does show why a true follower backtest must use SEC acceptance timestamps, not date-only filing labels.

## Limits and honesty rails

- A 13F reports quarter-end long holdings, not the manager's trade dates or cost basis. These are returns **after public disclosure**, not the manager's actual investment returns.
- The filing-to-return link cannot prove causality or manager skill. It is descriptive performance attribution.
- 13F omits shorts, most derivatives, private positions, non-US local listings, cash, and hedges. Total fund performance may differ sharply.
- Final book % is measured at March 31; market moves and trades before the May filing can change the true May exposure.
- Current Grade A membership is selected using a tracker updated through July 17. This creates in-sample selection and makes the study unsuitable as an independent validation of the grade.
- Returns use Yahoo adjusted closes. Of 370 resolved common-equity positions, 349 have complete 60-day paths. Those positions represent 97.6% of the cohort's estimated incremental book. Soros is the main coverage exception at 86.4%; RA Capital is 99.0%; every other fund is 100%.
- Five exchange-traded debt/preferred/warrant lines were excluded from the common-equity study. Remaining incomplete paths are printed in `excluded_or_unpriced.csv` rather than silently dropped.
