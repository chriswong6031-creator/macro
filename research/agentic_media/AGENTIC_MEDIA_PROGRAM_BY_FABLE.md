## 1. The boundary ruling (AM-R1, one time, for all three programs)

The operator's brief asks for accounts "not necessarily directly affiliated with our site" and blogs that produce "masked original content." Recon (2026-07-25 policy lanes + `research/marketing_dockets/D08_APPENDIX_X_POLICY_REDTEAM.md`) makes the honest assessment unambiguous, so it is ruled once here:

**AM-R1 (Provenance law).** Every property this program operates — X account, blog, newsletter, bot — is **honestly attributable to the Mastermind network**. Disclosure has two tiers:

- **Identity tier (always):** bio line / masthead / platform affiliation badge names the network. Distinct *content focus* is unlimited — an account may go its entire life without ever mentioning the product; a publication may have its own name, brand, and editorial identity. Concealed *ownership* is forbidden.
- **Post tier (endorsement contexts):** any post from a non-`branded` persona that names, links, screenshots, or recommends the product carries an in-post connection token (e.g. "our terminal", "we build this", "Mastermind network") — the FTC 2023 Endorsement Guides require material-connection disclosure *in the same location and format as the endorsement*; a bio line alone does not satisfy that for endorsement-style posts. Enforced by a disclosure lint in the persona validator profile (Persona §3/§5), and product screenshots count as product mentions for this rule.

Grounds (all three legs independently sufficient):

- **Platform survival.** X's platform-manipulation policy prohibits "operating multiple accounts that interact with the same or substantially similar content, or that exist to inflate or manipulate the prominence of content or accounts"; enforcement practice tolerates roughly ~10 accounts per operator for *different, non-duplicative purposes* (D08 Appendix R1 — vintage/medium-confidence figure, re-verify at Persona W3; the operative test is distinctness, not the count). Enforcement is **network-level**: 2025–26 saw a 1.7M-account bot purge, coordinated-network takedowns (~700-account ring, ~95% of the identified graph), and false-positive ban waves. Undisclosed coordinated fleets die as a graph — including the official accounts sharing registration/behavioral fingerprints. A burned account is negative ROI (the guerrilla doctrine's own law: "a governed footprint before a large one").
- **Legal exposure.** FTC Endorsement Guides (2023): same-location/format disclosure of material connections. 16 CFR 465 (effective 2024-10-21): fabricated testimonials by "someone who does not exist, such as AI-generated fake reviews" banned with civil penalties per violation — fake "independent trader" personas vouching for the product are squarely inside it; §465.8 separately bans buying/selling fake engagement indicators. A paid financial-signals business is a high-salience target.
- **Brand physics.** The entire moat (`TRENDSPIDER_GROWTH_SEO_AND_GUERRILLA_MARKETING_INTELLIGENCE_FOR_FABLE.md` §1, guerrilla doctrine §1) is *accountability* — receipts, falsifiers, public grading. "Ran a covert bot farm" is the single story that deletes that moat retroactively. The compliant version is not the constrained version; it is the only version whose reach **compounds** instead of resetting to zero at each ban wave.

**What AM-R1 kills** (registered in `research/DO_NOT_REBUILD.md` §1 this PR): undisclosed-affiliation persona fleets; cross-account amplification rings (self-RT/quote-boost rotations, synchronized posting); fake-human personas presented as real independent people (incl. testimonial-style product claims or fabricated personal trading experience); "masked rewrite" content spinning of third-party articles; registering domain variants whose purpose is spam-flag evasion or manufactured backlinks; **and purchased, exchanged, or incentivized engagement of any kind** (followers, likes, reposts, views, engagement pods, reciprocal-boost arrangements, paid third-party promotion without in-post #ad disclosure — 16 CFR 465.8 + X platform-manipulation policy).

**What AM-R1 explicitly permits** (this is most of the operator's ambition): fictional/stylized **character personas** with distinct voices (corporate desk, meme cartoon, stylized archetypes) that are disclosed network members; **labeled automated feeds** (X "Automated" label) as a lawful high-count class if ever wanted; multiple **real publications** with their own brands and disclosed ownership; unlimited content diversity; watermarked charts, signal mentions, and product screenshots (screenshots are subject to the post-tier disclosure rule above); aggressive scale of *quality*.

## 2. The scale ruling (AM-R2)

"~100 accounts" is re-based from *handles* to **surfaces**. The honest ceiling for one operator entity on X today is ~10 well-differentiated editorial accounts (a **review trigger**, not a law of nature — D08 R1, medium confidence, re-verify at W3; X's operative test is genuinely-distinct, non-duplicative purpose per account); past that, scale comes from surfaces platforms treat as legitimate: each new *publication* brings its own account; X **Verified Organizations affiliation badges** are the sanctioned mechanism for a visibly-related account family if/when we pay for it; labeled automated utility feeds are the sanctioned high-N class. Path: 6 desks (current config) → 10 (persona pilots, earned via D08 ramp) → 15–25 *surfaces* only as publications/affiliation justify → beyond that only with an explicit operator ruling and platform mechanisms that exist then. Every step is promotion-gated by scorecards (AM-R7), never front-loaded. 100 *surfaces* (accounts + publications + syndication + embeds + community bots) is reachable inside this law; 100 covert handles is not, and is DNR'd.

## 3. The value + transformation rulings

**AM-R3 (Value law — operator's own, extended).** No post or article ships without standalone value; repetition is spam even when compliant. Enforcement is mechanical, not aspirational: D08 Sentinel gates (cross-account near-dup shingles, caps, lexicon, disclosure, cherry-pick) + the voice-v3 cheese-test validator + post-time tape gates extend to every new lane; blogs get the equivalent press validators (Media §5). Engine-template lanes are capped per persona (tilt shares) so no account degenerates into a ticker-tape; LLM lanes must cite receipts.

**AM-R4 (Transformation law — applies to EVERY surface that renders third-party-derived text, including Chronicle events and the timeline page, not just press desks).** Aggregation re-reports **facts** with attribution + link and adds our data/analysis; it never close-paraphrases expression or lifts structure/ledes (AP v. Meltwater is the operative precedent — excerpt-heavy redistribution lost fair use). Third-party-sourced Chronicle events obey per-event constraints (Chronicle §2.1: fact-string length cap, publisher named + linked wherever the fact renders, no headline/lede reproduction). Institutional coverage works from our own catalog summaries with minimal quotation (≤2 short attributed quotes per piece); PDFs are never redistributed through press surfaces. **Corrected premise (rev 2):** vault summaries are *registration-walled on our site, not public* — so republishing `summary_points` into the public `/blog/` estate would widen the open MarketDesk licensing exposure (memory: research-vault-program). Ruling: the press Research Desk writes *commentary about* reports (facts re-stated in our words + short quotes), never wholesale summary republication, and its W1 go-live requires the operator to acknowledge the standing licensing flag (Media §0).

**AM-R5 (Chronicle constitution).** The timeline engine's **derived layer is rebuildable** from canonical committed ledgers/artifacts — never a hand-maintained parallel knowledge store (CXI-R12). Its **LLM prose layer is stored separately** (`data/chronicle/llm/`), is declared irreproducible, is never a truth source, carries only references into the derived layer, and its loss degrades but never breaks any consumer (Chronicle §2.2). Engines write events; LLMs write prose/threads/links between *existing* events and may only de-escalate — they never originate signals, scores, or escalations, and never raise salience (house epistemics law; deterministic salience rules live in the spine compiler). Audience fence: there is **no pre-existing shared public-safe filter** — Chronicle defines its own per-adapter public-safe field projection (modelled on `engine/research_vault/catalog.py` `_ITEM_FIELDS` and the marketing governor's public-safe subset), with a CI assertion, as a W0 deliverable *before* the brain lobe registers; repo internals never enter events (CXI-R23 untouched). Ledgers advance nightly-only; LLM compaction runs off the render path under an explicit token budget, writing staging artifacts the nightly promotes.

## 5. Answers to the operator's direct questions

1. **Multiple domains (`mastermindx.co/.info/.org`) to avoid X spam flags?** No. Stated-purpose evasion is the tell: Google's link-spam and scaled-content-abuse policies target exactly this shape (and buying aged domains for their residual signals adds expired-domain abuse), while X's detector is coordination behavior, not domain string diversity. Near-duplicate domains are also weak brands. Lawful form: **one flagship estate on `www.mastermind-x.com/blog/`** (compounds the money domain's authority; already crawler-public in `app/regwall.py` PUBLIC_PREFIXES + Caddy matchers — zero boundary work), then *genuinely distinct* publications (own name, own editorial identity, "A Mastermind Media publication" masthead) if/when a desk earns spin-out (Media §7 gates).
2. **How many X accounts can post one domain's links?** No published number exists; the enforced pattern is *duplicative content + coordinated amplification*, and link posts are both reach-suppressed (consistent 2025–26 reporting) and API-taxed (~$0.20/post with link vs $0.015 without on 2026 pay-per-use). Practice: **one primary account per publication**, native-first posts (value in-post, link in reply/card). A publication's own account and the flagship may each share a piece once with distinct framing; all other cross-citation is asynchronous (>24h) and radar-checked — never substantially-similar text for the same URL across accounts. Zerohedge's one-account model is the correct read of the platform.
3. **Brand the accounts how?** Persona names + character identities are free; the bio carries the network line (e.g. "Part of the @MastermindX research network · AI-assisted"); unattended-posting accounts carry X's Automated label pointing at the flagship; product-endorsing posts from non-branded personas carry the in-post token (AM-R1 post tier). No persona name/handle/avatar may evoke a real person, firm, or publication (X PCF regime applies name-level labels to real-entity depictions). Character ≠ concealment.
4. **Engine-only content = spam risk?** Yes when repetitive — the fix is already house: tilt caps per kind, Sentinel near-dup radar (extended cross-account at post time — Persona W2), template pools with rotation floors, and per-persona voice so the same fact never renders as the same sentence twice.
5. **Long-run thesis tracking for persona accounts?** Yes — per-persona append-only **thesis memory ledgers** (open calls seeded only from calibrated surfaces, invalidation levels, scheduled reopens; nightly-advanced, Growth-Science-graded) double as the receipts engine and the anti-repetition brake (Persona §4). This is the account-level version of the claim-passport/outcome-reopen machinery the lobe already defines.
6. **Do the parts together or separately?** Chronicle first (shared brainstem), then Persona W1 and Media W1 in parallel — both consume chronicle packs but degrade gracefully to today's voice-v3 floor without them. Detailed order in §6.
