# Why the risk read went "all-clear" at the top — 2026-06-22 → 06-23

**Question.** On **2026-06-22** the downstream risk read flipped to "calm / all clear,"
and the **next session (06-23) a real volatility event hit** (tech ETFs −3% to −7%).
Why did the read go benign right at the top?

**Answer (one line).** It was not a data glitch — it was a **structural property** of
the gauges. The only visible thing that "flipped" on 06-22 was the **capitulation /
washout flag clearing** (`capitulation.active`, logged as `cap_active`, went
**True → False**); the dislocation verdict had been **"calm" the entire window and was
never elevated**. With the last non-benign marker gone, and **every gauge that feeds the
downstream classifier being coincident or lagging by construction** — while the one
forward-looking gauge (breadth divergence) is firewalled out and the three *firing*
leading tells (GEX, cross-asset absorption at the **97th percentile**, breadth
divergence) are excluded — the contract printed "all clear" precisely at the top. A
**Juneteenth-driven staleness window** (asof `2026-06-18` read through the entire 06-22
session, with no freshness stamp) compounded it.

### Confirm / correct the recon hypothesis
- **CONFIRMED** — "the apparent flip came from a transient capitulation/relief read
  clearing." Yes: `capitulation.active` (= `capitulation_score ≥ 1`,
  `engine/conditions.py` L583-585) cleared on 06-22.
- **CORRECTED** — there was **no `elevated → calm` flip in the dislocation gate**. The
  verdict was `calm` on every row. `dislocation_active` is a pure OR of four *realized*
  stress triggers (`engine/dislocation.py` L400-408) — **none ever fired** in the
  window. So the perceived "risk read going benign" was *only* the last lingering
  marker (the washout flag) going quiet, read by a naive consumer as "back to normal."

### What `cap_active` actually was, and why it cleared
`capitulation_score` (`engine/conditions.py` L289-304) sums three contrarian-bounce
legs: VRP %ile > 0.90, VIX > 30, and a COT-ES net-spec **washout** (< 10th %ile, 3y).
With VIX 16–18 (< 30) and VRP 0.13–0.30 (< 0.90) the whole of 06-12…06-18, the only
firing leg was the **COT positioning washout**. By 06-22 that positioning leg
normalised → score 0 → `cap_active=False`.

Note the irony: capitulation is a **contrarian / risk-ON** ("buyable washout") marker,
not a risk-OFF one. Its *presence* was the only thing keeping the contract from reading
fully blank; its *clearing* read as "all clear" — the opposite of caution.

## 2. Why every leading tell was invisible to the risk read

The downstream classifier keys off five gauges. Each was benign on 06-22 **because each
is coincident or lagging by construction**:

| # | gauge | 06-22 read | nature | why it couldn't lead |
|---|-------|-----------|--------|----------------------|
| 1 | `dislocation.verdict` | `calm` | **coincident** | Pure OR of *realized* triggers: VIX>30 / SPY-dd≤−10% / VRP>0.90 / VIX-term≥1.0 backwardation (`dislocation.py` L400-408). Cannot fire until price/vol has *already* broken. |
| 2 | `drawdown_risk.band` | `low`, "8%" | **lagging** | 4 slow macro/credit legs: recession_risk, NFCI, EBP, HY OAS — and **HY OAS was compressing**. The "8%" is the *unconditional base rate*, branded as a forward probability (`conditions.py` L560-563). |
| 3 | `systemic_stress.state` | `calm` | **coincident** | OFR FSI percentile (≈0.06). The module's own evidence string (`conditions.py` L419-423) literally says: *"a coincident gauge."* |
| 4 | `risk_appetite.vix_term_state` | `contango (calm)` | **coincident** | VIX/VIX3M level (0.838). The most coincident vol gauge there is. |
| 5 | `complacency.state` | `watch` | **leading (firewalled)** | **This gauge DID see it:** `breadth_div=True` (SPY ≈1.4% from its high while %>200dma sat ≈38.8th %ile). But the in-code contract (`conditions.py` L306-314) firewalls it: *"surfaced as CONTEXT only — never folded into recession_risk / drawdown_risk / RORO / MRS / any axis."* |

### The decisive proof it's coincident, not early
On **06-23 — the event day itself** — VIX 19.5, SPY dd −3.2%, VRP 0.30, VIX-term 0.838.
**All four dislocation triggers were still off** (19.5 < 30; −3.2% > −10%; 0.30 < 0.90;
0.838 < 1.0), so the verdict **stayed `calm`**. A gauge that prints "calm" *during* a
−3% to −7% tech drawdown is not an early-warning system; it is a realized-stress
detector with extreme thresholds.

### The three firing LEADING signals — all excluded from the risk gauges
- **GEX / dealer gamma** — `engine/gex_engine.py`; `grep -E 'gex|gamma' engine/conditions.py`
  returns nothing. (Now surfaced as `market_gamma` in the contract by a sibling session,
  but it does not feed any of the five risk gauges.)
- **Cross-asset absorption** — `cross_asset.verdict="concentrated"`,
  `absorption_pctile_5y = 0.97` (**97th percentile**). Its own evidence string
  (Kritzman-Page 2010) calls high/rising absorption *"a fragile one-factor regime that
  has historically led drawdowns."* Feeds no gauge.
- **Breadth divergence** — firewalled display-only (row 5 above).

## 3. Staleness — the Juneteenth compounding factor

- **Build cadence** (`.github/workflows/daily.yml`): `cron: "40 22 * * 1-5"` — once a
  day at **22:40 UTC, after the US close.**
- **2026-06-19 was a Friday — Juneteenth, an NYSE holiday.** 06-20 Sat, 06-21 Sun. So
  the **last completed session before Mon 06-22 was Thu 06-18.**
- The 06-22 build only lands at 22:40 UTC on 06-22 — **after** the 06-22 close.
  Therefore for the **entire 06-22 trading day** the freshest contract a downstream
  consumer could read was **asof `2026-06-18`** (VIX 16.4, dd −1.4%, VRP 0.13) — four
  calendar days old — and `latest.json` **carried no freshness / asof-age stamp**, so a
  stale Thursday session was consumed as a live Monday "all clear."
- This **compounded but did not cause** the miss: even the live 06-22 and 06-23 builds
  printed `calm` (Section 2). Staleness widened the blind window; the structural gauge
  design is the root cause.

## 4. Root cause (definitive)

The downstream risk read went benign at the top because of **how the gauges are built,
not because of bad inputs**:

1. **Visible trigger of the "flip" (dated):** the capitulation / washout flag cleared —
   `capitulation.active` / `cap_active` **True → False on 2026-06-22** — removing the
   last non-benign marker. The dislocation verdict was `calm` the whole window; it was
   never elevated.
2. **Why nothing replaced it:** all five gauges feeding the classifier are **coincident**
   (dislocation OR-gate, systemic_stress OFR-FSI, vix_term level) or **lagging**
   (drawdown_risk macro/credit composite). The only **leading** gauge that perceived the
   fragility — `complacency.breadth_div` — is **firewalled display-only**.
3. **Why the real leading tells were invisible:** GEX, cross-asset absorption (97th
   %ile, "concentrated"), and breadth divergence — all firing — are **excluded from the
   risk gauges by construction**.
4. **Compounding:** a Juneteenth-holiday staleness window meant the 06-22 session read a
   four-day-old (06-18) contract with **no freshness stamp**.

## 5. Fixes shipped in this change (make the *existing* gauges honest)

> The new *leading-fragility composite* is **deferred** to the sibling "vol-shock
> scorecard" session — this change does not build a competing composite. Scope here =
> diagnosis + honesty of the existing gauges + the `lead_lag` tags the scorecard/bot
> will consume.

- **`drawdown_risk` no longer presents a lagging read as an 8% forward probability.** It
  is relabelled as a **macro/credit composite (lagging)**, the low-band figure is
  flagged as the **unconditional base rate** (`is_base_rate`), and an honest
  `lead_lag: "lagging"` + bilingual label is attached. (Relabel, not a new fast leg —
  the validated gauge is left intact per house rules.)
- **`lead_lag` tag on each of the five gauges** (`dislocation`=coincident,
  `systemic_stress`=coincident, `drawdown_risk`=lagging, `risk_appetite.vix_term`=coincident,
  `complacency`=leading) so the bot can **discount coincident gauges and weight leading
  ones**.
- **`complacency.breadth_div` promoted to a one-way risk-OFF caution**
  (`breadth_div_caution`) — surfaced in the contract **without** contaminating the
  validated quad / RORO / MRS (the firewall is preserved; only the *visibility* changes).
- **Staleness addressed** — `latest.json` now carries a `freshness` block
  (`asof`, `built_at`, `age_sessions`, `stale`) so a consumer can refuse to treat a
  holiday-stale contract as a live all-clear.

**Acceptance:** replaying 06-19..06-23 no longer prints an unqualified "all clear" on
06-22 — `drawdown_risk` reads "macro/credit (lagging), low = base rate," the
`breadth_div` caution is raised, and the contract is flagged stale through the holiday
window.
