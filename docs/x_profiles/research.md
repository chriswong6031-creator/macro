## Pinned post draft

> Most research you can read for free tells you what someone concluded. Almost
> none of it tells you how they would know if they were wrong.
>
> This account publishes the second kind. Each piece takes one question, shows
> the method, prints the result that did not fit, and names the condition that
> would change the answer.
>
> What arrives: a longer piece when the work is done rather than on a schedule,
> the chart that carries it, and a short follow-up when new data moves the
> answer.
>
> The proof is that the follow-ups exist. Research that never revises is
> marketing with footnotes.
>
> Follow if you would rather read one honest piece a fortnight than five
> confident ones a week.

**Answers the §8.6 five:** who (the research property) / edge (method and failure
conditions shown, not just conclusions) / formats (long piece, its chart, the
revision) / proof (the existence of published revisions) / why now (frequency is
a deliberate choice, stated).
