STRESS-DAY PLAYBOOK — when everything is red, the pattern of the red names the day.
The families, side by side (memorize the contrast — it's the fastest correct answer on the desk):
- GROWTH SCARE: stocks ↓, oil ↓, yields ↓ across the curve, long bonds ↑, gold firm, defensives outperform. The classic. Duration works as the hedge.
- INFLATION / SUPPLY SHOCK: stocks ↓, oil ↑, LONG yields ↑ while the front end holds or rallies, long bonds ↓, breakevens ↑. Both halves of 60/40 lose; cash and energy are the only shelter. NOT a flight-to-safety failure — bonds are falling because they're the asset being repriced.
- TERM-PREMIUM / FISCAL SCARE: long yields ↑ hard WITHOUT an oil or breakeven move — often after an ugly auction or supply news. Same stock/bond pain as the inflation family; the tell is quiet breakevens with rising real yields.
- HAWKISH REPRICING: SHORT yields ↑ hardest (front end leads), curve flattens, growth stocks hit worst. A central-bank message day, not a growth event.
- LIQUIDATION / DELEVERAGING: everything correlates — stocks, bonds, gold, crypto all ↓, vol and credit gapping, moves violent and pattern-less intraday. Forced flows; fundamentals are on hold until the selling exhausts.
Procedure:
1) Place today in ONE family using the contrasts above; name the single print that placed it ("front end rallying while the 20-year sells off — that's the inflation family, not a growth scare").
2) Show the user the family it is NOT, in two lines — the contrast is the clearest teaching device on a confusing day and inoculates against the next bad headline they read.
3) Answer the asset the user asked about from the family's logic (in the inflation family, long-duration bond funds fall mechanically — that's the design, not a malfunction).
4) Name the transition to watch: families hand off (inflation shock → growth scare once demand cracks; liquidation → whichever story remains when the forced selling ends). Say which handoff is live and its tell.
Invalidation of this playbook itself: a −0.5% drift is not a stress day — don't escalate an ordinary red session into a regime event.
