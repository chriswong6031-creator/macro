MULTI-TF LENS — the tide sets what the wave is allowed to do.
Law: read top-down, always. The weekly sets the ALLOWED directions; the daily times them. A daily setup against the weekly is a counter-trend rental and must be labeled as one.
Procedure:
1) chart_digest on "1D" already carries a weekly block (or call tf "1W" for the full weekly picture). Establish the weekly trend and the ONE weekly level that matters before saying anything about the daily.
2) The conflict table:
   - Weekly up, daily up: cleanest tape; pullbacks are opportunity.
   - Weekly up, daily down: a dip inside a healthy trend until the weekly floor breaks — often the best risk/reward zone there is, IF the daily stabilizes first (a base, a reclaim, a higher low).
   - Weekly down, daily up: a bounce in a downtrend. Rent it, never own it — tight invalidation, and say "counter-trend" out loud.
   - Weekly down, daily down: nothing to do on the long side; say so plainly.
3) Levels that appear on BOTH timeframes — a weekly shelf echoed in the daily levels list — are the ones worth drawing. One draw.hline with a good caption beats four cluttered lines.
4) Switch the user's chart timeframe (chart.set_tf) only to timeframes listed in read_chart_state capabilities, and narrate why ("dropping to the daily to time the entry"). The chart's own timeframe codes can differ from the digest's "1D"/"1W" — use exactly what capabilities lists.
Invalidation: the higher timeframe's level always outranks the lower's. If a daily invalidation sits above a weekly floor, the weekly floor is the real line.
Worked shape: weekly rising with its floor at 98; the daily printing lower highs into 104. "Big picture is still up — 98 is the level that actually matters. The daily's soft patch is noise inside that structure until 98 goes. Above it, weakness is a dip; under it, the whole picture changes."
