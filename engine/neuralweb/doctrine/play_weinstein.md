STAGE PLAYBOOK — where in its life is this name? (wired to the desk's live stage engine)
Definitions: the desk classifies every name weekly into four stages. 1 = basing (flat after a decline — sellers exhausted, nothing proven yet). 2 = advancing (price above a rising long-term average — the only stage that pays trend-followers). 3 = topping (flat after an advance — supply showing up). 4 = declining (below a falling average — no bottom-fishing). Stages come from the ENGINE, not your eyeball.
Procedure:
1) get_stage_peers(symbol) → the engine's stage read ("Stage 2 — advancing"), weeks in stage, its technical-attributes score, relative strength, and where it stands in its industry (a percentile). read_stage_analysis → the market-wide picture: what share of names are advancing, the market "weather", today's stage changes, and the current Stage-2 board (ask it for fresh-only names when age matters). These are context the engine publishes — not a buy signal by themselves.
2) Tie the stage to the chart: a Stage-2 name's pullbacks to support are buy-zone material; the SAME pattern in Stage 4 is a bounce to sell into. The stage decides which side of the chart you are allowed to argue.
3) Age matters: a fresh Stage 2 (roughly the first ten weeks) is where the engine focuses. A Stage 2 that has run for a year and gone vertical belongs to the topping-watch conversation. Say which chapter the name is in.
4) Stage CHANGES reported by the tools — a breakout into Stage 2, a slide into Stage 4 — are the headline of any read. Lead with them.
5) The engine's word beats your impression: if the chart "looks" strong but the tool says Stage 3, present the tension honestly and side with the engine, using the chart only to define the levels that would confirm or repair the read.
Invalidation: a stage thesis dies when the engine reclassifies, or when price closes through the level that defines the stage (the base top, the long-term average).
Worked shape: get_stage_peers → Stage 2, six weeks in, industry group leading. "Early in an advance by the desk's own engine — six weeks in, and its group is leading. The playbook says buy pullbacks that hold, not chase strength. The floor to own it against is the breakout shelf at 47."
