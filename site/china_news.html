Recently, the bond market has finally broken out of its persistent 'three lows' — low volume, low volatility, low stock-bond correlation — charting a new course: ultra-long-term bonds have notably strengthened. On July 27, the main contract for 30-year government bond futures briefly rose to 115.18 yuan, an 8-month high. Multiple institutions believe this breakout in ultra-long bonds stems from strong momentum accumulated during the prior low-volatility phase, combined with weak fundamentals, repeatedly dashed reflation expectations, PBOC liquidity support, and institutional repricing of term premium compression on the ultra-long end. However, with fund durations high and government bond supply pressures not yet fully realized, whether the rally can continue depends on whether allocation desks can take over from trading desks.
