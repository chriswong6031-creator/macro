After suspending sales for several months, four major state-owned banks have recently resumed issuing five-year large-denomination certificates of deposit (CDs), with top annual rates of 1.6%. A China Securities Journal reporter visiting bank branches found ample quota for five-year CDs, tepid investor demand, and weak promotion by bank relationship managers. Industry insiders believe the state-owned banks are restarting five-year CDs to lock in long-term funds, offset pressure from maturing large time deposits, and extend liability duration—a phased measure to stabilize liabilities during a rate decline cycle, not a signal of deposit rates bottoming out. Due to sustained net interest margin pressure, banks cannot issue long-term CDs on a large scale.
