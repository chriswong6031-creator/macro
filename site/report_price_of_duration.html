The Price of Duration久期的价格

The AI boom did not end. The debt problem did not wait for 2027. They collided. As Treasury, hyperscalers, utilities and the rest of the AI buildout compete for the same capital, long yields have become the market's governing price. Gold and Bitcoin have already begun reacting to what comes next. The question is no longer simply whether the Fed cuts—it is who absorbs the duration, at what price, and what policymakers do when that price becomes unacceptable.AI 热潮没有结束，债务问题也没有等到 2027 年——两者已经相撞。随着财政部、超大规模云厂商、公用事业公司与 AI 建设链的其余参与者争夺同一池资本，长端收益率已成为市场的治理价格。黄金与比特币已经开始回应下一步。问题不再只是美联储是否降息，而是谁来吸收久期、以什么价格吸收，以及当这个价格变得不可接受时，政策制定者会做什么。

1The anomaly异常信号

There are moments when markets stop behaving according to the story investors have been using to explain them. Those moments matter because the anomaly is often more informative than the trend.

This is one of those moments.

Gold is surging even though long-term interest rates are still high. Bitcoin has moved sharply before the easing cycle we once thought it would need. The dollar has weakened while the United States is still offering some of the highest real yields in the developed world. The 30-year Treasury yield has pushed into territory not seen since before the global financial crisis. At the same time, the VIX remains subdued, broad high-yield credit spreads remain calm, and the U.S. equity market is not behaving like a system in liquidation.

If this were simply an inflation scare, the dollar should be stronger and gold should be struggling under the weight of high real rates.

If this were simply a growth scare, long Treasuries should be rallying as investors seek safety.

If this were already a fiscal crisis, credit spreads, volatility and funding markets should look far worse than they do.

And if this were simply another chapter of the AI boom, the bond market would be a sideshow rather than the mechanism increasingly setting the price of the entire trade.

None of those explanations is sufficient on its own.

What has changed is more fundamental.

The United States is trying to finance two enormous projects at the same time.

The first is the federal government itself: persistent deficits, a vast stock of debt that must continually be refinanced, growing interest expense, defense spending, industrial policy and the normal obligations of a large state.

The second is the artificial-intelligence buildout: data centers, chips, memory, power generation, transformers, cooling systems, networking, land, construction, grid upgrades and the increasingly large amount of debt and outside capital required to finance all of it.

Both ultimately arrive at the same market.

They both need capital.

They both need investors willing to lock money away for years.

And when the amount of capital demanded rises faster than the amount of capital willingly supplied at yesterday's price, the price of long-term money has to rise until the market balances.

That price is the yield.

有些时刻，市场会突然不再按照投资者一直用来解释它的故事运行。这些时刻之所以重要，是因为异常往往比趋势更有信息量。

现在正是这样的时刻。

长期利率仍然高企，黄金却在急涨。比特币在我们曾认为它必须等待的宽松周期到来之前就已大幅走强。美国仍提供发达经济体中最高的一档实际收益率，美元却在走弱。30 年期美债收益率已进入全球金融危机前以来未见的区域。与此同时，VIX 依旧受抑，广义高收益债利差仍然平静，美国股市也完全不像一个正在被迫清算的系统。

如果这只是一场通胀恐慌，美元理应更强，而黄金理应承受高实际利率的重压。

如果这只是一场增长恐慌，投资者寻求安全时，长期美债理应上涨。

如果这已经是一场财政危机，信用利差、波动率与融资市场理应比现在糟糕得多。

如果这只不过是 AI 热潮的又一章，债券市场就应该只是配角，而不该越来越像是为整笔交易定价的核心机制。

这些解释没有任何一个能单独说清当前市场。

真正变化的东西更加根本。

美国正在同时为两个庞大工程融资。

第一个是联邦政府本身：持续赤字、必须不断再融资的巨额存量债务、不断增长的利息支出、国防开支、产业政策，以及一个大型国家的正常义务。

第二个是人工智能建设：数据中心、芯片、存储、电力、变压器、散热系统、网络、土地、施工、电网升级，以及为这一切融资所需、规模日益庞大的债务与外部资本。

两者最终来到同一个市场。

两者都需要资本。

两者都需要投资者愿意把资金锁定多年。

当资本需求的增长快于投资者按昨天价格自愿提供资本的增长时，长期资金的价格就必须上升，直到市场重新出清。

这个价格，就是收益率。

This is the idea at the center of this report:这就是本报告的中心观点：

The governing thesis · [I]统领全文的论点 · [I]

The next phase of the market is being decided by the price of duration.市场的下一阶段，正由久期的价格决定。

That sounds abstract. It is not.

The long-term yield determines what a mortgage costs. It determines what a data center must earn to justify being built. It determines how expensive it is for a hyperscaler to issue another bond. It changes the present value of a software company's future profits. It changes whether a pension fund prefers U.S. Treasuries, Japanese government bonds or something else. It changes the interest bill paid by the federal government. It changes the opportunity cost of owning gold. It changes the value of liquidity. It changes which AI projects survive and which were only economic when money was cheap.

For most of the last decade, investors could treat capital as background infrastructure. There was always a price for money, but that price was low enough that the market rarely had to ask which great ambitions could actually afford to be financed.

The AI boom was born in that world and then accelerated far beyond it.

Now the capital market is beginning to push back.

That does not mean the AI boom is over.

It means the AI boom has reached the stage where financing itself becomes part of the story.

And it does not mean the United States is in an imminent sovereign crisis.

It means the market is beginning to discover the yield at which the government's financing needs, the AI industry's financing needs and global investors' willingness to absorb duration begin to conflict.

That distinction is crucial.

The broad U.S. credit market is still functioning. Volatility remains relatively low. Treasury auctions continue to clear. Foreign investors have not abandoned U.S. government debt. Equities remain liquid, and market breadth can still improve after selloffs.

The system has not broken.

But the cost of keeping it all financed has become high enough that policymakers are beginning to reveal how they will respond.

Treasury has expanded long-duration buybacks intended to support market functioning. Stablecoin regulation is creating a potentially significant future buyer base for short-term government paper. Money-market funds already provide enormous demand for bills. The Federal Reserve, meanwhile, remains constrained by inflation and cannot simply erase the long-end problem without risking a new inflationary response.

That gives the market two reaction functions instead of one.

The Fed reacts to inflation, labor and expectations.

Treasury reacts to the functioning and cost of the debt market.

Those two objectives can align.

They can also conflict.

And it is in that conflict that gold, Bitcoin, the dollar and the next phase of the AI trade are beginning to move.

The old market question was simple:

When will the Fed cut?

The new question is harder:

Who absorbs the duration, at what price, and what happens when that price becomes economically or politically unacceptable?

Everything else in this report follows from that question.

这听上去很抽象，其实一点也不。

长期收益率决定按揭贷款的成本；决定一座数据中心必须赚到多少才值得建设；决定超大规模云厂商再发一笔债有多昂贵；改变软件公司未来利润的现值；改变养老基金究竟偏好美国国债、日本国债还是其他资产；改变联邦政府支付的利息账单；改变持有黄金的机会成本；改变流动性的价值；也改变哪些 AI 项目能够存活，哪些项目只在资金便宜时才有经济性。

过去十年的大部分时间，投资者可以把资本当作背景基础设施。资金当然一直有价格，但这个价格足够低，市场很少需要追问：哪些宏伟抱负实际上负担得起融资？

AI 热潮诞生于那个世界，随后又远远超出了那个世界的边界。

如今，资本市场开始反推回来。

这不意味着 AI 热潮已经结束。

它意味着 AI 热潮已经走到融资本身成为故事一部分的阶段。

这也不意味着美国正面临迫在眉睫的主权危机。

它意味着市场开始寻找这样一个收益率：在这个价位上，政府的融资需求、AI 行业的融资需求，以及全球投资者吸收久期的意愿开始互相冲突。

这个区别至关重要。

美国广义信用市场仍在正常运转。波动率相对较低。美债拍卖仍能顺利出清。外国投资者并未抛弃美国政府债务。股票仍具流动性，市场广度在抛售后仍可能改善。

系统没有坏。

但维持这一切融资的成本已经高到足以迫使政策制定者开始显露反应方式。

美国财政部扩大了旨在支持市场功能的长期债券回购。稳定币监管正在为短期政府债务创造一个潜在的重要未来买家群体。货币市场基金已经为国库券提供巨额需求。与此同时，美联储仍受通胀约束，不能在不冒重新激起通胀反应的风险下，简单抹掉长端问题。

于是，市场面对的不再是一套反应函数，而是两套。

美联储对通胀、就业与预期作出反应。

财政部对国债市场的功能与融资成本作出反应。

这两个目标可以一致。

也可以冲突。

黄金、比特币、美元与 AI 交易下一阶段的变化，正在这种冲突中发生。

过去的市场问题很简单：

美联储何时降息？

现在的问题更难：

谁来吸收久期、以什么价格吸收；当这个价格在经济或政治上变得不可接受时，又会发生什么？

本报告其余全部内容，都由这个问题展开。

2What changed since our last map上一张地图之后，哪些判断变了

We did not arrive at this view with a blank sheet of paper.

Over the previous two months, Mastermind published a series of reports trying to map the transition out of the first phase of the AI boom and into the next one. Those reports were written in real time, before the market supplied much of the evidence we have now.

Some of the calls were good.

Some were wrong.

More importantly, several were directionally right but temporally wrong: we identified the correct mechanism and then placed it too far into the future.

That is the type of forecasting error worth studying because it reveals exactly what the old model was missing.

The old map got several big things right:

Our earlier work correctly argued that the first stage of AI leadership had become dangerously crowded. Semiconductors and memory equities had run far enough that strong fundamentals no longer guaranteed good entries. That warning mattered. Korean memory stocks later suffered violent deleveraging even as the underlying HBM and DRAM economics remained exceptionally strong.

We were also right that the AI boom itself was not ending. The physical buildout continued to accelerate. Capital kept flowing into power generation, electrical equipment, cooling, networking and the physical systems required to turn model demand into functioning infrastructure.

We were right that the Magnificent Seven would stop behaving like a single trade. The difference between Microsoft, Meta, Alphabet, Apple and Nvidia increasingly comes down to how each company finances AI investment, how much of that investment converts into current revenue and how much free cash flow remains after the spending.

We were right to keep the structural gold thesis alive after the June washout, and we were right to argue that Bitcoin accumulation should begin before the perfect cycle low rather than wait for a single capitulation print everyone could see coming.

We were also right that Hong Kong's technology complex could outperform the miserable story told by China's household and property economy.

But the record was not clean.

It got several things materially wrong or incomplete:

Our base case for Brent returning to a structurally lower range was invalidated by the renewed Iran shock.

Our first explicit attempt to identify Meta as an asymmetric megacap haven failed its opening test. The evidence window we said should confirm the thesis instead produced a collapse in free cash flow and a sharp selloff.

We treated the memory stock cycle and the memory fundamental cycle too similarly. The equities had become crowded and vulnerable; the underlying profit pool had not yet rolled over. Those are different clocks.

We also placed far too much authority on an October 2026 multi-asset low because historical midterm-year patterns and crypto cycle timing seemed to line up there.

That was too rigid.

The most important mistake, however, was larger than any one asset call.

We expected the sequence to look roughly like this:

hawkish pressure first, then an autumn correction, then disinflation, then monetary relief, then a renewed debasement trade, then a larger sovereign-term-premium problem in 2027 or 2028.

The market refused to wait.

The debt-market stress arrived early.

The AI financing burden arrived early.

Gold's next leg arrived early.

Bitcoin's liquidity response arrived early.

The currency adjustment arrived early.

And energy inflation returned before the disinflation glide path had finished doing its work.

The processes we had imagined as sequential began overlapping.

我们并不是拿着一张白纸走到今天这个判断。

过去两个月里，Mastermind 连续发布报告，试图描绘市场如何走出 AI 热潮第一阶段、进入下一阶段。这些报告是在市场尚未提供今天大部分证据时实时写成的。

有些判断不错。

有些判断错了。

更重要的是，有些判断方向正确、时间却错了：我们找对了机制，却把它安排在过远的未来。

这种预测错误值得研究，因为它准确暴露了旧模型缺少什么。

旧地图做对了几件大事：

我们早先正确指出，AI 第一阶段的领导权已经变得危险地拥挤。半导体与存储股涨得太远，以至于强劲基本面不再自动意味着好买点。这个警告很重要。后来，韩国存储股经历了猛烈去杠杆，而底层 HBM 与 DRAM 的经济性依然异常强劲。

我们也正确判断 AI 热潮本身并未结束。物理建设继续加速。资本不断流入发电、电气设备、散热、网络，以及把模型需求变成可运行基础设施所需的实体系统。

我们正确判断“七巨头”不再作为单一交易运行。微软、Meta、Alphabet、Apple 与 Nvidia 之间的差异，越来越取决于各自如何为 AI 投资融资、这些投资有多少转化为当期收入，以及支出之后还剩多少自由现金流。

我们正确地在 6 月洗盘后保留黄金的结构性论点，也正确主张应该在完美周期低点出现之前就开始积累比特币，而不是等待一个所有人都能看见的单一投降式低点。

我们也正确判断，香港科技板块的表现可以好过中国居民与房地产经济所讲述的惨淡故事。

但记录并不干净。

它有几个判断在实质上错了，或者不完整：

伊朗冲击再起，使我们关于布伦特原油回归结构性低位区间的基准情形失效。

我们第一次明确把 Meta 识别为不对称巨头避风港的尝试，没有通过开场检验。我们说应当确认论点的证据窗口，反而出现自由现金流坍塌与股价大跌。

我们把存储股时钟与存储基本面时钟看得过于相似。股票已经拥挤而脆弱，底层利润池却尚未见顶。这是两只不同的钟。

我们还赋予 2026 年 10 月多资产低点过高权威，因为历史中期选举年模式与加密周期时点看似在那里重合。

这太僵硬了。

然而，最重要的错误比任何单一资产判断都大。

我们原本预期大致顺序如下：

先出现鹰派压力，再出现秋季调整，继而反通胀、货币缓解、贬值交易重新启动，最后在 2027 或 2028 年出现更大的主权期限溢价问题。

市场拒绝等待。

债券市场压力提前到来。

AI 融资负担提前到来。

黄金的下一段上涨提前到来。

比特币的流动性反应提前到来。

汇率调整提前到来。

能源通胀也在反通胀滑翔路径完成任务之前卷土重来。

我们原以为会依次发生的过程，开始彼此重叠。

That changes the way the forecast must be constructed.

The lesson is not that timing is impossible and therefore useless.

The lesson is that causal conditions must outrank calendar analogies.

If the condition that was supposed to arrive in October appears in August, the forecast should move to August.

If the market begins pricing a future policy architecture before the policy itself is implemented, we should not wait for the official meeting date to acknowledge it.

If a stock peaks while the industry it belongs to remains fundamentally strong, we should stop pretending one "cycle" controls both.

That is the repair being made in this report.

The previous framework was strongest at identifying the forces and the relative winners.

It was weaker at identifying when those forces would become dominant.

So the new framework is not organized around a predetermined month-by-month path.

It is organized around regimes, triggers and reaction functions.

The dates still matter where real events are scheduled: inflation releases, Treasury buybacks, Federal Reserve meetings, Bank of Japan decisions, stablecoin implementation milestones and earnings reports.

But the calendar no longer gets to overrule the evidence.

这改变了预测必须被构建的方式。

教训不是择时不可能、所以毫无用处。

教训是：因果条件的权威必须高于日历类比。

如果原本应该在 10 月出现的条件在 8 月就出现，预测就应该移到 8 月。

如果市场在政策真正实施前就开始为未来政策架构定价，我们不应该等到正式会议日期才承认它。

如果一只股票见顶时，它所在行业的基本面仍然强劲，我们就不该继续假装一只“周期”控制两者。

这正是本报告所做的修正。

旧框架最擅长识别驱动力与相对赢家。

它更不擅长识别这些驱动力何时会占据主导。

因此，新框架不再围绕一条预先设定的逐月路径组织。

它围绕体制、触发器与反应函数组织。

真实事件排定的日期依旧重要：通胀数据、美债回购、美联储会议、日本央行决议、稳定币实施里程碑与财报。

但日历不再拥有推翻证据的权力。

3The price of duration久期的价格

To understand why the current market feels internally contradictory, it helps to stop thinking about interest rates as something the Federal Reserve simply "sets."

The Fed controls a very short-term policy rate.

The market determines the price of lending money for ten, twenty or thirty years.

Those are related prices, but they are not the same price.

A 30-year Treasury investor is making a very different commitment from an investor holding overnight cash.

That investor is accepting decades of inflation risk, fiscal risk, opportunity cost and uncertainty. In exchange, the investor demands compensation.

When the supply of long-term bonds rises, or when investors become less willing to hold them at the previous yield, that compensation has to increase.

The easiest way to understand the present regime is to imagine a single giant auction for long-term capital.

On one side are all the borrowers.

The U.S. Treasury is there.

The hyperscalers are there.

Utilities are there.

Data-center developers are there.

Companies building power generation are there.

Defense contractors, industrial companies, mortgage borrowers and ordinary corporations are there too.

On the other side are the savers.

Pension funds.

Insurance companies.

Banks.

Money-market funds.

Foreign reserve managers.

Sovereign investors.

Households.

Asset managers.

Japanese institutions.

European institutions.

Every other pool of capital willing to hold a claim that matures years from now.

The yield is the price that brings those two sides together.

When the borrowers want dramatically more money, the yield rises until someone is willing to provide it.

That is not a malfunction.

That is the market working.

The problem begins when the yield required to clear the market becomes high enough to damage the very economy and financial system that created the borrowing demand.

This is where the current U.S. problem becomes unusual.

The federal government has enormous financing needs.

At the same time, the private sector has launched an infrastructure program of extraordinary scale.

Artificial intelligence is often described as software because the user interacts with models through software.

Economically, however, the current AI boom is one of the most physical technology cycles in modern history.

Training and running models requires accelerators.

Accelerators require memory.

The systems require networking.

The racks require power distribution.

The buildings require transformers.

The chips require cooling.

The data centers require land, substations, transmission, generators and increasingly dedicated power supply.

All of that requires capital before it produces output.

The early phase of the AI boom could be financed largely through the exceptional cash generation of the largest technology companies.

That is changing.

As spending grows, corporate borrowing grows with it.

Project-finance structures grow.

Utilities need more capital.

Data-center developers need more capital.

Infrastructure funds need more capital.

Even companies with enormous cash balances are increasingly using debt markets because the scale of the buildout is too large to treat financing as an afterthought.

That is why AI is no longer just affected by the interest rate.

AI is beginning to participate in setting the interest rate.

要理解当前市场为何显得内在矛盾，首先要停止把利率理解成美联储简单“设定”的东西。

美联储控制的是极短期政策利率。

而借出十年、二十年或三十年资金的价格，由市场决定。

这两类价格彼此相关，却不是同一个价格。

30 年期美债投资者所作的承诺，与持有隔夜现金的投资者截然不同。

这位投资者接受了几十年的通胀风险、财政风险、机会成本与不确定性，因此要求补偿。

当长期债券供给增加，或投资者不再愿意按先前收益率持有它们时，这份补偿就必须上升。

理解当前体制最简单的方法，是想象一场面向长期资本的巨型拍卖。

一边是所有借款人。

美国财政部在那里。

超大规模云厂商在那里。

公用事业公司在那里。

数据中心开发商在那里。

建设发电设施的公司在那里。

国防承包商、工业公司、按揭借款人和普通企业也在那里。

另一边是储蓄者。

养老基金。

保险公司。

银行。

货币市场基金。

外国储备管理者。

主权投资者。

家庭。

资产管理人。

日本机构。

欧洲机构。

以及所有愿意持有多年后才到期债权的其他资本池。

收益率，就是让两边相遇的价格。

当借款人突然需要多得多的资金时，收益率就会上升，直到有人愿意提供资金。

这不是故障。

这正是市场在工作。

问题始于：让市场出清所需的收益率，高到开始伤害创造借款需求的经济与金融体系本身。

这正是当前美国问题的特殊之处。

联邦政府有庞大的融资需求。

与此同时，私营部门启动了一项规模异常巨大的基础设施计划。

人工智能常被描述为软件，因为用户通过软件与模型交互。

但从经济意义上说，当前 AI 热潮是现代史上最具物理性的科技周期之一。

训练和运行模型需要加速器。

加速器需要存储。

系统需要网络。

机架需要配电。

建筑需要变压器。

芯片需要散热。

数据中心需要土地、变电站、输电、发电机，以及越来越多的专属电力供应。

这一切都要先投入资本，之后才会产生产出。

AI 热潮早期，主要可以依靠最大型科技公司异常强劲的现金创造能力融资。

这正在改变。

随着支出增长，企业借款也同步增长。

项目融资结构不断扩大。

公用事业需要更多资本。

数据中心开发商需要更多资本。

基础设施基金需要更多资本。

即便坐拥巨额现金的公司也越来越多地使用债券市场，因为建设规模太大，融资不可能再被当作事后问题。

因此，AI 已不再只是受利率影响。

AI 正开始参与设定利率。

The feedback loop works like this.

AI demand rises.

Companies spend more.

Capital expenditure rises.

Debt issuance rises.

Utilities and project vehicles borrow more.

The supply of corporate duration rises alongside the supply of government duration.

Investors demand more yield to absorb it all.

Long-term yields rise.

Higher yields increase the required return on new AI projects.

At the margin, some projects that looked attractive at a lower cost of capital no longer clear their hurdle rate.

Capital discipline improves.

Weak projects are delayed or canceled.

Stronger operators gain share.

The AI boom moves from a scarcity contest to a capital-allocation contest.

This is an important transition because it changes the kind of company the market should reward.

During the first stage, scarcity was enough.

If there were not enough GPUs, the GPU supplier won.

If there was not enough HBM, the memory supplier won.

If there were not enough optical modules, the optical supplier won.

The investor did not have to know the long-run return on the entire AI system. The bottleneck itself created the economics.

In the next stage, that becomes less sufficient.

The market begins asking:

How much incremental revenue did this AI investment produce?

How much cash did it consume?

How much debt was required?

How much dilution was required?

How long will the hardware remain economically useful?

How much depreciation is real rather than merely accounting?

What is utilization?

What is the residual value of the infrastructure if models or chips change faster than expected?

Those questions determine the capital efficiency of the AI buildout.

And capital efficiency is what matters when capital itself becomes scarce.

反馈回路如下运行。

AI 需求上升。

企业增加支出。

资本开支上升。

债券发行上升。

公用事业与项目载体借入更多资金。

企业久期供给与政府久期供给同时上升。

投资者要求更高收益率，才愿意吸收这一切。

长期收益率上升。

更高收益率提高了新 AI 项目的最低必要回报。

在边际上，一些在较低资本成本下看似有吸引力的项目，不再能够跨过门槛收益率。

资本纪律改善。

薄弱项目被推迟或取消。

更强的运营商获得份额。

AI 热潮从稀缺性竞赛转向资本配置竞赛。

这是一次重要转折，因为它改变了市场应该奖励哪一类公司。

第一阶段，稀缺就够了。

GPU 不够，GPU 供应商就赢。

HBM 不够，存储供应商就赢。

光模块不够，光模块供应商就赢。

投资者不必知道整套 AI 系统的长期回报。瓶颈本身就创造了经济性。

下一阶段，仅凭稀缺越来越不够。

市场开始追问：

这笔 AI 投资产生了多少增量收入？

它消耗了多少现金？

需要多少债务？

需要多少股权稀释？

硬件还能保持多久的经济用途？

有多少折旧是真实经济折旧，而不只是会计处理？

利用率是多少？

如果模型或芯片迭代快于预期，这些基础设施还剩多少残值？

这些问题决定 AI 建设的资本效率。

而当资本本身变得稀缺时，资本效率就是最重要的东西。

The sovereign side of the collision碰撞的主权一侧

The federal government does not have the same hurdle-rate discipline as a private company. It can continue borrowing for reasons that have nothing to do with a project's return on invested capital. That makes sovereign duration structurally different.

But the market that absorbs the debt still has alternatives. A pension fund can hold Treasury bonds. It can hold investment-grade corporate debt. A Japanese insurer can own JGBs. A global investor can own cash, equities, gold or foreign sovereign debt.

The U.S. government therefore retains immense financing power, but it does not get to dictate the price forever without consequence.

If the required yield rises too far, the consequences begin showing up across the economy. Mortgage rates rise. Housing activity weakens. Commercial real estate refinancing gets harder. Corporate interest expense rises. High-duration equity multiples compress. Government interest expense grows. The hurdle rate for AI projects rises. The currency can begin reacting.

At some point policymakers care. This is where the market enters a new stage.

The question is no longer simply: What yield does the market demand?

What do policymakers change so the market does not have to demand an even higher one?

They can reduce spending. They can raise taxes. They can shorten the maturity of issuance. They can create or encourage new sources of demand for government securities. They can change bank or dealer rules. Treasury can improve market liquidity. The Federal Reserve can eventually reduce short-term rates if inflation allows. In a more extreme regime, the central bank can purchase longer-duration securities.

The adjustment can also appear through the currency. If investors become less willing to hold long-duration dollar claims at a given real return, the dollar itself can weaken until U.S. assets become sufficiently attractive again.

That is why gold has become unusually informative. Gold has historically struggled when real yields are high because it pays no interest. If real rates rise because the market sees the United States as offering an increasingly attractive real return, gold should face a headwind. But if long yields rise partly because investors demand more compensation for fiscal or duration risk, gold can behave differently.

The same nominal yield can contain two very different messages. A 5% long yield produced by strong real growth and credible monetary policy is one thing. A 5% long yield produced by a deteriorating balance between debt supply and demand is another. The market is now trying to determine which component is becoming more important.

联邦政府没有私营企业那样的门槛收益率纪律。它可以继续借款，而借款理由与某个项目的投资资本回报毫无关系。因此，主权久期在结构上不同。

但吸收债务的市场仍有其他选择。养老基金可以持有美债，也可以持有投资级公司债；日本保险公司可以持有日本国债；全球投资者可以持有现金、股票、黄金或其他国家的主权债。

因此，美国政府仍拥有巨大的融资能力，但它不可能永远无代价地指定价格。

如果市场要求的收益率升得太高，后果就开始在整个经济中显现：按揭利率上升，住房活动转弱，商业地产再融资更难，企业利息支出增加，高久期股票估值倍数收缩，政府利息支出增长，AI 项目的门槛收益率上升，汇率也可能开始反应。

到某个时点，政策制定者会在意。市场由此进入新阶段。

问题不再只是：市场要求什么收益率？

政策制定者要改变什么，才能让市场不必要求一个更高的收益率？

他们可以削减支出、提高税收、缩短发行期限、创造或鼓励政府债券的新需求来源、改变银行或交易商规则；财政部可以改善市场流动性；若通胀允许，美联储最终可以降低短期利率；在更极端的体制下，央行可以购买更长期证券。

调整也可能通过汇率发生。如果投资者越来越不愿意在既定实际回报下持有长期美元债权，美元本身就可能走弱，直到美国资产重新变得足够有吸引力。

这就是黄金突然变得格外有信息量的原因。黄金不付利息，历史上往往在实际收益率高企时受压。如果实际利率上升是因为市场认为美国提供了越来越有吸引力的实际回报，黄金理应面临逆风。但如果长期收益率上升，部分原因是投资者要求更高的财政或久期风险补偿，黄金就可能表现不同。

同一个名义收益率可以包含两条截然不同的信息。由强劲实际增长与可信货币政策形成的 5% 长端收益率是一回事；由债券供需平衡恶化形成的 5% 又是另一回事。市场正在判断哪一部分正在变得更重要。

The four-quadrant signal四象限信号

One of the simplest ways to watch this is to compare the direction of long yields with the direction of the dollar. If long yields rise and the dollar rises, the signal is mostly conventional tightening. The United States is paying investors more, and capital is still attracted to the currency. If long yields fall and the dollar rises, the market is often seeking safety. If long yields fall and the dollar falls, the market is pricing easier financial conditions or some form of monetary accommodation.观察这一点最简单的方法之一，是把长期收益率方向与美元方向放在一起看。长端收益率上升、美元也上升，主要是常规紧缩：美国付给投资者更多回报，资本仍被该货币吸引。长端收益率下降、美元上升，市场往往在寻求安全。长端收益率下降、美元也下降，则通常是在定价更宽松的金融条件或某种货币缓解。

The most interesting quadrant is: long yields rising while the dollar falls. That combination says investors are demanding more compensation to hold the duration while simultaneously becoming less enthusiastic about the currency exposure. It does not prove a fiscal crisis. But it is the market signature that deserves the most attention if the sovereign-duration problem is deepening.最值得关注的象限是：长期收益率上升，而美元下跌。这组组合意味着，投资者一边要求更多补偿才愿意持有久期，一边却对货币敞口降低热情。它并不能证明财政危机，但如果主权久期问题正在加深，这正是最值得关注的市场签名。

This is why the present gold move matters more than its headline price alone. Gold is strengthening before the Federal Reserve has delivered the clean easing cycle we once expected to be necessary. That suggests the market is not simply pricing lower future policy rates. It is beginning to price uncertainty over how the duration problem will ultimately be resolved.这正是当前黄金走势的意义超过其标题价格的原因。黄金在美联储尚未兑现我们曾认为必要的干净宽松周期之前就已走强。这表明市场并非只是在定价未来更低的政策利率，而是开始为久期问题最终将如何被解决的不确定性定价。

What this thesis is not本论点不是什么

It is not a claim that the United States is about to default. It is not a claim that Treasury has imposed yield-curve control. It is not a claim that the Federal Reserve has begun monetizing the debt. It is not a claim that foreign investors have abandoned Treasuries. And it is not a claim that every rise in gold is evidence of fiscal collapse.它不是声称美国即将违约；不是声称财政部已经实施收益率曲线控制；不是声称美联储已经开始债务货币化；不是声称外国投资者已经抛弃美债；也不是声称黄金每一次上涨都是财政崩溃的证据。

Those conclusions go beyond the evidence. The current U.S. credit market remains healthy enough to make that clear. Broad high-yield spreads are still tight. The VIX remains relatively low. Equity funds continue attracting capital. Treasury auctions still function. Foreign ownership of U.S. debt remains enormous.这些结论超出了证据。当前美国信用市场仍足够健康，足以说明这一点：广义高收益债利差仍窄，VIX 相对较低，股票基金继续吸引资本，美债拍卖仍正常运行，外国持有的美国债务仍极其庞大。

What we are seeing is earlier in the process. The market-clearing price of duration has risen far enough that government financing, AI financing and monetary policy are beginning to interact in ways that were mostly theoretical two months ago.我们看到的是这个过程更早的阶段。久期的市场出清价格已经上升到足够高，使政府融资、AI 融资与货币政策开始以两个月前大多还停留在理论层面的方式相互作用。

That is why Ray Dalio’s recent work on sovereign debt dynamics feels more relevant today than it did at the beginning of summer. But it is also why the next step cannot simply be to declare that the endgame has arrived. The better question is: How does the system attempt to avoid the endgame?这就是为什么 Ray Dalio 最近关于主权债务动力学的研究，比初夏时更贴近当下。但也正因如此，下一步不能只是宣布终局已经到来。更好的问题是：系统会如何尝试避免终局？

That is where Treasury’s reaction function, the Federal Reserve’s inflation constraint and the stablecoin architecture become central. And that is where we turn next.财政部的反应函数、美联储的通胀约束与稳定币架构，正是在这里成为核心。接下来我们转向这些机制。

4The policy trap政策困局

Ray Dalio's recent essay on how countries move through late debt cycles arrived at an unusually useful moment.

The attraction of his framework is not that it predicts an imminent American default. The United States issues the world's dominant reserve currency, possesses an enormous tax base, deep capital markets and productive assets that make a simple emerging-market sovereign analogy misleading.

The useful part is the mechanism.

A government can accumulate debt for a long time without crisis so long as buyers willingly hold that debt at an acceptable yield. The critical transition comes when the quantity of debt that must be sold begins rising faster than demand at the prevailing price. Then the bond market clears through a higher yield.

At first, that can look healthy. Higher yields attract new buyers. They increase expected returns for savers. They discipline speculative capital. But higher yields also increase the government's own borrowing cost.

If enough debt has to be refinanced, interest expense begins compounding into the deficit that caused the supply problem in the first place.

The state then faces a difficult set of choices. It can reduce spending. It can raise taxes. It can issue debt at shorter maturities where demand is stronger. It can change financial regulation to make sovereign debt easier or more attractive to hold. It can tolerate higher long yields and accept the resulting economic slowdown. Or, if inflation allows, monetary policy can eventually make financing conditions easier.

The least attractive possibility is attempting to suppress nominal yields while inflation and fiscal demand remain too strong. History shows why that is dangerous: if the private market will not willingly finance a low yield, someone else has to absorb the securities or the currency has to bear more of the adjustment.

Dalio's framework is particularly relevant because several of the early symptoms he emphasizes have appeared together. Long U.S. yields have moved toward multi-decade highs. The dollar has weakened from its June peak. Gold has accelerated. Japanese holdings of Treasuries declined in the most recent available data. Treasury has enlarged buyback operations in the long end.

But there is an important difference between recognizing those symptoms and declaring that the late-stage outcome is already here. It is not.

Foreign ownership of U.S. Treasuries remains enormous and was still higher year over year in the latest data despite a monthly decline. The broad U.S. high-yield market remains calm. The VIX remains subdued. Treasury auctions have not stopped functioning. The United States can still issue at scale.

That means the useful interpretation is not: the debt crisis has begun.

the market is beginning to test how the system will adapt before a debt crisis is allowed to begin.

That makes the policy response more important than the headline yield.

Ray Dalio 最近关于国家如何走过债务周期后段的文章，出现得恰逢其时。

这个框架的吸引力不在于预测美国即将违约。美国发行全球主导储备货币，拥有庞大税基、深厚资本市场与生产性资产，简单套用新兴市场主权危机类比会误导。

真正有用的是机制。

只要买家愿意按可接受收益率持有债务，政府就可以长期累积债务而不发生危机。关键转折发生在必须出售的债务数量开始快于按现价计算的需求增长之时。随后，债券市场通过更高收益率出清。

起初，这甚至看起来很健康。更高收益率吸引新买家，提高储蓄者的预期回报，并约束投机资本。但更高收益率也会提高政府自身的借款成本。

如果需要再融资的债务足够多，利息支出就会复利式叠加进最初造成供给问题的赤字。

国家于是面对一组艰难选择：削减支出、提高税收、在需求更强的短期限发行债务、修改金融监管使主权债更容易或更值得持有、容忍更高长端收益率并接受随之而来的经济放缓；或者，在通胀允许时，由货币政策最终放松融资条件。

最不理想的选择，是在通胀与财政需求仍过强时试图压低名义收益率。历史说明了危险：如果私营市场不愿按低收益率融资，就必须有人吸收这些证券，或者让汇率承担更多调整。

Dalio 的框架尤其相关，是因为他强调的几个早期症状同时出现：美国长端收益率接近数十年高位，美元较 6 月高点走弱，黄金加速，日本持有美债在最新数据中下降，财政部扩大了长端回购操作。

但识别这些症状，与宣布后期结果已经到来之间，存在重要差别。它尚未到来。

外国持有美债的规模仍极其庞大，尽管环比下降，最新数据仍同比增长。美国广义高收益债市场保持平静，VIX 仍受抑，美债拍卖并未停止运转，美国仍能大规模发行。

因此，有用的解释不是：债务危机已经开始。

市场正开始测试：在债务危机被允许发生之前，系统会如何适应。

这使政策反应比标题收益率更加重要。

Treasury is no longer a passive character财政部不再是被动角色

For most investors, the macro story is built around the Federal Reserve. The Fed raises rates. The Fed cuts rates. The Fed shrinks its balance sheet. The Fed expands it. Treasury is often treated as the ministry that simply supplies the bonds the government requires.

That separation is becoming less useful. Treasury cannot set the long-term yield in an open market. But it can influence the composition and functioning of the market that produces that yield.

It chooses how much of the government's borrowing is financed through bills versus notes and bonds. It can conduct buybacks that remove older, less-liquid securities. It can change auction patterns. It can respond to investor demand across maturities. It can support market liquidity. And it can work with the regulatory system that determines how easily banks and dealers intermediate Treasury securities.

That does not give Treasury the power to repeal the bond market. It does mean Treasury has tools that matter before the Federal Reserve needs to intervene.

The expanded long-duration buyback program is the clearest recent example. Treasury has said that, beginning in September, it will at least double the maximum size of certain liquidity-support buybacks in the 10-to-30-year part of the curve.

The announcement came after the 30-year yield pushed toward roughly 5.34%. The market initially responded by buying long bonds. Then much of the move reversed.

That reversal is more interesting than a lasting rally would have been. If a relatively modest buyback program could permanently force the 30-year yield lower, the underlying problem would likely be a simple liquidity or dealer-capacity issue. Instead, the market quickly returned to asking the same questions about inflation, deficits, issuance and demand.

Treasury therefore revealed two things at once. First, it is willing to become more active when long-end market functioning becomes uncomfortable. Second, the current pressure is larger than a small liquidity operation can solve.

That is why we refer to an emerging Treasury reaction function.

There is no official 5.3% cap. No policymaker has promised to defend a particular yield. And the buybacks should not be misrepresented as quantitative easing.

Treasury has to finance itself elsewhere. The operation can change maturity composition and improve liquidity; it does not create central-bank reserves out of nothing.

But the market now has more information than it had before the announcement. It knows that sufficiently uncomfortable long-end conditions can change Treasury behavior. Investors will begin trading that behavior just as they trade the Fed's.

对大多数投资者而言，宏观故事一直围绕美联储构建：美联储加息、降息、缩表、扩表。财政部常被当成只是供应政府所需债券的部门。

这种切割越来越没有用。财政部不能在开放市场中设定长期收益率，却可以影响产生该收益率的市场结构与功能。

它决定多少政府借款用国库券融资、多少用中长期票据与债券融资；可以回购较旧、流动性较差的证券；可以改变拍卖模式；可以回应不同期限的投资者需求；可以支持市场流动性；也可以与监管体系协作，改变银行与交易商中介美债的难易程度。

这并不赋予财政部废除债券市场的权力，却意味着在美联储需要介入前，财政部已经有重要工具。

扩大长期债券回购计划，是最近最清楚的例子。财政部表示，从 9 月起，将至少把收益率曲线 10 至 30 年区间某些流动性支持回购的最高规模翻倍。

公告发布前，30 年期收益率曾逼近约 5.34%。市场最初买入长债，随后大部分涨幅回吐。

这次回吐比持续上涨更有信息量。如果一个相对温和的回购计划能永久压低 30 年期收益率，底层问题很可能只是流动性或交易商容量。事实却是，市场很快重新追问通胀、赤字、发行与需求。

因此，财政部同时透露了两点：第一，长端市场功能变得令人不适时，它愿意更积极行动；第二，当前压力大于一次小规模流动性操作能够解决的程度。

这就是我们把它称为新兴财政部反应函数的原因。

不存在官方 5.3% 上限。没有政策制定者承诺捍卫某个收益率。回购也不应被误称为量化宽松。

财政部必须在别处为自己融资。操作可以改变期限结构、改善流动性，却不会凭空创造央行准备金。

但市场现在掌握了公告前没有的信息：足够令人不适的长端条件，会改变财政部行为。投资者会像交易美联储反应一样交易这种行为。

Two reaction functions, one market两套反应函数，一个市场

This is where the macro problem becomes genuinely unusual. The Federal Reserve and Treasury do not optimize the same variable. The Fed cares about inflation, employment and the stability of expectations. Treasury cares about financing the government and preserving a liquid, functional government-securities market. Most of the time these goals coexist comfortably. Not always.

Imagine long Treasury yields rise because inflation expectations are rising. The Federal Reserve may consider that market move useful. Higher long rates tighten financial conditions. Mortgages get more expensive. Risky investment slows. Demand cools. The bond market is helping the Fed.

Treasury can experience the same move differently. Higher long rates raise the government's future interest burden. They can worsen housing affordability. They can reduce demand at long-maturity auctions. They can impair liquidity in older bonds. And they can create political pressure because the cost of financing the economy is rising.

That creates a potential tension: The Fed may want financial conditions to remain tight enough to restrain inflation. Treasury may want the long end to remain orderly and affordable enough to finance the government and the economy.

Neither institution needs to be acting improperly for this tension to exist. Their mandates are simply different.

The next several months will show whether those objectives can be reconciled without a larger policy shift.

If inflation continues cooling, the problem becomes much easier. The Fed can eventually lower the short rate. Real yields can fall. The long end can stabilize without the market interpreting policy as inflationary. That is the benign path.

If inflation stays too high, the policy trade becomes harder. Treasury can improve the plumbing. It can shift issuance. It can create more demand at the short end. But it cannot make inflation disappear.

And if investors believe attempts to lower long borrowing costs will weaken inflation discipline, long yields can perversely rise even as policymakers try to push financial conditions lower. This is the classic problem of trying to control the nominal price of capital without controlling the forces creating inflation.

宏观问题在这里真正变得不同寻常。美联储与财政部优化的不是同一个变量。美联储关注通胀、就业与预期稳定；财政部关注政府融资，以及维持一个流动、功能正常的政府证券市场。多数时候，两组目标可以舒适共存，但并非永远如此。

假设长端美债收益率因通胀预期上升而上升。美联储可能认为这种市场变动有用：更高长端利率收紧金融条件，按揭更贵，风险投资放缓，需求降温，债券市场在帮美联储。

财政部对同一变动的感受不同。更高长端利率提高政府未来利息负担，恶化住房可负担性，降低长期限拍卖需求，损害旧券流动性，并因经济融资成本上升而制造政治压力。

于是潜在张力出现：美联储可能希望金融条件保持足够紧，以抑制通胀；财政部可能希望长端足够有序、可负担，以便为政府与经济融资。

这种张力不要求任何一家机构行为不当。它们的使命只是不同。

未来几个月将展示，这些目标能否在不发生更大政策转向的情况下得到协调。

如果通胀继续降温，问题会容易得多。美联储最终可以降低短端利率，实际收益率可以下降，长端可以稳定，而市场不必把政策解读为通胀性的。这是温和路径。

如果通胀居高不下，政策取舍会更困难。财政部可以改善管道、调整发行、在短端创造更多需求，却不能让通胀消失。

如果投资者认为降低长期借款成本的尝试会削弱通胀纪律，那么即便政策制定者试图放松金融条件，长期收益率也可能反常上升。这正是试图控制资本名义价格、却没有控制通胀驱动力的经典难题。

Why 1998 is only half the analogy为什么 1998 只是一半的类比

Our earlier reports leaned on 1998 because the structure was attractive. A technology investment boom was alive. A financial-market shock created tightening. The Federal Reserve eased into that shock. The technology cycle then extended into a much larger final boom. That remains a useful upside analogy.早期报告偏重 1998 年，因为结构很有吸引力：科技投资热潮仍在，金融市场冲击制造紧缩，美联储对冲击进行宽松，科技周期随后延伸成更大的最终繁荣。这仍是有用的上行情景类比。

But it leaves out the critical difference. Inflation in 1998 was sufficiently contained that the Fed had room to ease without immediately threatening its credibility. Today, the Fed does not have that freedom. Energy remains a problem. Core inflation remains above target. The labor market is weakening, but not in an environment of clean price stability. That means the 1998 playbook cannot simply be repeated on command.但它遗漏了关键差别。1998 年通胀受到充分控制，美联储可以宽松而不立刻威胁自身信誉。今天，美联储没有这份自由。能源仍是问题，核心通胀仍高于目标，就业市场正在转弱，却并不处于干净的价格稳定环境。因此，1998 年剧本不能被按按钮重演。

Why the 1940s are only half the analogy too为什么 1940 年代也只是一半的类比

The other tempting analogy is the era when the Federal Reserve effectively capped Treasury yields to help finance World War II. That history demonstrates the power—and cost—of explicit yield control. If a central bank commits to a cap that private investors do not accept, the central bank has to purchase whatever quantity of bonds is necessary to enforce it. The constraint moves from the interest rate to the balance sheet and, ultimately, to inflation or the currency.另一个诱人类比，是美联储为资助二战而实际限制美债收益率的年代。那段历史展示了显式收益率控制的力量与代价。如果央行承诺一个私营投资者不接受的上限，就必须购买执行上限所需的任意数量债券。约束会从利率转移到资产负债表，最终转移到通胀或汇率。

But the present United States is nowhere near that regime. There is no formal long-bond peg. The Fed is not promising unlimited purchases. Treasury buybacks are not central-bank monetization. Using the 1940s as a description of today’s market would be dramatic and wrong.但当前美国远未进入该体制。不存在正式长债锚定，美联储没有承诺无限购买，财政部回购也不是央行货币化。用 1940 年代描述今天的市场，既戏剧化又错误。

Using them as a boundary condition is useful. They tell us what becomes possible if ordinary market absorption fails and policymakers later decide that high nominal yields themselves are unacceptable. We are not there. The point of this report is to watch the path before we get there.把它当作边界条件则有用。它告诉我们：如果正常市场吸收失败、政策制定者后来认定高名义收益率本身不可接受，什么会变得可能。我们还没到那里。本报告的目的，是观察抵达那里之前的路径。

Inflation is the veto通胀拥有否决权

The entire system turns on inflation because inflation determines how much freedom policymakers have.

If inflation falls convincingly, the number of available solutions expands. The Fed can lower short rates. Real yields can fall without the dollar necessarily collapsing. Treasury can finance more easily. Corporate credit becomes cheaper. AI project hurdle rates fall. Gold can continue appreciating through lower real yields without necessarily signaling fiscal panic. Bitcoin can benefit from improving liquidity. The economy can move toward a conventional easing cycle.

If inflation does not fall, every option becomes more dangerous. The labor market can weaken and still fail to produce rate cuts. Treasury can attempt to improve long-end liquidity and still see the currency weaken. Long rates can remain high because investors demand compensation for inflation. Gold can initially correct if the Fed responds by pushing real rates even higher. Risk assets can struggle even though nominal GDP remains strong.

This is why the recent energy shock matters far beyond oil. Oil is only the first input. Refining margins matter. Diesel matters. Gasoline matters. Transport costs matter. Utilities matter. Food distribution matters. The more those costs pass through into the broader price level, the harder it becomes for the Fed to respond to labor weakness.

The danger is a policy environment in which the economy is weak enough to want relief but inflation is high enough to deny it.

That is the inflation veto. It is one of the four regimes we will return to later in the report.

For now, the important point is simpler. Treasury may be developing more tools to manage the long end. But Treasury cannot overrule the inflation constraint.

That is why stablecoins are so interesting. They offer a way to change who buys the debt and which maturity they buy without requiring the Federal Reserve to immediately make money easier.

整个系统围绕通胀转动，因为通胀决定政策制定者拥有多大自由。

如果通胀可信地下降，可用方案会增加。美联储可以降低短端利率；实际收益率可以下降而美元未必崩溃；财政部融资更容易；企业信贷变便宜；AI 项目门槛收益率下降；黄金可以在实际收益率下降时继续升值，而不必然意味着财政恐慌；比特币可以受益于流动性改善；经济可以走向常规宽松周期。

如果通胀不降，每个选项都更危险。就业市场可能转弱却仍换不来降息；财政部可能改善长端流动性却仍看到美元走弱；长期利率可能因投资者要求通胀补偿而保持高位；如果美联储以更高实际利率回应，黄金起初可能大幅回调；即便名义 GDP 强劲，风险资产也可能挣扎。

这就是近期能源冲击意义远超原油的原因。原油只是第一个投入：炼油利润、柴油、汽油、运输成本、公用事业、食品流通都重要。这些成本越多传导进整体价格水平，美联储就越难回应就业转弱。

危险在于一种政策环境：经济弱到需要缓解，通胀却高到拒绝缓解。

这就是通胀否决权。它是本报告稍后回到的四种体制之一。

此刻更简单的重点是：财政部也许正在发展更多管理长端的工具，但财政部不能推翻通胀约束。

这正是稳定币如此有趣的原因。它们提供了一种改变谁买债、买哪个期限的办法，而不要求美联储立刻把资金变得更宽松。

5Stablecoins and the Treasury bill machine稳定币与国库券机器

Stablecoins are usually discussed as part of the cryptocurrency market. That description is becoming incomplete.

Their more important macroeconomic role may be as a new distribution system for dollar liabilities backed by short-term U.S. government assets.

That sounds technical. The mechanism is straightforward.

A regulated dollar stablecoin promises the holder something economically close to a digital dollar. To make that promise credible, the issuer has to hold safe, liquid reserves. Under the emerging U.S. framework, those reserves are concentrated in cash-like instruments: bank deposits, repos and short-dated Treasury securities.

That means every new dollar of stablecoin supply can create demand for the assets held behind it. If stablecoins grow from hundreds of billions of dollars into the multi-trillion-dollar system policymakers envision, the reserve pool becomes macroeconomically significant.

This matters to Treasury. But not in the simplistic way sometimes advertised.

稳定币通常被当作加密货币市场的一部分讨论。这种描述正在变得不完整。

它们更重要的宏观角色，可能是成为一套新的美元负债分销系统，而这些负债由短期美国政府资产支持。

听起来技术性很强，机制其实直接。

受监管的美元稳定币向持有人承诺一种经济上接近数字美元的资产。要让承诺可信，发行方必须持有安全且流动的储备。在正在形成的美国框架下，这些储备集中于类现金工具：银行存款、回购协议与短期美债。

因此，每新增一美元稳定币供给，都可能为其背后的资产创造需求。如果稳定币从数千亿美元增长为政策制定者设想的数万亿美元系统，储备池将在宏观层面变得重要。

这对财政部重要，但不是某些宣传所描述的简单方式。

Maturity law期限法则

Stablecoin bill demand is not equivalent to 30-year Treasury demand. A redeemable digital dollar naturally holds bills, repo and cash—not large unhedged positions in volatile long bonds.稳定币对国库券的需求，不等于对 30 年期美债的需求。可随时赎回的数字美元天然持有国库券、回购与现金，而不是大量未对冲、价格波动明显的长期债券。

Stablecoins do not naturally buy 30-year bonds

A stablecoin can be redeemed quickly. A 30-year Treasury can fluctuate substantially in price. Backing on-demand digital dollars with large amounts of long-duration bonds would create an obvious asset-liability mismatch.

So stablecoin issuers naturally prefer bills, repos and other extremely short-duration government collateral. That means stablecoins do not directly solve the problem of finding enough investors willing to hold 20- or 30-year Treasury bonds.

They can help Treasury in a different way.

Suppose the short end develops a much larger structural buyer base. Treasury has greater flexibility to finance part of the government's borrowing through bills instead of forcing every incremental financing need into longer notes and bonds. More bill demand can therefore reduce the amount of marginal duration the market has to absorb.

Not eliminate it. Reduce it.

That distinction is the key to understanding why stablecoins matter to the current report.

稳定币并不天然购买 30 年期债券

稳定币可以快速赎回，30 年期美债价格却可能大幅波动。用大量长久期债券支持按需赎回的数字美元，会形成明显的资产负债期限错配。

因此，稳定币发行方天然偏好国库券、回购与其他极短久期政府抵押品。这意味着稳定币不能直接解决寻找足够投资者持有 20 或 30 年期美债的问题。

它们可以用另一种方式帮助财政部。

假设短端形成了一个大得多的结构性买家群体。财政部便更有弹性用国库券为部分政府借款融资，而不必把每一笔增量融资需求都推向更长期票据与债券。因此，更多国库券需求可以减少市场边际上必须吸收的久期数量。

不是消除，而是减少。

这个区别，是理解稳定币为何与本报告相关的关键。

The Treasury bill machine

The potential cycle looks like this. A user in the United States or abroad acquires a regulated dollar stablecoin. The issuer receives dollars. The issuer buys Treasury bills or repos as reserves. The Treasury gets another structural source of short-term demand. As stablecoins grow, the short end of the Treasury market gains a larger buyer base. Treasury can lean more heavily on bills when long-duration demand is weak. That can reduce pressure on long-maturity issuance at the margin. The stablecoin then circulates globally, potentially increasing the reach of the dollar system.

If the next user acquires the stablecoin using money that previously sat outside dollar assets, the loop creates genuinely new dollar and Treasury demand.

This is why the U.S. government has reasons to view regulated stablecoins as more than a concession to crypto. They can strengthen dollar distribution. They can deepen demand for short-term government collateral. And they can place a growing part of global digital finance on top of U.S. Treasury bills. That is strategically valuable.

But not every stablecoin dollar is new Treasury demand

This is where the bullish story needs discipline. Imagine an investor has $100 in a money-market fund that already owns Treasury bills. The investor withdraws the money and buys a stablecoin. The stablecoin issuer then uses that $100 to buy Treasury bills. The stablecoin market cap rises by $100. Treasury demand did not rise by a full $100. The identity of the buyer changed.

The same basic problem appears if funds migrate from bank deposits whose balance sheets were already funding government securities. The source of the stablecoin inflow therefore matters as much as the gross growth of stablecoin supply.

Money that moves from a bill-holding money-market fund into a bill-backed stablecoin is mostly a rearrangement. Money that moves from a non-dollar foreign asset into a dollar stablecoin backed by bills can be much more incremental.

This is why the future stablecoin/Treasury relationship should be measured through net new demand by source, not celebrated solely through stablecoin market capitalization.

The hidden maturity trade

There is also a second-order consequence that is easy to miss. If stablecoins help create enormous demand for bills, Treasury may become more comfortable financing a larger share of the debt at shorter maturities. That can relieve some pressure on the long end today. But short debt has to be refinanced more frequently.

A government financed heavily through short maturities becomes more sensitive to the Federal Reserve's policy rate. That means the system trades one kind of duration risk for another. Long-term market risk is reduced at the margin. Short-term refinancing risk rises. The government's interest bill becomes more responsive to the front end. And that can make lower short rates more valuable to future fiscal policy.

The full loop therefore looks like this: stablecoins grow → bill demand rises → Treasury gains more short-end financing capacity → marginal long-duration supply pressure can fall → federal debt duration becomes shorter → the government's refinancing sensitivity to short rates increases → the economic value of eventually lower policy rates rises.

This is not a free lunch. It is a maturity transformation. That does not make it bad policy. It makes it important.

国库券机器

潜在循环如下：美国或海外用户获得受监管的美元稳定币；发行方收到美元；发行方买入国库券或回购作为储备；财政部得到另一个结构性短期需求来源；随着稳定币增长，美债短端拥有更大买家基础；长期久期需求薄弱时，财政部可以更多依赖国库券；这可以边际减轻长期限发行压力；稳定币随后在全球流通，扩大美元体系覆盖。

如果下一位用户购买稳定币的钱原先不在美元资产中，这个循环就创造了真正新增的美元与美债需求。

因此，美国政府有理由把受监管稳定币视为不只是对加密行业的让步。它可以强化美元分销，深化短期政府抵押品需求，并让全球数字金融中越来越大的一部分建立在美国国库券之上。这具有战略价值。

但不是每一美元稳定币都是新增美债需求

多头故事必须在这里保持纪律。假设投资者有 100 美元放在本来就持有国库券的货币市场基金，随后赎回并买入稳定币，稳定币发行方再用这 100 美元买国库券。稳定币市值增加 100 美元，但美债需求并未完整增加 100 美元，只是买家身份变化。

如果资金来自资产负债表原本已在融资政府证券的银行存款，同样问题也存在。因此，稳定币流入来源与稳定币总供给增长一样重要。

从持有国库券的货币基金流向国库券支持的稳定币，主要是重新排列；从非美元海外资产流向国库券支持的美元稳定币，则更可能是真正增量。

因此，未来稳定币/美债关系应通过按来源拆分的净新增需求衡量，而不是只为稳定币市值喝彩。

隐藏的期限交易

还有一个容易遗漏的二阶后果。如果稳定币帮助创造巨额国库券需求，财政部可能更愿意用更短期限为更大比例债务融资。这能缓解今天的部分长端压力，但短债必须更频繁再融资。

大量依靠短期限融资的政府，对美联储政策利率更敏感。系统因此用一种久期风险交换另一种：边际降低长期市场风险，却提高短期再融资风险；政府利息账单对前端利率更敏感，也使未来更低短端利率对财政政策更有价值。

完整循环因此是：稳定币增长 → 国库券需求上升 → 财政部获得更多短端融资容量 → 边际长久期供给压力可以下降 → 联邦债务久期缩短 → 政府对短端利率的再融资敏感度上升 → 未来降低政策利率的经济价值提高。

这不是免费午餐，而是期限转换。它未必是坏政策，却非常重要。

Stablecoins can strengthen the dollar even while changing the Treasury market

There is a common assumption that a "debasement" thesis and a stablecoin-dollar thesis must contradict each other. They do not.

The dollar can expand internationally even if its purchasing power slowly erodes over time. Those are different dimensions. A resident of a country with a weaker currency may want stable dollars precisely because U.S. monetary conditions are more credible than the local alternative. A global business may prefer dollar stablecoins because counterparties already price trade in dollars. A crypto participant may hold stablecoins for liquidity regardless of a long-run gold thesis.

The dollar can therefore increase its network share at the same time investors demand more compensation for holding long-duration dollar claims. This is another reason to distinguish bills from 30-year bonds. Demand for transactional dollars can be very strong. Demand for 30 years of fixed nominal dollar exposure can simultaneously become more price-sensitive. There is no contradiction.

The bridge between Treasury and crypto

This relationship also changes the structure of the cryptocurrency market. Stablecoins increasingly connect crypto-native liquidity to the same collateral system that finances the federal government. Treasury bills back stablecoins. Stablecoins provide settlement liquidity to crypto markets. Crypto and tokenized-finance activity can increase the demand for stablecoins. Stablecoin growth can then increase demand for Treasury bills.

That creates a financial loop between the digital-asset ecosystem and the sovereign collateral at the center of the dollar system.

Bitcoin remains outside that reserve mechanism. It is not backed by Treasuries. That distinction is essential.

But the liquidity environment surrounding Bitcoin can increasingly be built on Treasury-backed digital dollars. This is why stablecoin regulation can matter to Bitcoin even when no stablecoin issuer purchases Bitcoin itself. The plumbing changes.

What would make the stablecoin thesis genuinely important?

The answer is not one headline growth number. We would want to see several things happen together.

Stablecoin supply would need to keep growing after regulation becomes operational. A meaningful portion of the inflows would need to come from outside existing Treasury-holding channels. Reserve portfolios would need to create measurable incremental bill demand. Treasury would need to respond to that demand by changing issuance composition. The market would then need to show that long-end pressure is lower than it otherwise would have been.

Only then would we know that stablecoins had become a meaningful component of sovereign financing rather than simply a new wrapper around existing dollar liquidity. That experiment is beginning. It will take time to measure. But the architecture is now clear enough that investors should watch it.

Why this matters for the rest of the report

Stablecoins illustrate the larger point perfectly. The next market regime may not arrive through one dramatic Federal Reserve decision.

It can emerge through a collection of smaller structural changes: Treasury changes the maturity mix. Stablecoin issuers create bill demand. Dealer intermediation improves. The Fed remains restrictive. Real yields stop rising. The dollar weakens. Gold responds. Bitcoin responds later or with more volatility. AI financing remains available but expensive enough to impose discipline.

The system adapts without ever announcing: "We have entered a new monetary regime."

Markets rarely wait for that announcement. They price the adaptation while it is being built.

That is why the next place to look is not the Fed funds rate. It is the corporate cash-flow statement. If capital has become more expensive, the AI boom now has to prove which companies can actually earn enough to pay for it.

稳定币可以在改变美债市场的同时强化美元

常见假设认为，“货币贬值”论点与稳定币美元论点必然矛盾。其实并不矛盾。

即使美元购买力随时间缓慢侵蚀，它仍可以在国际上扩张。这是两个维度。弱势货币国家的居民可能恰恰因为美国货币条件比本地替代方案更可信，而需要稳定美元；全球企业可能因为交易对手本就用美元定价贸易，而偏好美元稳定币；加密参与者可能为流动性持有稳定币，而不论长期黄金论点如何。

因此，美元可以提高其网络份额，同时投资者要求更多补偿才愿意持有长期美元债权。这也是必须区分国库券与 30 年期债券的原因。交易性美元需求可以非常强，而 30 年固定名义美元敞口的需求同时可以变得更具价格敏感性。两者并不矛盾。

美债与加密资产之间的桥

这种关系也改变加密货币市场结构。稳定币越来越多地把加密原生流动性连接到为联邦政府融资的同一抵押品体系：国库券支持稳定币，稳定币为加密市场提供结算流动性，加密与代币化金融活动增加稳定币需求，稳定币增长又可以增加国库券需求。

由此，数字资产生态与美元体系核心的主权抵押品之间形成金融循环。

比特币仍在该储备机制之外。它不由美债支持。这个区别至关重要。

但比特币周围的流动性环境，可以越来越多地建立在美债支持的数字美元之上。因此，即便稳定币发行方本身不购买比特币，稳定币监管仍能影响比特币。改变的是金融管道。

什么会让稳定币论点真正重要？

答案不是一个标题增长数字。我们需要看到几件事同时发生。

监管投入运行后，稳定币供给必须继续增长；流入中有意义的一部分必须来自现有美债持有渠道之外；储备组合必须创造可测量的新增国库券需求；财政部必须通过改变发行结构回应这种需求；随后，市场还必须显示长端压力低于没有这些需求时的水平。

只有到那时，我们才能知道稳定币已成为主权融资的重要组成，而不只是现有美元流动性的新包装。这项实验已经开始，需要时间测量，但架构已足够清楚，值得投资者观察。

为什么这关系到本报告其余部分

稳定币完美说明了更大的观点：下一种市场体制未必通过一次戏剧性美联储决议到来。

它可能由一组较小的结构变化共同形成：财政部改变期限结构，稳定币发行方创造国库券需求，交易商中介改善，美联储保持限制性，实际收益率停止上升，美元走弱，黄金作出反应，比特币稍后或以更大波动作出反应，AI 融资仍然可得、但昂贵到足以施加纪律。

系统在从未宣布“我们已经进入一种新货币体制”的情况下完成适应。

市场很少等待公告，而是在适应被建造时就为它定价。

所以下一个该看的地方不是联邦基金利率，而是企业现金流量表。如果资本已变得更昂贵，AI 热潮现在必须证明哪些公司真正赚得足够多，能够支付资本成本。

6AI after the easy money宽松资金之后的 AI

The first phase of the AI boom rewarded the companies closest to scarcity.

There were not enough accelerators. Then there was not enough high-bandwidth memory. Then there was not enough optical networking. Then there was not enough power, cooling, switchgear, transformer capacity or data-center space.

When demand runs into a hard bottleneck, the economics are easy to understand. The scarce supplier earns extraordinary margins because the customer has few alternatives.

That environment can make almost every company attached to the bottleneck look like a great business. It can also make investors forget that scarcity eventually attracts capital.

More factories are built. More capacity is financed. More competitors arrive. Customers design around the bottleneck. Prices change.

The question slowly shifts from: Who controls the scarce thing? to: Who earns an adequate return on everything being built?

AI is entering that transition. The demand is still real. The capex is still real. The infrastructure shortage is still real. But the price of capital is no longer low enough for investors to treat every dollar of AI spending as equally valuable.

That is why the same earnings season can produce three very different market reactions from companies that are all "winning" AI.

Microsoft can spend aggressively and still generate enormous free cash flow because the new infrastructure feeds an existing enterprise distribution system. Meta can build strategically important compute and still disappoint shareholders because near-term free cash flow collapses under the weight of the investment. Alibaba can have genuine AI demand and still fall because shareholders are being asked to finance that future through a giant equity issuance.

The technology can be right. The capital structure can still be wrong. That distinction is the next source of alpha.

AI 热潮第一阶段奖励最接近稀缺性的公司。

加速器不够，随后高带宽存储不够，再随后光网络不够，继而电力、散热、开关设备、变压器产能与数据中心空间都不够。

当需求撞上硬瓶颈，经济性很容易理解。客户几乎没有替代方案，稀缺供应商因此赚取异常高利润率。

这种环境能让几乎每一家连接瓶颈的公司都看起来像伟大企业，也会让投资者忘记：稀缺最终会吸引资本。

更多工厂建成，更多产能获得融资，更多竞争者进入，客户围绕瓶颈重新设计，价格随之变化。

问题逐渐从谁控制稀缺品？转向谁能在所有正在建设的资产上赚取足够回报？

AI 正进入这个转折。需求仍真实，资本开支仍真实，基础设施短缺仍真实；但资本价格已不再低到允许投资者把每一美元 AI 支出视为同等有价值。

因此，同一财报季里，几家都在“赢得”AI 的公司可以得到三种完全不同的市场反应。

微软可以激进支出却仍产生巨额自由现金流，因为新基础设施接入了现有企业分销系统；Meta 可以建设战略上重要的算力，却因近期自由现金流被投资压垮而令股东失望；阿里巴巴可以拥有真实 AI 需求，却因股东被要求通过巨额股权发行资助未来而下跌。

技术可以是对的，资本结构仍可以是错的。这个区别，是下一处 alpha 来源。

Revenue growth is no longer enough收入增长已不再足够

The AI debate is still framed too often around one variable: revenue growth.

Revenue matters. But revenue is only the top half of an economic equation.

Suppose two companies each generate an additional $10 billion of AI-related annual revenue. Company A spends $8 billion to create it. Company B spends $40 billion. Those businesses do not deserve the same valuation simply because their AI revenue grew by the same amount.

Now add financing. Company A funds its investment internally. Company B issues debt.

Now add dilution. Company B also sells stock.

Now add depreciation. The hardware may become obsolete faster than traditional data-center accounting assumes.

Now add utilization. If servers run below expected capacity, the economic return falls further.

Now add energy. Power prices can change the operating economics.

Now add residual value. A data center designed around today's accelerator architecture may require expensive retrofits if hardware changes quickly.

This is why the next useful AI metric is something closer to:

incremental durable cash earnings relative to incremental invested capital.

Not because quarterly free cash flow is the only thing that matters. It is not. A company can rationally suppress current cash flow to build an asset with extraordinary future returns.

But the burden of proof changes once capital becomes expensive. The investor needs to know why the future return will exceed the new hurdle rate.

AI 讨论仍然过于频繁地围绕一个变量展开：收入增长。

收入重要，但只是经济方程的上半部分。

假设两家公司都增加 100 亿美元 AI 年收入。A 公司为此支出 80 亿美元，B 公司支出 400 亿美元。不能仅因 AI 收入增长相同，就给予两者相同估值。

再加入融资：A 公司内部融资，B 公司发行债务。

再加入稀释：B 公司同时出售股票。

再加入折旧：硬件过时速度可能快于传统数据中心会计假设。

再加入利用率：如果服务器运行低于预期容量，经济回报进一步下降。

再加入能源：电价可以改变运营经济性。

再加入残值：围绕今天加速器架构设计的数据中心，如果硬件快速变化，可能需要昂贵改造。

所以下一个有用的 AI 指标更接近：

相对于增量投入资本的增量、可持续现金收益。

不是因为季度自由现金流是唯一重要的东西。它不是。公司可以理性压低当期现金流，建设未来回报异常高的资产。

但资本变贵后，举证责任改变。投资者需要知道未来回报为什么能超过新的门槛收益率。

Microsoft: the clean monetization case

Microsoft currently offers the easiest version of that story. The company is spending extraordinary amounts of capital. But the spending is connected to a business that already knows how to monetize enterprise demand.

Azure has a direct revenue model. Microsoft 365 has a direct revenue model. Copilot subscriptions have a direct revenue model. The company has a giant installed customer base, recurring contracts, software distribution and a cloud backlog that turns infrastructure into identifiable future revenue.

That does not mean every dollar Microsoft spends will earn a good return. It means the transmission path from capital expenditure to customer revenue is comparatively visible. That matters more when yields are high. An investor can tolerate enormous capex if the cash engine behind it remains obvious.

Meta: strategic value without a clean cash bridge

Meta is more difficult. The company may eventually build enormously valuable AI infrastructure. Its models can improve advertising. Its recommendation systems can improve engagement. Its assistants may become products. Its compute could become a strategic asset in its own right. But the cash conversion is less direct.

Meta does not have the same mature cloud-resale business as Microsoft, Amazon or Google. So when capex explodes, investors have to make a larger leap from infrastructure spending to future monetization.

That is why the collapse in quarterly free cash flow mattered so much. It did not prove the AI strategy was wrong. It proved that the shareholder is financing a very expensive strategic option before the market has a clean way to measure the return.

Our earlier Haven Audition report gave Meta too much credit before that evidence arrived. That mistake is now useful.

Do not promote AI optionality into AI proof until the cash-flow bridge is visible.

Meta can still become one of the cycle's largest winners. But it has to earn that classification through evidence.

Alphabet: vertically integrated, increasingly capital intensive

Alphabet sits somewhere between Microsoft and Meta. Google has an enormous cloud business. It owns global distribution. It owns proprietary accelerators. It has one of the deepest AI research stacks in the world.

That vertical integration can become a major advantage because custom silicon allows Google to optimize cost across hardware, software and model architecture rather than buy the entire stack from outside suppliers. But that advantage has to be measured against the cash consumed by the buildout.

A vertically integrated system can generate exceptional return on capital. It can also encourage overinvestment because the company controls so many layers of the stack.

The correct Google question is therefore not: Are TPUs good? They are. It is: Does vertical integration create enough economic advantage to justify the capital absorbed by the integrated system?

That answer will become clearer as cloud margins, inference economics and free cash flow mature.

Nvidia: extraordinary fundamentals meet a higher discount rate

Nvidia is a different problem again. The company remains the clearest beneficiary of the compute buildout. Its revenue base is tied directly to the infrastructure customers are desperate to acquire. That produces phenomenal current economics.

But even phenomenal economics can be a poor stock at the wrong price. When the market pays an extreme multiple for a company, the discount rate becomes important even if earnings continue beating estimates.

A higher long-term yield lowers the present value investors assign to future profits. That means Nvidia can report strong demand and still trade poorly if the market simultaneously concludes that the required return on long-duration equities has risen.

This is not a contradiction. It is the difference between: company fundamentals and the price paid for those fundamentals.

Nvidia's next phase therefore depends on two clocks: the AI demand clock; and the duration-valuation clock. The first can remain excellent while the second creates a correction.

Apple: the opposite AI expression

Apple is useful precisely because it sits at the opposite end of the capital-intensity spectrum. Its AI strategy may be less dramatic. But it has exceptional current cash generation and does not need to build the same enormous external compute asset base to justify its existence.

That can make Apple comparatively resilient when capital is expensive. The trade-off is valuation. Safety becomes dangerous when everyone pays too much for it.

Our earlier work called Apple the "consensus haven" because its business structure can withstand a duration shock better than a highly leveraged AI optionality story. That remains true structurally. It does not mean Apple is immune from multiple compression. There is no asset whose valuation stops mattering simply because the business is high quality.

Alibaba: the global version of the same test

China's AI buildout is now producing the same shareholder question. Alibaba is raising a huge amount of equity to finance AI infrastructure, chips and model development.

The demand can be genuine. The strategic logic can be sound. The offering can even be oversubscribed. Yet existing shareholders still experience dilution.

Again: good AI strategy and good stock outcome are not synonyms.

This is why the capital-efficiency framework travels across borders. The AI boom is global. The cost of financing it is global too.

微软：最清晰的变现案例

微软目前提供了这套故事最容易理解的版本。公司投入异常庞大的资本，但支出连接着一门已经知道如何将企业需求变现的业务。

Azure 有直接收入模式，Microsoft 365 有直接收入模式，Copilot 订阅也有直接收入模式。公司拥有巨大的现有客户群、经常性合同、软件分销，以及把基础设施转化为可识别未来收入的云业务积压订单。

这不意味着微软每一美元支出都能赚取好回报，而是从资本开支到客户收入的传导路径相对可见。收益率高时，这一点更重要。只要背后的现金引擎依旧清楚，投资者可以容忍巨额资本开支。

Meta：有战略价值，却没有清晰现金桥

Meta 更困难。公司最终可能建成极有价值的 AI 基础设施；模型可以改善广告，推荐系统可以提高参与度，助手可能成为产品，算力本身也可能成为战略资产。但现金转换更不直接。

Meta 不具备微软、亚马逊或 Google 那样成熟的云转售业务。因此，当资本开支激增，投资者必须从基础设施支出跨越更大距离，才能抵达未来变现。

这就是季度自由现金流坍塌如此重要的原因。它没有证明 AI 战略错误，而是证明：在市场尚无法清晰衡量回报时，股东已经在为一份极昂贵的战略期权融资。

我们先前的《避险选角》在证据到来前给了 Meta 过多信用。这个错误现在很有用。

在现金流桥清晰可见之前，不要把 AI 期权性升级为 AI 证据。

Meta 仍可能成为本轮周期最大赢家之一，但必须用证据赢得这个分类。

Alphabet：垂直整合，资本强度越来越高

Alphabet 位于微软与 Meta 之间。Google 拥有庞大云业务、全球分销、自有加速器，以及全球最深厚的 AI 研究栈之一。

垂直整合可以成为重大优势，因为自研芯片允许 Google 跨硬件、软件与模型架构优化成本，而不是从外部购买整套技术栈。但这项优势必须与建设消耗的现金对照衡量。

垂直整合系统可以创造异常高资本回报，也可能因为公司控制技术栈太多层而鼓励过度投资。

所以正确的 Google 问题不是TPU 好不好？它当然好。问题是：垂直整合创造的经济优势，是否足以证明整套系统吸收的资本合理？

随着云利润率、推理经济性与自由现金流成熟，答案会更清楚。

Nvidia：异常强劲的基本面遇上更高贴现率

Nvidia 又是另一类问题。公司仍是算力建设最清楚的受益者，收入直接连接客户迫切需要的基础设施，因而拥有惊人的当期经济性。

但即便惊人的经济性，在错误价格上也可能是一只差股票。当市场为公司支付极端倍数时，即便盈利继续超预期，贴现率也会变得重要。

更高长期收益率会降低投资者赋予未来利润的现值。因此，Nvidia 可以报告强劲需求，却仍在市场同时认为高久期股票必要回报上升时表现不佳。

这并不矛盾，而是公司基本面与为基本面支付的价格之间的区别。

Nvidia 下一阶段因此取决于两只钟：AI 需求时钟与久期估值时钟。前者可以依旧优秀，后者却制造调整。

Apple：相反的 AI 表达

Apple 的价值恰在于它位于资本强度谱系另一端。它的 AI 战略可能不那么戏剧化，却拥有异常强劲的当期现金创造能力，也不需要建立同样庞大的外部算力资产基础来证明自身存在。

资本昂贵时，这可以使 Apple 相对更有韧性。代价则是估值：当所有人都为安全支付过高价格，安全本身就变得危险。

我们早期把 Apple 称为“共识避风港”，因为其业务结构比高杠杆 AI 期权故事更能承受久期冲击。这在结构上仍然成立，却不意味着 Apple 不会经历估值倍数收缩。没有任何资产会仅因业务高质量，就让估值变得不重要。

阿里巴巴：同一检验的全球版本

中国 AI 建设正在提出同一个股东问题。阿里巴巴正筹集巨额股权资本，为 AI 基础设施、芯片与模型开发融资。

需求可以真实，战略逻辑可以正确，发行甚至可以超额认购；但现有股东仍承受稀释。

再说一次：好的 AI 战略与好的股票结果不是同义词。

因此，资本效率框架可以跨越国界。AI 热潮是全球的，融资成本同样是全球的。

The four questions every AI equity now has to answer每只 AI 股票现在都必须回答的四个问题

For the next phase, we would reduce AI stock analysis to four economic questions.对下一阶段，我们会把 AI 股票分析归纳为四个经济问题。

1Is the demand real?需求真实吗？This is still first. No amount of financing engineering fixes an absence of customers. The strongest evidence comes from contracts, cloud backlog, utilization, recurring revenue and cash-paying users—not product demonstrations.这仍是第一位。任何融资工程都修复不了客户缺失。最强证据来自合同、云积压订单、利用率、经常性收入与付费用户，而非产品演示。

3Is the return higher than the hurdle rate?回报高于门槛收益率吗？The higher long yields go, the higher the economic return required from the project. This is where expensive capital begins killing weak AI investments before it kills strong ones.长期收益率越高，项目必须产生的经济回报就越高。昂贵资本正是从这里开始先杀死弱 AI 投资，然后才是强项目。

Why physical infrastructure still looks attractive

The shift toward capital discipline does not invalidate the physical-AI thesis from The Second Act. In some ways it strengthens it.

Power infrastructure, switchgear, transformers, cooling and networking are not merely speculative bets on a distant consumer AI product. They are required to complete projects whose budgets have already been approved. They convert existing capex commitments into physical capacity. That gives them shorter economic duration than many narrative-heavy software or model bets.

A transformer manufacturer does not need to know which model wins the AI race. It needs the data center to connect to power. A cooling supplier does not need to know whether a particular chatbot dominates consumer usage. It needs the rack to dissipate heat. A networking supplier does not need to know which AI agent becomes popular. It needs traffic between accelerators and clusters to continue growing.

That does not make these businesses risk-free. Their stocks can become expensive. Customers can delay projects. New capacity can eventually reduce margins. But the causal chain from AI investment to current orders is comparatively direct. This is why the second-act infrastructure thesis still holds.

The memory lesson

Memory is the cleanest demonstration of why the new framework needs multiple clocks.

The memory stocks became crowded. The stocks broke. That looked like a fundamental signal. But supplier economics remained extraordinary because HBM and other memory products were still scarce.

The mistake was to use one word—"cycle"—for two different processes. The equity cycle can peak when everyone already owns the story. The profit cycle can continue because physical supply remains tight. The customer cost cycle can become worse because those high supplier margins raise the cost of AI systems. And the capacity cycle can still be accelerating because suppliers respond to the profit signal by investing.

Those four clocks do not turn together. That is the way every mature AI subsector should now be analyzed.

Expensive money is not necessarily bearish for AI

This may sound paradoxical after everything above. Higher capital costs can actually strengthen the surviving AI companies.

Cheap money allows weak projects to live. It finances duplication. It encourages startups and incumbents to build capacity before proving demand. It rewards narrative.

Expensive money raises the standard. Projects with poor economics are canceled. Companies with weak balance sheets retreat. The businesses with genuine demand and internal financing keep investing. They emerge with more market share.

That means the current bond-market pressure can act as a selection mechanism rather than a terminal event. This is why a controlled 2026 financing scare can be consistent with a much larger 2027 AI boom.

The weaker projects die early. The strongest projects survive. Then, if real financing costs fall later, investors discover a smaller group of companies with: more infrastructure; less competition; visible revenue; and improving free cash flow. That is the setup that can produce a final rerating. The danger is that the market then mistakes survival for immortality.

The 2027 paradox

The most bullish path can create the most dangerous terminal setup.

Suppose the current duration problem is contained. Treasury keeps the market functioning. Inflation eventually cools. The Fed gains room to ease. Real yields decline. AI demand stays strong. The projects that survived 2026 begin generating visible revenue. Capital markets reopen more aggressively.

Then the companies that passed the hard-money test suddenly receive cheaper capital again. That is powerful. It can produce a genuine productivity boom. It can also produce overbuilding.

If investors conclude that the financing scare proved AI cannot fail, they may fund a second wave of projects with much weaker economics. That is when the 2000 analogy becomes more useful.

Not when semiconductor stocks merely fall after a strong run. But when: capital discipline disappears again; weak projects regain cheap access to financing; AI credit keeps expanding after returns deteriorate; IPO and speculative issuance explode; economic depreciation is ignored; and the market once again stops asking what the assets actually earn.

We are not there today. The present bond stress may be the process that decides whether we ever get there.

The next AI winners will be selected by the cash-flow bridge

The first AI trade was about scarcity. The second was about physical buildout. The third will be about the cash-flow bridge between the two.

The investor's job is no longer to ask whether a company is exposed to AI. Almost every large technology company is. The job is to ask:

How efficiently does this company convert AI capital into durable shareholder cash flow?

That question will separate the next leaders far better than another list of "AI beneficiaries."

And once that framework is established, the regional market map becomes much clearer. The United States, Hong Kong, mainland China, Korea and Japan are not simply different geographic expressions of one global risk trade. They sit at different points in the same capital cycle. That is where we turn next.

为什么物理基础设施仍有吸引力

转向资本纪律并未推翻《第二幕》的实体 AI 论点，某些方面反而强化了它。

电力基础设施、开关设备、变压器、散热与网络，不只是对遥远消费 AI 产品的投机下注。它们是完成预算已经获批项目的必需品，把现有资本开支承诺转化为物理容量，因此经济久期短于许多叙事浓厚的软件或模型押注。

变压器制造商不必知道哪一个模型赢得 AI 竞赛，只需要数据中心接入电力；散热供应商不必知道哪个聊天机器人主导消费使用，只需要机架排出热量；网络供应商不必知道哪个 AI 智能体走红，只需要加速器与集群之间的流量继续增长。

这不意味着这些企业无风险。股票可能变贵，客户可能推迟项目，新产能最终可能压低利润率。但从 AI 投资到当期订单的因果链相对直接。这就是第二幕基础设施论点仍成立的原因。

存储的教训

存储最清楚地展示了新框架为何需要多只时钟。

存储股变得拥挤，随后破位，看起来像基本面信号；但因 HBM 与其他存储产品仍然稀缺，供应商经济性依然异常强劲。

错误是用“周期”一个词描述不同过程。股票周期可以在所有人都已持有故事时见顶；利润周期可以因物理供给仍紧而继续；客户成本周期可能恶化，因为供应商高利润提高 AI 系统成本；产能周期仍可能加速，因为供应商以投资回应利润信号。

四只钟不会一起转动。今后每个成熟 AI 子行业都应如此分析。

昂贵资金未必利空 AI

这听起来与上文矛盾，但更高资本成本实际上可以强化存活下来的 AI 公司。

便宜资金让薄弱项目存活，为重复建设融资，鼓励初创与在位公司在证明需求前建设产能，并奖励叙事。

昂贵资金提高标准。经济性差的项目被取消，资产负债表薄弱的公司退却，有真实需求与内部融资的企业继续投资，最终获得更大份额。

因此，当前债市压力可以成为筛选机制，而不是终结事件。这就是受控的 2026 年融资恐慌可以与更大的 2027 年 AI 热潮同时成立的原因。

弱项目提前死亡，强项目存活。若实际融资成本后来下降，投资者会发现一组更小的公司拥有更多基础设施、更少竞争、可见收入与改善中的自由现金流——这是最终重估的设置。危险在于市场随后把存活误当成永生。

2027 年悖论

最看多的路径可能创造最危险的终端设置。

假设当前久期问题得到控制：财政部维持市场功能，通胀最终降温，美联储获得宽松空间，实际收益率下降，AI 需求保持强劲，熬过 2026 年的项目开始产生可见收入，资本市场更积极重开。

通过硬钱检验的公司突然再次获得廉价资本。它力量巨大，可以制造真正生产率繁荣，也可以制造过度建设。

如果投资者认为融资恐慌证明 AI 不会失败，他们可能资助第二波经济性弱得多的项目。此时，2000 年类比才更有用。

不是半导体股强劲上涨后下跌时，而是资本纪律再次消失、弱项目重新获得廉价融资、回报恶化后 AI 信贷仍扩张、IPO 与投机发行爆发、经济折旧被忽视、市场再次停止追问资产究竟赚取什么时。

今天我们尚未到那里。当前债市压力可能正是决定我们会不会抵达那里的过程。

下一批 AI 赢家将由现金流桥筛选

第一笔 AI 交易关乎稀缺，第二笔关乎物理建设，第三笔将关乎两者之间的现金流桥。

投资者的任务不再是问公司是否有 AI 敞口，几乎所有大型科技公司都有。真正该问的是：

这家公司把 AI 资本转换成持久股东现金流的效率有多高？

这个问题比另一份“AI 受益者”名单更能区分下一批领袖。

框架建立后，区域市场地图会清楚得多。美国、香港、中国大陆、韩国与日本，不只是同一笔全球风险交易的不同地理表达；它们处于同一资本周期的不同位置。接下来转向这张地图。

7The global market map全球市场地图

The easiest mistake in a global macro regime is to assume every market is expressing the same cycle. They are not.

The United States is dealing with a collision between sovereign financing, AI capital expenditure and high long-term yields. Hong Kong is dealing with global liquidity, offshore Chinese technology valuations and the shareholder cost of funding AI expansion. Mainland China is dealing with a weak household/property economy at the same time that strategic manufacturing and technology investment remain strong. Korea is dealing with a violent equity-positioning reset inside an unusually profitable memory cycle. Japan is dealing with the end of the zero-rate world and the global consequences of domestic capital suddenly having a meaningful yield again.

Those markets can all move together on a risk-off day. That does not mean they should be analyzed with the same model.

The purpose of this section is not to make five country calls. It is to identify what each market is actually pricing.

United States: the market is rotating under a bond constraint

The U.S. equity market is currently easier to misunderstand than it was during the clean first phase of the AI rally. The headline index can look resilient while the internal market becomes more fragile. That is exactly what has been happening.

Semiconductors and other long-duration growth assets have become increasingly sensitive to Treasury yields. When the long end rises, the market immediately asks whether the earnings growth is fast enough to justify a higher discount rate.

At the same time, broad credit remains calm. Volatility remains relatively subdued. Capital still flows into equities. Breadth can weaken sharply and then recover.

That is not the structure of a market already entering systemic liquidation. It is the structure of a market deciding which parts of the previous leadership can survive a higher cost of capital.

That distinction matters enormously.

If a broad credit crisis were beginning, we would expect the weakness to spread in a recognizable sequence. First the most expensive leadership breaks. Then breadth deteriorates. Then credit spreads widen. Then volatility stops mean-reverting. Then investors begin selling assets that had nothing to do with the original problem. Then the market stops distinguishing between good and bad balance sheets because liquidity itself becomes the scarce asset.

We are not there. The first stage is visible. The rest of the cascade is not.

That is why the most likely U.S. outcome remains dispersion before liquidation.

The market can still suffer a meaningful correction. A 5–10% index drawdown would not require a recession or credit crisis. It could emerge simply because higher real yields force investors to pay less for future earnings.

But that type of correction should not be treated like the end of the AI cycle. It should be treated like a repricing of which cash flows deserve to survive at a higher hurdle rate.

What a duration-constrained U.S. market should reward

Three types of business become relatively more attractive.

The first is the company with current cash flow. When investors can earn a high return in bonds, distant profits have to be discounted more heavily. The market therefore places more value on earnings that exist now. That is one reason capital-efficient software, high-quality platforms and businesses with large recurring cash flows can outperform speculative growth even inside the same technology sector.

The second is the business whose revenue is attached to already-committed physical spending. Power infrastructure is the clearest example. A company selling transformers, switchgear, cooling systems or electrical equipment is not primarily dependent on an investor believing in a distant AI consumer product. It is dependent on a data center actually being built. The order can exist now. The revenue can arrive now. That shortens the economic duration of the investment thesis.

The third is the business that benefits from nominal growth rather than purely from falling rates. Selected energy, industrial, materials and financial companies can perform relatively well in an environment where nominal activity remains strong enough to support revenue while long-duration growth multiples compress.

That does not mean every value stock becomes attractive. It means the market's internal hierarchy changes.

Why VIX matters less than people think—and more than they think

The VIX is often used as a binary market thermometer. Low VIX means calm. High VIX means fear. That is useful, but incomplete.

The more important question is whether volatility is persistent. A one-day spike in VIX during a semiconductor selloff can simply be a positioning event. A VIX that rises above 25 and stays there while credit spreads widen is a different regime.

The current market has repeatedly shown the first behavior. It has not yet shown the second.

That is why a low VIX should not be interpreted as evidence that the bond market does not matter. It means the bond stress has not yet turned into forced equity deleveraging.

That actually makes the current period more interesting, not less. The investor still has time to distinguish productive capital from speculative capital before a crisis removes that distinction.

全球宏观体制中最容易犯的错误，是假设每个市场都在表达同一周期。事实并非如此。

美国面对主权融资、AI 资本开支与高长期收益率的碰撞；香港面对全球流动性、离岸中国科技估值与资助 AI 扩张的股东成本；中国大陆在居民/房地产经济疲弱时，战略制造与科技投资依旧强劲；韩国在异常盈利的存储周期内部经历猛烈股票仓位重置；日本则面对零利率世界的终结，以及本土资本突然重新获得有意义收益率所产生的全球后果。

这些市场可以在避险日一起下跌，但不代表应该用同一模型分析。

本节不是要给出五个国家判断，而是识别每个市场究竟在定价什么。

美国：市场在债券约束下轮动

当前美国股市比 AI 上涨干净第一阶段更容易被误解。标题指数看似有韧性，内部市场却可能变得更脆弱。事情正在如此发生。

半导体与其他高久期成长资产对美债收益率越来越敏感。长端上升时，市场立刻追问盈利增长是否足够快，能证明更高贴现率合理。

与此同时，广义信用保持平静，波动率相对受抑，资本仍流入股票，市场广度可以大幅恶化后再恢复。

这不是已经进入系统性清算的市场结构，而是市场在决定：旧领导权的哪些部分能在更高资本成本下存活。

若广义信用危机开始，弱势理应按可识别顺序扩散：最昂贵的领导权先破，随后广度恶化、信用利差扩大、波动率不再均值回归，投资者开始出售与原问题无关的资产；最终，因为流动性本身成为稀缺资产，市场不再区分好坏资产负债表。

我们还没到那里。第一阶段可见，余下级联尚未出现。

所以，美国最可能的结果仍是先分化，后谈清算。

市场仍可能发生有意义调整。指数回撤 5–10% 不需要衰退或信用危机，只需更高实际收益率迫使投资者降低为未来盈利支付的价格。

但这种调整不应被当成 AI 周期终结，而应被视为重新定价：哪些现金流值得在更高门槛收益率下存活。

受久期约束的美国市场应奖励什么

三类企业相对更有吸引力。

第一类是拥有当期现金流的公司。投资者能在债券中赚取高回报时，遥远利润必须被更重地贴现，市场因此更重视已经存在的盈利。这也是资本效率高的软件、高质量平台与经常性现金流庞大的企业，可以在同一科技行业内跑赢投机成长的原因。

第二类是收入连接已承诺实体支出的企业。电力基础设施最清楚。销售变压器、开关设备、散热系统或电气设备的公司，主要不依赖投资者相信遥远的消费 AI 产品，而依赖数据中心真正开工。订单现在可以存在，收入现在可以到来，从而缩短投资论点的经济久期。

第三类是受益于名义增长、而非纯粹受益于利率下降的企业。在名义活动仍足以支撑收入、高久期成长估值倍数却收缩的环境中，部分能源、工业、材料与金融公司可以相对表现良好。

这不意味着所有价值股都变得有吸引力，而是市场内部层级发生变化。

为什么 VIX 没人们想得那么重要，却也更重要

VIX 常被当作二元市场温度计：低 VIX 意味平静，高 VIX 意味恐惧。有用，但不完整。

更重要的问题是波动是否持续。半导体抛售时 VIX 单日飙升，可能只是仓位事件；VIX 升破 25 并持续，同时信用利差扩大，则是另一种体制。

当前市场反复显示第一种行为，尚未显示第二种。

所以低 VIX 不应被解读为债市不重要，而意味着债市压力尚未变成股票被迫去杠杆。

这让当下更有趣，而非更无聊。投资者仍有时间在危机抹掉区别前，分辨生产性资本与投机性资本。

Hong Kong: AI optimism meets shareholder financing

Hong Kong's technology market has been one of the clearest places where our earlier work was directionally useful but incomplete.

The original thesis was that Hong Kong platforms could outperform China's weak domestic macro story because they were more sensitive to global liquidity, technology re-rating and offshore capital than to the property cycle alone. That worked.

But the next stage is no longer simply about whether China's technology companies deserve a higher multiple. It is about how they finance the AI race.

Alibaba's recent equity issuance captures the problem perfectly. The company can have real AI demand. Its cloud business can improve. Its models can become more valuable. Its long-term strategic position can strengthen. And the stock can still fall because existing shareholders are being diluted to finance the buildout.

This is exactly the same capital-efficiency question facing U.S. hyperscalers, expressed through a different financing channel. In the United States, the burden increasingly shows up through debt issuance, free-cash-flow compression and capital intensity. In Hong Kong, it can show up through direct equity issuance and dilution.

How much of the future AI value survives for the current shareholder after financing it?

That is why Hong Kong technology should no longer be analyzed as one broad beta trade. A weaker dollar and lower real yields can help the whole complex. But the companies with the strongest internal cash generation and the clearest AI monetization should increasingly separate from those that need the capital market to fund every next stage.

The liquidity regime determines the opportunity. The capital structure determines the winner.

Mainland China: one country, multiple capital cycles

China's macro data remain frustrating because the country contains several economies moving at different speeds. The household and property economy is weak. The strategic industrial economy is much stronger. The offshore technology market is different again. Trying to compress all three into one bullish or bearish call destroys useful information.

Household China. Property remains the central drag. Years of falling property confidence have damaged household wealth, construction, local-government finances and consumption. Retail demand is soft. Traditional fixed investment is weak. The domestic balance-sheet repair is slow. That is the China most investors see when they look at the broad macro data. It is real. It is not the entire country.

Industrial China. At the same time, capital continues moving toward sectors the state considers strategically important: semiconductors, robotics, industrial automation, grid equipment, electric vehicles and advanced manufacturing. These sectors can grow even while the property economy remains weak because their funding source, policy priority and end demand are different. This is why the phrase "China slowdown" can coexist with extraordinary growth in individual technology and manufacturing clusters. The weakness is concentrated. So is the strength.

Capital-market China. Hong Kong sits partly outside both stories. Its largest technology companies are valued by global investors. Their multiples respond to the dollar. Their capital raising responds to offshore liquidity. Their stocks can rally on AI monetization even when Chinese household confidence remains weak. They can also fall on dilution even when the strategic business outlook improves.

This is why the next useful China framework is not: China risk-on versus China risk-off. It is: Which Chinese balance sheet is connected to which capital cycle?

That is a much better question.

香港：AI 乐观遇上股东融资

香港科技市场最清楚地体现了我们早期研究方向有用却不完整的地方。

原论点认为，香港平台对全球流动性、科技重估与离岸资本的敏感度高于对单一房地产周期的敏感度，因此可以跑赢中国疲弱的境内宏观故事。这个判断奏效了。

但下一阶段不再只是中国科技公司是否值得更高倍数，而是它们如何为 AI 竞赛融资。

阿里巴巴近期股权发行完美捕捉问题：公司可以拥有真实 AI 需求，云业务可以改善，模型可以更有价值，长期战略地位可以增强；股票却仍可能下跌，因为现有股东被稀释，以资助建设。

这与美国超大规模云厂商面对的是同一个资本效率问题，只是融资渠道不同。美国的负担越来越多表现为债券发行、自由现金流压缩与资本强度；香港则可能表现为直接股权发行与稀释。

在完成融资之后，未来 AI 价值中有多少仍属于当前股东？

因此，香港科技不应再被分析成一笔宽泛贝塔交易。美元走弱、实际收益率下降可以帮助整个板块，但内部现金创造最强、AI 变现最清楚的公司，应越来越明显地与每个下一阶段都需要资本市场资助的公司分离。

流动性体制决定机会，资本结构决定赢家。

中国大陆：一个国家，多重资本周期

中国宏观数据持续令人困惑，因为国内包含几套以不同速度运行的经济。居民与房地产经济疲弱，战略工业经济强得多，离岸科技市场又是另一回事。把三者压缩成一个看多或看空判断，会摧毁有用信息。

居民中国。房地产仍是核心拖累。多年房地产信心下降损害居民财富、建筑、地方政府财政与消费。零售需求疲软，传统固定投资薄弱，国内资产负债表修复缓慢。这是大多数投资者从广义宏观数据中看到的中国。它真实存在，却不是全部中国。

工业中国。与此同时，资本继续流向国家认为具有战略意义的行业：半导体、机器人、工业自动化、电网设备、电动车与先进制造。即便房地产经济疲弱，这些行业仍能增长，因为资金来源、政策优先级与终端需求不同。所以“中国放缓”可以与个别科技和制造集群的惊人增长并存。弱势集中，强势也集中。

资本市场中国。香港部分位于前两个故事之外。最大科技公司由全球投资者估值，倍数回应美元，融资回应离岸流动性。即便中国居民信心疲弱，股票也可以因 AI 变现而上涨；即便战略业务前景改善，也可以因稀释而下跌。

因此，下一个有用的中国框架不是中国 risk-on 对 risk-off，而是：哪一张中国资产负债表连接哪一个资本周期？

Korea: the stock peaked before the industry did韩国：股票先于行业见顶

Korea may be the most valuable lesson in the entire forecast review. Our earlier work correctly identified the memory-equity trade as dangerously crowded. The stocks later suffered a violent reset. That seemed to validate the thesis. But only half of it.韩国可能是整份预测复盘最有价值的教训。我们早期正确识别存储股票交易已危险拥挤，股票随后猛烈重置，看似验证论点——但只验证了一半。

The underlying memory economics remained exceptionally strong. This is exactly why investors need to separate an equity cycle from an industry cycle.底层存储经济性依然异常强劲。这正是投资者必须把股票周期与行业周期分开的原因。

A stock can peak because everyone already owns it; the valuation assumes perfect execution; leverage is high; foreign positioning is crowded; or the marginal buyer has disappeared. None of those conditions requires the company’s profits to fall immediately. In fact, the most dangerous equity peaks often occur while the earnings numbers still look extraordinary. That is what makes them dangerous. The market is not reacting to a bad present. It is discounting a future in which the current profitability becomes less surprising.股票可以因为所有人都已持有、估值假设完美执行、杠杆很高、外资仓位拥挤，或边际买家消失而见顶。这些条件都不要求公司利润立刻下降。事实上，最危险的股票顶部常在盈利数字仍异常漂亮时出现，这正是它们危险之处。市场不是对糟糕当下作出反应，而是在折现一个当前盈利不再令人意外的未来。

Memory should therefore be analyzed through four different clocks. The first is physical supply and pricing: are HBM, DRAM and NAND actually becoming abundant? If supply remains tight, supplier economics can remain powerful. The second is supplier free cash flow: how much of the pricing power is turning into real cash after capital expenditure? This clock can remain strong even after the stock peaks.因此，存储应通过四只不同时钟分析。第一只是物理供给与定价：HBM、DRAM 与 NAND 是否真的正在变得充足？如果供给仍紧，供应商经济性可以保持强劲。第二只是供应商自由现金流：定价权在资本开支后有多少转化为真实现金？这只钟可以在股票见顶后继续强劲。

The third is equity positioning and valuation: how much of the good news has already been priced? This clock can turn first. The fourth is downstream customer cost: if memory suppliers keep earning extraordinary margins, someone else is paying them. That raises the cost of AI servers and data-center systems. In other words, a good memory cycle can become a headwind for the customer’s return on AI investment.第三只是股票仓位与估值：好消息有多少已经定价？这只钟可以最先转向。第四只是下游客户成本：如果存储供应商持续赚取异常高利润，就必然有人在付钱；这会提高 AI 服务器与数据中心系统成本。换句话说，强劲存储周期可以变成客户 AI 投资回报的逆风。

Those clocks are connected. They are not synchronized. That is the mistake our earlier framework made.这些时钟彼此连接，却不同步。这正是旧框架犯的错误。

The next good Korean memory entry may appear while the fundamental news still looks strong. That sounds counterintuitive. If the equity positioning has already reset while contract pricing, HBM demand and supplier cash flow remain strong, the investor suddenly owns a good business at a much less dangerous price.下一次好的韩国存储入场点，可能在基本面新闻仍然强劲时出现。这听起来违反直觉。如果股票仓位已经重置，而合约定价、HBM 需求与供应商现金流保持强劲，投资者会突然以危险性低得多的价格持有好企业。

The confirmation we would want is not simply another record earnings report. We would want to see foreign selling stabilize; retail leverage normalize; share-price volatility fall; memory contract economics remain firm; and management capital returns remain disciplined. That would indicate that the equity cycle has reset without the profit cycle collapsing. That is a much better setup than buying at the top simply because the earnings are good.我们要的确认不只是又一份创纪录财报，而是外资卖出稳定、散户杠杆正常化、股价波动下降、存储合约经济性保持稳健、管理层资本回报保持纪律。这将表明股票周期已经重置而利润周期未坍塌，比仅因盈利漂亮就在顶部买入好得多。

Japan: the global funding variable returns日本：全球融资变量回归

Japan has spent most of the last generation behaving like an exporter of cheap capital. Domestic yields were so low that Japanese institutions had powerful incentives to look abroad. That made Japan an important buyer of foreign bonds and an important funding source for global carry trades.

The end of zero rates changes that structure. When Japanese government bonds offer meaningful yields again, domestic assets become more competitive. A Japanese institution that once had almost no reason to hold long-term domestic government debt now has a real alternative.

That matters to the U.S. Treasury market. The investor does not need to become bearish on the United States. The investor only needs to decide that the domestic risk-adjusted return has improved enough to justify holding more capital at home. That reduces one source of marginal demand for foreign duration.

The yen matters because it funds leverage

The second channel is the currency. For years, investors could borrow yen cheaply and use the proceeds to buy higher-yielding assets elsewhere.

That works beautifully while Japanese rates are low; the yen is stable or weakening; and global risk assets rise. It becomes dangerous when the funding currency strengthens.

If the BOJ remains comparatively hawkish while U.S. policy moves toward lower real rates, the rate differential narrows. The yen can strengthen. Carry positions become less attractive. Investors unwind leverage.

That can produce a strange market combination: the dollar weakens; gold rises; U.S. rate expectations improve; and global equities still sell off temporarily because the funding structure is deleveraging.

That is not contradictory. It is a reminder that liquidity conditions depend on more than the Fed.

Japan has its own duration problem too

The irony is that Japan is not merely a source of capital for the U.S. duration problem. It has a sovereign-duration problem of its own.

Higher JGB yields increase Japanese government financing costs. They change bank balance sheets. They change insurer portfolios. They change pension allocations. They change the currency.

That creates a two-way feedback loop. Higher Japanese yields can pull capital home. That can reduce demand for Treasuries. Higher Treasury yields can then tighten global conditions. A global risk shock can strengthen the yen and alter the BOJ's path.

The two largest developed sovereign bond markets increasingly interact through capital flows rather than existing in separate macro stories. That is why Japan deserves a central place in the current U.S. market framework.

过去一代的大部分时间里，日本像廉价资本出口国。国内收益率太低，日本机构有强烈动力向海外寻找机会。因此，日本成为外国债券的重要买家，也是全球套息交易的重要融资来源。

零利率结束改变了这个结构。当日本国债再次提供有意义的收益率，国内资产竞争力提高。曾经几乎没有理由持有长期本国政府债的日本机构，现在拥有真实替代方案。

这关系到美国国债市场。投资者无需看空美国，只需认为国内风险调整后回报改善到足以让更多资本留在本土，就会减少外国久期的一处边际需求来源。

日元重要，因为它为杠杆融资

第二条渠道是汇率。多年来，投资者可以低成本借入日元，再用资金买入其他地方更高收益的资产。

当日本利率低、日元稳定或走弱、全球风险资产上涨时，这很好用；融资货币走强时，它就变得危险。

如果日本央行相对鹰派，而美国政策走向更低实际利率，利差收窄，日元可以走强，套息仓位吸引力下降，投资者解除杠杆。

这会产生看似奇怪的组合：美元走弱、黄金上涨、美国利率预期改善，全球股票却因融资结构去杠杆而暂时下跌。

这并不矛盾，而是提醒我们：流动性条件不只取决于美联储。

日本也有自己的久期问题

讽刺的是，日本不只是美国久期问题的资本来源，它也有自己的主权久期问题。

更高日本国债收益率提高政府融资成本，改变银行资产负债表、保险组合、养老金配置与汇率。

这形成双向反馈：更高日本收益率把资本拉回国内，可能减少美债需求；更高美债收益率随后收紧全球条件；全球风险冲击又可能强化日元、改变日本央行路径。

两个最大的发达经济体主权债市场，越来越多地通过资本流相互作用，而不是活在独立宏观故事中。这正是日本必须处于当前美国市场框架中心的原因。

The global map in one sentence全球地图的一句话版本

The United States is deciding which AI projects can survive expensive capital. Hong Kong is deciding how much dilution shareholders will tolerate to finance AI ambition. Mainland China is separating strategic industrial strength from household balance-sheet weakness. Korea is separating memory profits from memory positioning. Japan is separating domestic capital from the global zero-rate system that once pushed it abroad. Different markets. Same underlying transition. Capital has a price again.美国在决定哪些 AI 项目能在昂贵资本下存活；香港在决定股东愿为 AI 抱负承受多少稀释；中国大陆在分离战略工业强势与居民资产负债表弱势；韩国在分离存储利润与存储仓位；日本在把国内资本从曾迫使其流向海外的全球零利率体系中分离出来。市场不同，底层转折相同：资本再次有了价格。

8Hard assets and crypto硬资产与加密资产

A regime centered on the price of duration naturally pushes investors toward assets that appear to sit outside long-duration nominal claims. That creates a temptation to group everything together: gold, silver, Bitcoin, platinum, palladium, copper—“hard assets” and “debasement trades.” That language is useful at a distance. It becomes dangerous up close.以久期价格为中心的体制，天然把投资者推向看似位于长期名义债权之外的资产，于是人们很容易把黄金、白银、比特币、铂金、钯金与铜统称为“硬资产”或“贬值交易”。这种语言远看有用，近看危险。

These assets respond to very different combinations of real rates, liquidity, industrial demand, physical scarcity and risk appetite. The right question is not: Which hard asset is best? It is: Which asset is expressing which part of the regime?这些资产回应实际利率、流动性、工业需求、物理稀缺与风险偏好的不同组合。正确问题不是哪种硬资产最好？而是：哪种资产正在表达体制的哪一部分？

Gold: the cleanest signal

Gold is the most important asset in the current report because its behavior changed before the rest of the macro framework did.

Our earlier thesis assumed that gold's durable next leg would probably require a cleaner path toward lower real yields and eventual Federal Reserve easing. Instead, gold accelerated while long-term nominal yields remained high and real yields were still restrictive.

That means the market added something to the equation. Gold is no longer trading only on: real rates; the dollar; central-bank demand; and geopolitical reserve diversification. It is increasingly trading on uncertainty over how the long-duration financing problem will ultimately be resolved. That is a subtle but important distinction.

If a high Treasury yield represents attractive real compensation and policy credibility, gold should face a headwind. If the high yield represents increasing compensation for fiscal or duration risk, gold can rise alongside it. The nominal yield looks the same. The message inside it is different. That is why the combination of long yields and the dollar matters so much.

Gold is not a one-way trade

The structural thesis has strengthened. The tactical setup can still be poor after a vertical rally.

If inflation remains hot and the Fed responds by forcing real yields higher, gold can correct sharply. If the dollar rallies with real yields, the correction can be violent.

That does not automatically invalidate the long-run thesis. It means the path to a larger monetary adjustment can include a period where the central bank successfully tightens.

This is why we prefer to treat gold as the signal asset. It is the cleanest way to observe whether the market is becoming less comfortable with long-duration nominal claims. But even the cleanest signal can become overbought. Structural conviction is not a substitute for entry discipline.

Silver: monetary beta with an industrial engine

Silver is often described as gold with more upside. That is too simple.

Silver contains a monetary component and a meaningful industrial component. That gives it more convexity in a benign easing regime. If real yields fall, the dollar weakens and global activity remains healthy, silver can outperform gold substantially.

But the same structure makes it more vulnerable in a credit accident. Industrial demand expectations can fall. Leverage can unwind. Liquidity can disappear. Silver can drop much faster than gold.

That is why our hierarchy is: Gold = cleaner monetary signal. Silver = higher-beta monetary expression. The two should not be treated as interchangeable.

Platinum and palladium: the labels hide the fundamentals

Platinum and palladium are both grouped into the precious-metals complex. Their physical outlooks are increasingly different.

Platinum has a more attractive medium-term supply-demand balance, with constrained inventories and a stronger probability of persistent deficits. Palladium faces a more difficult structural backdrop because of the long-run transition in automotive demand and the possibility of surplus.

That makes our preferred relative view: Platinum over palladium.

This is not a prediction that platinum rises every month or that palladium cannot squeeze. Palladium can rally violently on supply disruptions, short covering or geopolitical events. But a tactical squeeze and a structural deficit are not the same investment thesis. The important point is that the PGM complex should be analyzed through physical balances rather than the word "precious."

Copper: the best structural story can still be a bad entry

Copper remains one of the strongest long-run industrial scarcity stories in the portfolio. The demand case is unusually broad. AI data centers require electrical infrastructure. Grids require reinforcement. Power generation requires transmission. Robotics requires motors and wiring. Electrification requires copper. Defense requires copper. Mine supply takes years to develop.

That is a powerful long-term setup. But a strong secular story does not make every price attractive.

The recent copper squeeze demonstrated that physical tightness, warehouse positioning and speculative structure can push the market far beyond what a long-term investor should comfortably chase.

This is another recurring lesson in the report: structural scarcity and tactical entry are different questions.

The best copper opportunity may come after the market digests an acute squeeze rather than during it. The thesis remains strong. The urgency does not.

黄金：最干净的信号

黄金是本报告最重要的资产，因为它的行为先于宏观框架其他部分发生变化。

旧论点假设，黄金下一段持久上涨大概需要一条更清晰的实际收益率下降与美联储最终宽松路径。事实却是，长期名义收益率仍高、实际收益率仍具限制性时，黄金就已加速。

这意味着市场向方程加入了新变量。黄金不再只交易实际利率、美元、央行需求与地缘政治储备多元化，而是越来越多地交易长久期融资问题最终将如何解决的不确定性。

如果高美债收益率代表有吸引力的实际补偿与政策信誉，黄金理应受压；如果它代表财政或久期风险补偿提高，黄金可以与收益率一起上涨。名义收益率看起来相同，里面的信息不同。所以长端收益率与美元的组合极其重要。

黄金不是单边交易

结构论点增强，不代表垂直上涨后的战术设置一定良好。

若通胀持续过热、美联储以更高实际收益率回应，黄金可以大幅调整；若美元与实际收益率一起上涨，调整可以很猛烈。

这不会自动推翻长期论点，只意味着通向更大货币调整的路径，可以包含央行成功紧缩的一段时期。

所以我们更愿把黄金当作信号资产：它最清楚地显示市场是否越来越不愿持有长期名义债权。但最干净的信号也会超买。结构信念不能替代入场纪律。

白银：带工业引擎的货币贝塔

白银常被描述为上行更大的黄金，过于简单。

白银同时包含货币属性与重要工业属性，在温和宽松体制中拥有更大凸性。若实际收益率下降、美元走弱、全球活动健康，白银可以大幅跑赢黄金。

但同一结构也使其在信用事故中更脆弱：工业需求预期可以下降，杠杆可以解除，流动性可以消失，白银可以比黄金跌得快得多。

因此，我们的层级是：黄金 = 更干净的货币信号；白银 = 更高贝塔的货币表达。两者不应互换。

铂金与钯金：标签遮住基本面

铂金和钯金都被归入贵金属，但物理前景越来越不同。

铂金的中期供需平衡更有吸引力，库存受限，持续短缺概率更高；钯金因汽车需求长期转型与潜在过剩，结构背景更困难。

因此，我们偏好的相对判断是：铂金优于钯金。

这不是预测铂金每月上涨，也不是说钯金不会逼空。供应中断、空头回补或地缘事件都能让钯金暴涨，但战术逼空与结构性短缺不是同一投资论点。PGM 应按物理平衡分析，而不是按“贵金属”三个字分析。

铜：最好的结构故事，也可能是坏入场点

铜仍是组合中最强的长期工业稀缺故事之一。需求异常广泛：AI 数据中心需要电气基础设施，电网需要加固，发电需要输电，机器人需要电机与线缆，电气化和国防都需要铜；矿山供给则要多年才能开发。

长期设置强大，却不意味着每个价格都有吸引力。

近期铜挤压说明，物理紧张、仓库仓位与投机结构可以把市场推到长期投资者不应舒服追逐的水平。

这再次说明：结构性稀缺与战术入场是两个问题。

最好的铜机会可能在市场消化剧烈挤压之后，而不是当中。论点仍强，紧迫性不强。

Bitcoin: the liquidity clock moved before the calendar

Bitcoin is the asset where our forecasting process changed the most. The earlier framework gave significant weight to the historical four-year cycle and the idea that a major trough would probably occur in the October-to-December 2026 window.

That view was not irrational. Past Bitcoin cycles have shown strong temporal patterns. Miner economics matter. Speculative leverage matters. Halving narratives matter. Behavioral reflexivity matters.

But the 2026 market is structurally different from the earlier cycles used to build that analogy. Bitcoin now trades through institutional vehicles. It responds directly to macro asset allocation. Stablecoins provide a much larger settlement base. Corporate balance sheets hold the asset. Regulation matters. Treasury-market policy matters. The investor base is more macro-sensitive.

That means a future liquidity transition can be front-run. And that is exactly what appears to have happened. The policy catalyst we expected later began arriving earlier. The market moved before the old cycle date.

That does not mean the four-year cycle has disappeared. It means the cycle clock is no longer the only clock.

The six Bitcoin clocks

We would now rank Bitcoin's governing variables roughly like this.

The first is the dollar regime. A weaker dollar generally improves the environment for alternative monetary assets.

The second is real yields. Falling real yields reduce the opportunity cost of non-yielding or alternative monetary assets.

The third is Treasury and Fed liquidity architecture. This includes more than conventional QE. Changes in market functioning, duration pressure and expected policy can alter crypto pricing before a central bank formally cuts.

The fourth is stablecoin liquidity. A larger digital-dollar base can improve settlement liquidity and the capacity of the crypto system to absorb risk.

The fifth is ETF and institutional flows. These turn traditional asset allocation directly into spot demand or supply.

The sixth is the historical cycle calendar. Still useful. No longer sovereign.

That hierarchy explains why the old October forecast lost authority without requiring us to pretend historical cycles never mattered.

Bitcoin is not gold

Gold and Bitcoin share a monetary story. They do not share the same risk profile.

Gold is held by central banks and conservative pools of capital. Bitcoin contains more leverage, reflexivity and speculative risk. That difference determines the sequencing in a stress event.

In a managed easing or repression regime: gold can rise; Bitcoin can rise faster.

In a genuine liquidity accident: Bitcoin can fall violently first. Gold can also fall as investors raise cash, but it should generally stabilize earlier. After policy responds and liquidity improves, Bitcoin can recover later with more beta.

This is why calling Bitcoin an immediate hedge against every Treasury-market problem is dangerous. It is better understood as a high-beta monetary optionality asset.

Its long-run thesis can strengthen because of a fiscal or monetary regime change while its short-run price collapses during the liquidity event that forces that regime change. Both can be true.

Stablecoins connect Bitcoin to Treasury without backing Bitcoin

The growing stablecoin system creates a particularly interesting bridge. Treasury bills back stablecoin reserves. Stablecoins provide liquidity inside crypto markets. Crypto activity increases demand for stablecoins. Stablecoin growth can increase demand for Treasury bills. The result is a financial loop connecting digital-asset market liquidity to U.S. sovereign collateral.

Bitcoin remains economically separate from that collateral. It is not backed by Treasury bills. But the liquidity system surrounding it increasingly is.

That makes crypto less detached from conventional finance than the ideological language around it often suggests. The more regulated stablecoins grow, the more the crypto ecosystem can become intertwined with the U.S. Treasury market. That is one reason the next crypto cycle is likely to look different from the last.

Ethereum and the rest of crypto need a stricter standard

Bitcoin can retain a monetary thesis without generating application revenue. Most other crypto assets cannot rely on that same argument.

Smart-contract networks need economic throughput. Stablecoin settlement. Tokenized assets. Applications. Fees. Developer activity. Real usage.

That makes the next crypto cycle a selection problem similar to AI. The question is no longer simply: Bitcoin rises, therefore everything else should rise more.

Which networks actually capture economic value from the growth of digital-dollar and tokenized-finance activity?

That will matter much more in a regulated institutional market than it did during earlier speculative cycles.

比特币：流动性时钟先于日历移动

比特币是我们预测过程变化最大的资产。旧框架高度重视历史四年周期，并认为重大低点大概会出现在 2026 年 10 至 12 月窗口。

这个判断并非不理性。过去比特币周期表现出强时间模式。矿工经济性、投机杠杆、减半叙事与行为反身性都重要。

但 2026 年市场与构建类比的早期周期有结构差异。比特币现在通过机构载体交易，直接回应宏观资产配置，稳定币提供更大结算基础，企业资产负债表持币，监管与美债市场政策都重要，投资者基础对宏观更敏感。

因此，未来流动性转折可以被提前交易。事情看起来正是如此：我们预期稍后到来的政策催化提前出现，市场在旧周期日期之前就已移动。

这不意味着四年周期消失，而是周期时钟不再是唯一时钟。

比特币的六只时钟

现在我们会大致如此排列比特币的支配变量。

第一是美元体制。美元走弱通常改善替代货币资产环境。

第二是实际收益率。实际收益率下降，降低无收益或替代货币资产的机会成本。

第三是财政部与美联储流动性架构。它不只包括传统 QE；市场功能、久期压力与预期政策的变化，可以在央行正式降息前改变加密资产定价。

第四是稳定币流动性。更大的数字美元基础可以改善结算流动性，提高加密系统吸收风险的能力。

第五是ETF 与机构资金流。它把传统资产配置直接转化为现货需求或供给。

第六是历史周期日历。仍有用，不再拥有最高权威。

这个层级解释了旧 10 月预测为何失去权威，同时无需假装历史周期从未重要。

比特币不是黄金

黄金与比特币共享货币故事，却不共享风险属性。

黄金由央行与保守资本池持有；比特币包含更多杠杆、反身性与投机风险。这个差别决定压力事件中的顺序。

在受管理的宽松或压抑体制中，黄金可以上涨，比特币可以涨得更快。

在真正流动性事故中，比特币可以先暴跌；黄金也会因投资者筹集现金而跌，但通常更早稳定。政策回应、流动性改善后，比特币可以稍后以更大贝塔恢复。

所以，把比特币称为每一次美债市场问题的即时对冲很危险。更好的理解是：高贝塔货币期权性资产。

它的长期论点可以因财政或货币体制变化而增强，而短期价格又在迫使体制变化的流动性事件中坍塌。两者都可以成立。

稳定币连接比特币与美债，却不为比特币提供支持资产

增长中的稳定币系统创造一座有趣桥梁：国库券支持稳定币储备，稳定币为加密市场提供流动性，加密活动提高稳定币需求，稳定币增长又可以增加国库券需求。结果是一条把数字资产市场流动性连接到美国主权抵押品的金融循环。

比特币在经济上仍与该抵押品分离。它不由国库券支持，但环绕它的流动性系统越来越多地由国库券支持。

这使加密市场不像其意识形态语言所暗示的那样脱离传统金融。受监管稳定币越增长，加密生态就越与美国国债市场交织。这也是下一轮加密周期很可能不同于上一轮的原因。

以太坊与其他加密资产需要更严格标准

比特币可以不产生应用收入而保留货币论点，多数其他加密资产却不能依赖同一论据。

智能合约网络需要经济吞吐：稳定币结算、代币化资产、应用、费用、开发者活动与真实使用。

因此，下一轮加密周期成为类似 AI 的筛选问题，不再只是比特币上涨，所以其他一切应该涨得更多。

哪些网络真正从数字美元与代币化金融活动增长中捕获经济价值？

在受监管的机构市场中，这比在早期投机周期中重要得多。

The current regime therefore produces a more nuanced map.因此，当前体制产生了一张更细腻的地图。

The hard-asset hierarchy硬资产层级

Gold remains the strongest structural monetary signal. Bitcoin remains the strongest liquidity-sensitive monetary optionality. Silver offers more convexity but more downside. Platinum looks more attractive than palladium on structural physical balance. Copper remains one of the best long-run industrial scarcity stories but is vulnerable to bad entries during physical squeezes.黄金仍是最强的结构性货币信号；比特币仍是最强的流动性敏感货币期权；白银凸性更大、下行也更大；按结构性物理平衡看，铂金优于钯金；铜仍是最好的长期工业稀缺故事之一，却容易在物理挤压中出现坏入场点。

Those are not competing conclusions. They are different expressions of the same central fact: capital is becoming more selective.这些结论并不互相竞争，而是同一核心事实的不同表达：资本正在变得更挑剔。

The final task is to convert all of this into a forward map. Not a calendar prophecy. A regime tree. What happens if policymakers successfully manage duration pressure? What happens if the market solves the problem without policy? What happens if inflation refuses to cooperate? And what happens if the duration stress finally enters credit? Those four futures are where the article turns from diagnosis to forecast.最后的任务，是把这一切转换成前瞻地图。不是日历预言，而是一棵体制树：如果政策制定者成功管理久期压力会怎样？如果市场无需政策就解决问题会怎样？如果通胀拒绝配合会怎样？如果久期压力最终进入信用又会怎样？这四种未来，是文章从诊断转向预测的地方。

9Four futures from here从此出发的四种未来

A forecast becomes more useful when it tells the reader how it can fail. That is the main repair we are making to the old Mastermind process.

Our previous reports often expressed a base case with enough specificity that the path itself began to acquire too much authority. The market then did what markets do: it respected some mechanisms, ignored some dates and introduced new evidence that should have changed the path faster than the narrative did.

The better approach is to define a small number of regimes and then let observable variables move probability from one regime to another.

As of August 24, 2026, we see four plausible futures.

These are not statistical confidence intervals. They are research-desk probabilities: a disciplined way to make our priors explicit and force ourselves to update them when the evidence changes.

当预测告诉读者它会如何失败时，它才更有用。这是我们对旧 Mastermind 方法做的核心修复。

以前的报告往往把基准路径写得过于具体，以致路径本身获得了过多权威。市场随后做了市场一贯会做的事：遵循一些机制，忽略一些日期，并提供了本应让路径比叙事更快调整的新证据。

更好的做法是定义少数几种体制，再让可观察变量在它们之间移动概率。截至 2026 年 8 月 24 日，我们看到四种合理未来。这些不是统计置信区间，而是研究台概率：一种把先验摆到台面上，并迫使我们在证据变化时更新的纪律。

Regime A — Managed Duration Pressure S

Current desk probability: 45%

This is our base case. The defining feature is not that long yields collapse. It is that policymakers and market structure gradually prevent long-duration stress from becoming disorderly while the Federal Reserve remains constrained by inflation.

Treasury continues to manage liquidity and maturity composition. Short-duration demand deepens through money-market funds, banks, stablecoins and other pools. The long end remains expensive enough to discipline weak projects but not expensive enough to break credit. Real yields eventually stop rising. The dollar fails to recover its earlier strength. Gold remains structurally bid. Bitcoin retains a better liquidity backdrop than it had in June, but remains volatile. U.S. equities rotate and correct rather than immediately enter a systemic bear market. AI investment continues, but the market rewards companies that can show return on capital rather than merely ambition.

This regime is "managed" because the system adapts. It does not require an explicit yield cap. It does not require QE. It does not even require the Fed to cut immediately. It requires the combination of Treasury financing choices, investor demand, regulatory plumbing and eventually less restrictive real rates to keep the long-end problem from turning into a credit problem.

The hidden cost is that every solution changes the system. If Treasury relies more heavily on short-term financing because bill demand is strong, the government's refinancing exposure to the policy rate rises. If stablecoins become large structural buyers of bills, the dollar system gains a new financing channel but becomes more tightly linked to the health of short-term sovereign collateral. If the Fed later lowers short rates, the relief can arrive into an economy where the AI infrastructure base is already much larger than it was during the original boom.

That is why this regime can be bullish without being safe. It can preserve the cycle long enough for the final stage to become larger.

Regime B — Benign Absorption S

Current desk probability: 20%

This is the cleanest outcome. The market solves the problem naturally. Inflation cools. Energy pressure fades. Labor-market weakness reduces the need for further tightening. Long Treasuries become sufficiently attractive at current yields that private and foreign demand returns. Corporate AI issuance continues but does not require increasingly large concessions. Treasury does not need to keep enlarging its market-support operations. The 30-year yield falls because investors want the bonds, not because the government has to create a stronger mechanism to support them.

This is the closest path to the old 1998-style extension. Financial conditions ease. Real yields fall. The dollar stabilizes or softens gradually. Breadth improves. Small caps participate. Semiconductors can regain leadership because the discount-rate pressure diminishes while demand remains intact. Gold can continue to perform, but the urgency of the fiscal-confidence component should fade. Bitcoin increasingly behaves like a conventional high-beta liquidity asset.

This is the regime that would most clearly prove that the duration-scarcity framework had been overinterpreting a temporary dislocation. It deserves a real probability precisely because the system is not yet broken.

Regime C — Inflation Veto S

Current desk probability: 25%

This is the regime we underweighted in July. The bond problem does not go away because the inflation problem refuses to cooperate. Energy remains expensive. Refined products stay tight. Core inflation fails to decelerate convincingly. The labor market weakens enough to create political pressure for easier policy but not enough to erase inflation. The Fed remains restrictive. Real yields rise again. The dollar strengthens. Long-duration equities de-rate further. Gold gives back part of its recent move. Bitcoin suffers a larger correction because it combines monetary optionality with high-beta liquidity exposure. The market begins to reward current cash flow and nominal beneficiaries while punishing distant optionality.

This regime can still be consistent with the long-run gold thesis. If high real rates ultimately prove fiscally or economically unsustainable, the eventual adjustment may be larger. But the path first runs through a successful tightening of financial conditions.

That is why "inflation is bullish for gold" is an incomplete rule. Inflation is bullish for gold when the policy response is inadequate or when the market loses confidence in the real value of nominal claims. Inflation can be bearish for gold when the central bank credibly raises real returns. The distinction matters.

Regime D — Term-Premium Accident S

Current desk probability: 10%

This is the real crisis branch. It is not "the 30-year yield is 5.3%." It is what happens when long-duration stress stops being contained to valuation and enters credit.

The sequence matters. First, long Treasury yields remain high or rise further despite support. Then auction concessions increase and market depth worsens. Corporate spreads widen. AI-linked debt becomes more expensive. The weakest credits deteriorate. Equity breadth weakens persistently. Volatility stops mean-reverting. Then the market begins selling assets that were not part of the original problem. That is when liquidity itself becomes the scarce asset.

In that environment, Bitcoin likely falls hard. Silver falls harder than gold. Korean and other high-beta equities sell violently. Even good AI infrastructure companies can be liquidated because the market is no longer distinguishing between good and bad projects. Gold may initially fall too as investors raise cash. Then the policy response becomes larger. Only after the liquidity response becomes credible should gold, Bitcoin and quality risk assets begin separating again.

This is why a crisis can be structurally bullish for monetary assets and tactically devastating to them. The timing of the policy response matters as much as the direction of the long-run thesis.

体制 A — 受管理的久期压力 S

当前研究台概率：45%

这是基准情景。它的定义特征不是长端收益率崩塌，而是在美联储仍受通胀约束时，政策与市场结构逐步防止长久期压力失序。财政部继续管理流动性与到期结构；货币市场基金、银行、稳定币等加深短久期需求；长端足够贵，能约束弱项目，却还没贵到击碎信用。实际收益率最终停止上升，美元不再恢复先前强势，黄金保持结构性买盘，比特币流动性背景好于 6 月但仍波动，美股轮动与调整而非立即进入系统性熊市；AI 投资继续，但市场奖励能证明资本回报而不只是展示野心的公司。

它之所以“受管理”，是因为系统在适应。它不需要明示收益率上限，不需要 QE，甚至不需要美联储立即降息；它需要财政部融资选择、投资者需求、监管管道与最终较少限制的实际利率联手，防止长端问题转成信用问题。隐性成本是每个解决方案都会改变系统：更依赖短期融资会提高政府对政策利率的再融资暴露；稳定币成为大型国库券买家会创造新渠道，也使美元体系更依赖短期主权抵押品的健康；美联储日后降低短端利率时，AI 基础设施已比最初热潮庞大得多。因此这一体制可以利多却不安全：它可以让周期延续到最后阶段变得更大。

体制 B — 良性吸收 S

当前研究台概率：20%

这是最干净的结果：市场自然解决问题。通胀降温，能源压力消退，劳动市场走弱减少进一步紧缩的必要，长期美债在当前收益率下足够吸引私人与海外需求回归。AI 企业发债继续，却无需越来越大的让步；财政部不必继续扩大市场支持操作；30 年期收益率下降，是因为投资者想买债，而非政府必须创造更强支持机制。

这最接近旧的 1998 式延长：金融条件放松，实际收益率下降，美元稳定或渐软，广度改善，小盘参与，半导体在需求完好、折现率压力减弱时重获领导；黄金仍可以走强，但财政信心部分的紧迫性消退；比特币更像传统高贝塔流动性资产。这一体制最能证明久期稀缺框架过度解读了一次暂时错位。正因为系统尚未损坏，它应得一个真实概率。

体制 C — 通胀否决 S

当前研究台概率：25%

这是我们 7 月低估的体制。通胀不配合，债券问题就不会消失。能源仍贵，成品油紧张，核心通胀不能有说服力地减速；就业弱到引发政治宽松压力，却不足以抹去通胀。美联储维持限制，实际收益率重升，美元走强，长久期股票进一步降估值，黄金回吐部分涨幅，比特币因货币期权性叠加高贝塔流动性暴露而更深回调。市场奖励当期现金流与名义受益者，惩罚遥远期权。

这仍可与黄金长期论点并存：若高实际利率最终在财政或经济上不可持续，最终调整可能更大，但路径会先经过金融条件的成功紧缩。所以“通胀利多黄金”只是不完整规则：政策回应不足，或市场不再信任名义债权的实际价值时，通胀利多黄金；央行可信地抬高实际回报时，通胀可以利空黄金。区别很重要。

体制 D — 期限溢价事故 S

当前研究台概率：10%

这才是真正危机分支。它不是“30 年期收益率到 5.3%”，而是长久期压力不再停留于估值，开始进入信用。顺序很重要：长期美债收益率在支持下仍高企或继续上升；拍卖让步扩大、市场深度恶化；企业利差走阔；AI 相关债务更贵；最弱信用恶化；股票广度持续走弱；波动率不再均值回归；随后市场开始抛售本不是问题源头的资产。此时稀缺品变成流动性本身。

在这种环境中，比特币很可能大跌，白银跌得比黄金更猛，韩国及其他高贝塔股票剧烈抛售；即使优质 AI 基础设施公司也会被清算，因为市场已不再区分好坏项目。黄金也可能因投资者筹措现金而先跌，然后政策回应变大；只有流动性回应变得可信后，黄金、比特币与优质风险资产才应重新分化。所以危机可以在结构上利多货币资产，战术上却毁灭性打击它们。政策回应的时点，与长期论点的方向同样重要。

10The trigger framework触发框架

The next step is to define the evidence that would make us change our mind.

The levels below are not magical technical boundaries. They are operating thresholds designed to force a research update. We care much more about combinations than individual prints.

Long Treasury yields

The 30-year yield is the first gauge. A move sustainably below roughly 5% without larger support would favor Benign Absorption. A move around 5.0–5.4% is uncomfortable but still consistent with Managed Duration Pressure. A move above roughly 5.5–5.6% that persists while Treasury support fails would materially increase the probability of a Term-Premium Accident. The exact level matters less than whether the market can find willing private buyers without increasingly active intervention.

Real yields

The 10-year real yield tells us whether monetary conditions are becoming more or less restrictive. A sustained move below roughly 2.25% would support the easing side of the framework. A move above roughly 2.55% would materially strengthen the Inflation Veto branch. The key is not one session. It is direction plus persistence.

Breakeven inflation

The breakeven rate helps distinguish high nominal yields caused by real compensation from those driven by inflation expectations. A long yield rising because real yields are rising is different from a long yield rising because investors are demanding more inflation compensation. Both can hurt equities. They imply different policy constraints.

Credit spreads

Broad high-yield OAS is the most important transmission gauge. As long as broad spreads remain tight, the duration problem is mostly a valuation and allocation problem. If they begin widening through roughly 3.5% and continue toward 4% or higher, the probability of systemic transmission rises. The weakest credits should move first. That makes CCC spreads a useful early warning even while the broad index remains calm.

VIX and breadth

Volatility needs to become persistent to confirm a crisis. A one-day VIX spike is not enough. A VIX that stays above the mid-20s while breadth deteriorates and credit spreads widen is a different state. Again, the combination is what matters.

The dollar

The dollar is most informative when read against yields. Long yields rising with a strong dollar is conventional tightening. Long yields rising with a weak dollar is the more dangerous signal because investors are asking for more compensation while showing less enthusiasm for the currency exposure. That is the quadrant that would make the duration-confidence thesis more urgent.

AI credit

AI corporate spreads are the bridge between the sovereign story and the technology story. If tech issuance remains large but easily absorbed, the boom can continue. If investors begin demanding large concessions, spreads widen materially and project financing becomes harder, the cost of capital starts directly rationing AI investment. That would be one of the clearest signs that the duration problem had moved from macro backdrop to real-economy selection mechanism.

下一步是定义哪些证据会让我们改变看法。下列水平不是神奇的技术边界，而是用来强制研究更新的操作阈值。我们对变量组合的重视，远高于单一数据点。

长期美债收益率

30 年期收益率是第一仪表。若无需更大支持就持续低于约 5%，将支持良性吸收；5.0–5.4% 不舒服，但仍符合受管理的久期压力；若持续超过约 5.5–5.6%，同时财政部支持失效，则期限溢价事故概率显著上升。比精确点位更重要的，是市场能否在无需日益积极干预下找到自愿私人买家。

实际收益率

10 年期实际收益率告诉我们货币条件正变得更还是更少限制。持续低于约 2.25% 支持宽松侧；超过约 2.55% 将显著强化通胀否决分支。关键不是一个交易日，而是方向加持续性。

盈亏平衡通胀率

盈亏平衡通胀率有助于区分高名义收益率来自实际回报补偿，还是通胀预期。因实际收益率上升而升的长端，与因投资者要求更高通胀补偿而升的长端不同。两者都会伤害股票，却意味着不同政策约束。

信用利差

广义高收益债 OAS 是最重要的传导仪表。只要广义利差仍紧，久期问题主要还是估值与配置问题。若突破约 3.5% 并继续走向 4% 或更高，系统性传导概率上升。最弱信用应先行，所以即使广义指数平静，CCC 利差也是有用的早期预警。

VIX 与市场广度

波动率必须持续才能确认危机。一日 VIX 飙升不够；若 VIX 在 20 多点中段以上维持，市场广度恶化、信用利差走阔，就是另一种状态。再次，组合才重要。

美元

美元与收益率一起读取时最有信息量。长端与强美元同升是传统紧缩；长端与弱美元同升更危险，因为投资者要求更多补偿，却对货币暴露更没热情。这个象限会让久期信心论点更紧迫。

AI 信用

AI 企业利差是主权故事与科技故事之间的桥。若科技发债仍大但轻松被吸收，热潮可继续；若投资者开始要求大幅让步，利差实质走阔，项目融资更困难，资本成本就开始直接配给 AI 投资。这将是久期问题从宏观背景进入实体经济筛选机制的最清晰信号之一。

Late August 2026: the first compression test

The immediate information cluster is unusually dense. Inflation data test the Inflation Veto. GDP revisions test whether growth is softening normally or moving toward a more stagflationary mix. Nvidia tests whether extraordinary AI fundamentals can still overpower a higher discount rate. Warsh's Jackson Hole communication tests whether the Fed distinguishes between monetary restraint and Treasury-market functioning.

The most important signal from Nvidia is not simply whether revenue beats expectations. It is whether the stock can hold strong fundamentals in a high-yield environment. If it can, earnings power still outruns the duration penalty. If it cannot, the market is telling us that the discount rate has become the controlling variable even for the strongest AI fundamental story.

September 2026: policy separation

Treasury's enlarged long-end buybacks begin in September. The Fed updates its policy outlook. The Bank of Japan meets immediately after. That sequence gives the market three different policy experiments. Treasury tests whether market structure can relieve long-end stress. The Fed tests whether inflation or labor has become the dominant constraint. The BOJ tests whether the global funding environment is changing through Japan.

The most interesting scenario is not one in which all three institutions move in the same direction. It is one in which they diverge. Imagine Treasury supporting long-end functioning while the Fed remains hawkish and the BOJ remains comparatively restrictive. The dollar can weaken. The yen can strengthen. Gold can rise. Carry trades can unwind. U.S. equities can temporarily fall even though the long-run liquidity architecture is becoming more supportive. That is why a simple "dovish equals risk-on" rule is no longer enough.

Autumn 2026: stress window, not ordained low

We still expect September through November to be a high-probability window for volatility and reallocation. The reasons are real. Heavy AI financing. Treasury issuance. Policy uncertainty. Midterm-year seasonality. Energy risk. A stretched leadership complex. A labor market that may be weakening faster than headline growth. Those conditions can produce a material correction.

What we are explicitly dropping is the belief that October itself must be the final low.

If by October: real yields remain high; long yields continue pressing higher; credit spreads are widening; breadth is deteriorating; and the dollar is behaving poorly against gold—then the old October thesis may still look prescient.

If instead: real yields are falling; Treasury-market demand is improving; credit remains calm; and the dollar is already weakening—then the market may have front-run the old autumn low.

The right response is to follow the state, not defend the date.

November 2026: Treasury's architecture checkpoint

The November refunding is a natural review point. If long-end pressure has eased, Treasury should have less reason to expand duration-management tools. If the problem persists and support grows, Managed Duration Pressure gains evidence. If support expands while the dollar weakens and inflation expectations rise, the conflict with the Fed becomes more serious. That would be the point where the market begins asking whether liquidity management is turning into a broader attempt to suppress financing costs. Again, the direction matters more than the label.

Year-end 2026: the labor–inflation crossover

By the end of the year, the central macro question should be clearer. Does labor weakness finally outrank inflation?

If energy pressure fades and core inflation continues cooling, the Fed's reason for maintaining a highly restrictive real rate weakens substantially. That would allow the sequence our previous reports expected to begin later: hike expectations die; real yields peak; the dollar weakens; gold strengthens; Bitcoin's liquidity regime improves; quality growth re-rates; broader risk participation follows.

The main revision is that parts of this sequence have already started before the policy rate changed. Markets trade expected architecture. They do not wait for the central bank to issue a ceremonial confirmation.

January 2027: the stablecoin experiment becomes real

The GENIUS framework becomes important not because one date changes markets overnight, but because the regulatory architecture begins moving from law to operating system. From that point onward, the relevant data become observable. How quickly does regulated stablecoin supply grow? Where does the money come from? How much of the reserve growth is incremental Treasury demand? How much merely relocates money-market or deposit assets? Does Treasury adapt issuance around the new short-end buyer base? Does stablecoin growth expand global dollar usage?

This is the point where the stablecoin thesis stops being mainly conceptual and starts becoming measurable.

H1 2027: the cleanest easing window

Our base case still gives the first half of 2027 the highest probability of a more conventional improvement in monetary conditions. But the reason is now broader than "inflation falls, therefore the Fed cuts."

By then, the cumulative effect of: high long yields; weaker labor; housing stress; government refinancing sensitivity; corporate capital consumption; and eventually softer energy pressure may make the current real-rate structure increasingly difficult to sustain.

If inflation permits, the Fed can finally join the easing process that Treasury and market structure had already begun indirectly. That is when the liquidity impulse becomes more powerful. The sequence would matter. Gold should already be acting well. Bitcoin should have a stronger macro base. High-quality growth should begin expanding its multiple. Small caps can participate if credit remains healthy. The AI companies that survived the hard-money phase with intact economics become particularly interesting.

H2 2027: the AI return-on-capital test

This is where the boom either graduates or fails. By the second half of 2027, investors should have much better evidence on the return produced by the enormous 2025–26 infrastructure buildout. Cloud revenue. Inference margins. Utilization. Depreciation. Power economics. Networking demand. Customer willingness to pay. Project cancellations. Free cash flow.

The story stops being about capex plans. It becomes about what the capex actually earned.

If monetization is strong and real financing costs are falling, the AI cycle can enter its most powerful stage. The market will see: visible revenue; improving free cash flow; a huge installed infrastructure base; less competition because weak projects were filtered out; and cheaper capital. That is the setup for a potential final blow-off.

If monetization disappoints while capex remains high, the same installed infrastructure becomes evidence of overbuilding. Then the cycle begins transitioning from scarcity to excess capacity. That is the true fork.

2027–28: when the 2000 analogy becomes useful

The 2000 analogy is too dramatic for the current market. Expensive technology stocks are not enough. A terminal technology bubble requires a broader capital-allocation failure.

We would become much more concerned if: weak AI projects regain easy funding; IPO and speculative issuance explode; credit spreads widen beneath rising equities; depreciation and asset obsolescence are ignored; capacity keeps expanding after return on capital deteriorates; and investors once again stop asking what the infrastructure actually earns. That would look much more like the terminal phase of a capital cycle.

The paradox is that successfully managing the current duration stress may be what allows that phase to happen. Avoiding today's crash can make tomorrow's bubble larger. That is why the most bullish path is not necessarily the safest path.

我们仍认为 9 至 11 月是波动与再配置的高概率窗口，原因真实存在：巨量 AI 融资、美债发行、政策不确定、中期选举年季节性、能源风险、过度伸展的领涨群以及可能比标题增长更快走弱的就业。这些条件可以引发实质调整。我们明确放弃的是“10 月本身必须是最终低点”。若到 10 月实际收益率仍高、长端继续上压、信用利差走阔、广度恶化、美元相对黄金表现差，旧的 10 月论点仍可能显得有远见；若实际收益率下降、美债需求改善、信用平静、美元已弱，市场可能已抢跑秋季低点。正确回应是跟随状态，不是捍卫日期。

2026 年 11 月：财政部架构检查点

11 月再融资是天然复盘点。若长端压力缓解，财政部扩大久期管理工具的理由减少；若问题持续、支持增加，受管理的久期压力获得证据；若支持扩大同时美元走弱、通胀预期上升，与美联储的冲突就更严重。市场将开始追问，流动性管理是否正变成更广泛的融资成本压制。方向比标签重要。

2026 年底：就业—通胀交叉

到年底，核心宏观问题应更清晰：就业走弱是否终于压过通胀？若能源压力消退、核心通胀继续降温，美联储维持高度限制性实际利率的理由大幅减弱，旧报告预期更晚开始的顺序便可启动：加息预期消失，实际收益率见顶，美元走弱，黄金走强，比特币流动性体制改善，优质成长重估，更广风险资产参与。主要修订是这个顺序的一部分已在政策利率改变前开始。市场交易预期架构，不等央行仪式性确认。

2027 年 1 月：稳定币实验进入现实

GENIUS 框架重要，不是因为一个日期会隔夜改变市场，而是监管架构开始从法律移向操作系统。从那时起，相关数据可观察：受监管稳定币供给增长多快？资金从哪来？储备增长有多少是增量美债需求？多少只是货币市场或存款资产搬家？财政部是否围绕新短端买家调整发行？稳定币增长是否扩大全球美元使用？至此，稳定币论点从主要是概念，转为可测量。

2027 上半年：最干净的宽松窗口

基准情景仍认为 2027 上半年最有可能出现更传统的货币条件改善，但理由已不只是“通胀下降，所以美联储降息”。到那时，高长端收益率、较弱就业、住房压力、政府再融资敏感性、企业资本消耗与最终较软能源压力的累积效应，可使当前实际利率结构越来越难持续。若通胀允许，美联储终于可加入财政部与市场结构已间接启动的宽松，流动性脉冲更强。顺序重要：黄金应已走强，比特币应有更强宏观基础，优质成长开始扩张倍数，若信用健康小盘可参与，在硬货币阶段保住经济性的 AI 公司尤其有吸引力。

2027 下半年：AI 资本回报考验

热潮将在这里升级或失败。到 2027 下半年，投资者应对 2025–26 年巨大基础设施建设所产生的回报有更好证据：云收入、推理利润率、利用率、折旧、电力经济性、网络需求、客户支付意愿、项目取消与自由现金流。故事不再是资本开支计划，而是开支实际赚了什么。若变现强、实际融资成本下降，AI 周期可进入最强阶段：可见收入、改善自由现金流、巨大装机基础、因弱项目被淘汰而减少的竞争，以及更便宜资本，构成潜在最后喷出。若变现失望而开支仍高，同一装机基础就成为过度建设证据，周期从稀缺转向过剩产能。这才是真正分叉。

2027–28 年：2000 年类比何时有用

对当前市场而言，2000 年类比过于戏剧化；贵的科技股还不够，终局泡沫需要更广泛的资本配置失败。若弱 AI 项目重获廉价融资、IPO 与投机发行爆炸、股价上涨下信用利差走阔、折旧与资产淘汰被忽视、资本回报恶化后产能仍扩张、投资者再度不问基础设施赚什么，我们才会明显更担忧。那才像资本周期终局。悶论是，成功管理当前久期压力，可能正是让该阶段发生的条件。避免今天崩盘，可以让明天的泡沫更大。所以最看涨的路径，不一定最安全。

13What would prove this report wrong什么会证明本报告错了

A serious research report should make its own invalidation conditions obvious.

The central thesis would weaken materially if the market solved the duration problem without increasingly active policy engineering.

The clean falsification would look like this: long Treasury yields fall sustainably; private and foreign auction demand improves; AI corporate issuance remains easily absorbed; credit spreads stay tight; core inflation trends cleanly lower; energy pressure fades; the dollar remains stable; and Treasury does not need to keep expanding duration-management tools.

That would mean the current episode was primarily a temporary mix of positioning, liquidity and inflation uncertainty. The market found a natural clearing price. The sovereign-duration story would deserve less weight. Gold's fiscal-confidence premium would deserve to compress. Benign Absorption would become the dominant regime.

The opposite constellation would make the thesis much more urgent.

If: long yields move materially higher and stay there; real yields remain extremely restrictive; the dollar weakens anyway; credit spreads widen; VIX becomes persistent; Treasury auction demand worsens; AI financing becomes visibly more expensive; and breadth deteriorates—then the market is no longer merely asking for a higher price to hold duration. It is beginning to question the mechanism through which that duration will ultimately be absorbed.

That is the point where the fiscal/monetary endgame becomes less theoretical.

严肃的研究报告应让自身的失效条件一目了然。若市场无需日益积极的政策工程就解决久期问题，中心论点将实质走弱。

干净证伪应是：长期美债收益率持续下降；私人与海外拍卖需求改善；AI 企业发债仍轻松被吸收；信用利差紧；核心通胀清晰趋降；能源压力消退；美元稳定；财政部不必继续扩大久期管理工具。

这将说明当前事件主要是仓位、流动性与通胀不确定的暂时组合，市场找到了自然出清价；主权久期故事应降权，黄金的财政信心溢价应收缩，良性吸收将成为主导体制。

相反的组合会让论点更紧迫：长端收益率实质上升并居高不下；实际收益率仍极具限制性；美元却走弱；信用利差走阔；VIX 持续；美债拍卖需求恶化；AI 融资明显更贵；市场广度恶化。此时市场已不只是要求更高久期持有价格，而在质疑这些久期最终通过什么机制被吸收。到那一点，财政/货币终局就不再那么理论化。

14The price of duration decides the next act久期的价格决定下一幕

The last decade trained investors to think about capital as if it were an unlimited background resource. Companies could invest aggressively because financing was cheap. Governments could refinance enormous debt stocks because nominal yields were low. Long-duration growth could command extraordinary valuations because the discount rate barely punished distant cash flows.

The AI boom began inside that inheritance.

But AI is not weightless. It requires factories. It requires chips. It requires memory. It requires transmission lines. It requires transformers. It requires cooling. It requires buildings. It requires generation. It requires debt.

The deeper AI moves into the physical economy, the more it has to compete with every other borrower for capital. That includes the U.S. government. And the U.S. government is not a small competitor.

This is why the current market is more important than a debate about whether Nvidia beats one quarterly estimate. The system is discovering what it costs to finance: a giant sovereign debt load; an industrial AI revolution; energy infrastructure; defense; housing; and the rest of the private economy at the same time.

The market's answer is the long-term yield.

If that yield remains tolerable, the cycle continues. If it falls naturally, the next phase becomes easier. If policymakers successfully manage it without reigniting inflation, the cycle can become larger. If inflation prevents relief, the market has to keep destroying weak demand. If the yield rises far enough to enter credit, the system eventually responds with much larger tools.

That is the decision tree.

Why gold moved first

Gold does not need to know which policy wins. It only needs uncertainty to rise around the real value of long-duration nominal claims.

The market is no longer certain whether the final adjustment occurs through: higher taxes; lower spending; shorter debt maturity; lower policy rates; higher inflation; a weaker dollar; regulatory changes; or eventually central-bank accommodation.

Gold benefits from the distribution of outcomes becoming less friendly to long nominal duration. That is why its move matters.

Why Bitcoin moved early

Bitcoin responded to a different part of the same architecture. The liquidity clock changed before the old calendar expected it to. Treasury-market stress. A softer dollar. Stablecoin architecture. Future policy expectations. Those variables altered the monetary backdrop before a conventional easing cycle had begun.

Bitcoin front-ran the transition. That does not make it safe. It makes the historical cycle calendar less authoritative.

Why AI is not over

AI demand did not disappear because long yields rose. The buildout is still real. But the rules changed. Scarcity alone is no longer enough. The next winners need to prove that they can convert capital into cash at a return above the new hurdle rate. That is healthier than indiscriminate enthusiasm. It is also more difficult. The market will become less forgiving. That is precisely what a mature capital cycle should do.

Why the market has not broken yet

Perhaps the most important fact in this entire report is the one that sounds least dramatic.

Credit has not broken. Volatility has not become persistent. Treasury auctions still clear. Equity capital is still available. The system has not moved from expensive capital to unavailable capital.

That means the current regime still gives investors time. Time to distinguish the companies building productive infrastructure from those financing narratives. Time to distinguish memory profits from memory positioning. Time to distinguish Hong Kong liquidity from mainland property weakness. Time to distinguish Japan's funding role from a simple equity call. Time to distinguish gold's monetary signal from silver's beta, platinum's physical balance and copper's industrial scarcity. Time to distinguish Bitcoin's liquidity clock from its old calendar.

A crisis would make many of those distinctions irrelevant for a while. The market is not there. That is the opportunity.

The lesson from our own mistakes

Our previous work got enough right to remain useful. But it placed too much confidence in the sequence. We thought the future would arrive in order. It did not.

The sovereign-duration problem moved early. Gold moved early. Bitcoin moved early. Energy interrupted the disinflation path. Memory profits stayed strong after memory equities broke.

That changed our view of forecasting more than it changed our view of the underlying cycle.

The repair is simple:

Mechanisms over dates.
States over stories.
Cash flow over narrative.
Triggers over calendar certainty.

The next article we write on this cycle should not have to explain away a missed October low. It should already know what evidence would make October irrelevant.

That is the standard.

The one sentence

If everything in this report had to be compressed into one line, it would be this:

The next market cycle will be decided by whether the United States can finance both its sovereign debt and the AI buildout at a tolerable long-term yield without forcing the adjustment into inflation, the dollar, credit, or some combination of all three.

That is the price of duration.

And the price of duration will decide whether the AI boom ends here—or becomes much larger before it ends.

过去十年训练投资者把资本视为无限的背景资源。融资便宜，企业可激进投资；名义收益率低，政府可为巨额债务再融资；折现率几乎不惩罚遥远现金流，长久期成长可获得非凡估值。AI 热潮在这份遗产中启动。

但 AI 没有重量为零。它需要工厂、芯片、存储、输电线、变压器、冷却、建筑、发电与债务。AI 越深入实体经济，就越需要与所有其他借款人争夺资本。其中包括美国政府，而美国政府不是小对手。

这就是为何当前市场比“英伟达是否击败单季预期”的辩论更重要。系统正在发现，同时为巨额主权债务、工业 AI 革命、能源基础设施、国防、住房与私营经济其余部分融资究竟要付多少。市场的答案是长期收益率。

若收益率仍可承受，周期继续；若自然下降，下一阶段更容易；若政策成功管理且不重燃通胀，周期可变得更大；若通胀阻止缓解，市场必须继续消灭弱需求；若收益率高到进入信用，系统最终用大得多的工具回应。这就是决策树。

为什么黄金先动

黄金不需要知道哪一种政策胜出，只需长久期名义债权的实际价值不确定性上升。市场已不确定最终调整是通过更高税收、更低支出、更短债务到期、更低政策利率、更高通胀、更弱美元、监管变化，还是最终的央行宽松完成。结果分布变得对长期名义久期更不友好，黄金受益。所以它的波动很重要。

为什么比特币提前移动

比特币回应了同一架构的不同部分。流动性时钟比旧日历预期更早变化：美债市场压力、较软美元、稳定币架构与未来政策预期，在传统宽松周期启动前就改变了货币背景。比特币抢跑了转折。这不使它安全，只是让历史周期日历权威下降。

为什么 AI 没有结束

AI 需求没有因长端收益率上升而消失，建设仍真实，但规则变了。只有稀缺已不够；下一批赢家必须证明，能把资本转换为高于新门槛收益率的现金。这比无差别狂热更健康，也更困难。市场会更不宽容，这正是成熟资本周期应做的事。

为什么市场尚未损坏

整份报告中最重要的事实，也许恰好最不戏剧化：信用没有破，波动率没有变得持续，美债拍卖仍出清，股权资本仍可得，系统尚未从昂贵资本转向不可得资本。

这意味着当前体制仍给投资者时间：区分建设生产性基础设施的公司与为叙事融资的公司；区分存储利润与存储仓位；区分香港流动性与大陆地产疲弱；区分日本的融资角色与一个简单股票判断；区分黄金货币信号、白银贝塔、铂金物理平衡与铜的工业稀缺；区分比特币流动性时钟与旧日历。危机会让许多区别暂时失去意义。市场还没到那里。这就是机会。

从自己的错误中学到什么

旧工作做对了足够多，仍有用；但它对顺序的信心过高。我们以为未来会按顺序到来，事实不是。主权久期问题提前，黄金提前，比特币提前，能源打断去通胀路径，存储股破位后存储利润仍强。这对我们预测观的改变，大于对底层周期观的改变。

修复很简单：

机制重于日期。
状态重于故事。
现金流重于叙事。
触发器重于日历确定性。

我们下一篇讨论这个周期的文章，不应再需要为错过的 10 月低点辩解；它应预先知道哪些证据会让 10 月不再重要。这就是标准。

那一句话

若必须把整份报告压缩成一句话，就是：

下一个市场周期，将由美国能否以可承受的长期收益率，同时为主权债务与 AI 建设融资而决定；它不能迫使调整进入通胀、美元、信用，或三者的某种组合。

这就是久期的价格。久期的价格将决定 AI 热潮是否在此结束——还是在结束前变得大得多。

3The price of duration久期的价格

The governing mechanism · one auction for long-term capital治理全局的机制 · 一场长期资本拍卖

To understand why the current market feels internally contradictory, stop thinking of interest rates as something the Federal Reserve simply “sets.” The Fed controls a very short-term policy rate. The market determines the price of lending money for ten, twenty or thirty years.要理解当前市场为何内在矛盾，就别再把利率想成美联储简单“设定”的数字。美联储控制的是极短期政策利率；借出十年、二十年或三十年资金的价格，由市场决定。

A long-term investor accepts decades of inflation risk, fiscal risk, opportunity cost and uncertainty. When duration supply rises—or buyers become less willing to hold it at the old yield—the yield must rise until the market clears.长期投资者承担的是数十年的通胀风险、财政风险、机会成本与不确定性。当久期供给上升，或买家不再愿意按原有收益率持有时，收益率必须上升，直到市场完成出清。

12The next act is decided by the price of capital下一幕由资本价格决定

Conclusion · which AI, which balance sheet, which regime结论 · 哪一种 AI、哪一张资产负债表、哪一种体制

AI changes the capital cycle because AI is becoming physical. Physical AI requires factories, chips, memory, power plants, transmission lines, transformers, cooling systems, buildings and debt. The deeper the boom moves into the physical economy, the more it must compete with every other borrower for capital—including the U.S. government.AI 改变资本周期，因为 AI 正在实体化。实体 AI 需要工厂、芯片、存储、电厂、输电线、变压器、冷却系统、建筑与债务。热潮越深入实体经济，就越必须与其他每一个借款人争夺资本——包括美国政府。

Gold moved first because it does not need to know which adjustment wins. Bitcoin moved early because its liquidity clock changed before the old calendar expected it. AI is not over; the rules changed. Scarcity alone is no longer enough. The next winners must convert capital into cash above the new hurdle rate.黄金先动，因为它不需要知道哪一种调整最终胜出；比特币提前，是因为其流动性时钟早于旧日历发生改变。AI 没有结束，规则变了。只有稀缺已经不够。下一批赢家必须把资本转成高于新门槛收益率的现金。

The next market cycle will be decided by whether the United States can finance both its sovereign debt and the AI buildout at a tolerable long-term yield without forcing the adjustment into inflation, the dollar, credit—or all three.下一个市场周期，将由美国能否以可承受的长期收益率同时为主权债务与 AI 建设融资，并避免把调整强行推入通胀、美元、信用——或三者同时——而决定。
