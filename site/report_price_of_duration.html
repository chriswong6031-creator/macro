The Price of Duration久期的价格

The AI boom did not end. The debt problem did not wait for 2027. They collided. As Treasury, hyperscalers, utilities and the rest of the AI buildout compete for the same capital, long yields have become the market's governing price. Gold and Bitcoin have already begun reacting to what comes next. The question is no longer simply whether the Fed cuts—it is who absorbs the duration, at what price, and what policymakers do when that price becomes unacceptable.AI 热潮没有结束，债务问题也没有等到 2027 年——两者已经相撞。随着财政部、超大规模云厂商、公用事业公司与 AI 建设链的其余参与者争夺同一池资本，长端收益率已成为市场的治理价格。黄金与比特币已经开始回应下一步。问题不再只是美联储是否降息，而是谁来吸收久期、以什么价格吸收，以及当这个价格变得不可接受时，政策制定者会做什么。

Bottom line核心结论

The market is not yet in a credit crisis. It is in something more interesting: the cost of long-term capital has risen enough to begin disciplining the AI buildout and the sovereign financing system at the same time. Gold is reacting before conventional easing arrives, Bitcoin’s liquidity clock has moved ahead of the old cycle calendar, and equities are beginning to separate according to capital efficiency rather than AI narrative alone. The next phase depends on whether the long end stabilizes naturally, is managed through Treasury and market structure, stays pinned by inflation, or finally transmits into credit.市场尚未陷入信用危机。它正处于更耐人寻味的阶段：长期资本成本已上升到足以同时约束 AI 建设与主权融资体系的程度。黄金在传统宽松到来前已作出反应；比特币的流动性时钟走在旧周期日历之前；股票也开始按资本效率分化，而不再只按 AI 叙事分化。下一阶段取决于长端收益率是自然稳定、通过财政部与市场结构得到管理、被通胀钉住，还是最终传导进信用。

1The market just told us something changed市场刚刚告诉我们：有些东西变了

There are moments when markets stop behaving according to the story investors have been using to explain them. Those moments matter because the anomaly is usually more informative than the trend.

This is one of those moments.

Gold has broken sharply higher even though long-term interest rates remain restrictive. Bitcoin has moved before the conventional easing cycle we once thought it would need. The dollar has weakened from its hawkish-squeeze highs. At the same time, VIX and broad high-yield credit still look far calmer than a genuine financial accident should look. If this were simply an inflation scare, gold should be struggling under high real rates. If it were simply a growth scare, long Treasuries should be acting like the haven. If it were already a fiscal crisis, credit and volatility should look much worse.

The missing piece is the price of duration.

The United States is trying to finance two enormous projects at the same time. The first is the federal government itself: persistent deficits, a vast stock of debt that must be refinanced, growing interest expense, defense spending and industrial policy. The second is the AI buildout: data centers, accelerators, memory, power generation, transformers, cooling, networking, construction and the increasingly large amount of debt and outside capital required to finance it.

Both ultimately arrive at the same market. Both need investors willing to lock money away for years. When the amount of long-term capital demanded rises faster than the amount investors willingly supply at yesterday's price, the yield has to rise until the market balances.

That sounds abstract. It is not. The long-term yield determines what a mortgage costs, what a data center must earn to justify being built, how expensive it is for a hyperscaler to issue another bond, what a software company's future profits are worth today and how much interest the federal government pays to refinance itself. It changes whether Japanese institutions prefer Treasuries or domestic JGBs. It changes the opportunity cost of holding gold. And it changes which AI projects survive once money is no longer cheap.

The AI boom was born in a world where capital could mostly be treated as background infrastructure. That era is over. AI is becoming physical, and physical AI has to compete with every other borrower for capital. The market is now discovering the yield at which the government's financing needs, the AI industry's financing needs and global investors' willingness to absorb duration begin to conflict.

The anomaly that matters

The important signal is not any one asset. It is the combination:

  • long yields remain high;
  • the dollar is softer than during the June hawkish shock;
  • gold is strong;
  • Bitcoin has repriced earlier than our old cycle map expected;
  • semiconductor leadership has become rate-sensitive;
  • yet broad credit remains relatively calm.

That combination says the cost of capital is uncomfortable, but the system is still absorbing it. This is why our base case is not an immediate fiscal crisis and not an immediate melt-up. It is a period of selection: expensive capital begins distinguishing productive investment from speculative investment before liquidity disappears altogether.

2What changed since July七月以来，哪些判断变了

The value of reviewing an old forecast is not to defend it. It is to find out where the model actually failed.

Between June and July, Mastermind published a series of reports arguing that the first stage of the AI boom had become dangerously crowded, that the physical buildout would continue, that leadership would migrate toward power and infrastructure, and that the dollar/debasement cycle would eventually turn. A surprising amount of that held up. What did not hold up was the sequence.

The broad calls that worked

Our earlier work correctly argued that AI infrastructure spending was not ending. Capital continued moving into power, electrical equipment, networking and the physical systems required to convert model demand into usable compute. We were also right that the Magnificent Seven would stop behaving like one trade; the difference between Microsoft, Meta, Alphabet, Nvidia and Apple increasingly comes down to how each company finances AI investment and how much cash remains after the spending.

We were right that semiconductor and memory equities had become dangerously crowded. Korea later demonstrated exactly why. We were right to keep the structural gold thesis alive after the June washout, and we were right that Bitcoin accumulation should begin before a perfect cycle low rather than wait for one capitulation print everyone could see coming. We were also directionally right that Hong Kong technology could behave better than the bleak story told by China's household and property economy.

The calls that failed—or arrived early

The record was not clean. Our Brent base case was invalidated by renewed geopolitical stress. Our first attempt to identify Meta as an asymmetric megacap haven failed its opening proof test. We treated the memory stock cycle and the memory fundamental cycle too similarly: the equities became crowded and vulnerable while the underlying HBM/DRAM profit pool remained exceptionally strong.

Most importantly, we placed too much authority on timing. We expected the rough sequence to be:

hawkish squeeze → autumn correction → disinflation → liquidity turn → larger 2027 debt/AI-financing stress.

The market refused to wait. The sovereign-duration problem arrived early. Gold's next leg arrived early. Bitcoin's liquidity response arrived early. AI financing became a bond-market issue earlier than we expected. Energy inflation returned before the disinflation glide path had completed its work.

The processes we imagined as consecutive began overlapping.

The repair: mechanisms over dates

The lesson is not that timing is useless. It is that causal conditions must outrank calendar analogies. If a condition we expected in October appears in August, the forecast should move to August. If a stock peaks while its industry's profit cycle remains strong, we should stop pretending one "cycle" controls both. If policy begins changing market structure before the Fed changes the overnight rate, we should not wait for the official easing cycle to recognize the liquidity shift.

That is why this report is organized around regimes, triggers and reaction functions rather than a predetermined month-by-month path. Dates still matter when real information arrives—FOMC meetings, Treasury refundings, BOJ decisions, stablecoin implementation and earnings. The calendar no longer gets to overrule the evidence.

3The price of duration久期的价格

To understand why the current market feels internally contradictory, stop thinking of interest rates as something the Federal Reserve simply "sets." The Fed controls a very short-term policy rate. The market determines the price of lending money for ten, twenty or thirty years.

A long-term investor is accepting decades of inflation risk, fiscal risk, opportunity cost and uncertainty. When the supply of duration rises—or when buyers become less willing to hold it at the previous yield—the yield has to rise until the market clears.

The easiest way to visualize the current regime is as one giant auction for long-term capital. On one side are all the borrowers: Treasury, hyperscalers, utilities, data-center developers, power projects, ordinary corporations and mortgage borrowers. On the other side are pensions, insurers, banks, money-market funds, sovereign investors, households, foreign reserve managers and every other pool willing to hold a claim that matures years from now.

The yield is the clearing price.

The problem begins when that clearing price becomes high enough to damage the economy that created the borrowing demand in the first place. Mortgage rates rise. Corporate refinancing becomes more expensive. Government interest expense grows. Equity multiples compress. And the required return on new AI projects rises.

AI is becoming its own cost of capital

Artificial intelligence is usually described as software because the user interacts with models through software. Economically, the current AI boom is one of the most physical technology cycles in modern history. Training and running models requires accelerators; accelerators require memory; clusters require networking; racks require cooling and power distribution; buildings require transformers, land, substations, transmission and increasingly dedicated generation.

All of that requires capital before it produces output.

The early phase could be financed largely through the exceptional cash generation of the largest technology companies. As the scale grows, that is changing. Corporate borrowing grows. Project finance grows. Utilities need more capital. Infrastructure funds need more capital. The supply of corporate duration rises alongside sovereign duration.

The feedback loop is straightforward: AI demand drives more capex; more capex drives more financing; more financing increases duration supply; investors require more yield; higher yields raise AI hurdle rates; weak projects stop clearing those hurdle rates; capital discipline improves; and stronger operators gain share.

Why the long bond suddenly matters to equity investors

The same future profit is worth less when investors can earn a higher return elsewhere. That is why Nvidia can report extraordinary demand and still experience multiple compression if long yields rise. It is why a company can be fundamentally right and still be a bad stock at the wrong price.

This is also why the present regime is not automatically bearish for AI. Expensive money can strengthen the survivors. Weak projects are canceled. Duplicative capacity is delayed. Companies with genuine demand and internal financing keep investing. If real rates fall later, those survivors can enter the next phase with more infrastructure, less competition and visibly better economics.

That is the paradox we will return to in 2027: successfully surviving the hard-money phase can create the conditions for a much larger final boom.

4Dalio, Treasury, and the policy trap达利欧、财政部与政策困局

Ray Dalio's recent work on late debt cycles is useful because it focuses on the mechanism rather than the headline level of debt. A government can carry enormous debt for a long time as long as buyers willingly absorb it at an acceptable yield. The critical transition comes when the quantity of debt that must be sold rises faster than demand at the prevailing price.

Then the bond market clears through a higher yield.

At first, that is simply the market working. Higher yields attract new buyers and discipline speculative capital. The problem is that higher yields also raise the government's own borrowing cost. If enough debt must be refinanced, interest expense can begin compounding into the deficit that created the supply problem in the first place.

That is where policy choices become harder.

Treasury has developed a reaction function

Treasury cannot repeal the bond market, but it can influence the composition and functioning of the market that produces the yield. Inflation is the veto

The entire system turns on inflation because inflation determines how much freedom policymakers have. If inflation falls convincingly, the menu of solutions expands. The Fed can lower short rates; real yields can fall; Treasury financing becomes easier; corporate credit becomes cheaper; AI hurdle rates decline; and gold can rise through lower real rates without necessarily signaling fiscal panic.

If inflation remains too high, every option becomes more dangerous. Labor can weaken and still fail to produce cuts. Treasury can improve the plumbing and still see the currency weaken. Long rates can remain high because investors demand inflation compensation. Gold can even correct if the Fed responds by forcing real yields higher.

That is why the recent energy shock matters beyond crude itself. Refining, diesel, gasoline, utilities, shipping and food distribution can keep the price level uncomfortable even if oil never returns to its absolute wartime high.

The danger is an economy weak enough to want relief but inflationary enough to deny it.

That is the Inflation Veto.

Stablecoins are usually introduced as part of the cryptocurrency market. In this cycle, that is too narrow. Their more important macro role may be as a new distribution system for dollar liabilities backed by short-term U.S. government collateral.

The mechanism is simple. A regulated dollar stablecoin promises the holder something economically close to a digital dollar. To make that promise credible, the issuer holds liquid reserves—cash, repos and short-dated Treasury securities. If stablecoins grow from hundreds of billions of dollars into the multi-trillion-dollar system policymakers envision, the reserve pool becomes large enough to matter to Treasury funding.

The critical word is short-dated.

A stablecoin can be redeemed quickly. A 30-year Treasury can move dramatically in price. Stablecoin issuers therefore do not naturally solve the problem of finding investors willing to hold 20- or 30-year bonds. The hidden maturity trade

If stablecoins make the short end easier to finance, Treasury can lean more heavily on bills when long-duration demand is weak. That can reduce some marginal pressure on long-maturity issuance. But short debt has to be refinanced more often. The system trades long-duration market risk for greater sensitivity to the policy rate.

That matters because the government's interest burden becomes more responsive to short rates precisely as stablecoins make short financing more scalable. A larger bill market can therefore strengthen Treasury's financing flexibility today while increasing the economic value of lower short rates tomorrow.

The full loop is:

stablecoins grow → bill demand rises → Treasury gains more short-end capacity → marginal long-duration pressure can fall → federal duration shortens → refinancing sensitivity to Fed policy rises.

This is not free money. It is a maturity transformation.

Why stablecoins can strengthen the dollar without ending the debasement thesis

There is no contradiction between a larger global dollar network and a long-run concern about the real value of long-duration dollar claims. A resident of a country with a weaker currency may prefer a dollar stablecoin even if U.S. inflation remains above target. A global business may use stablecoins because trade is already dollarized. A crypto participant may want stablecoin liquidity regardless of a gold thesis.

The dollar can gain network share while investors simultaneously demand more compensation to hold 30 years of fixed nominal dollar exposure.

That distinction is central to the report. Demand for transactional dollars can be extremely strong at the same time long-duration dollar claims become more price-sensitive.

Treasury and crypto are becoming connected through collateral

Stablecoins also create a new bridge between conventional sovereign finance and crypto-native liquidity. Treasury bills back stablecoin reserves; stablecoins provide settlement liquidity inside digital-asset markets; growing digital-asset activity can increase demand for stablecoins; and stablecoin growth can increase demand for bills.

Bitcoin remains economically outside that reserve loop. Bitcoin is not backed by Treasury bills. But the liquidity system surrounding Bitcoin can increasingly rest on Treasury-backed digital dollars.

That makes the next crypto cycle more connected to U.S. sovereign collateral than previous cycles were—even if Bitcoin's monetary thesis remains intentionally separate.

6AI after the easy money宽松资金之后的 AI

The first phase of the AI boom rewarded the company closest to scarcity. There were not enough accelerators, then not enough HBM, then not enough optical networking, power, cooling or transformer capacity. When demand runs into a hard bottleneck, the economics are easy: the scarce supplier wins.

That phase is becoming less forgiving.

AI demand remains enormous, but the market is beginning to ask a different question:

How much durable cash flow does each additional dollar of AI investment actually create?

Revenue growth is no longer enough. Two companies can each generate $10 billion of new AI revenue and deserve completely different valuations if one spent $8 billion to create it and the other spent $40 billion, issued debt, diluted shareholders and still has uncertain utilization.

This is the transition from AI exposure to AI capital efficiency.

AI 热潮第一阶段奖励的是最靠近稀缺性的公司。先是加速器不够，继而是 HBM、光网络、电力、冷却与变压器产能不足。当需求撞上硬瓶颈时，经济逻辑很简单：稀缺供应商取胜。

这一阶段正变得不再宽容。

AI 需求依然庞大，但市场开始追问一个不同的问题：

每多投入一美元 AI 资本，究竟能创造多少持久现金流？

仅有收入增长已经不够。两家公司都可以新增 100 亿美元 AI 收入，但如果一家投入 80 亿美元，另一家投入 400 亿美元、发行债务、稀释股东且利用率仍不确定，它们应得到完全不同的估值。

Why gold moved before the Fed

Our earlier model expected the durable next gold leg to require a cleaner path toward falling real yields and eventual Fed easing. Instead, gold accelerated while long yields were still high.

That tells us the market added another variable: uncertainty over how the duration problem will ultimately be resolved.

The forward map should not be a fake smooth price path. It should be a sequence of information gates.

The question at each gate is not "did the market follow our month?" It is "which regime gained evidence?"

PHASE I · Now → Early Autumn 2026

Information and policy separation

The immediate period tests three pillars at once: inflation, AI economics and policy reaction functions.

Inflation data tell us whether the Fed's constraint is becoming easier or harder. Major AI earnings tell us whether extraordinary fundamental growth can still outrun a high discount rate. Treasury operations tell us whether market-functioning tools can stabilize the long end without needing a conventional monetary pivot.

What to watch: core inflation · 30Y reaction to Treasury operations · real yields · AI equity response to strong/weak earnings · DXY.

If strong AI results stop supporting AI stocks while long yields remain high, the market is telling us duration has become the controlling variable even for the strongest fundamentals.

PHASE II · Autumn 2026

Stress and reallocation

We still expect autumn to be a high-probability volatility window. The reasons are not mystical seasonality: heavy AI financing, policy uncertainty, energy risk, stretched leadership, Treasury supply and a labor market that may be weakening faster than headline growth.

What we are dropping is the belief that October itself must be the final low.

If by autumn real yields remain high, credit spreads are widening and breadth is deteriorating, the old "October low" concept can still land. If real yields are already falling, Treasury demand is improving and credit remains calm, the market may have front-run the transition.

What to watch: HY OAS · VIX persistence · breadth · AI debt concessions · Treasury auctions · Korea deleveraging · HK dilution / capital raising.

UPDATED TIMING RULE: Autumn is a stress window, not a guaranteed date of capitulation.

PHASE III · H1 2027

The monetary transition

The first half of 2027 remains the cleanest window for a more conventional improvement in monetary conditions, but the mechanism is broader than "inflation falls, Fed cuts."

By then, the cumulative effect of high long yields, weaker labor, housing pressure, government refinancing sensitivity and corporate capital consumption may make the existing real-rate structure increasingly difficult to sustain. If inflation permits, the Fed can finally join the easing process that Treasury and market structure had already begun indirectly.

The sequence we would expect is:

hike expectations die → real yields peak → dollar softens → gold strengthens → Bitcoin's macro base improves → quality growth rerates → broader participation follows.

Parts of that sequence can begin before the actual first cut because markets price the expected architecture.

What to watch: labor trend · core PCE · real yields · policy-path pricing · stablecoin/bill growth · breadth.

PHASE IV · H2 2027

The AI return-on-capital test

By the second half of 2027, investors should finally have much better evidence on what the enormous 2025–26 infrastructure buildout actually earned.

The relevant variables become:

cloud revenue · inference margins · utilization · depreciation · power economics · project cancellations · free cash flow.

If monetization is strong while financing costs fall, the AI cycle can enter its most powerful phase. The survivors of the hard-money period have more infrastructure, less competition and visible proof just as capital becomes cheaper again.

That is the setup for the 2027 blow-off scenario.

If monetization disappoints while capex remains high, the same infrastructure becomes evidence of overbuilding. The cycle moves from scarcity to excess capacity.

That is the true fork.

What to watch: incremental AI revenue vs incremental invested capital · project cancellations · customer utilization · FCF inflection · credit quality.

云收入、推理利润率、利用率、折旧、电力经济性、项目取消与自由现金流。

若变现强劲而融资成本下降，硬资金阶段的幸存者就在资本变便宜时拥有更多基础设施、更少竞争与可见证据。

这构成2027 年最后加速情景的条件。

如果变现令人失望而资本开支仍高，同一套基础设施就会变成过度建设的证据，周期也会从稀缺转向过剩产能。

这才是真正的分岔。

12The next act is decided by the price of capital下一幕由资本价格决定

For most of the last decade, investors could treat capital almost like background infrastructure. Rates moved, the Fed spoke, stocks reacted—but financing was cheap enough that the deepest question rarely had to be asked.

AI changes that because AI is becoming physical.

Physical AI requires factories, chips, memory, power plants, transmission lines, transformers, cooling systems, buildings and debt. The deeper the boom moves into the physical economy, the more it has to compete with every other borrower for capital.

That includes the U.S. government.

The market's answer to that competition is the long-term yield.

If the yield remains tolerable, the cycle continues. If it falls naturally, the next phase becomes easier. If policymakers successfully manage it without reigniting inflation, the cycle can become larger. If inflation prevents relief, weak demand has to be destroyed. If the yield rises far enough to enter credit, the system eventually responds with much larger tools.

That is the decision tree.

Why gold moved first

Gold does not need to know which policy wins. It benefits when the distribution of outcomes becomes less friendly to long-duration nominal claims.

The final adjustment could come through lower spending, higher taxes, shorter debt maturity, a weaker dollar, lower policy rates, regulation or eventually central-bank accommodation. Gold only needs uncertainty around that adjustment to rise.

That is why its move before conventional easing matters.

Why Bitcoin moved early

Bitcoin responded to a different part of the same architecture. Its liquidity clock changed before the old calendar expected it to: a softer dollar, Treasury-market stress, stablecoin architecture and future policy expectations altered the backdrop before the Fed had delivered a clean easing cycle.

That does not make Bitcoin a safe Treasury hedge.

It makes the old cycle calendar less authoritative.

Why AI is not over

AI demand did not disappear because long yields rose. The rules changed.

Scarcity alone is no longer enough. The next winners have to prove that they can convert capital into cash at a return above the new hurdle rate. That is healthier than indiscriminate enthusiasm—and far more difficult.

The first AI trade rewarded conviction.

The next one will reward discrimination.

Not AI or no AI.

Which AI?

Not China or no China.

Which Chinese balance sheet?

Not hard assets or dollars.

Which asset under which monetary regime?

Not October low or no October low.

What evidence says the liquidity transition has actually occurred?

And not will America go broke?

The United States issues the world's reserve currency and has enormous productive capacity. A simplistic sovereign-default analogy misses the mechanism.

The more useful question is:

What price will holders of that reserve currency demand to finance the next stage of American expansion—and what will policymakers do when that price becomes unacceptable?

That is the price of duration.

The next market cycle will be decided by whether the United States can finance both its sovereign debt and the AI buildout at a tolerable long-term yield without forcing the adjustment into inflation, the dollar, credit—or all three.

在过去十年的大部分时间里，投资者几乎可以把资本当作背景设施。利率变化、美联储讲话、股票反应，但融资足够便宜，以至于最深层的问题很少必须被提出。

AI 改变了这一点，因为 AI 正在实体化。

实体 AI 需要工厂、芯片、存储、电厂、输电线、变压器、冷却系统、建筑与债务。热潮越深入实体经济，就越必须与其他每一个借款人争夺资本。

其中包括美国政府。

市场对这场竞争给出的答案，就是长期收益率。

如果这一收益率保持可承受，周期继续；如果它自然下降，下一阶段更容易；如果政策制定者在不重燃通胀的情况下管理它，周期可以变得更大；如果通胀阻止缓解，弱需求就必须被摧毁；如果收益率高到传入信用，体系最终会用更大的工具回应。

这就是决策树。

Why gold moved first为什么黄金先动

黄金不需要知道哪一项政策最终胜出。只要结果分布对长久期名义债权变得不那么友好，它就会受益。

最终调整可以来自削减支出、提高税收、缩短债务期限、美元走弱、降低政策利率、监管，或最终的央行宽松。黄金上涨只需要围绕这种调整的不确定性上升。

这正是黄金在传统宽松到来之前上涨的重要性。

Why Bitcoin moved early为什么比特币提前行动

比特币回应的是同一架构的另一部分。美元走软、美债市场压力、稳定币架构与未来政策预期，在美联储真正提供明确宽松周期之前就改变了背景，其流动性时钟也早于旧日历发生变化。

这并不会让比特币成为安全的美债对冲工具。

它只是让旧周期日历不再拥有那么高的权威。

Why AI is not over为什么 AI 尚未结束

AI 需求并未因为长端收益率上升而消失。改变的是规则。

仅有稀缺已经不够；下一批赢家必须证明自己能以高于新门槛收益率的回报把资本转化为现金。这比无差别狂热更健康，也困难得多。

第一笔 AI 交易奖励的是信念。

下一笔将奖励辨别力。

问题不再是要不要 AI。

而是哪一种 AI？

问题不再是要不要中国。

而是哪一张中国资产负债表？

问题不再是硬资产还是美元。

而是哪一种货币体制下的哪一种资产？

问题不再是十月有没有低点。

而是什么证据表明流动性转折确实发生？

同样，也不是美国会不会破产。

美国发行全球储备货币，也拥有庞大的生产能力。把它简单类比为普通主权违约，会错过真正的机制。

更有用的问题是：

储备货币持有人会要求什么价格，才愿意为美国下一阶段扩张融资？当这个价格变得不可接受时，政策制定者又会做什么？

这就是久期的价格。

下一个市场周期，将由美国能否以可承受的长期收益率同时为主权债务与 AI 建设融资，并避免把调整强行推入通胀、美元、信用——或三者同时——而决定。
