    Ch 01

    Dealers don't have opinions. They have inventory.

    When you trade an option, a market-maker usually takes the other side — and they don't want your directional risk, they want the spread. So they hedge with stock, mechanically, and they re-hedge every time price moves. Where option positioning concentrates, that re-hedging flow concentrates too. One caveat before anything else: true dealer inventory is not observable, so every map here assumes the standard convention — dealers long calls, short puts. It's an assumption, and we say so on every board.

    Ch 02

    Sticky tape, slippery tape.

    Net positive gamma means dealer hedging leans against price — selling into strength, bidding into weakness near their strikes — so moves get absorbed. That's sticky. Net negative gamma means hedging chases price, so the same shove travels further. That's slippery. Same market, same news; the terrain decides how far the move goes.

    Ch 03

    The Flip: where the terrain changes law.

    The flip is the price where net dealer gamma crosses zero — the border between sticky and slippery. It is a border, not a tripwire: nothing detonates on contact. But the same 1% shove behaves differently on each side, and that difference is worth knowing before it happens, not after.

    Ch 04

    Ceiling and Floor: walls that hold until they don't.

    The Ceiling is the largest positive-gamma strike overhead — advances into it meet hedging that leans harder the closer price gets. The Floor mirrors it below. Two honest caveats: containment is a tendency we measure in the track record, not a promise; and walls are rebuilt every session as positioning migrates. Yesterday's ceiling can be today's open air.

    Ch 06

    The other axis: what if vol moves first?

    GEX answers "what does hedging do if price moves." VEX asks the same question about a jump in implied volatility. A vol shock re-prices every option at once, and dealer vega hedging has its own terrain — strikes that look sleepy on the gamma board can be live on the vol board. One market, two weather maps; the toggle is the same everywhere in the product.

    Ch 07

    The move the market already paid for.

    From at-the-money IV you can back out the move options are pricing: spot ± spot · IV · √(t/252) · k. The textbook k = 1.96 draws the familiar ~95% band. We also draw a learned band: the smallest multiplier that would have contained the next session's range about two-thirds of the time, calibrated per ticker over years of history. Both are context, not fences — and we show you every miss.

    Ch 08

    Trust is a measurement, not a vibe.

    Three instruments keep this map honest. The Track Record: every "has tended to" on this page is reconstructed over years of history, with failures drawn as prominently as the wins. The Trust Index: one number per ticker for how well the map has described that specific name — thin options books earn low trust, and we print the low number instead of guessing. The Ledger: every pre-open board is SHA-256 hashed and sealed before the bell, so nobody can quietly redraw the map after the move.
